# Khirbet el-Qom Inscription (c. 750-700 BCE)

**Location:** Khirbet el-Qom (biblical Maqqedah?), West Bank, territory of ancient Judah (between Lachish and Hebron)
**Date:** Second half of 8th century BCE
**Discovery:** Excavated by William G. Dever, 1967 (Tomb 2)
**Medium:** Stone tomb inscription
**Script:** Paleo-Hebrew

---

## The Uriyahu Inscription (Tomb 2)

**Paleo-Hebrew text (reconstructed):**
  )ryhw h($r ktbh
  brk )ryhw lyhwh
  w[m.sryh l])$rth hw$( lh
  l)nyhw

(Reading debated, especially line 3)

**English Translation (scholarly consensus, Dever and others):**
"Uriyahu the honourable has written this.
Blessed is/be Uriyahu by Yahweh,
And from his oppressors by his asherah he has saved him.
By Oniyahu."

Alternative rendering:
"Uriyahu the rich has caused it to be written:
Blessed be Uriyahu to Yahweh;
And from his enemies, by his Asherah, he has delivered him.
— By Oniyahu."

**Key features:**
- *lyhwh* — "by/to Yahweh" (no place name qualifier, unlike Kuntillet Ajrud)
- *l)$rth* — "by/to his Asherah" (same formulation as Kuntillet Ajrud)
- Associated with a "magic hand" symbol (apotropaic)
- Unlike Kuntillet Ajrud, no geographic epithet for Yahweh (suggesting post-fall of Samaria date)

---

## Significance

Confirms the pattern seen at [[kuntillet-ajrud-inscriptions]] — Yahweh worshipped alongside Asherah in 8th century Judah. The absence of a place name for Yahweh may reflect the post-722 BCE situation after the fall of the Northern Kingdom: with Samaria destroyed, there was no longer a need to distinguish "Yahweh of Samaria" from other Yahwehs. In Judah, Yahweh could simply be "Yahweh."

The "magic hand" symbol suggests apotropaic (protective) function — the inscription was placed in a tomb, invoking Yahweh and his Asherah for protection of the deceased. This is domestic/popular religion, not state-sponsored temple worship.

The trajectory from Kuntillet Ajrud (YHWH + Asherah, with geographic epithets) through Khirbet el-Qom (YHWH + Asherah, no epithet) to [[ketef-hinnom]] (YHWH alone, priestly blessing) traces the gradual elimination of the goddess from Israelite religion over approximately 200 years.
