# Primary Source: Lachish Ostraca (Letters)

**Location:** Tel Lachish (Tell ed-Duweir), Shephelah region, Judah
**Date:** c. 590 BCE (final months before Nebuchadnezzar's destruction of Lachish and Jerusalem, 588/586 BCE)
**Discovery:** Excavated by James Leslie Starkey, January-February 1935, third campaign of the Wellcome excavations
**Published:** Harry Torczyner (Naftali Herz Tur-Sinai), *Lachish I: The Lachish Letters*, Oxford University Press, 1938
**Medium:** Clay ostraca (pottery sherds), carbon ink inscriptions
**Script:** Paleo-Hebrew / Phoenician script
**Present location:** British Museum, London (17 ostraca: ME 125701-ME 125707, ME 125715a); Rockefeller Museum, East Jerusalem (Letter 6 and others)
**Standard reference:** KAI 192-199

---

## Overview

The Lachish Letters are the most vivid primary source for late monarchic Judahite religion and military communication. Written by a junior officer named Hoshaiah (Hoshayahu) to his commander Yaush (Ya'osh) at Lachish, a major Judahite fortress, they capture the final weeks before the Babylonian army under Nebuchadnezzar II overran Lachish and then Jerusalem. Jeremiah 34:7 confirms that Lachish and Azekah were the last two fortified cities of Judah standing against Babylon.

The letters were probably written on fragments of the same broken clay pot over a short period. They open with standardized epistolary blessings invoking **YHWH** — providing direct evidence of Yahwistic religious formulae in everyday military correspondence. No other deities are mentioned, suggesting official monolatry (at minimum) in military contexts.

## The Texts: Letters 1-6

### Letter Number 1

**Text (English translation, scholarly consensus):**

> Gemariah son of Hissilyahu, Jaazaniah son of Tobshillem, Hageb son of Jaazaniah, Mibtahyahu son of Jeremiah, Mattaniah son of Neriah.

**Notes:** This is a list of names rather than a letter proper. All are theophoric Yahwistic names (ending in *-yahu*, "Yahweh").

---

### Letter Number 2

**Text:**

> To my lord, Ya'ush, may YHWH cause my lord to hear tiding(s) of peace today, this very day! Who is your servant, a dog, that my lord remembered his [se]rvant? May YHWH make known(?) to my [lor]d a matter of which you do not know.

**Key features:**
- Formulaic opening: "May YHWH cause my lord to hear tidings of peace" — identical pattern to every letter
- Self-deprecation: "Who is your servant, a dog" — standard ancient Near Eastern epistolary humility

---

### Letter Number 3 (THE KEY LETTER)

**Text:**

> Your servant, Hoshaiah, sent to inform my lord, Ya'ush: May YHWH cause my lord to hear tidings of peace and tidings of good. And now, open the ear of your servant concerning the letter which you sent to your servant last evening because the heart of your servant is ill since your sending it to your servant. And inasmuch as my lord said "Don't you know how to read a letter?" As YHWH lives if anyone has ever tried to read me a letter! And as for every letter that comes to me, if I read it. And furthermore, I will grant it as nothing. And to your servant it has been reported saying: The commander of the army Coniah son of Elnatan, has gone down to go to Egypt and he sent to commandeer Hodaviah son of Ahijah and his men from here. And as for the letter of Tobiah, the servant of the king, which came to Shallum, the son of Jaddua, from the prophet, saying, "Be on guard!" your ser[va]nt is sending it to my lord.

**Key features:**
- **"As YHWH lives"** (*ḥay YHWH*) — oath formula invoking YHWH's name, standard in biblical narratives
- **"The prophet"** — one of the few extra-biblical references to prophetic activity in Iron Age Judah. The letter from "Tobiah, servant of the king" was relayed via a prophet, showing prophets functioned as information channels
- **Literacy defense**: Hoshaiah is defensive about being accused of illiteracy — showing that literacy was expected of military officers
- **Coniah to Egypt**: a military commander has gone to Egypt (possibly seeking alliance against Babylon — cf. Jeremiah's warnings against Egypt)
- Physical: ~15 cm tall × 11 cm wide, 21 lines (front: lines 1-16, back: lines 17-21)

### Letter Number 4

**Text:**

> May YHWH cause my [lord] to hear, this very day, tidings of good. And now, according to everything which my lord has sent, this has your servant done. I wrote on the sheet according to everything which [you] sent [t]o me. And inasmuch as my lord sent to me concerning the matter of Bet Harapid, there is no one there. And as for Semachiah, Shemaiah took him and brought him up to the city. And your servant is not sending him there any[more ---], but when morning comes round [---]. And may (my lord) be apprised that we are watching for the fire signals of Lachish according to all the signs which my lord has given, because we cannot see Azeqah.

**Key features:**
- **Fire signals**: Hoshaiah's outpost is watching for signal fires from Lachish — a military communication network
- **"We cannot see Azekah"**: The most haunting line in the corpus. Azekah was the other fortress still holding out (Jeremiah 34:7). The fact that they can no longer see Azekah's signal fires means **Azekah has already fallen**. The Babylonian noose is tightening. Lachish is next.
- Confirms the Jeremiah 34:7 picture of the final campaign

### Letter Number 5

**Text:**

> May YHWH cause my [lo]rd to hear tidings of pea[ce] and of good, [now today, now this very da]y! Who is your servant, a dog, that you [s]ent your servant the [letters? Like]wise has your servant returned the letters to my lord. May YHWH cause you to see the harvest successfully, this very day! Will Tobiah of the royal family c[o]me to your servant?

**Key features:**
- "Who is your servant, a dog" — same self-deprecating formula as Letter 2
- **Tobiah of the royal family** — the same Tobiah mentioned in Letter 3 as relaying the prophetic message. A figure close to the king

---

### Letter Number 6

**Text:**

> To my lord, Ya'ush, may YHWH give my lord peace at this time! Who is your servant, a dog, that my lord should send the letter of the king and the letters of the officers, saying: 'Read, I pray you, and see.' But the words of the officers are not good, but to weaken your hands and to slacken the hands of the men who are working... And my lord, will you not write to them, saying: 'Why are you doing this?'... As YHWH your God lives... for since your servant... the king... And it came to pass when... your servant...

**Key features:**
- **"The letter of the king"** — letters from the king (Zedekiah) himself were circulating among officers
- **Accusation of demoralization**: unnamed officers are accused of "weakening the hands" — the exact phrase used in Jeremiah 38:4 for those who urged surrender to Babylon. Internal political conflict at the edge of disaster
- **"As YHWH your God lives"** — oath formula, evidence of personal Yahwism

## Significance for the Truth Project

1. **YHWH as the sole invoked deity in military correspondence**: Every letter opens with a YHWH blessing formula. No Baal, no Asherah, no other deities appear. This suggests that by the late 7th/early 6th century BCE, at least in official Judahite military contexts, YHWH was treated as the exclusive national god. This is consistent with the Deuteronomistic reforms but — critically — shows the reforms had actual practical reach, not just literary existence.

2. **"The prophet" as an operational figure**: Letter III mentions a prophet relaying messages from the king's circle. This is not a literary trope — it's a real functionary in the information network of a besieged fortress. Prophets were not just biblical characters; they were recognized social actors whose messages were taken seriously enough to forward in military correspondence.

3. **Literacy and administration**: The letters demonstrate that mid-ranking military officers (not just scribal elites) were literate and expected to read letters. Hoshaiah's defensive tone about being accused of illiteracy is telling — the accusation stung because literacy was expected.

4. **"We cannot see Azekah"**: The most direct archaeological confirmation of the Jeremiah narrative. The Lachish letters and Jeremiah 34:7 are the only two sources describing the final Babylonian campaign against Judah, and they agree: Azekah fell first, Lachish was the next domino, Jerusalem followed.

5. **Theophoric names**: Every personal name in the letters is Yahwistic (*-yahu* suffix). No Baal names, no El names. Name evidence is a strong indicator of which deity was culturally dominant.
