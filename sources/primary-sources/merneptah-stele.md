# Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BCE)

**Also known as:** The Israel Stele, Victory Stele of Merneptah
**Pharaoh:** Merneptah (r. 1213-1203 BCE), son of Ramesses II
**Date:** c. 1208 BCE (Year 5 of Merneptah)
**Discovery:** Flinders Petrie at Thebes, 1896
**Current location:** Egyptian Museum, Cairo
**Medium:** Black granite stele (reused back of Amenhotep III stele)
**Language:** Egyptian hieroglyphs, 28 lines

---

## The Canaan/Israel Section (Lines 26-28)

**Egyptian Hieroglyphs (transliteration of line 27 — the Israel line):**

  *Y.si.ri.A-r | fk.t | bn | pr.t | =f*
  Ysriar | feket | ben | peret | -ef
  "Israel | is laid waste | [negative] | seed/grain | his/its"

---

## English Translation (Lines 26-28, scholarly consensus)

The princes are prostrate, saying: "Peace!"
Not one raises his head among the Nine Bows.
Desolation is for Tjehenu (Libya);
**Hatti** is pacified;
Plundered is the **Canaan** with every evil;
Carried off is **Ashkelon**;
Seized upon is **Gezer**;
**Yanoam** is made non-existent;
**Israel is laid waste — its seed is no more;**
**Kharru** (Hurru/Syria) has become a widow because of Egypt.
All lands together are pacified.
Everyone who was restless has been bound.

---

## Key Epigraphic Details

1. **Determinatives are crucial:** The hieroglyphic writing uses different determinatives (classifying signs):
   - *Canaan* — determinative for **foreign land**
   - *Ashkelon, Gezer, Yanoam* — determinative for **city/town**
   - ***Israel*** — determinative for **a people/ethnic group** (man + woman + plural strokes) — NOT a land or city

   This tells us that in the Egyptian perception of c. 1208 BCE, **Israel was understood as a people group, not a settled state or territory** — consistent with a semi-nomadic or recently settled tribal population.

2. **"Its seed is no more"** — Typical Egyptian rhetorical hyperbole. "Seed" (*prt*) could mean literal grain/offspring; this is a standard victory boast, not a literal report of genocide.

3. **Merneptah's campaign:** The stele primarily records a Libyan war. The Canaan campaign may be a brief summary of an earlier action, or a propagandistic claim extending to Egypt's sphere of influence.

---

## Significance

- **Earliest mention of "Israel" outside the Bible** — and the only mention of Israel in any ancient Egyptian text.
- **Dated to the late 13th century BCE** — this is the *terminus ad quem*: Israel as a recognizable entity existed in Canaan by 1208 BCE.
- **Israel as a people, not a state** — the determinative confirms an early-stage tribal/ethnic entity.
- Together with the [[mesha-stele]], [[tel-dan-stele]], and Kurkh Monoliths, one of only four Iron Age inscriptions mentioning Israel by name.
