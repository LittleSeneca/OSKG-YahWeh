# Kuntillet Ajrud Inscriptions (c. 800 BCE)

**Location:** Kuntillet Ajrud (Horvat Teman), northeast Sinai Peninsula
**Date:** Late 9th / early 8th century BCE (carbon-14: 801-770 BCE)
**Discovery:** Excavated by Ze'ev Meshel, 1975-76
**Medium:** Inscriptions on pottery (pithoi) and wall plaster
**Script:** Paleo-Hebrew / Phoenician script

---

## Inscription 3.1 (Pithos A) — "Yahweh of Samaria and his Asherah"

**Paleo-Hebrew text (reconstructed):**
  )mr )$yw hm[lk] )mr lyhlyw wlyw($h wl[...] brkt )tkm lyhwh $mrn wl)$rth

**English Translation (scholarly consensus):**
"Says )A$iyaw the king: Say to Yahēliyaw, and to Yaw(āsah, and to [...] I bless you all by Yahweh of Samaria and by his Asherah."

**Key terms:**
- *yhwh $mrn* — "Yahweh of Samaria" (Yahweh associated with a specific location)
- *)$rth* — "his Asherah" (the goddess Asherah, or a cult object, with possessive suffix)

---

## Inscription 3.6 (Pithos B) — "Yahweh of Teman and his Asherah"

**Paleo-Hebrew text (reconstructed, multi-line):**
  (1) )mr
  (2) )mryw )-
  (3) mrl )dny
  (4) h$lm )t
  (5) brktk ly-
  (6) hwh tmn
  (7) wl)$rth yb-
  (8) rk wy$mrk
  (9) wyhy (m )dn-
  (10) y )rk .hym
  (11) b$lm )mr h)

**English Translation (scholarly consensus):**
(1) Says (2) )Amaryaw: ") (3) MRL, my lord, (4) is all well with you? (5) I bless you to Ya- (6) hweh of Teman (7) and to his Asherah. May he bl- (8) ess you and protect you, (9) and may he be with my lo- (10) rd as a long life (11) in peace." says he.

**Key terms:**
- *yhwh tmn* — "Yahweh of Teman" (Teman = southern region/Edom)
- *wl)$rth* — "and to his Asherah"
- Formulaic epistolary blessing structure

## Wall Plaster Inscriptions

Fragmentary plaster inscriptions also found, including partial references to *yhwh* (Yahweh, *yhw*), *)l* (El), and theophoric names.

---

## Significance

These inscriptions provide the earliest extrabiblical evidence of Yahweh paired with Asherah, suggesting that in popular Israelite religion of the 9th-8th centuries BCE, Asherah was worshipped as Yahweh's consort. The references to "Yahweh of Samaria" and "Yahweh of Teman" indicate multiple localized manifestations of Yahweh. The "Yahweh of Teman" reference connects Yahweh to the southern/Edomite region, consistent with the Kenite/Midianite hypothesis.

Together with the [[khirbet-el-qom-inscription]], they form the primary epigraphic evidence for Asherah as Yahweh's consort in Iron Age Israel and Judah. The pattern of YHWH-with-consort also appears centuries later at [[elephantine-papyri]] (Anat-Yahu). The trajectory runs from these 8th-century inscriptions through to the exclusive Yahwism of the [[ketef-hinnom]] silver amulets (c. 600 BCE).
