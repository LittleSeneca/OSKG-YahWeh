# Soleb Shasu Inscription (c. 1400 BCE)

**Location:** Temple of Soleb, Nubia (modern Sudan), built by Amenhotep III
**Also:** Amarah West, Nubia (copy by Ramesses II, 13th century BCE)
**Date:** 14th century BCE (Amenhotep III, r. 1390-1353 BCE)
**Language:** Egyptian hieroglyphs
**Medium:** Column base inscriptions listing Egypt's enemies

---

## Hieroglyphic Rendering

The name appears in a list of Shasu (nomadic) territories:

  tA SAsw yhwA

**Gardiner sign list:** N16 (tA) = "land", M8 (SA) = pool with lotus, M23 (sw) = sedge, phonetic complements w-w-y-h-h-V4-wA-G1-A

---

## Translation

**"The Land of the Shasu of YhwA"** (or "Yahweh-land of the Shasu")

Variant rendering: *tA SASw yhw* — "The Land of the Shasu: Yhw"

---

## The Amarah West List (Seti I / Ramesses II, 13th century BCE)

Six Shasu territories are listed on column bases at Amarah West, Nubia:

1. *s'rr* — Seir (Edom)
2. *rbn* — Laban/Labanah
3. *sm't* — possibly related to the Simeonites
4. *wrbr* — possibly Wadi Bar
5. ***yhw*** — **Yahweh**
6. *pysps* — unknown

---

## Scholarly Significance

1. **Earliest possible reference to Yahweh:** Michael Astour observed that "the hieroglyphic rendering corresponds very precisely to the Hebrew Tetragrammaton YHWH, or Yahweh, and antedates the hitherto oldest occurrence of that divine name — on the [[mesha-stele]] — by over five hundred years."

2. **Yahweh's southern origins:** The Shasu were nomadic pastoralists from the Edom/Midian/Seir region. This aligns with biblical traditions that Yahweh "came forth from Seir" (Judges 5:4, Deuteronomy 33:2, Habakkuk 3:3).

3. **Toponym or theonym:** *yhwA* could be a place name ("Yahweh-land") or a divine name. Most scholars see it as a toponym derived from the divine name — a region named after the god worshipped there.

4. **Pre-Israelite Yahweh worship:** K. Van Der Toorn concludes: "By the 14th century BC, before the cult of Yahweh had reached Israel, groups of Edomites and Midianites worshipped Yahweh as their god."

5. **The Kenite/Midianite Hypothesis:** This inscription supports the theory that Yahweh worship originated among the Midianites/Kenites of the southern Transjordan and was later adopted by early Israel. The [[deut-32-8-9-qumran-variant]] provides the theological framework — YHWH as a son of Elyon who received Israel as his territorial allotment.
