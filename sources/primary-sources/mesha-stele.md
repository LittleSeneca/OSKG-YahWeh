# Primary Source: Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone)

**Location:** Dibon (modern Dhiban, Jordan), ancient capital of Moab
**Date:** c. 840 BCE (reign of King Mesha of Moab, mid-9th century BCE)
**Discovery:** Discovered intact by Frederick Augustus Klein (Anglican missionary) in August 1868; deliberately smashed by the Bani Hamida Bedouin tribe in November 1869; fragments recovered and reconstructed from a papier-mache squeeze by Charles Simon Clermont-Ganneau
**Medium:** Black basalt stele, approximately 1 meter tall, 60 cm wide, 60 cm thick
**Script:** Moabite language in Paleo-Hebrew / Phoenician script, 34 surviving lines
**Present location:** Louvre Museum, Paris (since 1873)
**Standard reference:** KAI 181

---

## Overview

The Mesha Stele (also called the Moabite Stone) is the longest Iron Age inscription ever found in the Southern Levant and "a corner-stone of Semitic epigraphy and history." It records King Mesha of Moab's victories over Israel, attributing his success to **Chemosh**, the national god of Moab. It contains the **earliest certain extra-biblical reference to YHWH** — "I took from there the vessels of YHWH and I dragged them before Chemosh."

The stele's narrative parallels 2 Kings 3:4-27 (the revolt of Mesha against Israel) with some differences. It is one of only four known Iron Age inscriptions mentioning "Israel" (with the Merneptah Stele, Tel Dan Stele, and Kurkh Monolith).

---

## Text: English Translation

The most widely available translation is by James King (1878), based on the work of Clermont-Ganneau and Dr. Ginsberg. A century and a half of scholarship has refined the reading, but the core content is stable. A more recent scholarly translation by Shmuel Ahituv (2008) is authoritative.

**Translation (King 1878, with key line numbers noted):**

I am Mesha, son of Chemosh-gad, king of Moab, the Dibonite. My father reigned over Moab thirty years, and I have reigned after my father. And I have built this sanctuary for Chemosh in Karchah, a sanctuary of salvation, for he saved me from all aggressors, and made me look upon all mine enemies with contempt.

Omri was king of Israel, and oppressed Moab during many days, and Chemosh was angry with his aggressions. His son succeeded him, and he also said, I will oppress Moab. In my days he said, Let us go, and I will see my desire upon him and his house, and Israel said, I shall destroy it for ever. Now Omri took the land of Madeba, and occupied it in his day, and in the days of his son, forty years. And Chemosh had mercy on it in my time. And I built Baal-meon and made therein the ditch, and I built Kiriathaim.

And the men of Gad dwelled in the country of Ataroth from ancient times, and the king of Israel fortified Ataroth. I assaulted the wall and captured it, and killed all the warriors of the city for the well-pleasing of Chemosh and Moab, and I removed from it all the spoil, and offered it before Chemosh in Kirjath; and I placed therein the men of Siran, and the men of Mochrath.

**And Chemosh said to me, Go take Nebo against Israel, and I went in the night and I fought against it from the break of day till noon, and I took it: and I killed in all seven thousand men... women and maidens, for I devoted them to Ashtar-Chemosh; and I took from it the vessels of Jehovah, and offered them before Chemosh.** [Lines 14-18 — the critical YHWH reference]

And the king of Israel fortified Jahaz, and occupied it, when he made war against me, and Chemosh drove him out before me, and I took from Moab two hundred men in all, and placed them in Jahaz, and took it to annex it to Dibon.

I built Karchah the wall of the forest, and the wall of the Hill. I have built its gates and I have built its towers. I have built the palace of the king, and I made the prisons for the criminals within the wall. And there were no wells in the interior of the wall in Karchah. And I said to all the people, 'Make you every man a well in his house.' And I dug the ditch for Karchah with the chosen men of Israel. I built Aroer, and I made the road across the Arnon. I built Beth-bamoth, for it was destroyed. I built Bezer, for it was in ruins...

*(The remaining lines describe further building projects and are fragmentary.)*

## Key Scholarly Translation (Ahituv 2008)

Shmuel Ahituv's *Echoes from the Past* (2008) provides the most current scholarly translation. The YHWH passage reads:

> "I took from there the [ves]sels of YHWH, and I dragged them before Chemosh."

The word for "vessels" (*kly*) is partially reconstructed, and "dragged" (*sḥb*) implies ritual humiliation — the captured cult objects of YHWH are displayed before Chemosh as spoils of war.

---

## Key Features

1. **"Vessels of YHWH"** — The Tetragrammaton (*yhwh*) appears in clear, uncontested context. YHWH is treated as the god of Israel whose cult objects can be captured and humiliated. This is a Moabite perspective on YHWH: he is real but subordinate to Chemosh (when Chemosh is ascendant).

2. **Chemosh and YHWH operate on the same theological logic**: Both national gods get angry with their people, permit foreign oppression, then return to favor and grant victory. The Moabite understanding of Chemosh mirrors Israelite understanding of YHWH — national deity, divine warrior, covenant enforcer. This parallel is crucial evidence for the "national god" model of early Israelite religion.

3. **"And Chemosh said to me, Go take Nebo"** — Divine communication with Chemosh, identical in pattern to YHWH's instructions to Israelite kings in the Deuteronomistic History. Shows that direct divine speech was a shared Northwest Semitic religious convention, not unique to Israel.

4. **"I devoted them to Ashtar-Chemosh"** — The term for "devoted" (*ḥrm*) is the same Hebrew root as *ḥerem* (the ban, ritual destruction). The practice of herem warfare was not unique to Israel; it was a shared West Semitic practice of devoting conquered peoples to a deity.

5. **"House of Omri"** — Israel is called the House of Omri, the standard Assyrian designation for the Northern Kingdom. Omri's dynasty was the internationally recognized ruling house of Israel.

6. **"Son of Omri"** — This unnamed son is probably Ahab (or possibly Jehoram/Joram), dating the events to the mid-9th century BCE.

7. **Proposed David reference (line 31)** — Some scholars (including Andre Lemaire) read *bt[d]wd* ("House of David") in a damaged section of line 31. If correct, this would be the earliest David reference, predating the Tel Dan Stele. The reading is widely debated and not universally accepted. The reconstruction depends on a single damaged letter.

---

## Significance for the Truth Project

1. **YHWH as Israel's national god from a foreign perspective**: The Mesha Stele provides an external witness to YHWH worship in the 9th century BCE. A Moabite king recognizes YHWH as the god of Israel and treats his cult objects as valid spoils of war. This is not theological polemic — it's a victory monument assuming YHWH's reality.

2. **Chemosh as YHWH's theological mirror**: The stele demonstrates that the pattern "national god → anger → defeat → repentance → restoration" was not a uniquely Israelite theological framework. Moab understood Chemosh in precisely the same way. This supports the scholarly consensus that early Israelite Yahwism was a variant of a broader Northwest Semitic national-god pattern, not a unique revelatory framework.

3. **Dating YHWH worship**: With the Soleb Shasu inscription (c. 1400 BCE) as the earliest possible reference and the Mesha Stele (c. 840 BCE) as the earliest certain reference, we bracket the development of YHWH worship in the southern Levant across approximately 500 years.

4. **The vessels of YHWH**: The captured cult objects imply a functioning YHWH sanctuary at Nebo (in Moabite territory) with physical ritual vessels. This is evidence for Yahwistic cult sites outside Jerusalem and outside Israelite political control in the 9th century.
