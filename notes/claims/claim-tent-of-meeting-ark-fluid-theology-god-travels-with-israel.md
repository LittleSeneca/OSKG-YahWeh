# sommer-bog-4.2: The Tent of Meeting and the Ark reflect fluid theology

**Source:** [[Sommer — Chapter 4 — Sacred Space 1]] — Benjamin Sommer, *The Bodies of God* (2009), Chapter 4

## The Claim

The Tent of Meeting (Exodus 33) and the Ark are the oldest Israelite sacred space traditions, and both reflect fluid theology. The Tent contains one of God's bodies; the Ark is a portable throne for another. Both are mobile because God has multiple bodies — the heavenly body stays in heaven while the traveling body moves with Israel.

## Evidence

**The Tent of Meeting (Exodus 33):**
- Moses sets up a tent "outside the camp"
- "Yhwh would come down to the tent surrounded by (or in the form of?) a pillar of cloud, and Moses would speak with God face to face, as a man speaks with his friend" (Exodus 33:9, 11)
- This is J/E material — fluid theology
- God has a body (face!); Moses sees it
- The tent contains one of God's bodies
- Simultaneously, God also dwells in heaven
- No philosophical explanation needed — the fluidity model makes sense of it

**The Ark:**
- The Ark was a portable throne/footstool for one of Yhwh's bodies
- 1 Samuel 4:4: "Yhwh of hosts who sits enthroned on the cherubim" — the cherubim are the Ark's lid
- The Ark moves; Yhwh's embodied presence moves with it
- This is physically impossible if Yhwh has only ONE body — perfectly natural if he has MULTIPLE

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Exodus 33 describes Moses speaking with God "face to face" at a tent outside the camp; the Ark's mobility makes no sense if God has only one body but is perfectly natural under the fluidity model.

## Stakes

The Tent and Ark traditions are the most concrete evidence for fluid theology in early Israel. If the Exodus 33 tradition is genuinely old (J/E), then the earliest Israelite conception of sacred space assumed divine fluidity. The later anti-fluid revolution (D/P) had to work hard to suppress this older understanding.

## Disagreement

**Alternative reading:** The Tent and Ark contain God's "presence" metaphorically — they're symbols of divine accompaniment, not literal divine bodies. The language is poetic, not ontological.

## Assessment

The Tent of Meeting tradition (Exodus 33) is some of Sommer's best evidence. Moses speaks with God "face to face" — the text is unambiguous about physical encounter. And yet God also dwells in heaven. The only way both statements can be literally true is if God has multiple bodies. The Ark extends this logic: a portable throne for a portable body. The anti-fluid revolution had to explain away both traditions.
