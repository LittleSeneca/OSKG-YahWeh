# kaufmann-ri-8.4: The golden calves were YHWH worship in an illicit form, not pagan idolatry

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch VIII — The Monarchy]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch VIII

## The Claim

"It is perfectly plain that Jeroboam and the kings of Israel who perpetuated his 'sins' did not 'cast YHWH behind their backs' or regard the calves as 'other gods' (I Kings 14:9). Jehu, the prophetically appointed extirpator of Canaanite Baalism, does not remove the calves. Nor do Elijah and Elisha utter a word against them." The calves "must have belonged (like the image of Micah) to the cult of YHWH. They were NOT conceived of as representations of YHWH." Rather, "the calf or bull commonly served as the pedestal of Near Eastern gods... Jeroboam, however, adopted only the calf, without the figure of a standing or riding god. Corresponding to the cherubs of the ark, they represented only the vehicle, not the divine rider." "In essence, then, there is no difference between the calves and the cherubs."

## Evidence

- Jehu destroys Baalism but leaves the calves standing — clear evidence they weren't considered Baal worship
- Elijah and Elisha, fierce opponents of Baal, never mention the calves
- The biblical story blames Jeroboam for "inventing a festival and appointing non-Levite priests" — "what additional sin can possibly consist in worshiping golden calves in this manner?"
- The calves were likely of the winged type, "no more than a variation on the theme of the cherub"
- "The rejection of the image of a divine rider upon the calf is the crucial evidence for the transformation worked by the Israelite idea"
- Jeroboam's calves "were designed by Aaronide priests" — "It is difficult to avoid the conclusion that... the calves belonged to the same priestly tradition from which the cherubs of the ark stemmed"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Well-supported by Jehu's behavior, the cherub analogy, and the silence of Elijah and Elisha; broadly accepted in current scholarship.

## Stakes

Whether the northern kingdom was fundamentally apostate (Judean polemic) or practiced a legitimate variant of YHWH worship (plausible northern self-understanding). If Kaufmann is right, the Judean condemnation of Jeroboam's calves is theological polemic, not historical fact.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The biblical historians of Kings — Kaufmann is explicitly reading against the grain of the Deuteronomistic History. But in contemporary scholarship, Kaufmann's reading has considerable support. Römer agrees that Jeroboam's calves were YHWH images, not foreign gods. Cross argued that the bull iconography reflected El imagery originally associated with YHWH.

**Alternative reading:** Even if the calves were YHWH worship, the biblical polemic against them reflects a genuine theological dispute. The Deuteronomistic historians viewed ANY cult image — even of YHWH — as idolatrous. The aniconic principle that developed in Judah saw the northern calves as inherently corrupting, even if northerners believed they were worshipping YHWH.

## Assessment

This is the strongest claim in the chapter and one of Kaufmann's most important contributions. The evidence that Jehu preserved the calves while destroying Baalism is almost impossible to explain if the calves represented foreign gods. The cherub analogy is persuasive and helps make sense of how the calves could be genuinely YHWHistic while also genuinely offensive to aniconic sensibilities. This claim has aged well and is broadly accepted in contemporary scholarship, even by scholars who reject Kaufmann's larger thesis.
