# kaufmann-ri-5.4: P's High Priesthood and Tent Are Pre-Exilic, Not Second Temple

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch V — The Sources]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch V

## The Claim

The critical view that P's high priest and tent mirror the post-exilic theocracy "will not bear examination." P's portrayal reflects pre-exilic realities.

## Evidence

1. **P's attitude toward monarchy is favorable.** Genesis 17:6, 16; 35:11 promise kings. "P's representation of the monarchy as a divine blessing...is enough to show that its desert camp is not intended to depict the ideal form of Israelite society" — it's not polemicizing against monarchy as a theocracy would.

2. **P's camp is an armed camp, not a church.** Rules of war (Num 10:9), division of booty (31:25ff), conquest of Canaan — "its purpose is not cultic exercise under the protection of foreign rulers."

3. **The prophet Moses — not the priest Aaron — leads the camp.** "The importance of Aaron is restricted to the realm of the cult...In the camp itself, the priest has no authority." This contrasts with the post-exilic situation where priesthood held political authority.

4. **High priestly anointing was pre-exilic.** "Precisely in postexilic times high priests were no longer anointed. That P makes unction a mark of high priesthood is thus a sign of its antiquity."

5. **The tent is an oracular/prophetic vehicle, not a Second Temple reflex.** The Urim and Thummim, divine revelation, lawgiving functions — "by postexilic times the ancient channels of divine revelation were all but stopped up." The Second Temple "was never a priestly, let alone a prophetic, oracle."

6. **The roles of post-exilic temple personnel are absent from P.** Porters and singers of the Second Temple "are not even hinted at in P (contrast the anachronism of Chronicles which predates them to David's time)."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann identifies genuine discontinuities between P's tent and the Second Temple that expose a real weakness in simplistic "P = Second Temple program" readings. But archaic features could equally reflect deliberate archaizing rather than genuine antiquity.

## Stakes

If P's institutions are pre-exilic, the Wellhausen picture of an evolving priesthood culminating in post-exilic hierocracy is backwards.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Critics note that idealized portraits don't need to match present reality point-for-point. P's tent is an IDEAL — not a photograph of any actual institution. The mismatch between P's tent and the Second Temple doesn't prove P is early; it proves P is UTOPIAN. P presents what the priesthood SHOULD be (prophetic, oracular, led by an anointed high priest) — not what it actually was in the compromised reality of Persian Yehud.

**Alternative reading:** P's idealized picture functions as a charter for priestly authority precisely IN the post-exilic period. The mismatch between ideal and reality is the POINT — P provides the blueprint that the Second Temple FAILED to live up to. The archaic features (anointing, Urim) are nostalgic, not contemporary.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's observations about discontinuities between P and the Second Temple are genuinely interesting. But the presence of archaic features doesn't prove antiquity — it could equally prove deliberate archaizing. Kaufmann needs to show why archaizing is IMPOSSIBLE, and he doesn't. P may be an exilic/post-exilic UTOPIAN vision that deliberately looks back to an idealized Mosaic past.
