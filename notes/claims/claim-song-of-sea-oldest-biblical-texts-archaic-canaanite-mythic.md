# cross-cmhe-ii-iii.3: The Song of the Sea is among the oldest biblical texts

**Source:** [[Cross — Parts II-III — Divine Warrior and League]] — Frank Moore Cross, *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* (1973), Part II, Chapter 6

## The Claim

Exodus 15 (the Song of the Sea) is one of the oldest texts in the Bible — and it is constructed almost entirely from Canaanite mythic motifs. Cross dates it to the 12th-11th centuries BCE based on archaic linguistic features. If this dating is correct, the Song of the Sea is VERY early evidence for Yahweh's absorption of Ba'l's warrior mythology — earlier than the monarchy, earlier than the settlement, possibly contemporary with the events it describes.

## Evidence

| Exodus 15 | Ba'l Cycle (KTU 1.1-1.6) |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| "Yahweh is a man of war" | Ba'l the warrior |
| "Your right hand, Yahweh, shatters the enemy" | Ba'l's club drives back Sea |
| "At the blast of your nostrils the waters piled up" | Ba'l's meteorological weapons |
| "You led the people you redeemed to your holy abode" | Ba'l's temple on Saphon after victory |
| "Yahweh will reign forever and ever" | Ba'l enthroned as king after defeating Mot |

Cross's conclusion: "The Song of the Sea is a historicized version of the mythic conflict between the Divine Warrior and the Sea. The historical event (the escape from Egypt) has been shaped and interpreted by mythic patterns. This is not to say the event didn't happen. It is to say that the event was experienced and narrated through inherited mythic categories."

- **Archaic linguistic features:** The Hebrew of Exodus 15 preserves archaic verbal forms, case endings, and vocabulary that are absent from later biblical Hebrew.
- **Mythic parallels are structural, not superficial:** Five distinct elements of the Ba'l cycle map onto five elements of the Song — the transformation is systematic, not coincidental.
- **The absence of Jerusalem/Zion:** The Song's geography points to a southern sanctuary tradition (Sinai/Edom/Seir) rather than Jerusalem — consistent with a pre-monarchic date.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The mythic parallels are strong and structural, but archaic language can be consciously archaized — the dating is plausible but not certain.

## Stakes

If the Song of the Sea is 12th-11th century, it provides the EARLIEST evidence for Yahweh's character — and that character is thoroughly Canaanite. Yahweh the Divine Warrior is not a monarchic-period innovation but the ORIGINAL form of Israelite religion. This pushes the Canaanite borrowing back to Israel's very beginnings, making the "pure Mosaic Yahwism" model impossible.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-song-of-sea-egypt-withdrawal-canaan-not-exodus-from-egypt]] — Fleming reads the Song as describing Egyptian withdrawal from Canaan, not the Exodus. [[claim-old-poetry-monarchic-royal-ideology-not-pre-monarchic]] — some scholars date the old poetry to the monarchy rather than the pre-monarchic period.

**Alternative reading:** The Song of the Sea is a monarchic-period composition that uses archaizing language to claim antiquity — its mythic borrowings reflect 10th-8th century theology, not 12th-11th century origins.

## Assessment

The mythic parallels are strong and structural — this is not a case of vague thematic similarity but of systematic transformation of a specific mythic pattern. The dating question is genuinely uncertain, but even a monarchic-period date would still make Exodus 15 the oldest extensive witness to Yahwistic theology. The question is whether it's pre-monarchic (12th-11th century) or early monarchic (10th-9th century).
