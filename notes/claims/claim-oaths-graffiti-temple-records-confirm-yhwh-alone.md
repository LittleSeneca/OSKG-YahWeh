# tigay-nog-2.3: Oaths, graffiti, and temple records confirm YHWH-alone pattern

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Chapter II

## The Claim

Across every genre of pre-exilic Hebrew inscription — oaths, graffiti, temple records, amulets, and miscellaneous texts — the deity invoked is YHWH alone. This multi-genre consistency strengthens the case beyond what any single text type (onomastics or letters) could show.

## Evidence

- **Oath formulas** invoke YHWH alone
- **Cave graffiti** invoke YHWH
- **Temple vessel inscriptions** identify items as belonging to "YHWH" or "the house of YHWH"
- **Amulets** invoke YHWH's protection
- **"Other inscriptions"** (miscellaneous texts) show the same pattern
- The consistency across diverse genres and contexts (administrative, military, personal, cultic) makes it difficult to dismiss any single genre as a special case

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The evidence spans multiple genres and contexts; consistency across diverse text types strengthens the case beyond what any single genre could show.

## Stakes

The multi-genre consistency is the strongest part of Tigay's non-onomastic case. If only ONE genre showed YHWH-alone patterns, it could be dismissed as genre convention. But letters, oaths, graffiti, temple records, and amulets ALL point in the same direction. This convergence of independent evidence types is methodologically powerful.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-tigay-onomastics-does-not-disprove-polytheism]] (Smith), [[claim-dever-folk-polytheism-outlasted-monarchy-monotheism-book-religion]] (Dever)

**Alternative reading:** All these text types come from the LITERATE minority. They may reflect elite/official religion while the non-literate majority practiced a more diverse religious life (the Dever argument). But this counter-argument has its own problem: graffiti and amulets are not "elite" genres — they are among the most demotic forms of writing.
