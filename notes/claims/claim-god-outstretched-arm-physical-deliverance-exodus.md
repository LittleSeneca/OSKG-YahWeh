# stav-god-12-15.2: God's 'outstretched arm' (zĕrôa' nĕṭûyâ) was the muscle that physically delivered Israel from Egypt — the most repeated phrase in the Exodus tradition describing actual muscular intervention.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part IV — Arms and Hands]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 13

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that the "outstretched arm" of Yahweh is not metaphorical — it is a muscular, physical image. God reached into Egypt with his actual arm and pulled Israel out. Deuteronomy 26:8 makes this explicit: "Yahweh brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand and an outstretched arm, with great terror, with signs and wonders." The phrase appears in every layer of the Exodus tradition (Deuteronomy, prophets, psalms) and is not poetic flourish — it describes physical intervention.

## Evidence

- Deuteronomy 26:8: "Yahweh brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand and an outstretched arm"
- The phrase (*zĕrôa' nĕṭûyâ*) appears in every layer of the Exodus tradition: Deuteronomistic history, prophetic oracles (Jeremiah 27:5, Ezekiel 20:33-34), and Psalms (Psalm 136:12)
- The pairing of "mighty hand" and "outstretched arm" is a formula — it is the standard confessional language of deliverance
- *Zĕrôa'* means the upper arm/shoulder — a specific muscular body part, not a generic "power"
- *Nĕṭûyâ* means "stretched out, extended" — a physical action, not an abstraction
- ANE royal inscriptions describe kings with "mighty arm" doing physical acts of conquest — the divine arm follows the same pattern

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The "outstretched arm" is the single most repeated image in the Exodus tradition; if God doesn't have an arm, the most central confession of Israelite faith is gibberish.

## Stakes

This is Stavrakopoulou's strongest single claim. The Exodus — the foundational event of Israelite religion — is narrated as God physically reaching into history with his actual arm. If this language is "just metaphor," then the central confession of Israelite faith is built on a figure of speech. The alternative is that the biblical writers meant what they said: God has an arm, he stretched it out, and Egypt couldn't stop it.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Almost all modern readers who take the "arm" language as metaphor for divine power

**Alternative reading:** "Outstretched arm" is a standard ANE royal metaphor for military power transferred to the deity; it describes God's effective power, not his anatomy.

## Assessment

This is Stavrakopoulou at her most persuasive: the "outstretched arm" is the single most repeated image in the Exodus tradition. If it's just a metaphor, then the central confession of Israelite faith is a figure of speech. The exegetical burden is on the allegorist, not the literalist. The point is not that God's arm had biceps and triceps — it's that the arm is a body part that does body-things, and the text means it to be read that way.
