# smith-ehg-2.2: The Davidic monarchy deliberately adopted Baal's storm-warrior imagery to describe Yahweh

**Source:** [[Smith Chapter 2 — Yahweh and Baal]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Early History of God* (2002), Chapter 2: Yahweh and Baal

## The Claim

The Davidic monarchy deliberately adopted Baal's language and imagery to describe Yahweh as the national god and dynastic patron. This wasn't passive "influence" — it was an active political project. The monarchy both used Baal's imagery AND fought Baal's cult, creating a complex dynamic where Yahweh became more Baal-like even as Baal-worship was condemned. This is the convergence pattern Smith identifies: Yahweh absorbs a deity's profile while the cult of that deity is rejected.

## Evidence

### Shared Motif Catalogue

| Baal (Ugarit) | Yahweh (Bible) |
|--------------|----------------|
| Cloud-rider (*rkb 'rpt*) | Rider on the clouds / steppes (Ps 68:5, Ps 18:11) |
| Storm theophany: march → nature convulses → returns to mountain → thunders | Same pattern in Ps 18, 29, 68, 86 |
| Seven thunders ("voice" of Baal) | Sevenfold "voice of Yahweh" (Ps 29) |
| Defeats Sea (*Yamm*) with weapons | Crushes/stills/dries up Sea (Pss 74:13, 89:10) |
| Defeats seven-headed Leviathan (*ltn*) | Leviathan in Isa 27:1, Ps 74:13-14 |
| Defeats Death (*Mot*) | Swallows Death (Isa 25:8), taunts Death (Hos 13:14) |
| Holy mountain Saphon (*ṣpn*) | Zion called *yarkĕtê ṣāpôn* (Ps 48:3) |
| Temple produces rains | Temple → rains (Mal 3:10, Hag 1:7-11) |
| Bull iconography | Golden calves, "Bull of Jacob," Samaria ostracon "Young bull is Yaw" |
| Title "Most High" (*'ly*) | Yahweh's title in Pss 18, 68; personal names *yhw'ly* |

### The Mari Letter — Political Function

The storm-god Adad (equivalent to Baal) tells King Zimri-Lim (18th c. BCE): "When you sat on the throne of your father, I gave you the weapon(s) with which I fought against Sea." This explicitly shows the POLITICAL function: divine victory over chaos legitimates the king. The same logic operates in Israel: Psalm 89:26 extends Yahweh's cosmic power to the Davidic king — "I will set his hand on Sea and his right hand on River(s)."

### Enthronement Festival

Smith cautiously endorses the theory that the Feast of Tabernacles included an enthronement celebration for Yahweh modeled on Baal's kingship. Evidence: LXX Psalm 29 superscription, Zechariah 14:16-17 (Yahweh's kingship + Tabernacles + rain), and the Baal cycle's own seasonal structure tied to autumn rains.

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** The specific parallels — sevenfold thunder structure, identical divine enemies (Yamm, Leviathan), mountain name transfer — make "common idiom" explanations strained. The cumulative motif catalogue is overwhelming.

## Stakes

The Psalms you sing in church describing God's majesty, power over storms, and kingship over creation are — historically — repurposed Baal hymns. Psalm 29, one of the most majestic descriptions of God's voice in the Bible, was almost certainly written for Baal. This doesn't make the theology false, but it does mean the LANGUAGE of biblical worship is borrowed from the worship of a god the Bible condemns. If God inspired the Psalms, he inspired them using Canaanite mythological language. If humans wrote them, they wrote them using the religious vocabulary they inherited from their polytheistic neighbors (or, on Smith's reading, from their own polytheistic past).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservatives argue shared imagery reflects common ANE cultural idiom, not borrowing. The storm-god language is metaphorical — every culture in the region used similar metaphors. Hess: "imagery does not equal worship."

**Alternative reading:** The parallels are genuine but the direction of borrowing could go either way — or both could draw from a common older tradition. Smith assumes Israel borrowed from Canaan. It's possible that both Israelite and Ugaritic traditions descend from a shared West Semitic religious heritage without direct borrowing.

## Assessment

The density and specificity of the parallels make "common idiom" strained. When Psalm 29 has the EXACT sevenfold thunder structure of Baal's theophany, when Yahweh defeats a seven-headed sea dragon with the same name as Baal's enemy (Leviathan/*ltn*), when Zion is called by Baal's mountain's name (*ṣpn*), "coincidence" requires more faith than "borrowing." This is one of Smith's strongest arguments. The political function claim (monarchy used Baal imagery to exalt Yahweh) is more speculative but plausible.
