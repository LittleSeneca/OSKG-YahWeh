# kaufmann-ri-2.8: Biblical caricature of paganism as fetishism is evidence of Israelite innocence

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch II — Pagan Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter II

## The Claim

The biblical writers consistently mock idols as mere wood and stone, never engaging with actual pagan theology (myths, theogonies, metadivine realm). Kaufmann takes this as evidence that Israelites GENUINELY didn't understand paganism — not that they deliberately distorted it for polemical purposes. The Israelites were innocently ignorant of what paganism really was.

## Evidence (by implication from the entire chapter)

The entire chapter demonstrates what REAL paganism is — a sophisticated system of myth, magic, divination, and cult grounded in the metadivine realm. The biblical polemic against idolatry (mocked as fetishism, "they have mouths but do not speak") completely misses this sophisticated reality. If the biblical writers KNEW real paganism and were merely polemicizing, they'd engage the actual theology. Their failure to do so suggests they genuinely didn't understand it — which suggests Israelite religion was so fundamentally different that paganism was genuinely incomprehensible to Israelites.

## Confidence

**Rating:** low

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's most creative but least defensible move. Deliberate polemical caricature is standard rhetorical practice — far more parsimonious than claiming unique cultural ignorance despite centuries of coexistence.

## Stakes

If Kaufmann is right, the biblical caricature of paganism is positive evidence for Israel's uniqueness. If he's wrong, the caricature is just good polemic and tells us nothing about what Israelites actually believed.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Everyone. The prophetic mockery of idols (Isaiah 44:9-20, Psalm 115:4-8, Jeremiah 10:1-16) is universally understood as deliberate polemical caricature. The prophets KNEW pagans believed their idols were more than wood and stone — they chose to mock them as mere matter for rhetorical effect. [[claim-bible-consistently-characterizes-paganism-as-fetishism]] is Kaufmann's own positive formulation. [[claim-biblical-ignorance-paganism-genuine-not-polemical-strategy]] is the more general version from Ch I.

**Alternative reading:** The biblical caricature is exactly what we'd expect from monotheistic polemic against polytheism. The more interesting question is why THIS particular polemic (fetishism rather than immorality of the gods) became standard. Kaufmann's observation that the polemic is consistent is valuable; his explanation is not.

## Assessment

This is the weak link in Kaufmann's chain. He needs the biblical caricature to be evidence of Israelite innocence, not Israelite polemic. But the simpler explanation — monotheists mock polytheists by reducing their gods to inert matter — accounts for the data without requiring us to believe Israelites were uniquely ignorant of their neighbors' religions despite centuries of coexistence. Kaufmann's argument here treats a theological claim as a historical one.
