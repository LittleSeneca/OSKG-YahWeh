# cross-cmhe-pre-i.2: The God of the Fathers was a distinct deity type

**Source:** [[Cross — Preface + Part I — Canaanite Religion and El]] — Frank Moore Cross, *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* (1973), Chapter 1

## The Claim

The patriarchs worshipped a deity distinct from both Canaanite El and later Yahwistic religion. Cross called this deity "the God of the Fathers" — a personal clan god identified by the patriarch's name ("God of Abraham," "Fear of Isaac," "Mighty One of Jacob"). This was NOT the high god El of the Canaanite pantheon, but a distinct type of deity: a personal patron who protected a specific kinship group.

Cross argues that the God of the Fathers was ORIGINALLY distinct from El — they were merged later when Israel identified the patriarchal God with the Canaanite high god. This merger was the first step in the process that eventually absorbed El entirely into Yahweh.

## Evidence

- The patriarchal narratives consistently use epithet constructions: "God of Abraham" (*'elohê 'abrāhām*), "Fear of Isaac" (*paḥad yiṣḥāq*), "Mighty One of Jacob" (*'ăbîr ya'ăqōb*).
- These follow a Northwest Semitic pattern of naming a deity by the patriarch he protects — a personal patron deity type distinct from the cosmic high god.
- Cross distinguishes three entities: (1) the God of the Fathers (clan patron), (2) El (Canaanite high god), (3) Yahweh (later Israelite national god). The merger happened in stages: first the Fathers' God with El, then El with Yahweh.
- The epithet "El Shadday" in the patriarchal narratives reflects this intermediate stage — El with a qualifying epithet that preserves the distinctiveness of the patriarchal deity.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The typology is elegant but the evidence for three-way distinction is thin — the epithets could equally be forms of El worship or early Yahwistic devotion.

## Stakes

If Cross is right, Israel's religious prehistory is more complex than a simple El → Yahweh merger. There was an intermediate stage — the God of the Fathers — which means the eventual Yahweh-El identification was a THREE-way convergence, not a two-way merger. This complicates Smith's convergence model and Romer's southern origin model.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]] — Lewis and others see the patriarchal deity as simply El, without a distinct "God of the Fathers" category.

**Alternative reading:** The patriarchal epithets are forms of El worship (El of Abraham, El of Isaac) — not a distinct deity type. The "God of the Fathers" is El under different local titles.

## Assessment

Cross's typology is elegant but the evidence for a three-way distinction is thin. The strongest part is the Northwest Semitic parallel for patron-deity naming patterns. The weakest is proving the Fathers' God was NOT El — the epithets could equally be El under patriarchal clan titles.
