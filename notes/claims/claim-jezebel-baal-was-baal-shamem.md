# day-ygc-3.2: Jezebel's Baal was Baal-Shamem, not Melqart

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 3 — Yahweh Versus Baal]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 3: Yahweh Versus Baal

## The Claim

"A much better case can be made that Jezebel's Baal was Baal-Shamem, another important god worshipped at Tyre, whose role as a storm god suggests that he was essentially the same as the Ugaritic Baal and the Baal known elsewhere in the Old Testament" (p. 75). The majority view identifying him as Melqart is wrong. Baal-Shamem, not Melqart, was the god of 1 Kings 18 whose powers over lightning and rain Yahweh co-opts.

## Evidence

### Against Melqart

1. **Late sources:** The Melqart parallels (Herakles as philosopher, journey to Libya, awakening from winter sleep) come from "late sources" with "no certainty that they reflect traditions going back to the ninth century BCE" (p. 75)
2. **Wrong season for a storm god:** Melqart's awakening was celebrated in Peritios (Feb/March) — but Baal's death in summer and resurrection in autumn coheres perfectly with the rain cycle, which was "the point at issue between Elijah and the prophets of Baal in 1 Kings 18" (p. 75)
3. **Esar-haddon treaty** (7th c. BCE, only two centuries after 1 Kings 18): Lists Tyrian deities — Baal-sameme, Baal-malage, and Baal-saphon are invoked for storm curses (raising evil winds, sinking ships). Melqart is listed SEPARATELY with Eshmun for land destruction. This "clearly distinguishes Melqart from various Baal deities" and shows Melqart was NOT yet a Baal deity at this period (p. 76)
4. **"Baal of Tyre" is late:** The title is only attested for Melqart in a 2nd c. BCE inscription from Malta (KAI 47.1) — centuries after Elijah (p. 76)
5. **Classical equation:** Melqart was equated with Herakles, not Zeus. Baal-Shamem was identified with Zeus (p. 76)
6. **Royal names:** No Tyrian king names contain "Melqart," but many contain "Baal" (e.g., Ittobaal/Ethbaal, Jezebel's father) — most naturally referring to Baal-Shamem (p. 76)

### For Baal-Shamem

1. **Carmel = Zeus = Baal-Shamem:** The god of Mt Carmel was always equated with Zeus. A marble foot inscription from Carmel dedicates "to Heliopolitan Zeus [god of] Carmel." Pseudo-Scylax (4th c. BCE) attests Carmel as "holy mountain of Zeus." Zeus = Baal-Shamem, not Melqart (Herakles) (pp. 76-77)
2. **Shalmaneser III's annals** (841 BCE): Refer to "Mount Ba'li-ra'-si [Baal of the Headland]" — a local Baal of Carmel who was "simply a local form of the universal Baal" (p. 77)
3. **Political plausibility:** "Ahab would have committed political suicide had he attempted to promote" Melqart, a foreign city god. But Baal-Shamem was already worshipped by Canaanite populations in Israel — he "gained such a large following" because he was familiar (p. 77, citing Mulder)

### Polemical significance

In 1 Kings 18, Yahweh defeats Baal by sending fire (lightning) and then rain — the very powers Baal was supposed to control. "Accordingly, the polemic is especially marked, as Yahweh is shown as the God who can bring lightning and the rain, which were regarded as Baal's particular sphere of influence" (pp. 77-78)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Day's case is better argued than the Melqart consensus. The Esar-haddon treaty is the strongest single piece of evidence — it's contemporary (7th c.) and clearly distinguishes Melqart from Baal deities associated with storms. But the Melqart view remains the majority, and Day is fighting uphill.

## Stakes

If Jezebel's Baal was Baal-Shamem (not Melqart), then the Elijah conflict was not about a foreign import — it was about the SAME Baal the Canaanites already worshipped. The threat was internal syncretism, not external imposition. This changes the political and religious dynamics of the narrative.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Majority view (R. de Vaux, C. Bonnet, and most standard commentaries) identifies Jezebel's Baal as Melqart. Day is in the minority with O. Eissfeldt, M.J. Mulder, Mark S. Smith, and B. Mazar.

**Alternative reading:** De Vaux's Melqart identification has the advantage of explaining why this Baal was specifically TYRIAN and promoted by a Tyrian princess. Day's Baal-Shamem theory has to explain why Jezebel would promote a god already worshipped in Israel.

## Assessment

Day's Esar-haddon treaty argument is genuinely strong. The treaty clearly distinguishes Melqart from storm-associated Baal deities. The Carmel = Zeus = Baal-Shamem chain is also solid. But Day's argument that Melqart was "foreign" while Baal-Shamem was "familiar" cuts both ways — why would Jezebel need to PROMOTE a familiar god? The Melqart identification explains the Tyrian connection more naturally. I'd say the evidence tilts toward Day but not decisively.
