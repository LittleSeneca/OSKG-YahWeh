# lewis-ocg-2.3: The Ugaritic discoveries had the most profound impact of any 20th-century manuscript discovery

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 2 — History of Scholarship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"Over time the Ugaritic texts would come to have the most profound impact on the study of ancient Israelite religion of any manuscript discovery of the twentieth century (far more than even the Dead Sea Scrolls)."

## Evidence

Ugaritic provided: a Northwest Semitic language remarkably close to Hebrew; a full pantheon with deities directly comparable to biblical names (ʾIlu/ʾEl, Baʿlu/Baʿal, ʾAthiratu/ʾAsherah, ʿAnatu/ʿAnat, Rashpu/Reshep); cosmic monsters (Yammu/yām, Litanu/Leviathan, Tunnanu/tannin, Motu/māwet); cultic terminology for sacrifices; and — crucially — full mythological narratives (the Baal Cycle, Kirta, Aqhat) that show how a West Semitic pantheon actually functioned. The Dead Sea Scrolls, by contrast, illuminate Second Temple Judaism, not the origins of Israelite religion.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Smith makes the same claim in Origins. Cross's entire career was built on this premise. The Ugaritic texts are the single most important comparative corpus for understanding pre-exilic Israelite religion.

## Stakes

This claim justifies the heavy use of Ugaritic material throughout Lewis's book (especially Chapters 4-5 on El). If Ugarit is less relevant than Lewis claims, his comparative method is weakened.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars focused on Persian/Hellenistic period religion (the minimalist school) would say the Dead Sea Scrolls are more important for understanding the religion that actually shaped Judaism and Christianity. But for reconstructing *Iron Age* Israelite religion, Ugarit is unquestionably primary.

**Alternative reading:** The claim is about "most profound impact" — not about which is more important overall. It's a statement about the history of the discipline, and it's correct.

## Assessment

Uncontroversial among specialists. The Baal Cycle alone — with its divine council, combat myth, temple-building, and theomachy — illuminates dozens of biblical passages that were previously opaque.
