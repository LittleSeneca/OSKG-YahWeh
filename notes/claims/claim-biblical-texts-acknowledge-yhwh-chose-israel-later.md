# romer-inv-4.3: The biblical texts themselves acknowledge Yhwh and Israel were not originally connected

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 4 — How Yhwh Became Israel's God]] — Thomas Romer, *The Invention of God* (2014), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"Although these texts do not agree on the place where this link was first formed, they do agree that Yhwh chose Israel at a particular point in history and that this people had not been his people from all time." The Bible's own narrative admits the relationship between God and Israel was not eternal. It began. God "found" Israel, "chose" Israel, "made himself known" to Israel.

## Evidence

- **Hosea 9:10:** "I found Israel like grapes in the desert" — Yhwh "found" Israel; the relationship began at a specific moment. The language of discovery, not eternal possession.
- **Ezekiel 20:** Yhwh "chose" Israel in Egypt — "I raised up my hand to swear an oath to the descendants of the house of Jacob and I made myself known to them in Egypt." The choosing happened in history.
- **Exodus 6:2-3:** Yhwh was NOT known by that name to the patriarchs — "I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob as El Shaddai, but by my name Yhwh I did not make myself known to them." The name Yhwh was revealed at a specific point — the time of Moses.

**Romer's synthesis:** These texts disagree on WHERE the connection formed (desert? Egypt? Sinai?) but agree on the fact: the Yhwh-Israel relationship had a beginning. It was not primordial. It was not eternal. This is consistent with Romer's larger thesis — Yhwh was adopted by a people who previously belonged to El.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The biblical texts are explicit in their language — Yhwh "found," "chose," "made himself known." These are verbs of initiation, not eternal possession. The texts disagree on the location of this beginning, but they agree it happened. Exodus 6:2-3 is the clearest statement: the patriarchs did NOT know the name Yhwh.

## Stakes

The Bible's own narrative admits the relationship between God and Israel was not eternal. It began. God "found" Israel, "chose" Israel, "made himself known" to Israel. The covenant was formed in history, not in eternity. This is consistent with Romer's larger thesis: Yhwh was adopted by a people who previously belonged to El. The biblical authors preserved the memory of this adoption even as they theologized it into divine election.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Theological traditionalists who see the Yhwh-Israel relationship as eternal and primordial. Scholars who argue these texts are late theological constructions that project an adoption narrative backward for ideological reasons.

**Alternative reading:** The "finding" language is metaphor for a pre-existing relationship being renewed or remembered, not a description of a new relationship being formed.

## Assessment

Romer makes a simple but powerful observation: the Bible's own texts describe the Yhwh-Israel relationship as having a beginning. If even the biblical authors didn't claim the relationship was eternal, the historian has no reason to treat it as such. The disagreement among texts about WHERE it began (desert? Egypt?) actually strengthens the argument — they're independently testifying to the same underlying memory (the relationship started) while disagreeing on the details (where).
