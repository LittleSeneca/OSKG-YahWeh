# sommer-bog-intro.3: Divine fluidity cross-cuts the monotheism/polytheism distinction

**Source:** [[Sommer — Introduction]] — Benjamin D. Sommer, *The Bodies of God and the World of Ancient Israel* (2009), Introduction

## The Claim

Divine fluidity (multiple bodies, overlapping selves) is found in BOTH polytheistic cultures (Mesopotamia, Canaan) AND monotheistic ones (biblical Israel, later Judaism, Christianity), while it is absent in some polytheistic cultures (classical Greece). This means the polytheism/monotheism distinction is less fundamental than we think. The real distinction is between fluid and non-fluid conceptions of divinity — which cross-cuts the mono/poly divide.

## Evidence

This is Sommer's argumentative framework, not a claim supported by direct evidence in the Introduction. The evidence for fluidity in each cultural domain (Mesopotamia, Canaan, Israel, Greece) is presented in Chapters 1-3.

The key meta-claim of Sommer's book: "The notion of a God with multiple bodies and fluid selfhood is NOT polytheism. It is a different way of being monotheistic." This is the sophisticated counter-position that challenges the simple evolutionary narrative from polytheism to monotheism.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** This is Sommer's overarching thesis. Plausibility depends on the evidence marshalled in Chapters 1-6.

## Stakes

If Sommer is right, the entire "polytheism → monotheism" narrative that dominates the field (Smith, Romer, Dever) asks the wrong question. The interesting transition is not from many gods to one god, but from a fluid, multi-bodied conception of the one God to a discrete, transcendent one. This reframes Jewish and Christian theology not as "later corruptions" of an original polytheism but as recoveries of an older, suppressed biblical tradition.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Smith, Romer, Dever (who frame Israelite religion as polytheism → monotheism), and essentially the entire field of Israelite religious history.

**Alternative reading:** The polytheism/monotheism transition IS the fundamental story; Sommer's fluidity model is an interesting refinement but doesn't overturn the basic narrative.

## Assessment

This is the thesis that makes Sommer unique among our Tier 1 scholars. He accepts the critical methods but argues the field has been asking the wrong question. Whether the evidence supports his reframing will determine whether his book is a genuine paradigm shift or an interesting provocation. The Introduction doesn't provide the evidence — it stakes the claim.
