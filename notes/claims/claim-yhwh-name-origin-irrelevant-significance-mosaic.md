# kaufmann-ri-6.7: The Name YHWH — Origin Unknown, Significance Clear

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch VI — The Origins of Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch VI

## The Claim

"All that can be known beyond doubt is the value of the name since the time that Moses brought it to the Israelite tribes. Henceforth, YHWH was the symbol of the monotheistic idea." The etymology is irrelevant; what matters is that from Moses onward, YHWH functioned as the name of the one sovereign God who is both universal Lord and national patron.

## Evidence

Kaufmann doesn't engage the etymological debate (from hyh "to be"? from hwh "to blow"? causative "he causes to be"?). He simply asserts that the name's "value" — its function as the monotheistic symbol — is what matters, and this was established by Moses.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The functional observation (YHWH's role as monotheistic symbol in the biblical period) is correct. But the implication that pre-Mosaic meaning is irrelevant is strategically necessary for Kaufmann rather than intellectually justified. If YHWH was a pre-existing deity (as the Shasu-Yhwʒ evidence suggests), then Moses didn't create the name — he reinterpreted it. WHERE THE NAME CAME FROM matters enormously for Kaufmann's thesis.

## Stakes

Whether YHWH is a Mosaic creation or a pre-Mosaic deity that was transformed. If the latter, Kaufmann's "absolute beginning" claim is falsified.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The philological and historical scholarship that sees the name's origins as highly significant. The Shasu-Yhwʒ connection suggests a pre-Israelite YHWH cult in the south. The biblical tradition itself preserves a memory of this in the Midianite connection.

**Alternative reading:** YHWH was a deity from the southern regions (Edom/Midian/Seir) who was adopted by early Israel — perhaps through Moses, perhaps through a longer process of contact. The name's original meaning (whatever it was) became secondary to its new function as the name of Israel's covenant God. The identification of YHWH with El (the old patriarchal god) enabled the merger of southern Yahwism with northern El worship.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's refusal to engage the origins of the name is strategically necessary for his thesis but intellectually unsatisfying. If YHWH existed before Moses as a deity in the southern regions, then the "monotheistic revolution" wasn't the invention of a new God but the radical reinterpretation of an existing one. This is actually a MORE plausible historical model — religious revolutions rarely create entirely new deities; they transform existing ones. Kaufmann's insistence on absolute novelty forces him to bracket the very evidence that might help explain how the revolution actually happened.
