# smith-obm-8.2: True monotheism requires denying other gods' EXISTENCE — the Shema and First Commandment don't qualify

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch8 — Emergence of Monotheistic Rhetoric]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 8

## The Claim

The common inclusion of the Shema (Deut 6:4) and First Commandment as monotheistic is incorrect. True monotheism requires denying the reality of other gods, not just prohibiting their worship. "Therefore, in discussing monotheism, one must exclude the reality of other gods."

## Evidence

**Category 1 — Exclusivity formulas** (`*lĕbadd-`, "alone"; `*zûlat-`, "besides"):
- Deut 4:35: "Yahweh alone is God, there is none beside Him." (a "sermon" = rhetoric of persuasion)
- 2 Kings 19:15, 19 ∥ Isa 37:16, 20: "You alone are God of all the kingdoms of the earth"
- Neh 9:6: "You alone are Yahweh."
- Ps 86:10: "You are God alone."
- 2 Sam 7:22 ∥ 1 Chron 17:20: "there is no god except You"

Smith notes: "All but the first of these statements belong to prayers. Monotheism here belongs to the rhetoric of praise." — monotheism is *performative language*, not abstract theology.

**Category 2 — Denial of reality** (`'ên`, "there is not"; `'ĕlîlîm`, "nothings"; `mētîm`, "dead"):
- Deut 4:39: "Yahweh is god in heaven above and on earth below; there is no other"
- 1 Sam 2:2: "There is none beside You"
- Jer 16:19-20: "Shall a man make gods for himself / And they are no gods?"
- Ps 96:5 ∥ 1 Chron 16:26: "all the gods of the nations are nothings" (clever pun: `'ĕlōhîm` → `'ĕlîlîm`)
- Ps 82:7: "like a mortal you shall die" (language of *divine death*)

**What's excluded:**
- **The Shema (Deut 6:4):** Tigay: "This is not a declaration of monotheism, meaning that there is only one God." It declares exclusivity of relationship (Yahweh alone is Israel's God), not ontological exclusivity. Von Rad read it as anti-Baal polemic. Zech 14:9 may envision only one deity, but doesn't mean Deut 6:4 did.
- **The First Commandment (Ex 20:3 / Deut 5:7):** "no other gods before me" — prohibits worship, doesn't deny existence.
- **Deut 32:12, 15b-21, 37-39:** condemns veneration of other deities without commenting on existence.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The definitional rigor is necessary and Smith applies it carefully. The exclusion of the Shema is particularly important given its centrality in later Judaism.

## Stakes

If the Shema isn't monotheistic, the central confession of Judaism was originally monolatrous. Monotheism is a later interpretive overlay — even on the most famous "monotheistic" text.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional Jewish interpreters who read `'eḥād` as "alone/only." NJPS and NRSV both translate "alone."

**Alternative reading:** The Shema COULD be monotheistic depending on the semantic range of `'eḥād` — but Smith (with Tigay and von Rad) makes a strong case that it's about exclusivity of relationship, not ontology.

## Assessment

The definitional section is one of the chapter's most important contributions. By establishing clear criteria and excluding borderline cases, Smith forces the debate onto unambiguous ground. The pun on `'ĕlōhîm`/`'ĕlîlîm` in Ps 96:5 is a particularly nice touch — monotheism operates through *wordplay*, not philosophical argument.
