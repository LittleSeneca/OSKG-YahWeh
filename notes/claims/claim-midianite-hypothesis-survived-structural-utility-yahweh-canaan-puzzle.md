# fleming-ybi-1.2: The Midianite Hypothesis survived by solving a structural problem in scholarship, not by the strength of its evidence

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 1 — Introduction]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 1

## The Claim

"What is remarkable is that transformations in how the Bible is understood and in how the history of Israel is reconstructed have not dislodged the common conclusion about the southern origin of Yahweh" (p. 33). The hypothesis "suited well the constraints" of the new archaeological consensus that Israel was culturally Canaanite: El/Elohim could be local Canaanite, but Yahweh needed a separate origin to explain his absence from Ugarit and other Canaanite evidence (p. 35).

## Evidence

- Dating of biblical composition has shifted dramatically later (post-586 BCE for most texts), yet the Midianite Hypothesis persists (pp. 33-34)
- Archaeological consensus since the 1970s-80s: Israelites cannot be distinguished materially from Canaanites (p. 34, citing Dever, Finkelstein, Smith)
- Yahweh's absence from Ugaritic/Canaanite pantheon lists creates a puzzle that the Midianite Hypothesis solves (p. 35)
- The framework "offers a way to leave Israel in its Canaanite environment with an essentially Canaanite culture — and religion — while partaking of contact with its nomadic neighbors" (p. 36)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is an observation about the sociology of scholarship, not about ancient history. Fleming's diagnosis of why a 19th-century hypothesis survived 20th-century transformations is acute. The Midianite Hypothesis became functionally useful for a different intellectual project than the one that birthed it.

## Stakes

This is a meta-level claim about how scholarship works. It suggests the persistence of the Midianite Hypothesis owes more to its structural utility (solving the Yahweh/Canaan puzzle) than to the strength of its evidence. If Fleming is right, the field has been captive to a framework that outlived its evidential basis.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Defenders of the Midianite Hypothesis would say it persists because the evidence genuinely converges on a southern origin — not because it's structurally convenient. Römer's 2015 monograph represents the most sophisticated current defense.

**Alternative reading:** The hypothesis persists because the evidence genuinely supports it. The Egyptian Yhwʒ + Seir combination, the archaic poetry's southern geography, and the Moses-Midian connection ARE three independent lines of evidence, not a convenience.

## Assessment

Fleming's meta-analysis is one of the chapter's strongest contributions. He's not caricaturing his opponents — he acknowledges the Midianite Hypothesis "is still the right place to start" (p. 24). But his diagnosis of why it survived is devastating: it solved a problem created by the Canaanite-Israel continuity thesis in a way that left the intellectual furniture undisturbed.
