# schmid-ht-6-9.5: Albertz's History of Israelite Religion revived the religion-over-theology argument — but his own work is "fundamentally just as much a literary history"

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§6-9 — Jewish Reception to Implicit Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §8

## The Claim

Rainer Albertz argued that "history of religion" is a "more comprehensive Old Testament discipline" than "theology" — it better fits the HB's historical structure, doesn't separate religious statements from their background, and doesn't impose theological abstraction. But Schmid counters: "Albertz's own outline presents a mix of different approaches...his book is fundamentally just as much a literary history as it is a history of the theology of the Hebrew Bible."

## Evidence

- Albertz's four advantages of history of religion:
  1. Corresponds better to the historical structure of the OT
  2. Takes seriously that religious statements can't be separated from historical background
  3. Avoids the compulsion to abstract to a single "theological" level
  4. Need not present claims to absoluteness
- Schmid's counter: Albertz's book is organized by biblical themes and follows the biblical structure — it's not independent of the Bible's own framing
- "How differently a history of Israelite religion appears when not based primarily on the biblical witnesses and themes is demonstrated in the contemporary volume by Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Schmid's observation about Albertz's dependence on biblical structure is accurate. But his dismissal of Albertz's argument for history-of-religion is somewhat hasty — Albertz's four advantages are substantive and Schmid doesn't really answer them.

## Stakes

Albertz is the most prominent recent advocate for history-of-religion over theology. If Schmid can't convincingly answer Albertz, his project's rationale is weakened. Schmid's best move is to say: history-of-religion is legitimate, but it doesn't exhaust what can be said — the theological dimension (the texts' own reflective, synthesizing character) requires its own treatment.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Rainer Albertz, whose four-advantage argument for history-of-religion Schmid does not fully refute. Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger, whose iconographic approach Schmid cites as an example of religion history independent of biblical framing.

**Alternative reading:** Albertz's four advantages remain powerful — history-of-religion is more comprehensive than theology. Schmid's observation that Albertz's own work depends on biblical structure is a tu quoque, not a refutation.

## Assessment

Schmid's engagement with Albertz is disappointingly brief. Albertz's four advantages deserve more serious response. Schmid's counter (that Albertz's own work is implicitly theological/literary) is a tu quoque, not a refutation. The deeper question is whether "theology" adds anything to "history of religion" — Schmid needs Parts E-H to demonstrate that it does.
