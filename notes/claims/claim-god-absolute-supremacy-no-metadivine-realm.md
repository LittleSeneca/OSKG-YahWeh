# kaufmann-ri-3.1: God's absolute supremacy — no metadivine realm

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

"The basic idea of Israelite religion is that God is supreme over all. There is no realm above or beside him to limit his absolute sovereignty. He is utterly distinct from, and other than, the world; he is subject to no laws, no compulsions, or powers that transcend him. He is, in short, non-mythological. This is the essence of Israelite religion, and that which sets it apart from all forms of paganism." This was "not a product of intellectual speculation, or of mystical meditation" but "an insight, an original intuition" expressed in symbols — "the image of an omnipotent, supreme deity, holy, awful, and jealous, whose will was the highest law."

## Evidence

The entire chapter serves as evidence. Kaufmann argues the idea never received abstract systematic formulation but "working intuitively, it radically transformed the ancient mythological conceptions of Israel." The biblical texts consistently present YHWH as supreme, with no rival powers, no realm above or beside him, no necessity binding him.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's description of the IDEA is powerful and largely accurate as a characterization of biblical theology. The problem is his insistence that it's early, original, and popular.

## Stakes

Everything Kaufmann argues flows from this premise. If Israelite religion was monotheistic in essence from the start, all biblical evidence of pagan survivals become exactly that — survivals, fragments, fossils — not evidence of an earlier polytheistic stage.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The entire critical tradition. [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]] argues monotheism is a late rhetorical development. [[claim-romer-narrative-arc-desert-god-to-only-god]] traces a millennium-long sedimentary process. The claim that this idea was "not a product of intellectual speculation" but an "original intuition" is essentially theological, not historical.

**Alternative reading:** Israelite religion DID develop a unique conception of divine supremacy, but over centuries through conflict, prophetic innovation, and historical crisis — not as an "original intuition" present from the beginning.
