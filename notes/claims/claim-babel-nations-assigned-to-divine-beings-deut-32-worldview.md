# heiser-ur-14.1: At Babel, God dispersed the nations and assigned them to lesser divine beings — the Deuteronomy 32 worldview

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 3 — Divine Transgressions]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 14

## The Claim

The Tower of Babel event (Genesis 11) was not merely a confusion of languages but a cosmic reordering. God divided the nations and assigned each to a lesser divine being (*elohim*) from his council. Yahweh kept Israel as his own "portion" (Deut 32:8-9). This explains why the Old Testament refers to other nations as under the authority of other "gods" — they genuinely WERE, by Yahweh's own decree. The Babel event created the spiritual geography that the rest of the biblical narrative assumes: Yahweh rules over Israel while other *elohim* administer the other nations (corruptly, as Psalm 82 later reveals).

## Evidence

The chapter note provides only a one-line summary. Heiser's full argument (Chapter 14 of *The Unseen Realm*) includes:
- Genesis 11 (Babel) — God disperses the nations
- Deuteronomy 32:8-9 — "When the Most High gave the nations their inheritance, when he divided mankind, he fixed the borders of the peoples according to the number of the sons of God. But Yahweh's portion is his people, Jacob his allotted heritage."
- The connection: Babel is WHEN this division happened; Deut 32 is the theological interpretation
- The 70 nations of Genesis 10 correspond to the 70 sons of El (known from Ugaritic texts)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The Deut 32:8-9 connection to the divine council is well-established in scholarship (Smith, Römer, Day all discuss it). Heiser's specific move — reading Babel as the event where this distribution occurred — is plausible within his framework but requires more detailed argumentation than the note provides.

## Stakes

This is arguably Heiser's most important claim for understanding the broader biblical narrative. If the nations were genuinely assigned to other *elohim* by Yahweh's decree, then (a) the other gods of the nations are real beings, (b) the Old Testament's "cosmic geography" explains why Israel is uniquely Yahweh's, and (c) Psalm 82's judgment of the gods for corrupt administration makes sense — they were given real authority and abused it. The entire biblical narrative from Babel to Pentecost (where the nations are re-gathered) operates on this cosmic geography.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who reject the "sons of God" reading of Deut 32:8 (preferring "sons of Israel" from the MT). Traditional monotheists who read the other nations' gods as non-existent idols — the nations weren't REALLY assigned to divine beings; the nations wrongly WORSHIPPED as if they were. Kaufmann would see this entire framework as a paganization of Israelite theology.

**Alternative reading:** The Babel story is an etiology of linguistic diversity. Deut 32:8-9 is poetic metaphor — the nations were not literally assigned to divine beings. The "sons of God" reading is textually uncertain (MT reads "sons of Israel"). The cosmic geography Heiser describes imports ANE polytheistic concepts into the biblical text.

## Assessment

This is Heiser's most distinctive contribution to biblical theology — the "Deuteronomy 32 worldview" that he returns to throughout *The Unseen Realm*. The Deut 32:8-9 connection is not unique to Heiser (Smith, Römer, and Day all discuss it), but Heiser's integration of Babel as the event where this distribution occurred is his original synthesis. The claim makes the Old Testament's references to other nations' gods coherent rather than contradictory: they ARE real beings, appointed by Yahweh, and they will be judged for their corruption. This is hierarchical monotheism at its most fully developed.
