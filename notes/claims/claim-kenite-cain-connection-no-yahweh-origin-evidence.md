# fleming-ybi-3.4: The Kenite/Cain connection does not support Yahweh's origin among Kenites

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 3 — The Midianite Hypothesis — Moses and the Priest]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 3

## The Claim

"Here again we have no evidence that Yahweh was a Kenite god — no interest in Yahweh at all, except by extrapolation from Moses' 'father-in-law' in Exodus 18" (Fleming, p. 125). "The interpretation of Yahweh's first worship in Gen 4:26 represents the guiding hand of a fellow scholar, not the passing on of near-forgotten lore — from before the great flood!" (p. 126).

## Evidence

- Genesis 4:26 ("Then it was begun to call on the name of Yahweh") is part of a bridge between the Cain material (4:1-24) and the Priestly genealogy (ch. 5)
- David Carr (forthcoming) reads it as "non-Priestly" but still part of a late compositional effort to trace Yahweh worship back to primeval times
- "The passage envisions an early approach to Yahweh long before Moses, in contrast to P" (Carr, cited p. 127)
- The Kenites share their name with Cain, but the Cain narrative in Genesis 4 is an etiology of civilization, not a religious origin story
- The connection between Cain and Kenites is never developed in the Bible — no biblical text says "the Kenites worshipped Yahweh because they descend from Cain"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Kenite element was always the weakest part of the Midianite Hypothesis and Fleming dispatches it efficiently. Gen 4:26 is transparently a theological notice about primeval Yahwism, not a historical report about Kenite religion.

## Stakes

Low for the overall argument — even Blenkinsopp's four-part summary of the hypothesis treats the Kenite/Cain connection as supplemental. But Fleming's point is methodological: every alleged biblical support for the hypothesis dissolves on close reading.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Blenkinsopp (2008: 141), who reads Gen 4:26 as marking "a decisively new stage in religious history." Tiele's original formulation built heavily on the Kenite-Rechabite connection.

**Alternative reading:** The Cain-Kenite connection preserves genuine genealogical memory of a southern tribe associated with early Yahweh worship, even if the Genesis 4 text itself is late.
