# day-ygc-1.1: Yahweh and El were originally distinct deities

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 1 — Yahweh and El]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 1: Yahweh and El

## The Claim

"El and Yahweh were originally distinct deities that became amalgamated" (p. 15). Cross and de Moor are wrong to think Yahweh was originally a form or epithet of El. This has been the majority view since F.K. Movers (1841), supported by Eissfeldt and Mettinger. Yahweh was a foreign import who was later grafted onto El, meaning the character of Yahwism came from outside Canaan.

## Evidence

### 1. Character Difference
In Ugaritic texts El is "wholly benevolent," whereas Yahweh has a fierce as well as a kind side (p. 14, citing Løkkegaard). The earliest evidence (Judg 5:4-5) associates Yahweh with the storm — something El is NOT connected with. This is reminiscent of Baal, not El (p. 15, citing Mettinger).

### 2. Grammatical Argument Against Cross
Cross's formula *ˀil ḏū yahwī ṣabaˀôt* ("El who creates hosts") is "nowhere attested, whether inside or outside the Bible" (p. 15). Three specific objections:
- Cross's grammatical objection (that a proper name shouldn't appear in the construct) is wrong — "Yahweh of Teman" and "Yahweh of Samaria" at Kuntillet Ajrud show proper names CAN appear in construct (citing Emerton)
- *hyh* (hwh) is not attested in Hebrew in the hiphil ("cause to be, create"), though it is in Aramaic
- The name more likely means "he is" (qal), not "he creates" (hiphil)

### 3. OT Signals Discontinuity
E and P sources say the patriarchs did NOT know the name Yahweh; it was first revealed to Moses (Exod 3:13-15; 6:2-3). J has Yahweh known from primaeval times (Gen 4:26), showing the tradition was aware of and debated the discontinuity.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The grammatical case against Cross is decisive; Emerton's observation about Kuntillet Ajrud usage of proper names in construct is strong. But the character argument (benevolent vs. fierce) is weaker — "wholly benevolent" is an overstatement about Ugaritic El.

## Stakes

This is the foundational question for the entire field. If Cross is right, Yahweh IS El — Israel's god was the Canaanite high god from the beginning, just reformulated. If Day is right, Yahweh was a foreign import who was later grafted onto El, which means the character of Yahwism came from outside Canaan. Most scholars today follow Day/Eissfeldt/Mettinger, not Cross.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-]] F.M. Cross (*Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic*, pp. 60-75); J.C. de Moor (*The Rise of Yahwism*, pp. 223-60). Cross was Day's primary target and remains the most important counter-voice.

**Alternative reading:** Sommer's "divine fluidity" model partially reconciles both: Yahweh IS El in some sense but manifests differently. Cross's theory, while grammatically speculative, captures the genuine continuity between El and Yahweh that Day himself documents throughout the chapter.

## Assessment

Day wins on grammar — Cross's unattested formula is a real problem, and the Kuntillet Ajrud parallels are strong evidence. But Day's admission that the OT "has no qualms in equating Yahweh with El" (p. 15) shows the distinction is more theoretical than practical. They became the same god so thoroughly that the original separation barely matters for biblical interpretation. The character arguments (benevolent vs. fierce) are the weakest part of his case — Yahweh has plenty of benevolent passages, and ANE gods weren't so one-dimensional.
