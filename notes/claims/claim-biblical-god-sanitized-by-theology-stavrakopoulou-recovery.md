# stav-god-pro-1.1: The Bible's God has been systematically sanitized by centuries of Jewish and Christian theological interpretation, obscuring the real, embodied, corporeal deity present in the biblical texts.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Prologue + Chapter 1 — Dissecting the Divine]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Prologue

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that the God worshipped by Jews and Christians today is not the God of the Bible. Her theology professor and peers at university were "censoring the Bible, sanitizing its deity of any mythological, earthy or unsettling characteristics." The sanitized God is a product of centuries of theological interpretation. The real God — the one in the texts — is something else entirely: a corporeal, embodied deity with a physical body, who walks, eats, fights, and has sex. Stavrakopoulou positions herself as the honest reader cutting through centuries of theological censorship to reveal the scandal underneath.

## Evidence

- Stavrakopoulou's personal experience: her university theology professor and peers systematically avoided the embodied language of the Bible
- The book's project: "Stripping away the theological veneer of centuries of Jewish and Christian piety, this book disentangles the biblical God from his scriptural and doctrinal fetters to reveal a deity wholly unlike the God worshipped by Jews and Christians today"
- Stavrakopoulou is an atheist writing for a general audience; she does not claim neutrality

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** She genuinely intends to recover the embodied biblical God; the question is whether her reconstruction overcorrects.

## Stakes

Stavrakopoulou is the most radical voice in the debate. Where Sommer argues God had MULTIPLE bodies (fluidity), Stavrakopoulou argues God had ONE literal physical body — a supersized humanoid form — that later theology spiritualized away. If she is correct, the God of Western theism is a theological invention with little connection to the biblical texts.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The entire Jewish and Christian theological tradition, which has read the biblical anthropomorphic language metaphorically. More specifically: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]] (Kaufmann), who argues that Israelite religion was fundamentally non-mythological and non-corporeal; the Deuteronomic and Priestly anti-fluid traditions documented by [[claim-genesis-1-26-anti-fluid-manifesto-image-without-embodiment]] (Sommer).

**Alternative reading:** The biblical language is metaphorical — accommodation to human understanding, not a claim about God's actual physical composition.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou's rhetorical strategy positions her as the honest reader cutting through centuries of theological censorship. The facts are on her side — the texts DO describe an embodied God. But her tone (scandalous, provocative) is designed to unsettle, not just inform. She's writing for a popular audience that will be shocked by what she reports, not for scholars who already know it. Her relationship to Sommer: same data, different framework. Sommer: fluid embodiment — God has MULTIPLE bodies, overlapping selves. Stavrakopoulou: one literal body — God is a supersized humanoid.
