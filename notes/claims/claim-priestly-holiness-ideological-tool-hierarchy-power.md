# lewis-ocg-10.2: The cultic management of holiness in P was an ideological tool for constructing priestly hierarchy

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 10 — Holy One]] — Theodore Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 10

## The Claim

"It is easy to see how the concept of holiness is used ideologically to construct and reinforce rank, power, and privilege. This is especially true in the promotion of Moses, Aaron, and the Aaronid priests." Holiness "served as an indicator of rank among cultic personnel." P adamantly asserts that ONLY Aaronid priests (kōhănîm) are holy — in contrast to non-Aaronid Levites.

## Evidence

- P's gradations of holiness map directly onto social hierarchy: high priest > Aaronid priests > Levites > clean lay Israelites > unclean Israelites
- The ritual clothing of Aaron (Lev 8) is "an act of investiture that formally bestows and confirms the authority due his rank as high priest. Vestments are used to mark both his subservience before God and his elevated status before humans."
- The anointing ritual (oil poured on Aaron's head vs. sprinkled on ordinary priests) marks sacerdotal rank
- Moses is the preeminent authority but "the verb qdš is never used of Moses throughout the Hebrew Bible apart from a single (late) reference" — and Moses is never explicitly called a priest
- Lewis suggests this is because P needed to elevate AARON as the priestly ancestor, not Moses
- The Aaronid-only-holiness claim in P is so explicit and so polemical that it virtually announces itself as a contested claim — someone was arguing that Levites were holy too

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the ideological function of holiness in P. MEDIUM on the Moses omission being deliberate (it could be an accident of textual preservation).

## Stakes

Whether priestly religion is revealed ritual or constructed ideology. Lewis leans heavily toward the latter — holiness language is a tool of power, not a description of divine nature. The Aaronid-only-holiness claim in P is so explicit and so polemical that it virtually announces itself as a contested claim.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Milgrom and confessional scholars who see P's holiness system as divinely revealed. Even critical scholars might argue Lewis overstates the ideological reading — some gradations of holiness may reflect genuine functional distinctions rather than power plays.

**Alternative reading:** P's gradations of holiness reflect genuine theological distinctions about degrees of access to the divine presence, not ideological power-grabbing.
