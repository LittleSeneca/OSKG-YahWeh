# fleming-ybi-2.3: The Shasu-land names represent kinship-based people-groups (mhwt, "families/tribes"), not towns or landmarks

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 2 — Yhwʒ of Shasu-Land]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"It is central to my analysis that the names for the distinct Shasu units were not assigned by Egyptian scribes but rather are taken from indigenous Semitic identities" (Fleming, p. 68). "The names represent groups of people not identified by towns" (p. 69). "I insist that they are groups within the Shasu 'land,' and that the names only make sense in relation to people" (p. 85).

## Evidence

- The Egyptian term mhwt ("families/tribes") is applied to Shasu subdivisions in Papyrus Anastasi VI and Papyrus Harris I (late 13th-12th c.)
- O'Connor (1987: 35): mhwt "emphasizing the primacy of kinship within the social and political organization" — applied to Libyans and Shasu alike
- The names are entirely unknown — not part of any attested Late Bronze Age landscape of towns or landmarks (p. 65)
- This is the ONLY list naming Shasu subdivisions — unique and precious
- Anne Porter's work on kinship creating social bonds across distance (2009, 2012) provides a theoretical framework
- The Mari archives show exactly this pattern: kinship-based pastoralist groups integrated with settlement and agriculture
- None of the attempts to identify the names geographically (Astour's northern locations, Görg's animal traits, biblical cross-references) has produced convincing results

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The Egyptian use of mhwt is solid evidence for how Egyptians perceived Shasu organization. But we can't be certain the names reflect indigenous self-identification rather than Egyptian administrative categories. The argument that they're "not towns" is stronger than the positive claim that they're kinship groups.

## Stakes

If Yhw3 is a kinship-based people-group, then Yahweh-the-god is the god OF a people — the deity carries the people's name, not vice versa. This inverts the usual assumption (that a place or sanctuary named after the god gave rise to the name). It also aligns with Fleming's Ch 6 argument about 'am Yhwh.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Betsy Bryan (personal communication to Fleming): the choice is between "tribe" and "locale." Most Egyptologists and biblical scholars have treated the names as toponyms — place names, perhaps sanctuaries (Giveon: "Beth Yahweh, the house of Yahweh").

**Alternative reading:** The names could be geographical subdivisions of "Shasu-land" without implying kinship organization — simply administrative districts defined by Egyptian scribes for their own purposes.

## Assessment

Fleming's reading is plausible and well-argued but not proven. The Egyptian evidence for mhwt is later than Amenhotep III. The key evidence is really negative: every attempt to locate the names geographically has failed. That doesn't prove they're kinship groups, but it removes the main alternative. The Mari comparison is illuminating but analogical, not evidential.
