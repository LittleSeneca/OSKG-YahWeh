# day-ygc-3.3: Baal-zebub is a deliberate distortion of Baal-zebul ("Prince Baal")

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 3 — Yahweh Versus Baal]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 3: Yahweh Versus Baal

## The Claim

"The most probable explanation of the name Baal-zebub is that which sees the name as a deliberate distortion of Baal-zebul, 'Baal the Prince'" (p. 80). All attempts to justify the MT reading Baal-zebub as authentic are unconvincing. The distortion is part of a deliberate, sophisticated scribal strategy of theologically motivated name-distortion: "Baal the Prince" → "Lord of the Flies" is not a translation error — it's an insult.

## Evidence

### Rejecting "Baal of flies" readings

1. Fly oracles unattested in Semitic world (Macalister)
2. Philistine origin theories lack Semitic parallels for fly-as-death-symbol (Riemschneider/Gaster)
3. Ugaritic *dbbm* more likely means "enemies" or "words," not "flies" (Saracino's appeal rejected by other translators)
4. "Baal with fly ornaments" (Tangberg) — lacks parallels and doesn't sound like a divine title
5. Fensham's "Baal of the flame" (connecting to Ugaritic *dbb* "flame") — the vocalization *zebub* is wrong for this etymology

### For Baal-zebul ("Prince Baal")

1. **Ugaritic:** Baal's frequent epithet is *zbl bʻl* "Prince Baal" (KTU²). The reverse order *bʻl zbl* is attested in a 2nd c. BCE Punic name from Sardinia (KAI 67.1-2) (pp. 80-81)
2. **Symmachus** presupposes Baal-zebul in 2 Kgs 1:2 (mss j and z) (p. 80)
3. **NT Beelzebul:** Almost all Greek mss read Beelzebul (not Beelzebub). Only Vaticanus and Sinaiticus have Beelzeboul. Vulgate/Peshitta read Beelzebub — clearly secondary harmonization with the OT (p. 81)
4. **Pattern of distortion:** This fits other OT distortions: *bošet* "shame" for Baal, *Ashtoreth* (vowels of *bošet*) for Ashtart, and the vowels of Molech (p. 81)
5. **Demonization trajectory:** The name of the leading god, when abominated, became the name of the leading demon (Satan). "The idea that pagan gods are demons is found in Deut 32:17; Ps 105:37; Bar 4:7; Ps 95:5 LXX; also 1 Cor 10:20 and Rev 9:20" (p. 81)
6. **NT wordplay:** Mt 10:25 plays on *zebul* meaning "dwelling" — "If they have called the master of the house Beelzebul, how much less will they malign those of his household" (pp. 81-82)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is one of Day's strongest arguments in the chapter. The convergence of Ugaritic attestation, Symmachus, NT manuscript evidence, and the broader pattern of scribal polemical distortion makes this close to certain.

## Stakes

This reveals a deliberate, sophisticated scribal strategy of theologically motivated name-distortion. "Baal the Prince" → "Lord of the Flies" is not a translation error — it's an insult. The same pattern holds for *bošet* "shame" replacing Baal in personal names (day-ygc-3.4).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those defending the MT reading (Saracino, Tangberg, Fensham with "Baal of the flame"). None are convincing.

**Alternative reading:** Fensham's "Baal of the flame" reading attempts to find a dignified original, but the vocalization doesn't work. The MT reading *zebub* is easier to explain as a distortion than as a divine epithet.

## Assessment

Day's case is essentially unanswerable. The NT Beelzebul evidence is the clincher — if early Christians knew the name as Beelzebul (not Beelzebub), and Beelzebul matches Ugaritic *zbl bʻl*, the MT reading is a secondary corruption. The broader pattern of scribal theologically-motivated distortions seals it.
