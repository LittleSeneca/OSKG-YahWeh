# tigay-nog-2.2: Votive inscriptions and blessing formulas invoke YHWH alone

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Chapter II

## The Claim

Across all pre-exilic Hebrew votive texts and blessing formulas, the deity invoked is YHWH alone. The Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom inscriptions are the only significant exceptions. Tigay addresses them specifically and argues they do not demonstrate worship of Asherah as an independent deity.

## Evidence

- Votive inscriptions and blessing formulas uniformly invoke YHWH alone
- **On Kuntillet Ajrud specifically, Tigay argues:**
  1. Asherah may be a cult object, not a goddess (the "his Asherah" = cult symbol reading)
  2. Even if a goddess, she is invoked alongside YHWH, not independently
  3. The site is a remote way-station, not representative of mainstream practice
  4. "At most they show that some Israelites conceived YHWH as having a consort, but they do not demonstrate worship of her as an independent deity."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** HIGH on the pattern (votive texts overwhelmingly invoke YHWH alone); MEDIUM on his Kuntillet Ajrud interpretation — the inscriptions clearly pair YHWH with Asherah, and dismissing the site as a "remote way-station" is his weakest argument.

## Stakes

Kuntillet Ajrud is the strongest counter-evidence to Tigay's thesis. If the inscriptions there demonstrate goddess worship alongside YHWH, Tigay's "YHWH alone" pattern has a significant exception. Tigay's interpretation — that it's a cult object, a remote outlier, or at most evidence of a consort-concept rather than independent goddess worship — is his attempt to neutralize this counter-evidence. Most scholars find this unconvincing.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-proves-consort]] (Smith/Römer/Dever), [[claim-yahweh-consort-debate-requires-iconographic-evidence-not-texts-alone]] (Keel/Uehlinger)

**Alternative reading:** The Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions are not an exception to be explained away — they are direct evidence that Asherah was worshipped as YHWH's consort goddess at a Yahwistic site. The "remote way-station" argument doesn't work because religious diversity is often MORE visible at peripheral sites, not less.
