# kaufmann-ri-intro.3: Israelite religion was absolutely different from paganism from the very beginning

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Introduction]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Introduction

## The Claim

"We shall see that Israelite religion was an original creation of the people of Israel. It was absolutely different from anything the pagan world ever knew; its monotheistic world view had no antecedents in paganism. Nor was it a theological doctrine conceived and nurtured in limited circles or schools... It was the fundamental idea of a national culture, and informed every aspect of that culture from its very beginning."

## Evidence

Kaufmann points to two types of evidence: (1) the Bible's consistent misrepresentation of paganism as fetishism — which he takes as evidence that Israelites genuinely didn't understand pagan religion, not that they polemically distorted it; (2) "the peculiar biblical conceptions of sin, impurity, sacrifice, magic, dreams, prophecy and inspiration, priest and prophet, and the blanket prohibition of divination, unparalleled in the pagan world."

## Confidence

**Rating:** low

**Rationale:** LOW for the "from the very beginning" claim; MEDIUM-HIGH that Israelite religion developed distinctive features. The archaeological evidence (figurines, inscriptions, standing stones) strongly suggests popular religion was more continuous with Canaanite practice than Kaufmann allows.

## Stakes

The entire nature of the Yahweh phenomenon. If Kaufmann is right, Israelite religion is a genuine revolution — a radical break with everything that came before. If he's wrong, it's one more example of how religions evolve from polytheism through henotheism to monotheism, with Israel being unusual primarily in the thoroughness of its eventual break.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Effectively every scholar we've read except Tigay (on onomastics) and Sommer (on the fluidity model). Smith's entire corpus is an argument for continuity. Dever's archaeological work directly contradicts the claim that popular religion was fundamentally different. Römer traces the gradual emergence of monotheism from polytheistic roots.

**Alternative reading:** Israelite religion WAS distinctive — but distinctiveness doesn't require absolute difference "from the very beginning." The evidence suggests a gradual differentiation: early Israelite religion shared substantial features with Canaanite religion (El worship, divine council, possibly Asherah), with monotheism emerging as a hard-fought position over centuries.

## Assessment

This is where the rubber meets the road. Kaufmann's position is falsifiable: if we find evidence that early Israelites worshipped a pantheon, had a consort for Yahweh, or conceived of God in mythological terms, his "absolute difference from the beginning" claim fails. And we HAVE found that evidence — at Kuntillet Ajrud, in the figurines, in the Ugaritic parallels. The question is whether Kaufmann can account for this evidence without collapsing his thesis.
