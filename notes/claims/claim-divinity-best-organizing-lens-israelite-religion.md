# lewis-ocg-1.3: Studying Israelite religion through the 'lens of divinity' is the best organizing principle

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 1 — Introductory Matters]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 1

## The Claim

"By using divinity, I do not mean to narrow the topic theologically; rather, writing as a historian of religion, I intend to explore divinity as a window to the historical, the sociological, the performance of cult, the ideological, and the aesthetic." Lewis aligns himself with Smith and van der Toorn, who also use divinity as a conceptual category.

## Evidence

Lewis marshals three supporting voices:
- **Schwemer (2016):** Hittite cult was organized around the gods — their houses, their care, their favor. "The observance of the cult is regarded as a prerequisite of the gods' favour."
- **Kugel (2011):** "The peoples [of the ancient Near East] had always deferred, and referred, to God or the gods in all things."
- **Smith (2001b):** "To understand divinity is to extrapolate from the human condition... and to understand humanity is in turn to make some sense of divinity."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH that divinity is a *good* organizing principle. HIGH that Lewis's cited authorities support it. But "best" is harder to defend — other principles (sociology as in Albertz, material culture as in Zevit, literary genre as in Niditch) have distinct advantages.

## Stakes

The entire structure of the book. If divinity is the wrong lens, the book misses what's most important about Israelite religion. If it's the right lens, Lewis has found the thread that ties together El worship, Yahweh's origins, iconography, and characterization.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Zevit would argue for material culture (archaeology) as the more objective starting point. Albertz would say social structure (family → tribe → state) reveals more about how religion actually functioned. Niditch would foreground individual religious experience.

**Alternative reading:** Lewis isn't actually claiming divinity is "best" in absolute terms — he's saying it's the lens he's chosen and it has demonstrable advantages. The other lenses are complementary, not competing.

## Assessment

Lewis's choice is well-defended and internally coherent. His real contribution (judging from the roadmap) will be integrating iconographic and epigraphic evidence with textual analysis — and divinity is the natural category for that integration. The test is whether the lens of divinity illuminates or obscures the religion of ordinary Israelites who may not have been theologically sophisticated.
