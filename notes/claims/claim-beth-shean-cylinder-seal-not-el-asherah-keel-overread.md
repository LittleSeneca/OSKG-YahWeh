# lewis-ocg-5.5: Beth Shean Cylinder Seal Does NOT Show El and His Asherah

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 5 — Iconography of Divinity — El]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 5

## The Claim

"The difficulties in interpreting the evidence from this cylinder seal provide enough reasonable doubt that such a certain verdict cannot be rendered." Lewis objects that the "servant with fan or brush" motif "never appears in front of a god in contemporary glyptic art" but does appear before kings — suggesting the seated figure is human, not divine. The "armed man" behind the figure is unexplained. The asherah identification relies on a "conflation" of separate motifs.

## Evidence

- Keel and Uehlinger themselves admit the servant-with-fan appears in Neo-Assyrian scenes before "a standing or seated king" — making their conclusion that the scene "portrays a local deity" inconsistent
- The "armed man" behind the seated figure is unexplained in Keel and Uehlinger's reading
- The asherah identification relies on a "conflation" of separate motifs
- "While ʾIlu and ʾAthiratu are attested as a pair at Late Bronze Age Ugarit, our Iron Age Judean sources (i.e., epigraphic material and the biblical tradition) suggest the pairing of Baal and/or Yahweh with the asherah, not El"
- If the figure is a king (as the servant-with-fan comparanda suggest), the seal provides no evidence for El-Asherah pairing

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Lewis's critique is convincing — Keel and Uehlinger's own iconographic comparanda point toward a KING, not a god.

## Stakes

Whether we have ANY clear Iron Age iconographic evidence pairing El with Asherah. If the Beth Shean seal doesn't show this, then the Kuntillet ʿAjrud inscriptions ("Yahweh and his asherah") remain our only epigraphic evidence for divine pairing — and that's Yahweh/Asherah, not El/Asherah.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Keel and Uehlinger (1998) explicitly state the seal provides "completely independent confirmation" of the "Yahweh... and his asherah" interpretation. They use it as evidence that El-aspects "advance into the foreground of the Palestinian religious symbol system during Iron Age IIC."

**Alternative reading:** Keel and Uehlinger's reading — the seal shows El and his asherah, confirming the consort interpretation of the Kuntillet ʿAjrud inscriptions. But Lewis's critique of the servant-with-fan motif undermines this reading.

## Assessment

Lewis's critique is convincing. Keel and Uehlinger are the leading authorities, but Lewis catches them in a contradiction: the iconographic comparanda they cite point toward a KING, not a god. This is exactly the kind of careful iconographic critique Lewis's methodology chapter promised.
