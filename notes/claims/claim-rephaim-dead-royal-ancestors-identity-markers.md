# smith-obm-4.2: The Rephaim and mlkm represent dead royal ancestors functioning as cultural identity markers

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch4 — Pluralities Pairings and Other Divine Relations]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 4

## The Claim

The Rephaim (rp'um) and mlkm ("kings") in Ugaritic ritual texts represent deceased royal ancestors — the Rephaim as older tribal predecessors, the mlkm as more recent monarchs. At Ugarit they mark cultural IDENTIFICATION with the monarchy. In deuteronomic texts, the Rephaim mark cultural DISTANCE — they become the ancient "Canaanites" whom Israel is to disidentify from.

## Evidence

- CAT 1.161 distinguishes rp'um (older predecessors) from mlkm (deceased monarchs).
- rp'u bears the same address of 'ttrt as mlk (1.108.2; 1.107.42; 4.790.17), suggesting identification or conflation.
- El's blessing in 1.15 III 2-4, 13-15 evokes rp'um alongside high deities — royal cult integrated both.
- Israelite parallel: Bloch-Smith's 1992 Judahite Burials shows popular Israelite devotion to the dead. Deuteronomists react AGAINST this.
- "Both the Ugaritic and biblical views of the Rephaim are the products of their societies... Rephaim signal cultural distance or 'disidentification'" in deuteronomic texts.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Smith admits "the evidence supports little more than conjectures."

## Stakes

If the Rephaim function as identity markers, the transition from polytheism to monotheism involves not just theology but CULTURAL SELF-DEFINITION. Monotheism becomes partly an exercise in saying "we are NOT them."

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who read rp'um as a distinct category of chthonic deities rather than ancestors. The reconstruction of 1.161 is debated.

**Alternative reading:** The Rephaim could be chthonic deities proper, not dead kings — the ancestor interpretation over-reads fragmentary ritual texts. The deuteronomic "Rephaim" might reflect independent traditions rather than a polemical inversion.
