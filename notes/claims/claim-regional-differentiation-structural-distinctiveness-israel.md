# albertz-hir-intro.5: Regional differentiation is necessary alongside sociological stratification; Israel's distinctiveness is structural, not elemental

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — Introduction — History, Method, Dividing Lines]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §1.3

## The Claim

Beyond chronological and sociological divides, the pre-exilic period requires North/South differentiation (Jerusalem vs. Bethel, etc.), and the post-exilic period requires homeland/Diaspora differentiation. Moreover, "there are only a very few individual elements which are unique, and parallels to almost everything can be found in the environment... It is not the individual elements but the structure as a whole which makes the religion of Israel distinctive."

## Evidence

- North/South religious differences are "widely accepted" in scholarship
- The texts themselves have been "largely levelled out" by exilic reform groups and "polemically caricatured from the perspective of the South"
- In comparative perspective, Israel shared the ANE "religious heritage" — parallel prayers, sacrifices, kingship theology
- The "dynamic power within the social controversies" is what distinguishes Israel, not any single concept

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the factual claim about regional differentiation (this is standard); the "structure not elements" distinctiveness claim is harder to verify.

## Stakes

If Israel's distinctiveness is structural rather than elemental, then apologetic approaches that point to "unique" features (monotheism, covenant, aniconism) miss the point. Distinctiveness emerges from the *process* — the particular history of social and theological controversy. This aligns Albertz with the broader shift away from *sui generis* claims about Israel.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Kaufmann and his school argued for Israel's radical distinctiveness at the elemental level (no theogony, no divine sex, no myth). Conservative scholars (Block, Hess) argue specific elements like Israel's transcendent territorial claim are genuinely distinctive.

**Alternative reading:** Albertz's "structure not elements" argument could be a sophisticated way of maintaining Israel's distinctiveness while conceding the comparative evidence. It's unfalsifiable in practice — if every parallel is explained away as "element" while distinctiveness is always "structural," the model can absorb any contrary evidence.

## Assessment

This is a sensible middle position in the comparativist debate. It avoids both apologetic special pleading and flattening reductionism. But it also risks being a "just-so" story: Israel's distinctiveness is whatever can't be reduced to borrowed elements, and "structure" becomes a black box. I want to see how this plays out in the detailed historical reconstruction.
