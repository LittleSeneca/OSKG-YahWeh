# schmid-ht-6-9.8: The HB is "an observably multi-perspectival theological discussion" — not disconnected theologies but interconnected ones

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§6-9 — Jewish Reception to Implicit Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §9

## The Claim

"Thanks to the nature of the Hebrew Bible as scribal literature, these theologies are connected and interlinked with one another such that the Hebrew Bible appears as an observably multi-perspectival theological discussion. Therefore, the theology of the Hebrew Bible should not be broken up into the description of its various theologies, even though it comprises these as well."

## Evidence

- The internal interconnections of Fortschreibung literature create a genuine conversation
- The theologies "do not stand side by side in a disconnected fashion"
- The canonical shape and the scribal process both point toward interconnection

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** This claim is the structural warrant for the whole book: that there IS a "theology" (singular) of the HB, not just "theologies" (plural). The evidence for interconnections will need to be demonstrated in Parts E-H. As a programmatic statement, it's clear; as a proven claim, it's pending.

## Stakes

If Schmid can't demonstrate genuine interconnection — if the HB is just a collection of independent theological voices that happen to share a canon — then his singular "theology" framing collapses. The book should be "Theologies of the Hebrew Bible."

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue the HB contains theologies (plural), not a theology (singular) — e.g., Erhard Gerstenberger's *Theologies in the Old Testament*, Georg Fischer's pluralist approach. Canonical critics might argue the canon DOES give theological unity to diverse voices but through a different mechanism than Schmid's Fortschreibung model.

**Alternative reading:** The interconnections Schmid observes may be editorial stitching that doesn't create genuine theological dialogue. The diversity of voices may be more significant than the editorial bridges between them.

## Assessment

Schmid has set himself a high bar. The claim that the interconnections create a genuine "discussion" rather than just proximity is what distinguishes his project from the pluralists (Gerstenberger, Fischer). Whether he clears this bar depends on Parts E-H. The Jeremiah example in §9.2 is a promising start — it shows genuine theological dialogue across texts. But one example isn't enough.
