# schmid-ht-10-12-13.1: "Neither the Old Testament nor the Hebrew Bible exists" — there are multiple canonical forms

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§10, 12, 13 — Canon Pluriformity and Relativity]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §10

## The Claim

The Jewish and Christian traditions preserve different arrangements AND different inventories of books. The Hebrew Bible has 24 books in three parts; the Septuagint expands this with additional books; the Vulgate adds still more; Protestant Bibles return to the Hebrew inventory but keep the Septuagint order; Eastern churches have their own canons.

## Evidence

- Detailed comparison tables showing four canonical orders (Hebrew Bible, Septuagint, Vulgate, Protestant) across 3 pages
- The Torah is the only section with fixed order across all traditions
- The Nevi'im has at least 9 variations in the Latter Prophets; the Ketuvim at least 70 different orders
- The Babylonian Talmud (b. B. Bat. 14b-15a) orders the Major Prophets as Jeremiah-Ezekiel-Isaiah-Twelve — with a THEOLOGICAL justification ("entirely judgment...half judgment, half consolation...entirely consolation") that Schmid shows is a rationalization of an order based on book LENGTH (Jeremiah 21,835 words > Ezekiel 18,730 > Isaiah 16,392 > Twelve 14,355)

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** Empirically verifiable from any Bible comparison.

## Stakes

If the canon's shape is historically contingent (not divinely ordained), then theology cannot appeal to canonical order as carrying theological authority. The order-and-arrangement arguments that some canonical theologies rely on are undercut.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Canonical approach scholars (Childs, Seitz) who argue the community's canonical decisions carry theological weight even if multiple forms exist. It's not that "neither exists" — it's that each community has ITS canon.

**Alternative reading:** The pluriformity of canons doesn't negate the theological significance of any particular canon. Each community's canon IS its Bible. Schmid's "neither exists" framing is hyperbolic.

## Assessment

Schmid's deconstruction of the Babylonian Talmud's "theological" ordering is a perfect example of his method: what looks like a theological decision turns out to be a material one (scroll length), retroactively theologized.
