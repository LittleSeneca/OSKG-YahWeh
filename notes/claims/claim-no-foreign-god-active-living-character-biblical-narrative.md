# kaufmann-ri-1.5: No foreign god is ever an active, living character in biblical narrative

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch I — The Basic Problem]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter I

## The Claim

"Just as no foreign god is active in the creation stories, so no god other than YHWH ever appears at work in Israel's early history or in the battles between Israel and its neighbors. YHWH fights Israel's enemies, but no god ever appears as his living antagonist." When the Bible describes YHWH "judging" the gods of Egypt or battling Bel and Nebo, "it is always idols that are meant."

## Evidence

- **Creation (Gen 1-11):** "Precisely in the creation legends, where the bulk of mythological matter is imbedded, paganism is entirely absent; primeval man knows only the god YHWH."
- **The Exodus:** YHWH "works judgments" on the "gods of Egypt" — but these are idols, not living deities.
- **The Dagon story (1 Sam 5):** The model narrative. Dagon is a statue that falls and breaks. "We hear nothing of Dagon proper, Dagon the living god; not even the Philistines are said to suggest that the fall of the image portends evil for the god."
- **Sennacherib's boasts:** The Assyrian king says he "cast [the gods] into the fire" — a fetishistic conception. No hint that Sennacherib ascribes his triumphs to Ashur.
- **Prophetic judgments:** Jeremiah interprets "Bel is shamed, Merodach dismayed" with "her images are shamed, her idols dismayed." The gods ARE their images.
- **The humiliation of idols:** "It is characteristic that instead of fleeing, the pagan gods must be borne away on pack animals, or are carried off into exile with their priests."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the descriptive observation (the Bible really does depict foreign gods primarily as passive statues); LOW on the inference that this reflects Israelite belief rather than literary convention. Divine council imagery (1 Kings 22, Isaiah 6, Job 1-2, Psalm 82) shows active non-YHWH divine beings.

## Stakes

If Kaufmann is right that even the *narratives* — not just the polemics — depict pagan gods as mere statues, then the biblical worldview is fundamentally different from the pagan one at the level of basic storytelling.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Smith shows that the divine council imagery (1 Kings 22, Isaiah 6, Job 1-2) depicts living divine beings — not idols — even if subordinate to YHWH. Lewis documents that early Israelite religion conceived of other divine beings. The Bible DOES contain active non-YHWH divine beings — they're just not the *named* gods of the nations. The "sons of God" in Gen 6:1-4, the divine council in Psalm 82, the Satan in Job — these are living supernatural agents.

**Alternative reading:** The biblical narratives' treatment of foreign gods as statues is consistent with the monotheistic theology of their authors. But traces of an older view survive — Jephthah's acknowledgment of Chemosh (Judges 11:24), the divine council, the "sons of God." Kaufmann's generalization that "no foreign god is ever active" is true of the final form but ignores the vestiges of an older worldview.

## Assessment

The Dagon story is genuinely striking and deserves the attention Kaufmann gives it. It really is remarkable that even the Philistines seem to share the biblical view of Dagon as a statue — this suggests the story was composed entirely from within an Israelite conceptual framework, with no attempt at "native informant" perspective. BUT — this tells us about the literary conventions of biblical narrative, not about what ordinary Israelites believed. The Dagon story is a theological satire, not a documentary. Its depiction of Philistine religion tells us about Israelite theology, not Philistine religion.
