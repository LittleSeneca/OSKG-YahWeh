# schmid-ht-2-4.6: Gabler created biblical theology as historical discipline but his solution failed

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§2-4 — Concept of Theology from Plato to Gabler]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §4

## The Claim

Johann Philipp Gabler's 1787 inaugural lecture *Oratio de justo discrimine theologiae biblicae et dogmaticae* is the conventional starting point for biblical theology. Gabler distinguished biblical theology (historical, reconstructing what biblical authors thought) from dogmatic theology (contemporary, formulating theology for the present). He further distinguished "true biblical theology" (historically bounded) from "pure biblical theology" (universal, timeless perspectives) — the latter being the bridge to systematics.

## Evidence

- Gabler's lecture formalized earlier efforts: Hofmann (1770), Turretini (1776), Büsching (1756, 1758), Cocceius, Calixt
- "Gabler in no way lamented the breakup of the Bible and systematics; he instead attempted to conceive of an appropriate determination (*discrimen*) of the process"
- Gabler's four-part model: Bible → true biblical theology → pure biblical theology → systematics
- But "Gabler's edifice was not stable. Specifically, 'pure biblical theology' was shown to be a Trojan horse"
- Gotthilf Traugott Zachariä's alternative (1780): biblical theology as polemical critique of systematics — but he "was in danger of replacing traditional dogmatism with the biblicism that also consumed him"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is standard disciplinary history. Gabler is universally recognized as the founder of biblical theology as a distinct discipline.

## Stakes

Gabler's move is the beginning of modern biblical theology — AND the beginning of its problems. By trying to save both history (what the Bible MEANT) and normativity (what it MEANS), Gabler created a tension that has never been resolved. Schmid's own project (descriptive historical theology) is essentially choosing the "true biblical theology" side of Gabler's dichotomy and abandoning the "pure biblical theology" bridge to normativity.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who maintain that Gabler's program can be salvaged — that the bridge between historical description and normative theology can be built on better foundations than Gabler's.

**Alternative reading:** Gabler's failure was not in the concept of bridging history and normativity but in the specific execution. The task he set remains valid even if his solution was flawed.

## Assessment

This is Schmid setting up his own project in the lineage of Gabler's historical side while rejecting Gabler's normative ambitions. Schmid is more honest than Gabler: he admits the bridge to normativity can't be built, rather than pretending "pure biblical theology" can extract timeless truths from historically contingent texts.
