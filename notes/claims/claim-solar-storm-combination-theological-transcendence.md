# smith-ehg-4.2: The combination of solar and storm imagery expressed theological transcendence, not just syncretism

**Source:** [[Smith Chapter 4 — Yahweh and the Sun]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Early History of God* (2002), Chapter 4: Yahweh and the Sun

## The Claim

By combining storm and solar imagery for Yahweh, biblical texts expressed that Yahweh was not identified with any single natural phenomenon. He had power over AND transcended nature. This was a theological innovation, not just borrowing — the biblical writers didn't just copy Baal; they combined Baal's storm with the sun god's radiance in ways that transcended both deities. This complicates the simple "Israel just borrowed everything" narrative.

## Evidence

- **Psalm 50:1-3**: combines storm theophany with solar radiance
- **Ezekiel 43:1-5**: describes Yahweh's return to the temple with both storm and solar elements
- **Hosea 6:3**: "His going forth is sure as the dawn; he will come to us as the showers, as the spring rains that water the earth" — combines solar (*šaḥar*, dawn) with rain imagery in a single description of Yahweh
- **ANE parallels**: The Assyrian god Ningirsu's return to his temple is rendered in both storm and solar terms. An enameled tile of Tukulti-Ninurta II depicts Assur riding the winged sun disk with rain clouds on either side. *Enuma Elish* applies both solar and storm language to Marduk

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The combination of imagery is real. Whether it was a deliberate theological strategy (as Smith suggests) or just accumulated tradition is harder to determine.

## Stakes

This is actually a positive theological argument within Smith's critical framework. The combination of different nature imageries wasn't just syncretism — it was a way of saying "God is bigger than any one metaphor." This anticipates later theological moves toward divine transcendence. Smith at his most sympathetic to the biblical theologians: not just cataloguing borrowings but recognizing genuine theological creativity.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see all divine nature imagery as undifferentiated metaphor. Those who argue the combination was accidental — different traditions accumulated without deliberate theological intent.

**Alternative reading:** The combination could reflect the practical reality that storm and sun were both royal divine symbols, and the monarchy used whichever was convenient. No deliberate transcendence argument required.
