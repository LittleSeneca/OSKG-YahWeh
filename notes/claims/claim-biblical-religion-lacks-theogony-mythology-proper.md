# kaufmann-ri-3.2: Biblical religion lacks theogony and mythology proper

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

"The store of biblical legends lacks the fundamental myth of paganism: the theogony. All theogonic motifs are similarly absent. Israel's god has no pedigree, fathers no generations; he neither inherits nor bequeaths his authority. He does not die and is not resurrected. He has no sexual qualities or desires and shows no need of or dependence upon powers outside himself."

## Evidence

- NO THEOGONY: YHWH has no origins, no parents, no children, no consort
- NO DIVINE BIOGRAPHY: biblical legends tell of God's dealings with men and creation, never of His private life. Rare survivals (God walking in garden "at the cool of the day," "mount of meeting" in Isa 14:13) are embedded in passages about God's relationship to creation, not personal biography
- Even YHWH's battles (Rahab, Leviathan, dragon, sea) are NOT theogonically significant — they involve creatures of God who rebelled, not primordial divine rivals. Explicit contrast with Marduk's defeat of Tiamat
- Divine council passages (1 Kings 22:19ff; Isa 6; Zech 3:1) "never involve feasting and entertainment, like those of the pagan gods; they are solemn assemblies, concerned with the judgment of men"
- Refutes alternatives: not Semitic "poverty of imagination" (Babylon and Canaan had rich mythologies); not pre-mythological stage; not "divine king" concept (Ashur acquired consort/pantheon through syncretism; YHWH did not)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Bible really does lack theogony and divine biography — a demonstrable textual fact. This is Kaufmann's strongest argument.

## Stakes

If the absence of theogony is PRIMARY rather than the product of editorial purging, Israelite religion was non-mythological from the start. If it's the product of censorship, there WAS an earlier mythological stage — which supports the evolutionary model.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-family-model-polytheism-conceptual-unity]] — Smith argues the Ugaritic texts show Israel DID share mythological conceptions (divine council, Baal's battle with Sea/Leviathan, El as father god) preserved in fragmentary, polemically-transformed form. [[claim-yhwh-originated-southern-deserts-edom-seir]] offers alternative: absence of theogony reflects YHWH's non-Canaanite origins, not an anti-mythological "idea."

**Alternative reading:** YHWH's non-Canaanite origins explain the theogony gap. Gods entering a pantheon from outside don't have genealogies within it. YHWH was a southern deity identified with El, the father god. As El, He already HAD mythology (divine council, sons of God) — but His own origins weren't narrated because He wasn't born into the Canaanite system.

## Assessment

Kaufmann is strongest HERE. The absence of theogony is a genuine datum any theory must explain. The critical explanation ("it was edited out") is inadequate — there are no detectable seams where theogonic material was excised. The Ugaritic texts give us a control: if the Bible preserved Canaanite mythology, we'd expect at least fragments of divine birth, sexual activity, or death/resurrection. We don't find them. This doesn't prove original monotheism, but it's a serious problem for the view that Israelite religion was just Canaanite polytheism with Yahweh swapped in.

That said, Kaufmann minimizes genuinely mythological material present: Gen 6:1-4, divine council, Chaoskampf allusions. He assimilates ALL to his non-mythological framework, but the Ugaritic parallels are too close to dismiss.
