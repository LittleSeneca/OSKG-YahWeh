# day-ygc-5.2: Shamgar ben Anat is an honorific military title meaning "son of (the warrior goddess) Anat"

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 5 — Yahweh and Astarte and Anat]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 5

## The Claim

Shamgar ben Anat (Judg 3:31; 5:6) is "metaphorically described as 'son of (the goddess) Anat'" — the term functions as "an honorific military title" (p. 135). Anat was a warrior goddess, and other Canaanites called *bn ʻnt* were warriors.

## Evidence

1. **Against the "resident of Beth-Anat" theory:**
   - *bn ʻnt* is found in Canaanite contexts where a place name isn't credible
   - "It is much more usual for a person's ancestry than his or her place of origin to be cited in the Old Testament"
   - We'd expect *bēt-hāʻănātî* for a Beth-Anat resident, not *ben-ʻănāt* (p. 134-135)
2. **Against Fensham's "Hanaean" theory:** The Mari Hanaeans are "about five hundred years prior to the time of Shamgar ben Anat" (p. 135)
3. **Positive evidence:**
   - Ugaritic texts attest *bn ʻnt* for individuals (KTU² 4.307.6; 4.320.4)
   - Two inscribed ARROWHEADS owned by warriors bear *bn ʻnt* — "which fits the honorific military explanation" (p. 135-136). One from El-Khadr reads *ʻbdlbʼt bn ʻnt*; another from the Beqaʻ valley has *bn bn ʻn[t]*
   - Anat was a warrior goddess — the military title makes cultural sense

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The arrowhead evidence is the strongest single point — warrior weapons inscribed with "son of Anat" make the military-title reading natural, but the evidence is circumstantial, not explicit.

## Stakes

If correct, this reveals that Anat was sufficiently known in early Israel that "son of Anat" could function as a military honorific — implying cultural familiarity with the goddess beyond what the OT text explicitly acknowledges.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Albright, Alt, Noth, Mazar — "resident of Beth-Anat" (majority older view). Day's counter-arguments are solid.

**Alternative reading:** Shamgar was from a town called Beth-Anat (Josh 19:38; Judg 1:33), and *ben* here means "resident of" rather than "son of."

## Assessment

The arrowhead evidence tips the balance decisively. It's hard to imagine a "resident of Beth-Anat" inscribing that location on a weapon. "Son of [warrior goddess]" as a military title makes immediate sense analogously to "son of Mars" or similar martial epithets.
