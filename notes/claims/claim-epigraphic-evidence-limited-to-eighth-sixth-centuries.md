# tigay-nog-2.4: Epigraphic evidence applies primarily to 8th-6th centuries

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Chapter II

## The Claim

"Since the inscriptional evidence sets in for the most part in the eighth century, our conclusions apply mainly to the period beginning then. The evidence does not directly argue against the greater prevalence of polytheism earlier." This is Tigay's own temporal caveat — an important limitation on his thesis.

## Evidence

- The bulk of Hebrew epigraphic material dates to the 8th-6th centuries BCE (the late monarchy period)
- Earlier periods (Judges, early monarchy) have far fewer inscriptions
- Tigay argues the biblical onomastic evidence — which goes back further — is consistent with the epigraphic evidence, suggesting the YHWH-dominance pattern may hold for the Divided Monarchy and perhaps earlier
- But he acknowledges this extrapolation is inferential, not directly attested

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Tigay himself explicitly states this temporal limitation; it is a fact about the epigraphic record, not an interpretation.

## Stakes

This caveat is crucial for the debate. The consensus position (Smith, Römer, Dever) claims that polytheism was MORE prevalent in earlier periods. Tigay's epigraphic data cannot directly refute this because it doesn't cover the earlier periods. His argument about earlier periods must rely on the biblical onomastic evidence, which is more contested. The temporal limitation means Tigay and the consensus could BOTH be right: Tigay for the 8th-6th centuries, the consensus for earlier periods.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** No one disagrees with the factual limitation. The disagreement is about its significance.

**Alternative reading:** Smith/Römer would argue the temporal limitation is FATAL to Tigay's broader thesis. If polytheism was rampant in the 10th-9th centuries and only declined by the 8th (when the epigraphic record begins), Tigay's data captures the END of a process, not the norm.
