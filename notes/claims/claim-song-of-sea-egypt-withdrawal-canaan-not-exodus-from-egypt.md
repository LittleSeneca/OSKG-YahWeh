# fleming-ybi-6.7: The Song of the Sea recalls Egypt's withdrawal from Canaan, not an exodus from Egypt

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 6 — The People of Yahweh]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 6

## The Claim

"The Song of the Sea is an origins story. A people identified by its worship of Yahweh in a space inhabited by Yahweh comes to share this land and enjoy security from its neighbors after Pharaoh's defeat" (p. 239). Following Hendel, "the exodus preserves a 'cultural memory' of Egypt's domination of Canaan during the dynasties of the New Kingdom" (p. 239).

## Evidence

- The people in the poem are never in Egypt — they enter the land from outside, passing between four awestruck peoples (Philistia, Edom, Moab, Canaan)
- Pharaoh's army is defeated at "the Sea of Reeds" (Yam Suph) without reference to any human enemy present — "Egypt's army is put out of action without reference to any human enemy, so we cannot picture any military conflict except in prospect" (p. 239)
- "The four peoples named in verses 14b-15 are all groups that have territorial associations, effectively enclosing a particular space to be occupied by the human protagonists" (p. 239) — they also benefit from Egypt's defeat
- Hendel's "cultural memory" thesis: the poem recalls Egypt's New Kingdom domination of the Levant, not a literal escape from Egypt
- The poem's geography — Peleset (Philistia), Edom, Moab, and Canaan — frames the southern highlands, suiting the core territory of Judah, not northern Israel
- The poem was embedded in the Moses-exodus narrative secondarily — "the separate story of a 'sea' episode originated in the poem" (p. 239)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The observation that the poem's people are never in Egypt is textually solid. The "cultural memory" reading is plausible and has scholarly support (Hendel). But the transition from "Egyptian withdrawal from Canaan" to "exodus from Egypt" is a major reinterpretation, and the poem's original setting remains uncertain.

## Stakes

If the Song of the Sea originally described Egyptian withdrawal from Canaan (not Israelite escape from Egypt), then the entire exodus narrative is a secondary development, and the poem's "your people, Yahweh" refers to highland populations who benefited from Egypt's departure — not to escapees from Egypt.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional readings that treat the poem as celebrating the exodus from Egypt. Also, European scholars who date the poem post-exilic and dependent on the prose — if the prose came first, the poem can't preserve older tradition.

**Alternative reading:** The poem always described an exodus from Egypt — the absence of explicit Egypt-as-starting-point is poetic compression, not evidence of a different original setting.

## Assessment

This is one of Fleming's most creative arguments and deserves more attention. The observation that "the beneficiaries have never been in Egypt" is undeniable on a plain reading of the text. Whether this reflects an older tradition or a poetic choice is debatable, but Fleming has shifted the burden of proof.
