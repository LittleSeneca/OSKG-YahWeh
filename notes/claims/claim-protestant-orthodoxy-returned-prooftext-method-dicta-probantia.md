# schmid-ht-2-4.5: Protestant orthodoxy returned to scholastic systematizing via prooftext method

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§2-4 — Concept of Theology from Plato to Gabler]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §3

## The Claim

"Protestant orthodoxy of the later sixteenth and seventeenth centuries returned to the systematizing forms of theology that were common prior to the Reformation." The *dicta probantia* (prooftexts) method treated the Bible as a source of quotations to support doctrinal positions.

## Evidence

- Melanchthon preferred *doctrina christiana* to "theology"
- Bartholomäus Keckermann's *Systema S.S. Theologiae* (1602) listed theology alongside other academic disciplines
- Johann Gerhard's *Loci theologici* (1610-1622) as comprehensive academic presentation of doctrine
- Sebastian Schmidt's *Collegium biblicum* (1671) — the paradigmatic prooftext work, listing OT and NT quotations by theological topic

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Standard history of Protestant scholasticism. The key figures and their works are well-documented.

## Stakes

This sets up the crisis: Protestant orthodoxy's prooftext method collapsed when historical-critical scholarship showed the Bible didn't say what the prooftexts needed it to say. The "slackening of the unity between text and systemization" is what created the need for biblical theology as a mediating discipline. Without understanding the prooftext crisis, Gabler's project is unintelligible.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Minimal disagreement about the historical facts. Some might argue Schmid overstates the dominance of the prooftext method.

**Alternative reading:** The prooftext method was never as dominant as Schmid implies; it was one approach among several in post-Reformation Protestantism.

## Assessment

Schmid's genealogy is clear: Luther existentialized theology → Protestant orthodoxy re-systematized it → historical criticism exposed the gap → Gabler tried to bridge it with "biblical theology." This is compressed but accurate.
