# kaufmann-ri-ep.2: The Torah's contradictions prove its antiquity and faithful transmission

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Epilogue and Prospect]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Epilogue

## The Claim

The Torah was "compiled from ancient scrolls, embodying divergent and at times mutually contradictory matter." This very fact proves the compilers "dared not alter a jot or tittle of, much less add anything to, the ancient revelation." The contradictions aren't evidence of late, fluid composition — they're evidence of reverent preservation of ancient, already-fixed materials.

## Evidence

The divergent and contradictory materials in the Torah are preserved side by side without harmonization. This suggests the compilers treated their sources as sacrosanct — already canonical in effect, even before formal canonization.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-low

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's argument is clever but cuts both ways. Contradictions can also indicate multiple authors over a long period of composition, not just reverent compilation of ancient materials.

## Stakes

The dating of the Torah literature. If contradictions prove antiquity, then the sources are pre-exilic. If they prove fluidity, the sources developed over centuries.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Source critics see the contradictions as evidence of different authors writing at different times, with the final combination happening late.

**Alternative reading:** The contradictions result from a long process of composition and combination — not from reverent compilation of already-ancient texts, but from the gradual accretion and combination of traditions that eventually achieved canonical status.

## Assessment

Kaufmann isn't wrong that contradiction-preservation CAN indicate reverence for source materials. But he needs independent evidence for the antiquity of those sources, which is what Chapter V was supposed to provide.
