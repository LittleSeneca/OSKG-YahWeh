# smith-obm-4.6: Divinized qualities represent semi-independent divine entities, especially in liturgical contexts

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch4 — Pluralities Pairings and Other Divine Relations]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 4

## The Claim

Ugaritic texts list qualities alongside deities (CAT 1.65: ḥnn 'il, nṣbt 'il, šlm 'il; CAT 1.123: ṣdq mšr, kbd w nr). These are "divinized qualities" that function as divine attendants, especially in theophanic/liturgical settings.

## Evidence

- CAT 1.65: "The Mercy of El, The Constancy of El, The Well-being of El" invoked after El and Athirat.
- CAT 1.123.12-16: nġh, ṣdq mšr, ḥn bn 'il, kbd w nr alongside standard deities.
- ṣdq mšr parallels Philo of Byblos' Misor and Sydyk.
- Biblical parallels: nōgāh, kābôd, nûr appear in theophanic contexts (2 Sam 22:13; Ezek 10:4; Hab 3:4).
- Psalm 43:3: "'Light' and 'Truth' are personified as two of Yahweh's attendants, sent from the divine council as 'guardian angels' to guide the psalmist to the Temple."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The biblical parallels (especially Psalm 43:3) make the pattern convincing — Ugaritic data alone could be read differently but the Israelite reflexes confirm the conceptual category.

## Stakes

If qualities could be divinized, the line between "deity" and "attribute of deity" was fluid in West Semitic thought. This matters for monotheism: when Israel attributes Mercy, Truth, Glory to Yahweh alone, it may be absorbing what were once separate divine entities into the single god's personality.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those who read the lists as abstract nouns (liturgical invocations of qualities) rather than hypostatized entities. The boundary between "invoking a quality" and "invoking a semi-divine personification of that quality" is blurry.

**Alternative reading:** These could be scribal shorthand — ways to list what the deity provides to worshippers (mercy, constancy, well-being) rather than independent entities.

## Assessment

The biblical reflexes are the strongest evidence here. When Psalm 43:3 treats Light and Truth as divine guides (not just abstractions), it confirms that this pattern of thought was alive in Israel. Smith's continuum from quality → personified quality → attendant deity is plausible.
