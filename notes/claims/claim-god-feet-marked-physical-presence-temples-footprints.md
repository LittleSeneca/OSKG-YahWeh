# stav-god-2-4.1: God's feet marked his physical presence on earth — temples were his footprints, and standing stones (massebot) marked the places where God had stood, rendering him locally present at specific sites.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part I — Feet and Legs]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 2

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that God's feet were not metaphorical but the mechanism by which he rendered himself present in specific places. "The force of his feet splits mountains. They shake the earth as he strides out from the desert. They crush the bodies of his enemies. They transform dust and dirt into holy ground." Temples were the "place of my feet," the "place for the soles of my feet." A footprint "is a material memory of its owner treading upon the surface on which the print appears, conjuring the presence of the whole body — the whole person."

## Evidence

**The 'Ain Dara temple (material culture):**
- 40 miles NW of Aleppo, Syria. "The closest we can come to the temple that stood in Jerusalem"
- Two giant footprints, about a metre in length, carved into the threshold, pointing INTO the temple
- A left footprint on the vestibule entrance, a right footprint inside the holy of holies
- "There were no exiting footprints; no indication that the deity had left the temple. Rather, the footprints signalled the permanent presence of the god within"
- Stavrakopoulou's personal account: "I stepped into these enormous, yet delicate feet. They dwarfed my own. I was standing in the bare footsteps of a god"

**Biblical evidence — God's feet on earth:**
- Genesis 3:8: Adam and Eve hear Yahweh's FOOTSTEPS approaching in the garden
- Genesis 18: Abraham sees Yahweh STANDING with two divine beings
- Exodus 24:9-10: "They saw the God of Israel. And under his feet there was something like a brick pavement of lapis lazuli, like the very heavens for clarity" — a close-up of the floor beneath God's feet
- Isaiah 60:13: Temple garden trees "glorify where my feet rest"
- Ezekiel 43:7: "This is the place for the soles of my feet, where I will reside among the people of Israel forever"

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** The biblical texts describing God's feet are extensive and explicit; the 'Ain Dara temple footprints provide material-culture confirmation of the ANE pattern.

## Stakes

God's feet render him LOCALLY present. He is HERE, not everywhere. This is a radically localized deity — unlike the omnipresent God of later theology. Temples were not merely "houses of worship" but literal dwelling places where the deity stood, leaving his footprints in stone. This materializes the abstract theological claim that God dwells among his people into a concrete, physical, architectural reality.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-genesis-1-26-anti-fluid-manifesto-image-without-embodiment]] (Sommer — the Priestly and Deuteronomic anti-fluid theologies explicitly resist the idea that God is locally present in the temple; the kavod dwells, but God himself remains in heaven).

**Alternative reading:** The foot language is metaphorical — God's "presence" is real but not physically locatable; the temple houses God's "name" (Deuteronomic) or "glory" (Priestly), not his actual feet.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou's strength in Part I: her ability to connect material culture (the 'Ain Dara footprints) to biblical texts (God's feet on the lapis lazuli pavement) to ANE social practice (feet as property markers). This is interdisciplinary scholarship at its best. She's not just reading texts — she's reconstructing an embodied world.
