# albertz-hir-3.3-3.4.3: Jerusalem temple theology was fundamentally alien to pre-state liberation Yahwism

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §3.3-3.4 — State Cults of Jerusalem and Bethel]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §3.3

## The Claim

Jerusalem theology revolved around two poles: "the kingship of Yahweh and Jerusalem as the city of God." It combined El's static kingship (eternal, head of divine assembly) with Baal's dynamic kingship (victory over chaos), then "transposed into the sphere of political history" — Yahweh's victories over Israel's neighbors proved his universal kingship. "Yahweh changed from being liberator of the oppressed to being promoter of his own policy of expansion and oppression."

## Evidence

- **Yahweh Sabaoth**: "enthroned on the cherubim" — a heavenly court of divine beings implementing his rule. Isaiah's vision (Isa 6) shows how central this was
- **Divine kingship**: "king over all gods" (Ps 95:3), enthroned above the flood (Ps 29:10), ruling the nations (Ps 47:3-9)
- **Zion theology**: Yahweh made his dwelling on Zion (Ps 76:3); "Yahweh is in her midst" (Ps 46:5f.); "the holiest of Elyon's dwellings" — "almost complete identification of Yahweh with the capital"
- Zion was identified with "the peak of Zaphon" (Ps 48:3) — the mythical mountain of the gods in northern Syria — "not with Sinai in the south!"
- **Creator theology**: "creator of heaven and earth" (qōnē šāmayim wā'āreṣ, Gen 14:19), taken over from El-Elyon
- **Other deities**: Asherah (1 Kings 15:13; 2 Kings 23:7), Shemesh the sun-god (Ps 19:5-7; 2 Kings 23:11; Ezek 8:16ff.), Zedek ("righteousness" as a hypostasis)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** HIGH on the existence of these theological elements (the Psalms are unambiguous). MEDIUM on the interpretation that this represents a betrayal of pre-state Yahwism — that's Albertz's normative judgment.

## Stakes

Albertz is arguing that Jerusalem theology was not a development OF Yahwism but a replacement OF Yahwism — substituting El/Baal kingship theology and Canaanite sanctuary traditions for the Exodus-liberation core. If true, the prophetic critique of Jerusalem (Isaiah, Jeremiah) was not innovation but restoration.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see Jerusalem theology as a legitimate and creative development of Yahwism — incorporating ANE royal ideology was a theological achievement, not a betrayal. The Psalms' universalism and creation theology are among Israel's greatest theological contributions.

**Alternative reading:** Jerusalem theology represents a creative synthesis (not a betrayal) — Yahweh's liberation character was extended to cosmic scale. The identification of Zion with Zaphon is mythological poetry, not syncretistic replacement.

## Assessment

Albertz's normative judgment ("betrayal of liberation Yahwism") is analytically powerful but normatively loaded. The existence of these theological elements in the Jerusalem cult is uncontroversial; the question is whether they represent betrayal or development. The key insight — that Jerusalem theology suppressed the Exodus tradition in favor of El/Baal kingship and Zion ideology — is textually demonstrable: compare the absence of Exodus themes in the royal psalms with their centrality in Hosea and Deuteronomy.
