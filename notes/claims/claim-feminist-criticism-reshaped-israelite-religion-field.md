# lewis-ocg-2.7: Feminist criticism has fundamentally reshaped the study of Israelite religion

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 2 — History of Scholarship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 2

## The Claim

Lewis traces feminist contributions from Elizabeth Cady Stanton's *The Woman's Bible* (1895) through the second wave (Trible, 1978) to the current integration of feminist criticism with archaeology, iconography, and social-scientific approaches. He singles out Carol Meyers as the scholar whose work "stands out among all others" for blending archaeological and textual analysis of women's roles in household religion.

## Evidence

- Extensive bibliography: Trible, Bird, Ackerman, Brenner, Meyers, Schroer, Bloch-Smith, Stavrakopoulou, Nakhai, Marsman, Ebeling, Chapman, Hackett, Frymer-Kensky, and others
- Meyers's argument that "women were *not* much more disadvantaged in their participation of *communal* religious activities than were non-priestly males" — a revisionist claim that challenges the default assumption of patriarchal exclusion

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** VERY HIGH that feminist criticism has transformed the field. MEDIUM on Meyers's specific claim about women's participation — the evidence is more ambiguous than Lewis's favorable citation suggests.

## Stakes

If feminist criticism is right that women were central to household and family religion, then our picture of Israelite religion as male-dominated is an artifact of our sources (written by male elites), not a reflection of actual practice.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** More traditional scholars would argue that the biblical text's patriarchal assumptions reflect genuine social realities. The debate isn't about whether women practiced religion (they obviously did) but about their status and autonomy within it.

**Alternative reading:** Feminist criticism has opened up essential questions, but the evidence for women's religious agency is limited by the nature of the sources. The contribution is more in asking better questions than in providing definitive answers.

## Assessment

Lewis gives feminist scholarship significant space — more than Dever does in his comparable historiography chapter. This is appropriate for a 2020 volume. The challenge is whether the rest of Lewis's book actually integrates feminist insights (e.g., about Asherah, about family religion) or merely nods to them in this chapter and moves on. His focus on El and Yahweh (both male deities) means goddess religion gets less sustained attention than it deserves.
