# romer-inv-1.4: The biblical narrative itself acknowledges that Yhwh was not originally Israel's god

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 1 — The Name]] — Thomas Romer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 1: The God of Israel and His Name

## The Claim

"Both of these narratives [Exodus 3 and Exodus 6] assert... that the relation between Yhwh and Israel had not existed from the beginning, but was instead the result of a certain encounter." Yhwh reveals himself to Moses as a previously unknown god. The Bible itself preserves the memory of Yhwh's foreign origin. "The idea that the god Yhwh has a non-Israelite origin has become the established consensus in scholarly circles."

## Evidence

- Exodus 3: Yhwh appears to Moses while Moses is pasturing his Midianite father-in-law's flocks. The encounter happens at a "mountain of god" outside Israel — between Egypt and Judea.
- Exodus 6:2-3: God explicitly tells Moses: "I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as El Shadday, but by my name Yhwh I did not make myself known to them."
- Romer's summary: "Scholars are virtually unanimous in thinking that the god Yhwh was not at the beginning the god of Israel. In other words, the history of Israel's religion did not begin with a 'Yhwh alone' revelation that only later was corrupted by polytheism."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH that Exodus 6:2-3 acknowledges Yhwh was not known by that name to the patriarchs. MEDIUM that this reflects genuine historical memory rather than theological retrojection.

## Stakes

This is the same claim Smith and Romer both make: the Bible's own narrative admits Yhwh was a latecomer. The patriarchs worshipped El, not Yhwh. The "God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob" was, historically, El — and only later identified with Yhwh.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional/conservative readers who read Exodus 6:2-3 as progressive revelation (God revealed his true name Yahweh later, but was always the same deity) rather than as evidence of distinct deities later merged. Heiser reads the passage as revealing a new covenantal name, not a new deity.

**Alternative reading:** Exodus 6:2-3 can be harmonized with traditional theology: the patriarchs knew God AS El Shadday, but it was the same God under a different title. The text doesn't require that the patriarchs worshipped a different deity — only that they knew God by a different name.

## Assessment

Romer's claim is textually secure: Exodus 6:2-3 unambiguously states Yhwh was NOT known by that name to the patriarchs. The question is historical: does this preserve genuine memory or is it a theological construction explaining why different biblical traditions use different divine names? Smith reads it as memory; Schmid would read it as retrojection. The text itself is clear; its historical value is debated.
