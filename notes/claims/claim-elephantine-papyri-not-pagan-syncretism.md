# kaufmann-ri-4.8: Elephantine papyri do not show pagan syncretism

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch IV — The Religion of the People]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter IV

## The Claim

"The religion of the Jewish garrison of Elephantine... must not be viewed as representative of the ancient popular religion of Israel." Even there, "what is decisive is the complete absence of a mythological conception of [YHWH]." The minor deities found in the papyri (Herembethel, Anathbethel, Anathiahu, Ashambethel) were at most akin to satyrs, members of YHWH's entourage — not evidence of genuine polytheism.

## Evidence

- Garrison was isolated for over a century in alien environment: "They had become assimilated linguistically and intermarried with their neighbors"
- "No Israelite writing was found among them, although the pagan Ahikar romance was"
- "In contrast to the Babylonian colony of exiles they had no prophets among them, though they did have priests" — their religion "can therefore be used only in a most qualified way"
- Despite pagan god names in onomasticon, "none of the 'gods of the nations' after whom biblical Israel strayed (Baal, Ashtoreth, Chemosh, etc.) are worshiped by the Elephantine Jews"
- Peculiar divine names (Herembethel, Anathbethel, Anathiahu, Ashambethel) "are never found in these forms in the Bible. If they were originally Israelite divinities... they can at most have been akin to the satyrs of the popular religion"
- "Only YHWH is described: he is the 'Lord' or 'God' of heaven. Only he has a temple and festivals"
- "Ashambethel and Anathbethel have a treasury in the temple of YHWH, but only YHWH is represented as a universal God"
- Elephantine Jews distinguish their priests (kahanayya) from pagan priests (kumrayya) — using biblical terminology

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** Right that Elephantine shouldn't represent pre-exilic popular religion. But Anat-Yahu formulation looks like consort theology, not "satyrs." Having a treasury in YHWH's temple means these beings received cult.

## Stakes

Whether the Elephantine papyri provide evidence that even diaspora Jews practiced something looking like polytheism — challenging Kaufmann's claim that monotheism was deeply rooted.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who use Elephantine as evidence of continuing polytheistic tendencies in Judaism. Theophoric names combining YHWH with Anat (Anathiahu) are particularly hard to read as anything other than recognition of Anat as a deity alongside YHWH. Having a treasury in YHWH's temple suggests cultic attention.

**Alternative reading:** Kaufmann is right that Elephantine is an extreme case — a garrison community isolated for over a century, without prophetic leadership, in a polytheistic environment. It may tell us more about Yahwism under diaspora conditions than pre-exilic popular religion. BUT: the specific form of Elephantine's syncretism IS interesting — if these Jews brought polytheistic tendencies from Palestine, why don't they worship Baal, Asherah, or the Queen of Heaven? The Elephantine pantheon looks different, which could support Kaufmann's diaspora-development claim.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's dismissal of Elephantine as evidence for popular polytheism is partly justified — Elephantine is a weird case. But his characterization of Anat-Yahu and Anat-Bethel as analogous to "satyrs" is special pleading. "Anat-Yahu" means "Anat of YHWH" — a consort formulation. Having a treasury in YHWH's temple means cultic attention. "Members of his entourage" is doing a lot of work to make polytheistic practice sound monotheistic. That said, he's right that even at Elephantine, YHWH is supreme in a qualitatively different way — the question is whether that difference is enough to call the religion "monotheistic."
