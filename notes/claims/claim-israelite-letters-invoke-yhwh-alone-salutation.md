# tigay-nog-2.1: Israelite letters invoke YHWH alone in salutations

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Chapter II

## The Claim

"The letters are very instructive in this regard. The formula of salutation regularly invokes the blessing of a deity on the addressee... In ALL Israelite letters, the deity invoked is YHWH, and YHWH alone." The formula "I bless you by YHWH" (*brktk lyhwh*) is standard in letters from Arad, Lachish, and elsewhere. No letter invokes any other god.

## Evidence

- Standard salutation formula: *brktk lyhwh* ("I bless you by YHWH") — consistent across all known Israelite letters
- Letters from Arad (military fortress correspondence), Lachish (military/administrative), and other sites all show the same pattern
- **The contrast with neighboring cultures:** Phoenician letters invoke multiple deities. Assyrian letters invoke Ashur and other gods. Israelite letters invoke ONLY YHWH.
- "The usage in the letters corresponds to that of the onomastic evidence: YHWH is overwhelmingly the only deity invoked."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The textual evidence from Arad, Lachish, and other sites is clear and consistent; Tigay surveyed all known pre-exilic Hebrew letters.

## Stakes

Letters are a different genre from personal names. Names might be conventional/traditional. Letters are deliberate communication — the writer CHOOSES which deity to invoke. The universal invocation of YHWH alone in letter salutations (a genre where neighboring cultures freely invoked multiple gods) is strong evidence for practical monolatry as a social norm.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-tigay-onomastics-does-not-disprove-polytheism]] (Smith)

**Alternative reading:** Letters are formal/official communication. They may reflect epistolary convention rather than personal belief. But the contrast with neighboring cultures' letter formulas makes this hard to sustain — if polytheism were widespread, why wouldn't Israelite letters invoke multiple deities like Phoenician and Assyrian letters do?
