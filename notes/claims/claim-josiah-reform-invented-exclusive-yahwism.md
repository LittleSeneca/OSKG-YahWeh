# romer-inv-11.2: The reform INVENTED exclusive Yahwism — it didn't restore it

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 11 — Reform of Josiah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 11

## The Claim

"Before Josiah, kings who worshipped Yhwh at multiple sanctuaries with steles and asherah poles were the NORM. After Josiah's reform, this became 'idolatry.' The reform didn't restore an original purity — it INVENTED a new standard and projected it backward."

## Evidence

- The high places had been legitimate. The Deuteronomists themselves admit this with the footnote at 1 Kings 3:2: "the people were sacrificing at the high places, however, because no house had yet been built"
- The asherah had been acceptable in the Jerusalem temple for generations
- Josiah's reform was "as much political as religious — an assertion of Judean independence as Assyrian power waned"
- Every king before Josiah who tolerated multiple sanctuaries was judged by the Dtr editors as "doing evil in the sight of Yhwh" — but the historical reality was that these kings were practicing NORMATIVE Yahwism as it was understood in their time
- Josiah's reform retroactively criminalized what had been standard Yahwistic practice for centuries

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH. The evidence that these practices were originally legitimate is overwhelming — the Deuteronomistic editors themselves provide the evidence in their own footnotes and the pattern of their judgments.

## Stakes

The key irony: Josiah is remembered as the great reformer who restored pure Yahwism. Historically, he invented it. The "restoration" was an innovation. This inverts the entire biblical narrative: the golden age of pure monotheism at Israel's origins never existed. What looks like a return to origins was actually a radical break — the invention of a new kind of religion that claimed to be the old one.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue Josiah's reform was a genuine restoration of older traditions (perhaps a "Mosaic" Yahwism that had been corrupted by syncretism). The biblical text itself is the primary counter-claim: it presents Josiah as restoring, not inventing.

**Alternative reading:** The reform restored a "pure" Yahwism that existed among some circles (prophetic, priestly) but had been suppressed by the monarchy. The innovations were in enforcement and scope, not in the theology itself.

## Assessment

This is the claim that makes Römer's book subversive. The entire biblical narrative of decline-and-restoration — pure origins, syncretistic corruption, prophetic reform — is backwards. The "corruption" was the norm. The "reform" was the innovation. Josiah didn't restore anything; he created a new religion and gave it an ancient pedigree. This pattern — innovation disguised as restoration — is Römer's master key for unlocking the entire history of Israelite religion.
