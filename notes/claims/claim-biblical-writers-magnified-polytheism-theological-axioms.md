# tigay-nog-2.5: Biblical writers magnified polytheism, not minimized it

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Conclusions

## The Claim

The prophets and historiographers were working with "theological axioms rather than historical data." The doctrine of collective responsibility generalized the sins of individuals to the entire nation. The exile demanded an explanation; polytheism provided it. The sweeping indictments "are based more on theological axioms than historical data."

This inverts the standard critical reading. The consensus assumes the prophetic indictments reflect reality — polytheism was rampant, and the prophets railed against it. Tigay argues the opposite: the prophets NEEDED to portray polytheism as rampant because their theological framework (collective guilt, exile-as-punishment) required it. The indictments are theological rhetoric, not sociological reporting.

## Evidence

- The doctrine of collective responsibility: the sins of a few became the sins of the nation
- The exile demanded an explanation — polytheism provided a theologically satisfying cause
- The onomastic evidence contradicts the picture of rampant polytheism in the prophetic texts
- If polytheism was actually limited (as the onomastic data suggests), then the prophetic indictments are MAGNIFYING a minority phenomenon into a national crisis
- The same pattern appears in other ancient Near Eastern texts: royal inscriptions magnify threats and sins to justify actions

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Tigay's argument that the prophets generalized individual sins to the entire nation via the doctrine of collective responsibility is well-supported by the tension between the indictments and the onomastic evidence.

## Stakes

This is the most radical implication of Tigay's work. If the biblical writers magnified polytheism rather than minimized it, the entire consensus narrative — which takes the prophetic indictments at face value as evidence of widespread polytheism — is built on a misreading. The Bible is not a reluctant witness to Israel's polytheism; it is an eager prosecutor exaggerating the crime.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-dever-folk-polytheism-outlasted-monarchy-monotheism-book-religion]] (Dever), consensus critical scholarship

**Alternative reading:** The prophetic indictments are so frequent and so vehement that they MUST reflect a real social phenomenon. "You don't rail against things that aren't happening" (Dever). The onomastic evidence may capture the NAMING practices while the prophets capture the WORSHIP practices — two different dimensions of religious life that don't necessarily contradict.
