# lewis-ocg-2.1: Schematizing intellectual history is necessary but always falsifies

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 2 — History of Scholarship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 2

## The Claim

Quoting C.S. Lewis: "All divisions will falsify our material to some extent; the best one can hope is to choose those which will falsify it least." Lewis also invokes Kuhn's "paradigm shift" model as an alternative to gradualist narratives, acknowledging that both continuity and novelty coexist.

## Evidence

Lewis doesn't argue this — he asserts it as a methodological premise. The entire chapter structure (Enlightenment → German criticism → Wellhausen → Robertson Smith → Gunkel → Biblical Theology → Myth-and-Ritual → Ugarit → Albright/Kaufmann → Cross → Iconography → Revisionists → Archaeology → Social Sciences → Family Religion → Feminism) is his chosen scheme.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH that the claim is true. MEDIUM that Lewis's specific scheme is optimal. He notably leaves out several traditions (Catholic biblical scholarship, Israeli archaeology's particular development, the impact of the Dead Sea Scrolls).

## Stakes

The categories structure what students of the field will see as important. If Lewis's scheme overweights German Protestant scholarship and underweights other traditions, it perpetuates a distorted picture of the field.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Dever covers similar ground in Ch. II of *Did God Have a Wife?* but organizes around archaeology vs. text rather than intellectual movements. Römer barely covers historiography at all. Different scholars would draw different maps.

**Alternative reading:** Any scheme is necessarily reductive — the test is whether it illuminates more than it obscures. Lewis's scheme is conventional and defensible for an English-language audience.

Lewis's scheme is conventional and defensible for an English-language audience. The chapter is stronger on the 19th-20th century than on contemporary developments, which get compressed into the final few pages.
