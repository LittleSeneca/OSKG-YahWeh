# lewis-ocg-4.7: Israelite El Was a Family/Clan Deity, Not a National Deity

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 4 — El Worship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"Israelite El is not portrayed as a national deity whose worship is entwined with the support of a royal hierarchy... El is not a combat deity who slays cosmic creatures... Nor is he a storm deity... His origin is never said to have been from the lands south/southeast of the Dead Sea... El narratives do not exhibit the intolerance that comes to characterize later Yahwism."

## Evidence

- El's functions cluster around family and clan: father (ʾāb), divine kinsman (Ammi-Shadday = "Shadday is my kinsman"), benevolent protector (Jacob-el = "May El protect"), provider of children (Genesis 49:25 — "blessings of breasts and womb"), shepherd (Genesis 48:15, 49:24), rock/refuge (ʾElî-ṣûr, Ṣûrî-ʾēl)
- Sacred space associated with El is small-scale: altars, wells, springs, standing stones, trees — not grand temples
- Dreams and visions are El's mode of revelation (contrast Baal's storm theophany)
- El is associated with the divine council, wisdom, and eternal decrees
- Negative profile: NOT a storm god, NOT associated with the southern wilderness, NOT intolerant, NOT a combat deity who slays cosmic monsters
- All of those features belong to Yahweh's original profile, not El's

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** High on the contrast; medium-high on the full picture — some of the more specific claims (e.g., that El had no connection to war at all) may overstate the contrast, as Lewis himself acknowledges El's protective warrior side.

## Stakes

Whether the El/Yahweh merger was a fusion of equals or an absorption of a clan deity by a national one. Lewis's portrait implies the latter — Yahweh brought the warfare, the nation-building, the intolerance, and the centralized cult; El brought the family devotion, the benevolence, the wisdom, and the fatherhood.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Albertz would agree on the family/national distinction. Cross would emphasize continuity more than Lewis does. Heiser would collapse the distinction entirely (El = Yahweh from the start).

**Alternative reading:** The family/national distinction may be more about the sources that happen to survive than about actual religious practice. El worshippers may have had richer, more "national" forms of devotion that simply aren't preserved.

## Assessment

Lewis's portrait of El as family deity is compelling and aligns with the Albertzian turn toward family religion. The most important insight is the negative one: El was NOT a storm god, NOT associated with the southern wilderness, NOT intolerant. All of those features belong to Yahweh's original profile, not El's. This means Yahweh brought something genuinely new to Israelite religion — not just a new name for the same god.
