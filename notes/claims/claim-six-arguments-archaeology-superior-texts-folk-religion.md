# dever-dghw-3-5.1: Six arguments establish archaeology as equal or superior to biblical texts for reconstructing Israelite religion

**Source:** [[Dever — Chapters III-V — Sources and Archaeological Evidence]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Chapters III-V

## The Claim

Archaeology is not merely supplementary to the biblical texts — it is equal or superior for reconstructing Israelite religion, especially folk religion. Dever presents six specific arguments:

| Argument | Claim |
|----------|-------|
| 1. Quantity | "Archaeological data are already more extensive than all the biblical texts put together, and they will be much more so in future." The Bible is static; archaeology is dynamic. |
| 2. Variety | "Artifacts come not only from the sacred ideas and institutions that the Hebrew Bible focuses on... but from the whole range of 'secular' activities of ordinary people." |
| 3. Chronology | Biblical texts in their present form cover "only about one-third of Israel's history." Archaeological artifacts "are by nature contemporary with all the periods." Carbon 14 dating delivers ±50 years; biblical scholars disagree by centuries. |
| 4. Representation | "The biblical texts, produced by a small circle of literati, are elitist. Yet the archaeological data... are 'populist' by nature." They represent every class, every profession, and especially women. |
| 5. Objectivity | "What we call 'the archaeological record' has not been edited by anyone." Biblical texts were edited from the beginning and reinterpreted for 2,000 years. |
| 6. Secular vs. theocratic | The Bible is "theocratic history." Archaeology produces "a secular history... often more realistic, more comprehensive, better balanced, and ultimately more satisfying." |

## Evidence

- Dever accepts mainstream critical scholarship on the sources: J (9th-8th c., southern), E (8th c., northern), P (late monarchy or post-exilic), Dtr (7th c. core, edited through exile)
- If the revisionists are right that J is post-exilic, "this source would tell us nothing whatsoever about supposed 'ancestral religion'"
- P is "Irrelevant for folk religion during the monarchy"
- Dtr is "A call to repentance... a demand for the wayward people of Israel to restore the pure, monotheistic ideals of its original 'pure' religion"
- Dever's "middle ground" on historicity: "I reject absolutely the assertion of some 'revisionists' that the Bible is not about history at all. On the other hand, the Hebrew Bible is obviously not history in the modern sense... I shall often regard the Hebrew Bible as 'historicized fiction'"
- Key methodological insight: "The prophets... it would not be misleading to say that the real religions of ancient Israel consisted precisely of all the things that the prophets condemned, all of them incidentally illustrated by archaeological discoveries."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Each of the six arguments is well-supported on its own terms. The debate is about whether they collectively justify archaeology's superiority — not about whether the arguments are true.

## Stakes

If Dever's six arguments hold, then text-only scholarship on Israelite religion (like Smith's approach) is methodologically insufficient. The texts can tell us what the elite thought should have happened; archaeology tells us what actually did happen. For folk religion — what ordinary Israelites practiced — archaeology is the primary source, not a supplement.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Text-focused scholars who treat the Bible as the primary source and archaeology as supplementary illumination. Mark S. Smith ([[claim-dever-criticizes-smith-ignoring-archaeological-evidence]]) — Dever explicitly criticizes Smith for not engaging archaeological evidence.

**Alternative reading:** The biblical texts, even if elite and edited, preserve genuine historical memory and theological traditions. Archaeology provides context but cannot replace the texts as the primary source for Israelite religion. The texts tell us what Israelites *believed*; archaeology tells us what they *did* — and belief is the proper object of religious history.

## Assessment

Dever's six arguments are compelling for folk religion. The third argument (chronology) is particularly strong: the texts post-date the events they describe by centuries, while artifacts are contemporary. But "superiority" is domain-specific. For reconstructing practice, Dever is right. For reconstructing belief and theology, texts are indispensable. The best approach integrates both.
