# schmid-ht-2-4.1: "Theology" originates in Greek philosophy as myth, not systematic doctrine

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§2-4 — Concept of Theology from Plato to Gabler]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §2

## The Claim

"The concept of theology originates from philosophical discourse." In Plato, theologia means "legend of the gods" — myths requiring "critical evaluation" for educational purposes. Aristotle lists it among the "theoretical philosophies" but "primarily uses it to denote mythology." Josephus and Philo use it as "almost an alternate term for religion" — not a reflective or synthesizing approach.

## Evidence

- Plato, *Republic* 379a: theologia as myth needing critical assessment
- Aristotle, *Metaphysics* 1026a.18-19, 1064b.1-3: theology as one of three theoretical philosophies alongside mathematics and physics, but still primarily mythology
- Josephus, *Contra Apionem* 1.225: "our theology" = Jewish religion
- Philo, *De Vita Mosis* 2.115: Moses as "theologian"

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** This is philological fact. The Greek sources are unambiguous.

## Stakes

If theology ORIGINALLY meant myth, then the later development into systematic doctrine is a historical contingency, not the term's "natural" meaning. This supports Schmid's argument that "theology" can legitimately mean something other than normative systematics — including his own historical-descriptive approach.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** No serious disagreement about the philology. Some might argue that the term's ORIGIN is less important than its developed meaning, but Schmid is making a genealogical point, not an etymological one.

**Alternative reading:** The term's original meaning is historically interesting but irrelevant to how it should be used today. Schmid's genealogy doesn't prove that descriptive "theology" is legitimate — it just shows the term has changed before.

## Assessment

This is standard lexicography, not controversial. Schmid's use of it is effective: if the term started as "mythology," the claim that "real" theology must be normative/systematic is historically false. The term's meaning has shifted repeatedly.
