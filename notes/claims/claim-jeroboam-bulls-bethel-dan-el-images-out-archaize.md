# lewis-ocg-5.4: Jeroboam I's Bulls at Bethel and Dan Were Likely El Images

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 5 — Iconography of Divinity — El]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 5

## The Claim

"Jeroboam I's cult was devoted to the deity El as he wooed the people away from the Jerusalemite worship of Yahweh to his version of a venerated El worship, especially at Bethel, an old El sanctuary. ... With this strategy Jeroboam I was attempting to 'out-archaize' David, who was so skillful in his use of the Ark of the Covenant, a venerated symbol of divine presence from the days of the tribal league (Cross 1973: 74)."

## Evidence

- Bethel and Dan were old cult sites
- Jeroboam rebuilt Shechem and Penuel (1 Kgs 12:25), both associated with El worship
- The Deuteronomist's polemic equates Jeroboam's bulls with Aaron's golden calf — which is explicitly called ʾĕlōhîm ("gods"/"God")
- The word ʿēgel ("calf/bull") may be a pejorative Deuteronomistic substitution for šôr ("bull")
- Had Jeroboam introduced Baal worship, "tradition should have preserved that fact, in vivid invective"
- Knoppers: "Like the authors of Exodus 32, the Deuteronomist implicitly concedes the antiquity and appeal of Jeroboam's cultus"
- The "out-archaize" strategy: David used the Ark (tribal league symbol); Jeroboam countered with bull images at ancient El sanctuaries

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Lewis himself admits "all interpreters are forced into a good deal of speculation." The Deuteronomist's polemical language is our only source, and it's designed to discredit.

## Stakes

Whether northern Israelite religion was fundamentally El-oriented rather than Yahweh-oriented. If Jeroboam's bulls were El images, northern Yahwism may have been much thinner than the Bible suggests — the northern kingdom may have primarily worshipped El, with Yahweh as a secondary or merged figure.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see the bulls as Yahweh images (the majority view), or as Baal images, or as pedestals for an invisible deity rather than divine images themselves. The Exodus 32 connection could argue for Yahweh — the golden calf is made in response to Moses' delay and is introduced with "These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up from Egypt."

**Alternative reading:** Jeroboam may have been syncretizing — using bull imagery (an El symbol) but directing worship to Yahweh. The "these are your gods" language in Exodus 32 may be polemical exaggeration — Jeroboam may have intended the bulls as pedestals, not as images of the deity himself.

## Assessment

Lewis leans toward the El interpretation but is appropriately tentative. This is one of those questions where the data won't settle the debate. What's valuable is Lewis's contextualization: Jeroboam's actions make more sense as an appeal to archaic northern El traditions than as a Yahwistic innovation. The "out-archaize" framing (from Cross) is elegant.
