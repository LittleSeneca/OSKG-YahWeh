# keel-ggi-10.5: Yahweh's identity was "open at the back" — the Thomas Mann metaphor captures how Yahweh absorbed and overflowed into the identities of other deities

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Ch X — Summary and Conclusion]] — Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Chapter X

## The Claim

Quoting Thomas Mann's Joseph and His Brothers: "the old man's ego was not quite clearly demarcated, that it opened at the back, as it were, and overflowed into spheres external to his own individuality both in space and time" — and adding: "we would add here, is the deity." "Anyone who examines the pictorial world of Palestine and Israel from the Middle Bronze Age through the Iron Age will have no trouble seeing how deep the roots go, and even the little roots as well, that describe Yahweh's power, his 'colorfulness,' and the rich variety of the ways he shows himself."

## Evidence

- The entire diachronic survey of the book serves as evidence for this claim
- Yahweh absorbed: the bull of El, the solar disk of Re, the winged protectors of Baalshamem, the blessing tree of Asherah, the cherub throne of the Phoenician king
- Each absorption added to Yahweh's "colorfulness" without requiring a new name or a new image

## Confidence

**Rating:** debatable

**Rationale:** This is a theological-metaphorical claim, not an empirical one. But as a SYNTHESIS of the empirical evidence, it is beautiful and apt.

## Stakes

This is the book's deepest theological intuition. Yahweh's power is not in his uniqueness but in his ABSORPTIVE capacity. The "open ego" metaphor captures what Smith calls convergence and what the biblical texts themselves perform: Yahweh becomes whatever the other gods were, and in becoming them, renders them obsolete.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Theological exclusivists who insist on Yahweh's radical otherness from Canaanite religion. Also scholars who emphasize conflict and polemic (the "Yahweh alone" movement as rejection) rather than absorption.

**Alternative reading:** The "absorption" model might be too irenic. The biblical texts do not just absorb — they MOCK, POLEMICIZE, and SUPPRESS. The "open ego" metaphor captures the absorptive side but not the violent side of Yahwistic differentiation.

## Assessment

The Thomas Mann quote is a genuinely inspired choice. It captures the paradox: Yahweh is both utterly distinctive (the "jealous" God who tolerates no rivals) and completely composite (made of pieces borrowed from every neighboring deity). The "open ego" resolves this paradox: Yahweh's distinctiveness IS his absorptiveness. No other god in the Levantine pantheon absorbed so much from so many while maintaining a single name and identity.
