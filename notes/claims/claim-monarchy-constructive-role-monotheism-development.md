# smith-ehg-intro.3: The monarchy played a constructive role in monotheism's development, not just a corrupting one.

**Source:** [[Smith Chapter 0 — Introduction]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Early History of God* (2nd ed., 2002), Introduction

## The Claim

"The monarchy fostered the inclusion of various deities, or their features, into the cult of Yahweh... In the first half of its existence, the monarchy fostered some features of convergence in exalting Yahweh as the national god." Kings like Hezekiah and Josiah drove the centralization that made monotheism possible. The monarchy gets blamed for the polytheism but deserves partial credit for the monotheism.

## Evidence

- Mesopotamian and Egyptian parallels: Marduk absorbed other gods' traits as Babylon rose; Amun-Re did the same in Egypt.
- Royal psalms (Ps 2, 18, 89) use Baal's storm-warrior language to exalt Yahweh as the dynastic patron.
- Hezekiah's and Josiah's reforms centralized worship in Jerusalem, creating the conditions for monotheism.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The ANE parallels are solid. The claim that the monarchy "fostered convergence" is plausible but hard to prove — we don't have pre-monarchic texts to compare against. Smith acknowledges this: pre-8th century evidence is "barely enough."

## Stakes

If the monarchy drove monotheism's development, then monotheism is a POLITICAL achievement, not a revelation. The God of Israel became "one" because kings needed a unified national cult, not because God revealed his oneness to prophets. This doesn't disprove revelation — God could work through politics — but it removes the "supernatural intervention" necessity from the historical explanation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservatives would argue the prophets drove monotheism AGAINST the monarchy (Elijah vs. Ahab is the paradigm). Smith's counter is that the monarchy's relationship to religion was complex, not uniformly hostile.

**Alternative reading:** The monarchy was consistently a force for religious corruption. Elijah, Hosea, and Jeremiah all condemned the monarchy's religious policies. The Deuteronomistic History blames the monarchy for Israel's downfall. The prophets, not the kings, drove monotheism.

## Assessment

Smith is right to complicate the "monarchy = villain" narrative. But his evidence for the monarchy's CONSTRUCTIVE role is thinner than his evidence for its role in promoting Baal. The ANE parallels (Marduk, Amun-Re) show that state religion CAN absorb other deities — they don't show that it DID in Israel. Track whether Smith demonstrates this or just asserts it by analogy.
