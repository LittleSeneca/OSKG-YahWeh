# schmid-ht-10-12-13.2: Canonical differences do NOT serve denominational theological interests — they're accidents of philological history

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§10, 12, 13 — Canon Pluriformity and Relativity]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §10

## The Claim

"It is theologically noteworthy that the different Old Testaments of the various denominations do not serve the special theological interests or accentuations of the various churches. The books of Sirach and Tobit contribute just as little to the support of Roman Catholic doctrine as does the book of Jubilees to the Ethiopian church. The divergent canonical boundaries are instead based in different philological and historical developments and decisions."

## Evidence

- Roman Catholic canon includes Sirach, Tobit, Wisdom, Maccabees — none of which directly support distinctive Catholic doctrines
- Ethiopian canon includes Enoch and Jubilees — not distinctively Ethiopian theological concerns
- The Council of Trent (1545) was the "one and only decree regarding the canon" — a Counter-Reformation political decision, not a theological one
- Protestant churches "stipulated that only books of the Hebrew Bible should remain in the canon" — a humanistic/philological decision, not a theological one

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Schmid's observation that canonical differences don't map onto theological distinctives is counterintuitive but persuasive.

## Stakes

This undercuts any argument that "my canon is theologically superior." The differences are accidental, not driven by theological content. It's also a subtle argument FOR Schmid's own project: if canon boundaries are historically contingent, then a historical theology of the HB shouldn't be bound by any particular canon's boundaries.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Denominational theologians who would argue that their canon IS theologically motivated. Catholic theologians might argue the deuterocanonical books DO support distinctively Catholic doctrines (purgatory in 2 Maccabees, almsgiving in Tobit).

**Alternative reading:** The absence of strong theological distinctives may reflect the shared patristic heritage of the main canons. The differences are small because the traditions share deep roots. The Ethiopian canon's inclusion of Enoch and Jubilees DOES reflect a distinctive theological orientation toward apocalyptic literature and angelology.
