# romer-inv-9.4: Josiah's reform removed the asherah and the Deuteronomists rewrote her story

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 9 — Yhwh and His Asherah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 9: Yhwh and His Asherah

## The Claim

Josiah's reform (late 7th century) removed the asherah from the Jerusalem temple (2 Kings 23:6-7). The Deuteronomists then associated her with Baal — the foreign enemy god — to delegitimize her. By the time the canon was finalized, the memory of Yhwh's consort had been largely erased. But the inscriptions prove she was there.

Römer directly contradicts Smith here: where Smith argues the asherah was a cult symbol that had lost its goddess connection, Römer argues the goddess was real and actively worshipped, and her removal required active erasure.

## Evidence

- **2 Kings 23:6-7:** Josiah brings out the asherah from the temple, burns it, and grinds it to dust. The "houses of the qedeshot" (consecrated persons) where women wove garments for Asherah were destroyed.
- **The reform's logic:** Centralization of worship in Jerusalem meant eliminating all competing cult sites and symbols. The asherah — paired with Yhwh for centuries — was the most embedded competitor.
- **Deuteronomistic rewriting:** The association of Asherah with Baal (see [[claim-bible-deliberately-obscures-yhwh-asherah-link]]) was part of this erasure. By linking her to the enemy god, the Dtr editors delegitimized her without having to explain that she had once been Yhwh's partner.
- **The inscriptions survive as uncensored evidence:** Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom were not preserved in Jerusalem's archives — they survived because they were on sherds at desert way-stations. The biblical editors couldn't erase what they didn't control.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** High on Josiah removing the asherah (the biblical account is plausible and consistent with the reform program). Medium-high on the Deuteronomistic rewriting strategy (the Baal association is suspiciously convenient).

## Stakes

Yhwh had a wife. The goddess was worshipped alongside the god in the Jerusalem temple. She was removed by reformers, not because she was foreign, but because she was too deeply embedded in Israelite religion. The God of the Bible was once part of a divine couple. This is the single most explosive claim in Römer's book.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Smith (see [[claim-asherah-was-yahwistic-symbol]]) would argue the asherah Josiah removed was a cult symbol, not a goddess, and its removal was part of aniconic reform — destroying cult objects that had become objects of worship in themselves, not suppressing a consort goddess.

**Alternative reading:** The asherah removal was part of Josiah's centralization and aniconic reform. It wasn't about a consort goddess — it was about eliminating local Yahwistic cult sites and their traditional paraphernalia. The Dtr editors inflated the significance for polemical effect.

## Assessment

The biblical account of Josiah removing the asherah from the temple is one of the more historically plausible narratives in Kings — it describes specific objects, specific locations, and specific actions that align with the reform program. The question is whether the asherah was a goddess's symbol or "just" a Yahwistic cult object. Römer's case that it was the former — and that the Deuteronomists actively rewrote the memory — is compelling. The fact that the inscriptions survive as uncensored evidence, outside the control of Jerusalem's scribes, is a powerful point: we have the before picture (Kuntillet Ajrud) and the after picture (the Bible), and they don't match.
