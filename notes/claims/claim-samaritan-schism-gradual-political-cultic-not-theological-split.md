# albertz-hir-5.7: The Samaritan schism was gradual political/cultic split, not an early theological break over syncretism

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol2 — Chapter 5 — Post-Exilic Period]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume II* (1994), Chapter 5

## The Claim

"The formation of the Samaritan community alongside the Jewish community was not the consequence of a theologically motivated split, but much more a political and cultic schism." The standard Deuteronomistic polemic in 2 Kings 17.24-41 does NOT refer to the Samaritans.

## Evidence

- 2 Kings 17.24-34a describes foreign settlers (not Israelites) worshipping their national gods alongside Yahweh at Bethel — it's a still-exilic warning to Judahites not to participate in the Bethel cult
- 2 Kings 17.34b-40 is a later addition that addresses Israelites who remained in the land, calling them to abandon syncretism and come to Jerusalem — this reflects early post-exilic outreach to northern Israelites
- The exclusion of "the people of Samaria" from temple rebuilding (Ezra 4.1-5), if historical at all, was political, not theological — the "enemies of Judah and Benjamin" were the foreign upper class
- The actual split developed gradually through: (a) political rivalry between Samaria (provincial capital) and Jerusalem (temple city), (b) competition between Sanballat's dynasty and the Jerusalem priesthood, (c) Nehemiah's expulsion of a high priest's son who married into Sanballat's family (Neh 13.28 — Josephus dates the Gerizim temple founding to this event)
- The real break came with the building of the Samaritan temple on Mount Gerizim (late 4th century under Alexander or early Hellenistic period) and the Samaritan adoption of their own Torah recension

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The reconstruction of 2 Kings 17's compositional history is detailed but contested. The dating of the Gerizim temple is disputed. But the overall model of political/cultic rather than theological schism is increasingly accepted.

## Stakes

If Albertz is right, the Jewish-Samaritan split wasn't about "pure vs. syncretistic Yahwism" — it was about political rivalry and cultic centralization. This matters for understanding Samaritan identity: Samaritans aren't "half-pagan" but represent a parallel development of Yahwism that didn't go through the Jerusalem-centered reshaping of the exile.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional readings that see 2 Kings 17 and Ezra 4 as describing theological exclusion of syncretistic Samarians. Scholars who date the Gerizim temple differently. Those who see deeper theological differences from the start.

**Cross-reference:** The Elephantine papyri (AP 30/31) show the Jewish community writing to BOTH Jerusalem and Samaria for support in rebuilding their temple — suggesting the split wasn't complete even in the late 5th century.

## Assessment

Albertz's careful literary analysis of 2 Kings 17 is convincing — the text is layered and was written before "Samaritans" as a distinct group existed. The model of gradual political estrangement is more historically plausible than sudden theological rupture. However, I'd want more evidence on the dating of the Gerizim temple. Josephus's account is late and tendentious. Archaeological evidence from Gerizim suggests a temple existed by the Hellenistic period, which is consistent with Albertz's model but doesn't confirm all the details.
