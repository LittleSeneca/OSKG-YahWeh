# heiser-ur-39.1: The new creation dissolves the divine-human realm distinction

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 8 — The Kingdom Not Yet]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 39

## The Claim

The new heavens and new earth (Revelation 21-22) represent the final fusion of the divine and human realms. No more temple — "its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" — because the DISTINCTION between realms is dissolved. What was separated at Eden is reunited. The cosmic story that began with the separation of divine and human space ends with their reunion.

## Evidence

- Revelation 21:22: "I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" — the temple, which represented the boundary between divine and human space, is no longer needed because the boundary is gone
- Revelation 21:3: "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man" — God and humanity share the same space permanently
- Revelation 22:3-5: "The throne of God and of the Lamb will be in it, and his servants will worship him. They will see his face" — direct, unmediated access to God's presence

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The Revelation text clearly describes the elimination of the temple and the descent of the heavenly Jerusalem. Heiser's specific interpretation as "realm dissolution" — the reversal of the Edenic separation — is one interpretive option among others.

## Stakes

If Heiser is right, the end of the biblical story is not "heaven" as a separate realm where believers go, but the fusion of heaven and earth into a single, undivided reality. This reframes the Christian hope from "going to heaven when you die" to "heaven coming to earth" — which is, in fact, what Revelation 21 actually describes.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Dispensationalists and others who maintain a strict heaven/earth distinction in the eternal state. Also, scholars who read Revelation 21-22 as symbolic rather than as a description of actual cosmic restructuring.

**Alternative reading:** The absence of a temple in the New Jerusalem represents the perfection of worship, not the dissolution of the divine-human realm distinction. The "dwelling place of God is with man" describes intimate relationship, not ontological fusion.

## Assessment

The new creation as realm-fusion is the logical endpoint of Heiser's entire framework. If holiness is realm separation (Part 5), and the Babel distribution was the administrative implementation of that separation (Part 4), and Christ's work defeated the corrupt administrators (Part 7), then the new creation must be the dissolution of the separation itself. Heiser's framework is a single, coherent arc from Genesis 1 to Revelation 22.
