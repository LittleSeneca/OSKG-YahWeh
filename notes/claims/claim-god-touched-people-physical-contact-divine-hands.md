# stav-god-12-15.3: God touched people — his hands made physical contact with Moses, Jeremiah, and others — and divine touch was an instrument of both intimacy and commissioning.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part IV — Arms and Hands]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 14

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that God's hands were not just instruments of power — they were instruments of intimacy. He touched people. His hand covered Moses in the cleft of the rock (Exodus 33:22). His finger wrote the tablets (Exodus 31:18; Deuteronomy 9:10). Yahweh touched Jeremiah's mouth to commission him as a prophet (Jeremiah 1:9). This is not metaphorical touch — it is physical contact between divine and human bodies.

## Evidence

- Exodus 33:22: God's hand covers Moses in the cleft of the rock — physical shielding, not metaphorical protection
- Exodus 31:18 / Deuteronomy 9:10: "the tablets of stone, written with the finger of God" — a specific digit performing a specific action
- Jeremiah 1:9: "Yahweh put out his hand and touched my mouth" — physical contact as prophetic commissioning
- Isaiah 6:6-7: The seraph touches Isaiah's mouth with a coal — while this is an intermediary, the logic is that divine touch purifies
- Daniel 10:10, 10:18: A divine hand touches Daniel and sets him upright — physical repositioning
- The hand that crushes enemies (Exodus 15:6) is the same hand that covers and protects (Exodus 33:22) — versatility implies a real hand

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The texts describe divine touch in explicit physical terms — a hand covers, a finger writes, a hand touches the mouth.

## Stakes

If God's touch is physical — skin on skin — then the boundary between divine and human bodies is porous. God can reach across it. The hand that commissioned Jeremiah is the same kind of thing as the hand that crushed Pharaoh: a real divine body part making real physical contact.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those who read divine touch as visionary experience or metaphorical commissioning

**Alternative reading:** The hand-touch is a prophetic vision formula — not physical contact but a vision of divine authorization using embodied imagery.

## Assessment

The touch texts are hard to allegorize. Jeremiah 1:9 does not say "Yahweh commissioned me through an interior spiritual experience." It says Yahweh reached out his hand and touched his mouth. The finger that wrote the tablets is irreducibly physical — a finger is a specific body part performing a specific manual action. Stavrakopoulou is right: these texts describe a God who makes physical contact with humans.
