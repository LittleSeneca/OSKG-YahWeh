# fleming-ybi-summary.2: Exodus 3 and 6 are theological bridge-work, not historical memory

**Source:** [[Fleming — Book Summary]] — Daniel E. Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021)

## The Claim

Fleming argues that Exodus 3 (the burning bush theophany) and Exodus 6 (the Priestly revelation of the divine name) do not preserve historical memory of a moment when Yahweh was first introduced to Israel by Moses. These are schematic texts that serve a literary-theological function: bridging the patriarchal narratives of Genesis (where the deity is El Shaddai) with the Exodus narrative (where the deity is Yahweh). They are redactional bridge-work, not ancient tradition, and therefore cannot serve as evidence for the Midianite Hypothesis's claim that Moses mediated Yahweh from Midian to Israel.

## Evidence

- Exodus 3 and 6 both explicitly address the name transition from patriarchal religion (El Shaddai) to Mosaic religion (Yahweh) — a classic redactional concern
- Exodus 18 does not show Jethro as priest of Yahweh introducing the god to Moses — Jethro responds to demonstrated power (Ex 18:11: "Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"), he does not introduce Yahweh
- The connecting function between Genesis and Exodus is transparent: the patriarchal deity needs to be identified with Moses' deity for the narrative to cohere

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The literary-redactional reading of Exodus 3 and 6 as bridge texts is broadly accepted in critical scholarship. Even Römer, Fleming's primary opponent, acknowledges the redactional character of these passages.

## Stakes

If Exodus 3 and 6 are not historical memory, the biblical pillar of the Midianite Hypothesis collapses. The hypothesis requires a historical Moses introducing a foreign god to Israel — but the texts that narrate this moment are late theological constructs, not early traditions. The Hypothesis has no biblical foundation independent of the late Priestly and Deuteronomistic redaction of the Pentateuch.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Defenders of the Midianite Hypothesis who treat Exodus 3 and 6 as preserving genuine historical memory. Thomas Römer treats the Moses-Midian connection as the strongest surviving element of the hypothesis.

**Alternative reading:** The Moses-Midian connection in Exodus preserves authentic memory of a historical mediator who introduced Yahweh to Israel from Midianite territory (the Midianite Hypothesis). Fleming's response: the texts are schematic, not historical.
