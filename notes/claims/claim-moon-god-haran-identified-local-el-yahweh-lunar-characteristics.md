# keel-ggi-6.2: The Moon God of Haran Was Identified with El — Yahweh Absorbed Lunar Characteristics

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part VI — Iron Age IIC — Astralization and Orthodox Reaction]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part VI

## The Claim

"From this evidence, one can draw the conclusion that in Palestine, during Iron Age IIC, the Moon God of Haran (who was also venerated by the Assyrians as the highest god in the western provinces) and the local El were identified with each other, at least in certain circles of the population, as two manifestations of the same 'Most High God.'" The crescent moon standard — the cultic emblem of Sin, the Moon God of Haran — appears on local Palestinian seals alongside the enthroned El figure. The two were identified. Yahweh, already identified with El, absorbed lunar characteristics through this channel.

## Evidence

- The crescent moon standard appears on Aramean and locally produced seals throughout Palestine (illuss. 291, 295-301)
- A local seal group shows an enthroned, bearded deity (El type) with lunar features — "in the act of blessing, as an anthropomorphic moon god" (illuss. 303-307)
- The Beth-Shean cylinder seal (illus. 308): an "iconographic anthology" showing the enthroned god (El) with "his asherah" (stylized tree) in the lower register — independent confirmation of the Iron IIB reading
- "Anthropomorphic representations and cultic symbols appear to be interchangeable entities, used according to regional preference"
- Two lunar-name seals with El/moon god imagery from Judah: the owner could seal documents with the "orthodox" name side while wearing the deity image as an amulet on the reverse
- The stylized tree (asherah) appears next to the crescent moon standard on clay amulets from Tell Keisan (illuss. 309a-b) and on a conoid from the Amuq region — "emblem and a stylized tree also decorate two sides of a squared stone"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The identification of the enthroned figure as El is solid. The lunar characteristics are clear. The step to "this is Yahweh" is inferential but well-supported by the Yahwistic names on these seals and the broader identification of Yahweh with El.

## Stakes

If Yahweh absorbed lunar characteristics during Iron IIC, this adds a crucial piece to Smith's convergence thesis: Yahweh absorbed not just solar traits (Iron IIB) but also lunar traits (Iron IIC) from El through the Moon God of Haran identification. The "Most High God" (El Elyon) becomes increasingly celestial and abstract — a god of the heavens broadly, not just the sun. This is the theological pathway to the universal God of Second Isaiah.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see Yahweh's lunar traits as original (the "Shasu-Yahweh" originating from a moon-worshiping region). Also those who minimize El-Yahweh identification.

**Alternative reading:** The lunar motifs on Judean seals could be passive cultural borrowing without theological significance — people bought seals with moon motifs because they were fashionable Assyrian/Aramean designs.

## Assessment

The dual-sided seals where the owner separates the image from the name strongly suggest conscious theological management, not passive borrowing. The owner knew the lunar image was religiously significant and chose to keep it — but on the reverse, not alongside his name. This is exactly the kind of religious negotiation you would expect in a culture under imperial pressure.
