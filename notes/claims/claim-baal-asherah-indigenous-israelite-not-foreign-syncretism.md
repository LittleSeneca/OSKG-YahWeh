# smith-ehg-intro.2: Baal and Asherah worship was indigenous Israelite religion, not foreign syncretism.

**Source:** [[Smith Chapter 0 — Introduction]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Early History of God* (2nd ed., 2002), Introduction

## The Claim

This follows directly from Claim 1. If Israel IS Canaanite, then Baal and Asherah weren't foreign imports corrupting pure Yahwism. They were part of Israel's own religious heritage. "Baal and Asherah were part of Israel's 'Canaanite' heritage, and the process of the emergence of Israelite monolatry was an issue of **Israel's breaking with its own 'Canaanite' past.**"

## Evidence

This is the argument the rest of the book builds. The Introduction just states it programmatically. The evidence comes in Chapters 1-4.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH that Baal and Asherah were worshipped in Israel (biblical and archaeological evidence is strong). MEDIUM that this represents "indigenous heritage" rather than "syncretism" — the distinction may be semantic. If a practice is 200 years old, is it "indigenous" or "syncretistic"? Depends on your timeframe.

## Stakes

Enormous. If Smith is right, the biblical prophets weren't calling Israel back to original purity — they were calling Israel to ABANDON its own heritage for something new. "Yahweh-alone" wasn't conservation; it was revolution. The Bible's own story (pure origins, later corruption) is exactly backward.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservatives argue the prophets preserved an original monotheism that had been corrupted. Tigay's onomastics suggests popular religion was more Yahwistic than Smith allows. Kaufmann argued monotheism was the original revolution, not a late development.

**Alternative reading:** Baal and Asherah worship did exist but WAS foreign syncretism. Israel originally had a distinctive faith that was later corrupted by Canaanite neighbors. The prophets were calling Israel back to its own heritage, not away from it.

## Assessment

This is the central claim of Smith's entire project, and it depends entirely on Claim 1. If Israelite culture = Canaanite culture, this follows naturally. If the cultures were more distinct than Smith allows, the "indigenous vs. syncretistic" framing shifts. The evidence for Baal/Asherah WORSHIP (not just cultural borrowing) is strong but not uniform — track when Smith has direct evidence of cultic devotion vs. when he infers it from literary parallels.
