# heiser-ur-35.1: Paul's "principalities and powers" are the corrupt *elohim* of Deut 32

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 7 — The Kingdom Already]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 35

## The Claim

Paul saw Christ's work as defeating the cosmic powers — the corrupt *elohim* of the nations. Paul's "principalities and powers" (*archai*, *exousiai*, *thronoi*, *kyriotētes*) are not abstract political forces or demonic hierarchies invented by later theology. They are the *elohim* — the corrupt divine beings who received the nations at Babel. Christ's death and resurrection defeated them. The church's mission is to announce this victory to the nations they once ruled.

## Evidence

- Colossians 1:16: "by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities" — Christ created the *elohim*; they answer to him
- Colossians 2:15: Christ "disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, by triumphing over them in him"
- Ephesians 1:20-22: God seated Christ "far above all rule and authority and power and dominion... and put all things under his feet"
- Ephesians 3:10: "so that through the church the manifold wisdom of God might now be made known to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places"
- 1 Corinthians 10:20: Paul cites Deut 32:17 — "what pagans sacrifice they offer to demons and not to God" — explicitly connecting Gentile worship to the Deut 32 *elohim*

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** HIGH on Paul's cosmic language being about spiritual beings. MEDIUM on Heiser's specific identification with the Deut 32 *elohim* (Paul never explicitly cites Deut 32:8-9, though he does cite Deut 32:17 in 1 Cor 10:20).

## Stakes

If Heiser is right, Paul's soteriology is cosmic rather than merely personal: Christ's work defeats the spiritual powers that govern the nations. Salvation is not just forgiveness of sins but liberation from the authority of corrupt divine beings. This connects Paul's theology directly to the Deut 32 worldview.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who interpret Paul's "principalities and powers" as human political structures, abstract spiritual forces, or a later development in Pauline theology rather than the specific *elohim* of Deut 32. The "Christus Victor" model of atonement is itself contested.

**Alternative reading:** Paul's "principalities and powers" refer to structural evil — political, social, and economic systems that oppose God — not to individual spiritual beings with administrative authority over nations.

## Assessment

The cosmic reading of Paul: Paul's "principalities and powers" are not abstract political forces. They are the *elohim* — the corrupt divine beings who received the nations at Babel. Christ's death and resurrection defeated them. The church's mission is to announce this victory to the nations they once ruled. The 1 Cor 10:20 citation of Deut 32:17 is the strongest evidence for this reading; the rest depends on accepting the framework.
