# albertz-hir-2.2.2: Yahweh was a southern Palestinian mountain/storm god worshipped by Midianites/Kenites before Moses — Yahweh is older than Israel

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §2.2 — The Exodus Group and Yahweh's Origins]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §2.2

## The Claim

"The god Yahweh is older than Israel; he was a southern Palestinian mountain god before he became the god of liberation for the Moses group." Moses encountered Yahweh through his Midianite father-in-law Jethro, a priest. Yahweh's original character was a storm god of the Hadad/Baal type, whose "dynamic properties... are twisted round into the political and historical sphere."

## Evidence

- **Egyptian lists** from Amenophis III (14th c.) and Ramses II (13th c.) attest "Y-h-w3" as a geographical/ethnic designation in "Š3św land" — the Shasu (nomads) of Yhw, in the region of southern Palestine/Edom
- **Old poetic texts** locate Yahweh's origin in the south: "Yahweh came from Sinai, dawned from Seir, shone forth from Mount Paran" (Deut 33:2; Judg 5:4-5; Ps 68:8-9; Hab 3:3). Yahweh is called "the one from Sinai" (Judg 5:5)
- **The Moses-Midian connection**: Moses fled to Midian, married the daughter of a Midianite priest (Ex 2:15ff.; 3:1; 18:1), and Jethro "invites the Israelites to a sacrificial meal for Yahweh on the mountain of God" (Ex 18:12). "This account cannot have been invented" because later relations with Midianites were hostile
- **Kenites** were regarded as notable Yahweh-worshippers later (Gen 4:15; Judg 4:17ff.) and settled within Judah (Judg 1:16)
- **Elijah's pilgrimage** to Horeb (I Kings 19), "forty days' journey south of Beersheba," suggests Yahweh was connected with a mountain in that southern desert region
- The mountain of God tradition was deliberately obscured later — Deuteronomists relocated Sinai to the remote peninsula and used "Horeb" as a cryptic cipher ("desolate land") to avoid Edomite associations after 587

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the general southern origin (Egyptian Shasu-of-Yhw lists + old poetry + Moses-Midian tradition form a strong triangulation). MEDIUM on the storm-god/Hadad typology (the evidence is epiphanic imagery which could be generic theophanic language rather than distinctive storm-god identity).

## Stakes

This is the Kenite/Midianite hypothesis — Yahweh was NOT originally an Israelite god. He was borrowed/imported from southern nomadic tribes. If true, Israelite religion began with adoption of an alien deity, not with the revelation of a hitherto-unknown god. This directly contradicts any "early monotheism" model (Kaufmann) and supports the evolutionary consensus.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Fleming (2021) argues Yahweh was NOT taken from outsiders but the name reflects Israel's own diverse origins. Others argue the Shasu-Yhw connection is a coincidence of similar-sounding names. Evangelical scholars would reject the Midianite origin.

**Alternative reading:** The Shasu-of-Yhw could be an early Israelite group already worshipping Yahweh, not a pre-Israelite group. The direction of borrowing could be Israel → Midian rather than Midian → Israel. Or the Moses-Midian connection could be a literary device explaining later Kenite integration into Judah.

## Assessment

This is one of the strongest sections in the book. The triangulation of Egyptian inscriptions, old poetry, and the Moses-Midian tradition (with its criterion of embarrassment — Israel wouldn't invent a Midianite origin for its god) is compelling. Albertz's explanation of WHY an alien god could become a liberation symbol (not yet incorporated into any state pantheon, worshipped by freedom-loving nomads) is elegant. The deliberate obscuring of Sinai's Edomite location by later tradition (Horeb as "cipher") is a clever observation.
