# keel-ggi-5.6: Solar Imagery Is Dominant in Iron Age IIB and Absorbed into Yahwism

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part V — Iron Age IIB — Baal El Yahweh and His Asherah]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part V

## The Claim

"The strong influence that came from Egypt must not be underestimated, producing a pronounced tendency toward the celestialization and solarization of divine powers." The dominant religious iconography of Iron Age IIB in Israel/Judah is not Baal, not Asherah, but *Egyptian solar motifs* — and these were integrated into official Yahwism through royal ideology. This directly supports Smith's argument in *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* that solar imagery was absorbed into Yahwism during the monarchy.

## Evidence

- The sun god as a child in a lotus blossom "is seen most frequently" on the Samaria ivories — the most common anthropomorphic deity depiction
- The motif appears on Hebrew name seals: a seal belonging to a certain *'yw bn ywqm*, another belonging to *'byw 'bd 'zyw* — possibly an official of the Judean king Azariah/Uzziah (ca. 773-735). The owner's title *'bd* ("servant, minister") confirms high status
- On these seals, the sun child holds one hand to his chest and raises the other "in the gesture of greeting or blessing" — sometimes with cow horns and solar disk (Hathor/Isis attributes adapted for solar theology)
- Winged protective powers (falcons, uraei, sphinxes/cherubs) are the most characteristic motifs of the period, combining "unapproachable distance and effective protection" — the celestial aspect of the divine
- Falcon-headed sphinxes with solar disks, winged uraei flanking royal cartouches, and the falcon with spread wings (royal symbolism derived from Egyptian New Kingdom ideology)
- Gender "plays no special role and opposition between male and female deities is not an issue" — solarization absorbs both male and female divine figures into a unified solar sphere
- Osiris-Re union iconography (Djed pillar flanked by Isis and Nephthys with solar disks) — the integration of solar and chthonic divine aspects

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The evidence is overwhelming and based on the largest catalogue of Iron Age seals ever assembled. The solar motifs are quantitatively dominant, not just occasional.

## Stakes

This directly supports Smith's argument that solar imagery was absorbed into Yahwism during the monarchy. It also connects to Cross's Divine Warrior as a solar figure and to the "Yahweh as sun god" traditions detectable in the Psalms (e.g., Psalm 84:11: "Yahweh is a sun and shield"). The solarization of Yahweh was NOT a late or fringe phenomenon — it was the *mainstream* religious development of Iron Age IIB.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who minimize Egyptian influence on Israelite religion. Also those who see Baal as the primary influence on Yahwistic imagery. Keel & Uehlinger's data shows Baal imagery exists (the "four-winged Baal") but is quantitatively secondary to solar imagery.

**Alternative reading:** The solar motifs on seals may reflect fashion rather than theology — people bought seals with Egyptian-looking designs because they were prestigious, not because they were solar worshipers. The name seals with solar motifs could simply be using conventional decorative patterns.

## Assessment

The prestige argument works for anepigraphic seals but fails for name seals. A Judean official who commissioned a seal with his Yahwistic name (*'byw* = "Yahweh is my father") chose to pair that name with solar imagery. That is a deliberate religious statement, not just fashion. The solarization of Yahweh in Iron Age IIB is one of the most important and underappreciated findings in the study of Israelite religion. Smith intuited it from texts; Keel & Uehlinger prove it from artifacts.
