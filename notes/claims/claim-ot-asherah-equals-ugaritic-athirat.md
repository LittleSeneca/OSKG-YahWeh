# day-ygc-2.2: OT Asherah = Ugaritic Athirat (El's consort) — the equation is secure

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 2 — Yahweh and Asherah]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 2: Yahweh and Asherah

## The Claim

The equation of Asherah with Ugaritic Athirat is "the natural assumption" — phonetic equivalence is clear, and "it would be very odd if these equivalent forms did not denote the same deity" (p. 48).

## Evidence

1. **Phonetic equivalence:** Ugaritic *ʼaṯrt* → Hebrew *ʼăšērâ* via intermediate form *ʼšrt* (attested at Ekron, and the base for "his Asherah" at Kuntillet Ajrud/Khirbet el-Qom) (p. 48)
2. **Structural expectation:** Every other major Ugaritic deity appears in the OT. It would be bizarre for Athirat to be absent and for a different unknown goddess to appear instead
3. **Theological logic:** Yahweh was equated with El. Athirat was El's wife. It's "entirely expected for Yahweh to have appropriated El's wife" (p. 48)
4. **Mother of the gods:** Athirat was mother of the gods at Ugarit. Asherah is mentioned four times alongside "the host of heaven" (2 Kgs 17:16; 21:3; 23:4, 6-7) — and the host of heaven corresponds to the sons of God/El (cf. Job 38:7; KTU² 1.10.I.3-4)
5. **Shared tree symbolism:** Both Athirat and Asherah were symbolized by a stylized tree (see [[claim-asherah-was-stylized-tree]])

### Replies to Bernhardt's Critique (the most detailed challenge to the equation)

- **Objection 1:** Athirat is mother of gods at Ugarit, but Asherah is a fertility goddess in the OT. Day: Athirat is called Qudshu at Ugarit, and Egyptian Qudshu depictions (contemporary with Ugaritic texts) show her as an erotic fertility goddess (p. 49, citing ANEP plates 470-474)
- **Objection 2:** Athirat is El's consort, but Asherah is paired with Baal in the OT. Day: This is a Deuteronomistic polemical move — guilt by association — not an authentic tradition (p. 49)
- **Objection 3:** Athirat is "Lady of the Sea" but Asherah has no sea connection. Day: "weak argument from silence" (p. 49)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Day's structural argument (every other major Ugaritic deity appears in the OT) is particularly elegant. Bernhardt's objections are each individually weak and collectively insufficient.

## Stakes

If Athirat = Asherah, we have a continuous goddess tradition from Late Bronze Age Ugarit through monarchic Israel into the Persian period. The goddess didn't appear from nowhere in Israel — she was inherited Canaanite religious furniture. This changes the entire frame: the "idolatry" the prophets condemned wasn't foreign importation, it was native tradition.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** A. Caquot ("Athirat and Asherah have nothing in common but their names"), T. Binger ("insufficient evidence"), K.-H. Bernhardt (three detailed objections). All are minority positions.

**Alternative reading:** None that's credible. Day's structural argument is close to decisive.

## Assessment

This is one of the most secure equations in the entire field. Day dispatches the critics efficiently. The Ekron inscriptions (ʼšrt) provide the missing link in the phonetic chain between Ugaritic *ʼaṯrt* and Hebrew *ʼăšērâ*. The mother-of-gods / host-of-heaven connection is the clincher. If you grant that Yahweh absorbed El's identity, the transfer of El's consort is the expected outcome, not a surprising discovery.
