# smith-obm-intro.6: The divine family provided polytheism with its own form of "oneness"

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Introduction]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Introduction

## The Claim

"The notion of the family household perhaps provided polytheism with the sort of 'oneness' that monotheists associate with monotheism. Indeed, in a society where the highest level of social association and identification was the family and not the individual, the polytheism of a divine family would have been far more intelligible than any notion of monotheism. Stated differently, the divine family was for polytheism a sort of 'mono-theism.'"

## Evidence

(Developed in Part I, previewed in the Introduction):

- J.D. Schloen's work on Ugaritic society: four tiers of the pantheon correspond to four levels of the family household.
- Familial terminology is widespread for the pantheon (father, son, brother, wife).
- The patrimonial household provided conceptual unity — just as a human family is "one" household, so the divine family is "one" pantheon.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** This is an intriguing hypothesis but the evidence is previewed, not demonstrated — the Introduction only sketches the argument. The claim needs testing against the data in chapters 1-4.

## Stakes

If Smith is right, polytheism wasn't chaotic multiplicity but an ordered system with its own coherence. This radically reframes the monotheism-vs-polytheism contrast. It also suggests monotheism wasn't the invention of "oneness" but a REINTERPRETATION of an existing unity concept.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** This is Smith's own synthetic claim, not a consensus position. Scholars who see polytheism as inherently fragmented would disagree. The claim needs testing against the data in chapters 1-4.

**Alternative reading:** The family metaphor may have been one of several organizing principles for the pantheon, not THE unifying concept. Royal/administrative metaphors might compete with familial ones.
