# albertz-hir-3.8.2: Deuteronomic theology was a mediating theology that synthesized pre-state liberation traditions with state institutions, decisively subordinating kingship and temple to the law

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §3.8 — The Deuteronomic Reform Movement]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §3.8

## The Claim

"One can say without exaggeration that the Deuteronomists created the very first through-structured conceptual and intellectual theological outline of Yahweh religion." It was a "large-scale mediating theology" with two crucial syntheses:

**Synthesis 1 — The king:** Kingship is accepted but stripped of sacral dignity. The "law of the king" (Deut 17:14-20) accepts divine election but "almost all the political and sacral competences which kingship theology derived from this are negated." The king may not multiply horses/chariots, make alliances with Egypt, take many wives (diplomatic syncretism), or enrich himself. He is subject to the law and "no longer the decisive guarantor of Israel's salvation." The Deuteronomists "want to create a kind of constitutional monarchy" — the king as primus inter pares.

**Synthesis 2 — The temple:** Zion theology is adopted (Jerusalem is the chosen place) but transformed. The "centralization formula" accepts Jerusalem's special status but historicizes it (baḥar, "chose") and detaches it from monarchy. "Name theology" replaces direct divine presence: Yahweh no longer "dwells" (šākan) directly on Zion; only his "name" (šēm) is there. "Thus the cultic presence of God... a priori takes on a historical and ethical dimension." The ark is reinterpreted as a container for the Decalogue — "only in connection with his commandments is Yahweh also present for Israel in the cult."

## Evidence

- Deut 17:14-20: law of the king as systematic limitation of royal power
- "Centralization formula": "the place which Yahweh will choose to make his name dwell there" (lěšakkēn šěmô šām)
- Ark = Decalogue container (Deut 10:1-5; cf. 1 Kings 8:9, 21)
- Cherubic throne and "Yahweh Sabaoth enthroned" language almost completely avoided
- Exodus theology placed above Zion theology (Deut 7:6; 14:2)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The theological analysis of Deuteronomy's transformation of kingship and temple theology is standard and well-supported.

## Stakes

This is Albertz's resolution of his master narrative. After 400 years of monarchy, Yahweh religion finally "coped with the challenge" of the state — by systematically subordinating state institutions to pre-state liberation values. The king becomes a brother subject to law; the temple becomes a place of the name, not direct divine presence. The liberation DNA of pre-state Yahwism wins — but only by transforming state institutions from within.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see Deuteronomy as primarily a royal document (not a subversion of kingship). Those who question whether "name theology" represents a genuine theological innovation or simply a different idiom for divine presence.

**Alternative reading:** Deuteronomy reinforces rather than subverts royal authority; the "law of the king" is a royal self-limitation, not a populist check on power.
