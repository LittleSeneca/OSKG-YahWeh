# kaufmann-ri-2.1: The metadivine realm is the fundamental idea of all pagan religion

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch II — Pagan Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter II

## The Claim

The fundamental idea distinguishing pagan thought is the existence of a realm of being prior to and above the gods — a metadivine realm. "Deity belongs to, and is derived from, a primordial realm." The gods are not the source of all that is, nor do they transcend the universe. They are part of a realm precedent to and independent of them. This realm appears across cultures as darkness, water, spirit, earth, sky — "always as the womb in which the seeds of all being are contained."

## Evidence

- Cosmogonies universally begin with primordial substance: Nammu (Sumer), Apsu-Tiamat (Babylon), Nun (Egypt), Chaos (Greece), primeval waters (India), Ginnungagap (Germanic), Tao (China)
- Gods are BORN out of this realm through natural, involuntary procreation — never created by sovereign will
- Multiplicity of gods stems from the "manifold powers and 'seeds' of the primordial realm, each of which is conceived as a self-contained divine entity"
- Even "primal gods" are fathers who engender offspring with no more control than a human father
- Ruling gods are typically second or third generation — the son who dethrones or murders his father is a "standing feature of pagan mythologies"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The cross-cultural cosmogonic pattern is real; the claim that it amounts to a single "fundamental idea" is an interpretive imposition.

## Stakes

If Kaufmann's "fundamental idea" accurately captures what all non-Israelite religions share, then Israelite religion's lack of theogony, metadivine realm, and self-operating magical forces makes it genuinely different in kind. If circularly defined, the entire Kaufmannian edifice collapses.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The entire critical tradition. [[claim-convergence-differentiation-framework-israelite-religion]] argues Israel shared many Canaanite patterns. [[claim-israelite-culture-was-canaanite-no-separation]] directly contradicts absolute difference. [[claim-israelite-religion-absolutely-different-paganism-from-beginning]] is Kaufmann's own positive formulation.

**Alternative reading:** Kaufmann identified a real pattern in ANE cosmogonic structures, but (a) claimed it's universal across ALL non-Israelite religions, (b) insisted Israel was entirely free of it, and (c) treated it as an "idea" rather than a shared cultural grammar. The critical consensus can accept the phenomenology as a useful ideal type without accepting its totalizing exclusivity.

## Assessment

Magnificent synthetic scholarship — Kaufmann's command of comparative data from Sumer to Japan is staggering. But the claim is overdetermined. Kaufmann needs paganism to be ONE thing so Israel can be absolutely OTHER. The evidence shows shared PATTERNS, not a shared IDEA. Patterns can be transformed; an "idea" is all-or-nothing. Kaufmann's insistence on "idea" is what allows him to draw the absolute boundary — and what makes his thesis vulnerable to evidence of continuity.
