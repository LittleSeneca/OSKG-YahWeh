# albertz-hir-2.3-2.4.4: Family piety continued largely uninfluenced by official Yahweh religion

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §2.3-2.4 — Tribal Alliance and Family Piety]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §2.4

## The Claim

"The kind of religion practised in the families of this time largely proves to be uninfluenced by the recent rise of Yahweh religion... much though Yahweh as a symbol of religious liberation made his unmistakable stamp especially on the political life of society as a whole, he made little impact on the religious everyday life of the families." This is the clearest demonstration of his "internal religious pluralism" model.

## Evidence

- **Personal names**: "The predicative elements of theophoric names show virtually no relationship to the events which were so constitutive of Yahweh religion: Exodus, Sinai, 'settlement' or wars of liberation." Specific Exodus verbs (hôṣî', he'ĕlâ) are absent. "Verbs which could have alluded to its immigration are completely lacking." (This directly supports Tigay's onomastic findings — names don't reflect official salvation-history themes.)
- **Individual laments**: The psalms of individual lament use language of personal distress (illness, enemies, death) not national salvation-history
- **Vows and offerings**: Family-level practices like vows (neder), first-fruit offerings, and individual sacrifices continued with their own logic
- **Circumcision**: Probably originated as a family-level rite (apotropaic, associated with fertility/marriage) before being theologized as a "covenant sign" by P
- **Family cult**: Continued at local sanctuaries (bāmôt) and household shrines; the teraphim and other family cult objects persisted

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The onomastic evidence is strong and specific. Albertz's observation that personal names don't reference salvation-history is verifiable and important. This is one of his strongest empirical arguments.

## Stakes

If family piety was genuinely uninfluenced by official Yahweh religion, then (a) appeals to "what Israelites believed" must specify WHICH Israelites at WHICH social level, (b) the official religion's claim to represent "Israel" is an aspiration, not a description, and (c) the history of Israelite religion is the story of official religion gradually penetrating (and being resisted by) family practice.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see more integration between family and official religion. Van der Toorn's work emphasizes how family religion was "Yahwized" rather than remaining separate.

**Alternative reading:** The absence of Exodus/Sinai themes in personal names could reflect naming conventions (names express hopes for the child, not national theology) rather than a genuine religious divide. People with Exodus-theology might still name their children "Yohanan" (YHWH is gracious) rather than "Exodus-deliverance."

## Assessment

This is solid evidence for Albertz's model, and it converges with Tigay's findings (94% YHWH names but no salvation-history content). The gap between official theology and personal piety is real. The question is whether it indicates "two strata" or just different applications of the same religion in different contexts — like a modern Christian who names their child "Grace" rather than "Calvary" but still believes in the atonement.
