# albertz-hir-2.1.1: "Patriarchal religion" is not a pre-Yahwistic stage but a substratum of family piety, retrojected onto the ancestors by later tradents

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §2.1 — Patriarchal Religion as Family Piety]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §2.1

## The Claim

"Patriarchal religion is to be defined not as a preliminary stage but as a substratum of Yahweh religion. This stratum of family religion is a preliminary stage only to the degree that it shows an amazing similarity to other Near Eastern religions, going as far back as Sumero-Babylonian religion." The patriarchal narratives preserve authentic material about family-level religious practice — but this practice was contemporaneous with (and older than) Yahweh religion, not chronologically prior to it. The tradents described what they knew of family piety in their own time and projected it onto the ancestors.

## Evidence

- Alt's "God of the Fathers" hypothesis (1929) tried to reconstruct a pre-Yahwistic nomadic patriarchal religion from the Genesis designations "God of my father," "Fear of Isaac," etc. — but this has collapsed under criticism (Diebner 1975, Köckert 1988)
- Alt's key claims can't be sustained: "God of the fathers" is not specifically nomadic (attested in sedentary cultures too); the cultural milieu of Gen 12-50 doesn't predate Iron Age I (1200 BCE); the genealogical framework presupposes tribal conditions of the late pre-state period
- **Yet** the religious world depicted in Genesis is "clearly distinct from the world of the symbols of Yahweh religion" — this difference demands explanation
- Albertz's sociological explanation: the difference is between *levels* (family vs. people), not *periods* (before vs. after Yahweh). Genesis depicts the small group of the family; Yahweh religion belongs to the wider group of tribe/tribal alliance/people
- The patriarchal stories relate "the beginnings of the people of Israel from the aspect of family history" — so they accumulate the religious patterns native to the family

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The negative case (Alt's hypothesis is broken) is well-established and widely accepted. The positive case (family piety as substratum) is elegant but depends on Albertz's "internal religious pluralism" framework being correct. If the family/state distinction is artificial, this collapses into circular reasoning.

## Stakes

This is foundational for Albertz's entire method. If patriarchal religion is family piety rather than a pre-Yahwistic stage, then (a) Yahweh religion didn't evolve FROM patriarchal religion — it was always distinct and operated at a different social level, (b) the tensions in Israelite religion (tolerant vs. exclusive, unwarlike vs. warlike) are tensions between social strata that coexisted, and (c) the history of Israelite religion is not a straight line but a constant negotiation between family piety and official religion.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Any scholar who still treats patriarchal religion as a chronological predecessor to Mosaic Yahwism. The Alt school (and its successors) treated the "God of the fathers" as a pre-Yahwistic nomadic type. Evangelical scholars who accept the historicity of the patriarchal narratives would reject the claim that the religious material is retrojected.

**Alternative reading:** One could accept Alt's collapse while rejecting Albertz's alternative. Van Seters (1980) argued the patriarchal narratives are late literary creations with no historical value whatsoever — not family piety, not anything pre-exilic. The "difference" from Yahweh religion could be explained by literary artistry rather than authentic family tradition.

## Assessment

This is Albertz's most creative move and the one that most clearly distinguishes his project from competitors. It's productive and makes sense of otherwise puzzling features (why patriarchal religion has no polemic, no exclusiveness, no warlike characteristics). But it also carries risk: Albertz is simultaneously claiming the patriarchal narratives are NOT historically reliable (the chronology is fictitious, Alt's reconstruction is wrong) AND that they DO preserve authentic material about family piety (because the tradents knew what family religion looked like and projected it). This is a subtle hedge — he wants to eat his critical cake and have his traditional material too.
