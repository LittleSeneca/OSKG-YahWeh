# schmid-ht-5.3: OT theology disappeared into history of Israelite religion

**Source:** [[Schmid — §5 — Romantic Devaluation of Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §5

## The Claim

"As a result, theology of the Old Testament as a project and as a nominally identifiable discipline basically disappeared from the stage." Key figures simply treated "theology of the OT" and "history of Israelite religion" as synonymous — or replaced the former with the latter.

## Evidence

- Hermann Schultz (1896): "The task of biblical theology is a purely historical presentation" — theology = historical description of "faith perspectives and customary conceptions"
- Bernhard Stade (1905): "By biblical theology of the OT one understands the history of the religion under the old covenant"
- Karl Marti (1897): Simply retitled Kayser's *Theologie des Alten Testaments* to *Geschichte der Israelitischen Religion* — "By History of Israelite Religion, as well as by Theology of the Old Testament is meant that discipline concerned with the presentation of the religious and ethical content of the Old Testament"
- Rudolf Smend (1893): *Lehrbuch der alttestamentlichen Religionsgeschichte*
- Even conservative theologies (Oehler 1891) adopted historical-genetic framing, though within salvation-history framework
- New Testament parallel: William Wrede's *Über Aufgabe und Methode der sogenannten Neutestamentlichen Theologie* (1897) — "strictly historical character of New Testament theology"

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** The titles and quotations are direct evidence. The merger of theology into history of religion is an empirical fact of 19th-century scholarship.

## Stakes

This is the crucial historical precedent for Schmid's own project. The 19th century tried to solve the theology/history problem by ABSORBING theology into history. Schmid's project is the opposite: he wants to restore theology as a distinct (but still historical) enterprise. He needs to show why the 19th-century absorption was inadequate.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** No one disputes the historical facts. The titles don't lie. The question is whether the 19th-century absorption was an error or a correct recognition that "theology of the HB" IS history of Israelite religion.

**Alternative reading:** The 19th century was right. Theology of the HB IS history of Israelite religion. Schmid's attempt to restore "theology" as a distinct category is a regression to pre-critical confusion.

## Assessment

Schmid's genealogy is compelling. The pendulum swung from theology (pre-critical) → history of religion (19th century) → neo-orthodox theology (early 20th) → crisis (now). Schmid proposes a synthesis: historical theology. Whether this is a genuine synthesis or just a relabeling of history of religion with "theology" is the question the rest of the book must answer.
