# romer-inv-6.2: Yhwh was worshipped in diverse local manifestations

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 6 — Cult of Yhwh in Israel]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 6

## The Claim

Before the centralization of worship in Jerusalem, Yhwh was worshipped in multiple local forms across Israel and Judah. Different sanctuaries had their own "Yhwh of [place]" — a pattern shared with other Levantine deities. This was not "syncretism" or "apostasy." It was the normal mode of ancient West Semitic religion. Three levels of religion operated simultaneously: family/clan (personal gods, deified ancestors), local (open-air sanctuaries on "every hill and under every green tree"), and national/royal (the king as mediator).

## Evidence

| Inscription/Source | Manifestation | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Kuntillet Ajrud | "Yhwh of Samaria" and "Yhwh of Teman" | Northeastern Sinai |
| Khirbet Beit Lei | "Yhwh, god of Jerusalem" | Judah |
| 2 Samuel 15:7 | "Yhwh at Hebron" | Judah |
| Psalm 99:2 | "Yhwh in Zion" | Jerusalem |

- "Shared gods existed elsewhere in the Levant. This was particularly the case for the god 'El,' but also for Yhwh himself."
- The three-level model (family, local, national) was not unique to Israel — it was the standard structure of ancient Near Eastern religion.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on diverse local manifestations (inscriptional evidence is clear, multi-sourced, and geographically distributed). The three-level model is well-established in the study of ancient religion.

## Stakes

The idea of "Yhwh" as a single, unified deity whose worship was identical everywhere is a product of later centralization. In the monarchic period, there were many "Yhwhs" — local manifestations with distinct characters and cults. The centralizing reforms of Hezekiah and Josiah attempted to collapse this diversity into a single Jerusalem cult. The biblical condemnation of "high places" (*bāmôt*) was a polemic against legitimate, traditional local worship.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see the local manifestations as evidence of poly-Yahwism (multiple distinct Yahwehs) rather than local manifestations of one deity. Conservatives who read the "high places" as genuinely idolatrous.

**Alternative reading:** The local manifestations were all the same Yhwh — the place-names simply identify WHERE he was worshipped, not distinct divine personalities. "Yhwh of Samaria" means "Yhwh as worshipped in Samaria," not a different Yhwh.

## Assessment

The inscriptional evidence is unambiguous. Yhwh had local manifestations just like Baal, just like El, just like every other West Semitic deity. This is not controversial among specialists. What IS interesting is Römer's framing: this diversity was the NORM, not the deviation. The centralization that produced "biblical religion" was the innovation — and it required suppressing a vibrant, legitimate tradition of local Yahwism.
