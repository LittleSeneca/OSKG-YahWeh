# lewis-ocg-4.1: El Was the Original God of Israel

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 4 — El Worship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"The logical place to begin looking at ancient Israelite divinity is with El, the deity whose name is found in the designation of the eponymous ancestor 'Israel.' ... It is often argued that the absence of Yahweh in this name, together with the name of the Shechemite deity 'El, the god of (the patriarch) Israel' (Gen 33:20) and the absence of Yahweh (and Baal) in anthroponyms and toponyms in early literature, indicates the antiquity of El worship over Yahweh worship."

## Evidence

- "Israel" most likely means "El reigns supreme" or "El contends"
- Genesis 33:20 refers to ʾēl ʾĕlōhê yiśrāʾēl — "El, the god of Israel" — which Lewis says can't be a redundant "god, the god of Israel" given the parallel with the common formula "YHWH ʾĕlōhê yiśrāʾēl"
- Exodus 6:3 (P) explicitly states Yahweh revealed himself to the patriarchs as El Shadday, not as Yahweh
- Van der Toorn (1996b): El anthroponyms and toponyms appear before Yahweh ones in the onomastic record
- The Shechemite deity formula (Gen 33:20) preserves a pre-Yahwistic cult title
- Absence of Yahweh in early theophoric names; abundance of El names

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** Near-consensus in critical scholarship; the name "Israel" with its El theophoric element is the single most compelling piece of evidence.

## Stakes

The entire narrative of Israelite religion. If El was first, Yahweh is a latecomer who absorbed El's attributes. This inverts the biblical narrative (where Yahweh is the eternal God who revealed himself to the patriarchs) and supports a historical reconstruction where Yahweh originated separately and was later identified with El.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Confessional scholars who hold to Mosaic Yahwism from the beginning. Kaufmann would reject this outright. More moderately, some scholars argue that Yahweh and El were always the same deity under different names.

**Alternative reading:** The name "Israel" could theoretically contain a generic "el" (god) rather than the proper name El. Lewis himself acknowledges the appellative/proper name ambiguity. But the cumulative case (Shechemite El, patriarchal El deities, Exodus 6:3, onomastics) makes the proper-name reading much stronger.

## Assessment

This is Lewis's foundational claim and it's well-supported. The question is not whether El was original — it's how early Yahweh arrived and how the merger happened. That's Chapter 6's territory.
