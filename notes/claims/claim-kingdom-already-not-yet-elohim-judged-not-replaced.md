# heiser-ur-36.1: The kingdom is already inaugurated but not yet consummated — the *elohim* are judged but not replaced

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 8 — The Kingdom Not Yet]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 36

## The Claim

Christ's first coming DEFEATED the corrupt *elohim*. His second coming will REPLACE them. In the meantime, believers live in the "already/not yet" tension: the *elohim* know they've lost, but they still exercise influence. This explains both the NT's confidence ("he disarmed the rulers") and its warnings ("we wrestle against... cosmic powers").

## Evidence

- Colossians 2:15: Christ "disarmed the rulers and authorities" — past tense, the defeat is accomplished (the "already")
- Ephesians 6:12: "we wrestle... against the cosmic powers over this present darkness" — present tense, the conflict continues (the "not yet")
- Revelation 20:10-15: The final judgment of the devil and his angels is still future
- 1 Corinthians 15:24-25: "Then comes the end, when he delivers the kingdom to God the Father after destroying every rule and every authority and power. For he must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The already/not-yet framework is widely accepted in NT theology. Heiser's specific application to the *elohim* — that they are the "rules and authorities" currently being subdued — is a framework-dependent reading but internally consistent.

## Stakes

If Heiser is right, the already/not-yet tension is not abstract theological paradox but a concrete cosmic reality: the *elohim* are defeated but still in their administrative posts pending final judgment. This explains why evil persists despite Christ's victory — the corrupt administrators haven't been replaced yet.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who read the already/not-yet in terms of individual salvation (justified but not yet glorified) rather than cosmic governance. Also, realized eschatology proponents who emphasize the "already" to the point of minimizing the "not yet."

**Alternative reading:** The already/not-yet tension describes the gap between Christ's accomplished work and its full application to believers — not a cosmic administrative transition between corrupt *elohim* and glorified humans.

## Assessment

This is Heiser's framework applied to standard NT eschatology. The already/not-yet structure is uncontroversial. What's distinctive is Heiser's identification of WHAT is already-but-not-yet: the replacement of the corrupt *elohim* by glorified believers. This gives concrete content to a theological abstraction — and that is Heiser's project in miniature.
