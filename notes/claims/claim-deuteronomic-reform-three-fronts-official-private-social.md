# albertz-hir-3.8.3: The Deuteronomic reform fought on three fronts simultaneously: official polytheism, private syncretism, and social abuses

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §3.8 — The Deuteronomic Reform Movement]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §3.8

## The Claim

The Deuteronomic reform was "not just a cultic reform" but a comprehensive social and religious program. It fought:

1. **Official syncretism**: Abolition of Assyrian cult installations in the Jerusalem temple (2 Kings 23:4-7, 11-12); destruction of the Bethel sanctuary (23:15-18); elimination of provincial sanctuaries (centralization)
2. **Private syncretism**: Extension of reform to family-level practices, including the Queen of Heaven cult, Molech sacrifice, roof-top altars, teraphim, and necromancy
3. **Social abuses**: Debt release every 7 years (Deut 15:1-11), slave manumission (15:12-18), protection of wages, gleaning rights, impartial justice

## Evidence

- 2 Kings 23 catalogs the purges: vessels for Baal/Asherah removed, "houses of the qědēšîm" destroyed, Topheth defiled, horses dedicated to the sun removed, roof-top altars destroyed
- Deuteronomic social legislation: šěmiṭṭâ (debt release), protection of the poor, limitation of corporal punishment (Deut 25:1-3)
- The reform extended official religion's reach into family life for the first time — "internal religious pluralism came under criticism"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Scope of the reform is well-documented; actual geographical reach beyond Jerusalem is less certain.

## Stakes

This claim establishes that the Deuteronomic reform was uniquely comprehensive — it didn't just purge foreign cults from the temple but targeted the family-level practices that previous reforms (Hezekiah's) had left untouched. The extension into private religion is the critical innovation: for the first time, official Yahwism claimed authority over what families did in their homes. This is the moment when "internal religious pluralism" — Albertz's key analytical category — came under systematic attack.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who question how far the reform actually penetrated beyond Jerusalem. The biblical account may exaggerate the reform's reach.

**Alternative reading:** The reform was limited to Jerusalem and its immediate environs; the 2 Kings 23 catalog is idealized or exaggerated.
