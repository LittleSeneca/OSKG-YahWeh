# lewis-ocg-6.2: The meaning of Yahweh relates to "being" and "active presence" — the non-causative is preferred

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 6 — Origin of Yahweh]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 6

## The Claim

The consensus that YHWH represents a verbal form of HYH "to be" is well-founded. The Exodus 3:14 wordplay (ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh) is "irrevocably" tied to the name. But rather than abstract existence ("He Who Is" in Greek metaphysical terms), the primary meaning is "active, helping presence" — God WILL BE with his people. The non-causative (Qal) stem is preferred because the causative never occurs in Hebrew for this root despite 3,561 attestations.

## Evidence

Against the causative theory: (1) The verb HYH "to be" never occurs in Hebrew as a causative despite massive statistical sampling (Mettinger: 3,561 attestations, "not a single example in the causative stem"). (2) HYH is never explicitly used for creation — Hebrew uses BRʾ, ʿŚH, YṢR instead. (3) The grammatical argument (that a proper name can't be in construct state, so "Yahweh of Hosts" must be a sentence name) is weakened by Kuntillet ʿAjrud inscriptions showing YHWH used in construct with place names (Yahweh of Samaria, Yahweh of Teman). (4) The theory requires rewriting Exodus 3:14 to a causative vocalization, ignoring the obvious wordplay. (5) The Arabic "blow/fell" etymology has the same problem — it can't be reconciled with Exodus 3:14 and the verb isn't productive in Hebrew.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Exodus 3:14 wordplay is the single best interpretive key; the statistical argument against the causative (no Hiphil of HYH in the entire Hebrew Bible) is devastating.

## Stakes

If the causative theory were right, Yahweh's name means "creator" and the deity originated as an epithet of El — making Yahweh originally a northern/Canaanite figure. If the non-causative is right, Yahweh's name means "the one who is/will be present" — fitting a deity of active, personal presence, which could originate anywhere, including the south.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Cross, Freedman, and their students (including many of Lewis's own teachers) maintain the causative theory. It has genuine elegance — explaining Yahweh Sebaoth as a sentence name and tying everything to El. Lewis's rejection of it is a notable departure from the Cross school.

**Alternative reading:** Both meanings may have coexisted in different communities. The biblical tradition (Exod 3:14) preserves the non-causative understanding, but a pre-biblical causative understanding may have existed among groups who first formulated the name.

## Assessment

Lewis makes a strong case against the causative. The statistical argument (no causative of HYH in Hebrew) is devastating. The Kuntillet ʿAjrud counter-evidence to Cross's grammatical claim is also strong — proper names CAN be in construct. I think Lewis is right to prefer the non-causative, but I suspect the question will remain debated because the causative theory is so elegant.
