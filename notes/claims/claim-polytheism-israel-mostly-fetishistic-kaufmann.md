# tigay-nog-2.7: Israelite 'polytheism' was mostly fetishistic

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Conclusions

## The Claim

Following Kaufmann, Tigay argues that much of what the Bible condemns as "polytheism" was actually fetishism: "Defined as Kaufmann defined it, fetishism involves the belief that divine or demonic forces inhabit the image or are represented by it, not that the image itself is a god." This distinction is crucial — fetishism is compatible with practical monotheism (YHWH is the only real god, but other forces/spirits exist), while genuine polytheism (multiple real gods with independent mythologies) is not.

## Evidence

- Tigay explicitly cites Kaufmann's definition of fetishism
- The biblical writers consistently characterize pagan worship as fetishistic (worshipping wood and stone, things that cannot see/hear/speak) — see [[claim-israelite-idolatry-vestigial-fetishism-not-genuine-polytheism]] and [[claim-bible-consistently-characterizes-paganism-as-fetishism]]
- If most Israelite "polytheism" was fetishistic, it would explain how the onomastic evidence (overwhelmingly Yahwistic) can coexist with archaeological evidence of figurines — the figurines were charms/symbols, not objects of genuine theological polytheism
- The distinction between fetishism and polytheism is also a distinction between practice and belief: people USED figurines without BELIEVING in a full pantheon

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The fetishism-vs-polytheism distinction is analytically useful but difficult to verify archaeologically — a figurine doesn't come with a label indicating whether its user believed in a full mythology behind it.

## Stakes

The fetishism argument is Tigay's bridge between his onomastic data (which shows YHWH-dominance) and the archaeological data (which shows figurine use). If most figurine use was fetishistic rather than genuinely polytheistic, the two datasets are compatible. The debate then shifts from "was Israel polytheistic?" to "what KIND of non-Yahwistic practice was happening?"

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-dever-folk-polytheism-outlasted-monarchy-monotheism-book-religion]] (Dever)

**Alternative reading:** Dever would argue the fetishism-vs-polytheism distinction is a distinction without a difference. If people are using Asherah figurines to pray for fertility, they are practicing goddess religion regardless of what abstract theology they might articulate. The archaeological evidence shows REAL religious practice, not just superstitious charm-use.
