# smith-obm-9.4: Woman Wisdom in Proverbs 1-9 takes over Asherah's roles and imagery as a monotheizing strategy

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch9 — Formation of Monotheistic Theologies]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 9

## The Claim

Proverbs 1-9 "presents a divine invitation from the female personification of Wisdom" that draws on Canaanite goddess imagery. B. Lang suggests Wisdom functions as "the divine patroness of the Israelite school system." If Proverbs is a Levitical product, "one might argue further that the female personification of Wisdom is the divine patron of the Levitical duties of teaching and writing."

## Evidence

- Scholars have long compared Wisdom to Asherah: "The 'tree of life,' which recalls the tree of the *asherah*, appears in Israelite tradition as a metaphorical expression for Wisdom (Proverbs 3:18; cf. 11:30, 15:4; Genesis 3:22; Revelation 2:7)."
- "Like the symbol of the *asherah*, Wisdom is a female figure, providing life and nurturing."
- Proverbs 3:13-18 forms a chiasm: the unit opens with *'asre* ("happy," v. 13) and closes with *me'ussar* ("made happy," v. 18), and "the inside terms of the chiasm are *hokma*, 'wisdom,' and *'es-hayyim*, 'a tree of life.'" Smith suggests the terms *'asre* and *me'ussar* "perhaps allude to the *asherah*, the tree symbolizing life and well-being."
- This represents "a move to 'monotheize' the imagery of the Asherah in a wisdom framework."
- Proverbs' verbal links with Deuteronomy suggest Levitical authorship — and Levites were assigned teaching and writing roles. Wisdom would then be the divine patron of specifically Levitical scribal-educational functions.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The general comparison between Wisdom and Asherah has a long scholarly pedigree, but the specific verbal allusions (*'asre*/*'ussar* -> *asherah*) are speculative. The Levitical connection is similarly suggestive rather than proven.

## Stakes

If Woman Wisdom is a monotheized Asherah, then Israelite monotheism didn't simply eliminate the goddess — it absorbed and transformed her into a subordinate, personified figure. This is a more subtle strategy than outright rejection and suggests the goddess's symbolic power was too strong to simply discard.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see Woman Wisdom as an indigenous Israelite development unrelated to goddess traditions. Those who read the verbal links with Deuteronomy differently (post-exilic rather than Levitical).

**Alternative reading:** Woman Wisdom could be a hypostasis of Yahweh's own wisdom attribute, developed from within Israelite sapiential tradition, with any resemblance to Asherah being coincidental or at most a very distant echo. The tree-of-life metaphor need not imply Asherah — trees as symbols of life are nearly universal.

## Assessment

Smith's argument here is more suggestive than demonstrative. The *'asre*/*'ussar* wordplay with *asherah* is clever but thin — it's one consonant (*s*) and the association relies on the reader making the jump. The broader pattern (female personification + tree of life + nurturing/life-giving role) is more persuasive as a cumulative case. The Levitical connection is underdeveloped and needs more evidence.
