# albertz-hir-2.2.5: The Sinai covenant and image prohibition are late Deuteronomistic retrojections, not Mosaic; pre-exilic Yahweh worship used images freely

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §2.2 — The Exodus Group and Yahweh's Origins]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §2.2

## The Claim

"It is quite certain that we cannot yet speak of a covenant in the full theological and legal sense in the early period." The covenant conception "only goes back to the Deuteronomic/Deuteronomistic theologians." Similarly, the image prohibition developed late: "the battle against divine images only began with the prophet Hosea" (8th c.), not with Moses. Pre-exilic Yahweh worship freely used cultic images — a bull statuette was found in northern Samaria, massebas stood in the Arad temple, a possible Yahweh figurine at Hazor.

## Evidence

- The pre-Deuteronomic Sinai narrative "probably first spoke of an obligation of Israel to the law" (Ex 24:3ff.*), expanded to a formal covenant "only in the early post-exilic period" (KD)
- Hosea was the FIRST to polemicize against divine images (Hos 4:17; 8:4f., 6; 10:5; 11:2; 13:2; 14:4, 9) — before him, Yahweh worship with images was uncontroversial
- Archaeological evidence: massebas in the Arad temple's holy of holies; Yahweh worship at Dan with images (Judg 17f.); the Nehushtan serpent at Jerusalem (II Kings 18:4); the Bethel bull
- The image prohibition developed gradually: late 8th c. = ban on precious metal statues (Ex 20:23b); 7th c. = ban on any divine images (Ex 20:4a); later = ban on traditional cult symbols like massebas and asheras (Deut 16:21f.); exilic period = extension to house cults
- "Yahweh is not originally a god who could be drawn into the world through an image as a guarantor of the existing social order, but a god who with his promise of liberation transcends the existing world"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the archaeological evidence for image-use in pre-exilic Yahwism (multiple sites, multiple artifact types). HIGH on the gradual development of the image prohibition (stages can be traced textually). MEDIUM on the covenant being entirely late (some form of formalized relationship before Deuteronomy is plausible).

## Stakes

If the covenant and commandments are late theological constructs, the entire "Mosaic law" tradition is a retrojection. The distinctive elements of Israelite religion (aniconism, covenant, law) are products of the monarchy and exile, not the founding period. Moses shrinks from lawgiver to liberator/organizer.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Evangelical and Jewish traditional scholars who maintain Mosaic origins for the covenant and commandments. Also scholars who see pre-Deuteronomic covenant traditions (e.g., the Shechem covenant in Joshua 24).

**Alternative reading:** Even if the full-blown "covenant" concept is Deuteronomic, a less formalized "obligation" or "relationship" that later developed into covenant theology is entirely plausible and arguably presupposed by the pre-Deuteronomic material.

**Supports:**
- [[claim-no-anthropomorphic-yahweh-statue-jerusalem-temple]] — Albertz's archaeological evidence for pre-exilic image-use provides the positive side of Lewis's negative argument (no statue in Jerusalem); images existed elsewhere
- [[claim-jeroboam-golden-calves-yahwistic-el-bull]] — Albertz's treatment of the Bethel bull as a pre-exilic Yahwistic image aligns with the standard reading of Jeroboam's calves
- [[claim-deuteronomy-written-josiah-reform]] — Albertz's dating of the covenant concept to the Deuteronomic/Deuteronomistic theologians aligns with Römer's argument that Deuteronomy was written for Josiah's reform

**Contradicts:**
- [[claim-golden-calves-yhwh-worship-not-pagan-idolatry]] — Kaufmann argues the golden calves were Yahwistic (not pagan), which Albertz would agree with, but Kaufmann's larger claim that this was legitimate pre-Josianic Yahwism contradicts Albertz's argument that Hosea's image polemic was a NEW development that eventually became normative
- [[claim-israel-monotheistic-desert-period-idolatry-deviance]] — Kaufmann's model that Israel was monotheistic in the desert and idolatry was later deviance is directly contradicted by Albertz's claim that images were present from the beginning and the prohibition developed late
- [[claim-covenant-image-prohibition-late-deuteronomic-retrojection]] — the covenant as late construct contradicts Kaufmann's Torah-priority thesis and Moses-as-lawgiver model

**Challenged by:**
- [[claim-josiah-reforms-endured-no-pagan-revival]] — Kaufmann argues Josiah's reforms endured and there was no post-Josiah pagan revival; Albertz's model implies the image prohibition was a long, contested process rather than a one-time reform

**Primary sources:**
- Hosea 4:17; 8:4-6; 10:5; 13:2 — earliest image polemic
- Exodus 20:23b — ban on precious metal statues (late 8th c.)
- Exodus 20:4a — general image prohibition (7th c.)
- Deuteronomy 16:21-22 — ban on massebas and asheras (Josianic)
- II Kings 18:4 — Nehushtan serpent at Jerusalem

## Assessment

This is solid critical scholarship. The archaeological evidence for images in Israelite cult is decisive — Albertz is right that pre-exilic Yahwism was aniconic only in aspiration, not in practice. The gradual development of the image prohibition is well-documented. I'm slightly less convinced about the covenant — the total absence of any pre-Deuteronomic covenant concept seems overstated. But the basic point (the fully developed Sinai covenant is a later projection) is standard critical consensus.
