# kaufmann-ri-11.10: The prophets established a hierarchy of value — morality is essentially godlike, cult is only symbolic

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch XI — Amos and Hosea]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter XI

## The Claim

Classical prophecy "established a hierarchy of value; both cult and morality are God's command and part of his covenant, and both are expressions of 'knowledge of God.' But while the cult is sacred only as a symbol, morality is essentially godlike, being a reflection of the qualities of God." This hierarchy was possible because Israelite religion, "essentially non-mythological and non-magical, could conceive of the relation of God and the world only in terms of free grace." God is not dependent on the cult — the cult is a manifestation of HIS grace to MAN. "Hence its value is conditional, not absolute."

## Evidence

- Torah literature "does not distinguish cultic from moral prescriptions, but indiscriminately juxtaposes them"
- The prophets, "taking their departure from the tradition of Israel's election, declare that its object was to publish the 'knowledge of God' and realize his moral will"
- "Morality... is an absolute value, for it is divine in essence. The God who demands righteousness, justice, kindness, and compassion is himself just, gracious, kind, and compassionate"
- "Moral goodness makes man share, as it were, in the divine nature"
- The cult becomes "an empty and broken vessel, an abomination of YHWH" through sin — its value is conditional

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** This is Kaufmann at his most theological and least historical. He's constructing a systematic theology of the prophets' view of cult vs. morality that the texts themselves don't articulate in these terms. It's elegant, but it's Kaufmann's construction, not the prophets'.

## Stakes

Whether the prophetic critique of cult reflects a THEOLOGICAL PRINCIPLE (cult is inherently conditional) or a HISTORICAL SITUATION (the cult was corrupted, so the prophets attacked it). Kaufmann needs the former to support his claim that Israelite religion was essentially different from paganism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see the prophetic cult-critique as situational rather than principled. Isaiah's vision of nations streaming to Zion (Isa 2:2-4) envisions a future WITH cult. Ezekiel's temple vision (Ezek 40-48) is entirely cultic. The prophets didn't reject cult in principle — they rejected corrupted cult.

**Alternative reading:** The prophets' hierarchy of morality over cult is not a theological innovation — it's already present in texts like 1 Samuel 15:22 ("to obey is better than sacrifice") and Psalm 51:16-17 ("you do not delight in sacrifice... a broken spirit"). The prophets are drawing on existing wisdom, not creating a new theological framework.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's framework is theologically rich but historically thin. He's systematizing what the prophets do into a coherent "hierarchy of value" that may be more Kaufmann than Amos or Hosea. The claim that Israelite religion was "essentially non-magical" is particularly problematic — the prophets' very critique of cult-as-magic implies that many Israelites DID treat cult magically. Kaufmann attributes to "the Israelite idea" what may have been the prophetic minority position.
