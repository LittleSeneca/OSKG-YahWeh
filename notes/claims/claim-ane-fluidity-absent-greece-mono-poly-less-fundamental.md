# sommer-bog-1.3: ANE fluidity was absent from ancient Greece — the key divide is fluid vs. non-fluid divinity

**Source:** [[Sommer — Chapter 1 — Mesopotamia and Canaan]] — Benjamin D. Sommer, *The Bodies of God and the World of Ancient Israel* (2009), Chapter 1

## The Claim

"Greek culture provides no evidence that multiple objects could contain the presence of a particular deity at any one moment. Ancient Greek religion furthermore maintained that deities' selves were consistently distinct from each other."

The Greek gods had discrete bodies and discrete selves LIKE HUMANS. The ANE gods were fundamentally UNLIKE humans in their fluidity.

"Greek religion assumed a basic resemblance between mortals and immortals... ancient Near Eastern religions posited a radical contrast."

The implication: The traditional distinction between monotheism and polytheism is less fundamental than the distinction between FLUID and NON-FLUID divinity. Greece was polytheistic but non-fluid. Israel was monotheistic but fluid (in J/E). This cross-cuts the standard taxonomy.

## Evidence

- Greek cult statues were understood as the god's dwelling place or representation — not the god itself in another body. A specific statue of Athena was Athena's dwelling, not Athena herself.
- Greek gods did not have multiple local manifestations treated as partially distinct beings. There was one Athena, not "Athena of Athens" and "Athena of Sparta" as distinct-yet-overlapping entities.
- The line between one god and another was firmly drawn in Greek religion — no texts describe one god's body as composed of other gods, no rituals offer to combined deities.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the ANE evidence. MEDIUM-HIGH on the Greek contrast — Sommer's documentation is persuasive but some possible counter-examples in mystery cults (Dionysus, Isis) might challenge the generalization.

## Stakes

This is the linchpin of Sommer's argument that the mono/poly divide is the wrong frame. If fluidity is a feature of ANE religion (polytheistic) AND biblical J/E religion (monotheistic), but absent from Greek religion (polytheistic), then the categories "monotheism" and "polytheism" don't capture the interesting religious difference. The real difference is between cultures that understood divinity as capable of fragmentation and multiplication, and cultures that didn't.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who treat monotheism/polytheism as the fundamental religious divide; classicists who might find counter-examples in Greek mystery religions or Hellenistic syncretism.

**Alternative reading:** The Greek/ANE contrast is overdrawn. Greek religion had its own forms of divine fluidity (Dionysus's multiple epiphanies, the identification of Greek gods with foreign ones in the Hellenistic period). The difference is one of degree, not kind.

## Assessment

The Greek contrast is Sommer's most original and potentially most vulnerable claim. If it holds, it fundamentally reorganizes the comparative study of ancient religion. If it doesn't — if Greek religion had its own fluidity — then Sommer's ANE evidence remains valuable but his cross-cutting thesis collapses. The burden of proof is on him to show the contrast is categorical, not a matter of emphasis.
