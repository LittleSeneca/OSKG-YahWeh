# smith-obm-2.3: Israel inherited the four-tier pantheon but it collapsed through convergence

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch2 — The Divine Council]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"The paradigm of the pantheon went through a process of collapse and telescoping (aspects of a larger process of convergence in Israelite religion). There is no full-scale second tier represented in the extant biblical texts." Israel's pantheon was structurally similar to Ugarit's four-tier model, but convergence collapsed the tiers: El merged with Yahweh, Asherah became "his asherah," and second-tier deities were reduced to Yahweh's servants or demons.

## Evidence

- Kuntillet 'Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom inscriptions (8th century): "Yahweh and his asherah." Most scholars see these as evidence for Yahweh and Asherah as divine couple — tier 1.
- Temple asherah in Jerusalem: "the image of the asherah/Asherah" (2 Kings 21:7). Prophetic condemnations imply the worship existed — "why condemn something unless it is a problem?"
- Second-tier vestiges: Resheph and Deber appear in Habakkuk 3:5 as part of Yahweh's theophanic retinue — "already the reduction of other members of the second tier to Yahweh's servants similar to Baal's theophanic retinue in CAT 1.5 V 6-9."
- Angelic powers in Dead Sea Scrolls called 'lym ("gods") — but "in the wake of the earlier telescoping of the pantheon... this word probably conveyed the sense of heavenly powers (under the One Power) rather than full-fledged deities."
- Exodus 15:11 ("who is like You among the gods?") — when reused in the Hodayot (1QH 7:28), "the question does not carry the same freight... devoid of its earlier polytheistic context."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Convergence is well-attested; exact four-tier structure in Israel is vestigial but the mechanism is clear.

## Stakes

This is the structural mechanism of monotheism's emergence. It's not that Israel "became monotheistic" as a philosophical decision — it's that the pantheon's structure collapsed. The identification of El with Yahweh (tier 1 + tier 2) pulled the whole structure down. What's left in the Bible is rubble, with occasional intact architectural fragments (Psalm 82, Deut 32:8-9).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Tigay's onomastic evidence suggests widespread popular monolatry much earlier than Smith's model would predict — if most Israelites only named their children after Yahweh by the 8th century, the four-tier pantheon may have been more of a royal/scribal construct than a widely-held belief. Also, scholars who deny Asherah was a goddess (reading 'šrth as "his shrine/sanctuary" rather than "his Asherah").

**Alternative reading:** The Israelite pantheon may never have been as fully articulated as the Ugaritic one. "Vestigial" references might reflect Israel's AWARENESS of neighboring pantheons rather than its own internal pantheon structure. The four-tier model might be an Ugaritic phenomenon projected onto Israel.

## Assessment

Smith's model is elegant but the Israelite side of the evidence is thin. The Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions are genuinely important, but they're two sites. The temple asherah evidence depends on how one reads 2 Kings. The model works better as an account of what Israelite scribes KNEW about pantheon structures (from Ugaritic/West Semitic heritage) and progressively rejected, rather than what Israelite popular religion actually practiced. That said, the convergence mechanism itself is independently attested by the El/Yahweh identification.
