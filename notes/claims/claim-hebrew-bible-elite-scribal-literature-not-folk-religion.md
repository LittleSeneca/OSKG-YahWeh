# schmid-ht-1.5: The Hebrew Bible is elite scribal literature, not a window into folk religion

**Source:** [[Schmid — §1 — Is There a Theology of the Hebrew Bible]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §1

## The Claim

"The Hebrew Bible does not provide an unmediated window into the lived experience of folk religion, as many scholars supposed in the heyday of form-critical studies. The Hebrew Bible...bears witness to a literary religion, which, as such, was never lived, but instead gave rise to an extraordinarily strong reception history." (Citing Stephen Geller, *Sacred Enigmas*, 1996.)

## Evidence

- "The texts of the Hebrew Bible were produced and read within the comparably small circle of individuals who were able to read and write quite proficiently within a widely illiterate society"
- Comparative evidence from Greece and Egypt supports this
- Writing in Israel only became pronounced in the 9th c. BCE, in Judah in the 8th c. BCE (Finkelstein and Sass 2017)
- The HB's character as "agglutinative (growing by supplementation) interpretive literature" requires that books existed as single copies — "It is hardly imaginable that the process of literary updating, consisting of multiple steps...could have taken place if numerous copies of the books were in circulation"
- Deut 17:18 assumes the king's copy is the sole additional copy
- 2 Macc 2:15 shows "not even the Jewish community of Alexandria possessed a complete Bible in the second century BCE"
- The Jerusalem temple likely housed a model exemplar used as basis for new augmented copies
- "The copying of an Isaiah scroll in the rabbinic period equaled about six months' income for a scribe"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The elite-literature thesis is now mainstream in Hebrew Bible scholarship (van der Toorn, Carr, Schniedewind). The material evidence (inscription distribution, production costs, single-copy model) is strong.

## Stakes

This claim is DEVASTATING for the standard evolutionary model of Israelite religion. If the HB is elite scribal literature from Jerusalem (Persian period onward), it does NOT document what ordinary Israelites believed. It documents what a small group of Jerusalem scribes WANTED TO BELIEVE about Israel's past. This is the opening Schmid needs for his late-dating argument — and it's a claim that the American consensus (Smith, Dever) has not fully absorbed.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who maintain that form criticism can recover oral traditions and folk religion behind the texts. Scholars who argue (contra Schmid) that the texts preserve genuine pre-exilic material (Cross, Freedman, early poetry school). Scholars who think the "elite literature" claim overstates the literacy gap.

**Alternative reading:** Even elite literature can preserve genuine traditions. Scribes don't invent from nothing — they work with inherited material. The question is HOW MUCH filtering has occurred, not WHETHER filtering has occurred.

## Assessment

This is Schmid's strongest empirical claim and the one with the most direct relevance to the Truth project. If the HB is elite literature from the Persian period, then Smith's use of Genesis-Judges as evidence for pre-exilic polytheism is methodologically indefensible. The texts may describe polytheism — but they're descriptions by monotheists of what they IMAGINE their polytheistic past looked like. That's a radically different kind of evidence.
