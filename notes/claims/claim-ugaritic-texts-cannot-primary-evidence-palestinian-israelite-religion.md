# keel-ggi-1.7: Using Ugaritic or other northern Syrian texts to interpret Palestinian/Israelite religion is methodologically unsound

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Ch I — Starting Point and Points of Departure]] — Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Chapter I

## The Claim

"Conclusions drawn from an interpretation of Bronze Age texts discovered in northern Syria... cannot be used uncritically to explain the religious history of Canaan during the second millennium and... certainly not to clarify what happened in Israel during the first millennium." Ugarit "lies far outside the borders of the province of Canaan."

## Evidence

- Ugarit is geographically and politically distinct from Canaan proper
- The time gap: Ugaritic texts are Late Bronze (14th-13th c.), while Israelite developments are Iron Age (10th-6th c.)
- The proper method: "compare initially with contemporary evidence from Canaan. When there is a positive correlation... only then can we take the second step"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** They're right about the geographic and chronological problems. But this is also self-serving — it privileges THEIR dataset over the Ugaritic textual corpus that Smith, Cross, and others built their careers on.

## Stakes

If accepted, this would fundamentally challenge Smith's and Cross's methodological foundations.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Essentially everyone in the Ugaritic-studies-to-Hebrew-Bible pipeline: Smith, Cross, Day, Clifford, Miller.

## Assessment

Their position is more nuanced than it appears. They explicitly "do not deny the relevance" of texts from Ebla, Mari, Ugarit. Their objection is to using these texts WITHOUT first establishing local Palestinian correlates. In practice, they DO use Ugaritic evidence — they just insist on diachronic and geographic controls.
