# tigay-nog-2.8: Indifference to nature gods implies implicit monotheism

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Conclusions

## The Claim

"A unilatry which ignores the gods of other nations can be classified as monolatry, but a unilatry which ignores phenomena on which all nations depend looks implicitly like monotheism." Israelites ignored sun gods, rain gods, fertility gods — deities whose spheres weren't limited to specific nations. Even Ammonites included moon/sun gods in their onomasticon. Israelites didn't. This pattern of indifference to universal/nature deities suggests something stronger than mere national monolatry.

## Evidence

- Ammonites included moon-god and sun-god names in their onomasticon; Israelites did not (despite sharing the same environment and dependence on the same natural phenomena)
- Sun gods, storm gods, fertility gods appear in the onomastica of virtually all neighboring cultures
- The Israelite onomasticon's exclusive focus on YHWH — ignoring deities whose spheres are universal rather than national — goes beyond what national monolatry would predict
- National monolatry says: "Our god is YHWH; YOUR god is Chemosh" (each nation has its god). Nature-god indifference says: "There is no sun god at all; YHWH handles the sun."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The absence of nature-god names is a real pattern, but the inference from absence to implicit monotheism is inferential — absence could have other explanations (e.g., YHWH absorbed nature-god functions, making separate nature-god names redundant).

## Stakes

This is Tigay's most ambitious theoretical claim. He's arguing that Israelite religion wasn't just monolatrous (worshipping one god while acknowledging others exist) but IMPLICITLY monotheistic (functionally treating YHWH as the only god, even without an explicit philosophical denial of other gods' existence). The distinction between explicit and implicit monotheism is subtle but important — it allows for a form of monotheism that predates Second Isaiah's explicit declarations.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-tigay-onomastics-underestimates-pre-exilic-polytheism]] (Day), consensus critical scholarship

**Alternative reading:** YHWH absorbed the functions of nature gods (Baal as storm god, Shemesh as sun god) — so nature-god names disappeared not because Israelites were implicitly monotheistic but because YHWH had functionally REPLACED those gods. The absence reflects syncretistic absorption, not monotheistic exclusion. Additionally, Smith's solarization thesis (YHWH absorbed solar imagery) and Day's Baal-YHWH competition model both explain the pattern without invoking monotheism.
