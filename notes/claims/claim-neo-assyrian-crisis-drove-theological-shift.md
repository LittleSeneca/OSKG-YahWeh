# smith-obm-2.5: The neo-Assyrian empire's disruption of the old world order catalyzed a new theology

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch2 — The Divine Council]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"Other gods in their nations represented no threat to Israel and its patron god as long as they were not imported into Israel. As long as other gods did not affect worship of Yahweh in Israel, they could be tolerated as the gods of other peoples and nations. This state of affairs perhaps began to change in the eighth century when the neo-Assyrian empire presented a new world order."

## Evidence

- Pre-8th century: Deut 32:8-9 theology — each nation has its own divine son as patron. Israel's patron is Yahweh. The system works as long as boundaries are respected.
- 8th century: neo-Assyrian empire disrupts. Political boundaries collapse. "Only after this alteration of the world scene did Israel require a different 'world theology' that not only advanced Yahweh to the top but eventually eliminated the second tier altogether insofar as it treated all other gods as either non-entities or expressions of Yahweh's power."
- By the 8th century, El-Yahweh identification had occurred (since Asherah had transitioned from El's consort to Yahweh's consort by the time of the Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions).
- Texts like Isaiah 6 and 1 Kings 22:19 show Yahweh presiding over the divine council by this period.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The correlation (theological shift + imperial crisis) is plausible historically, but the causal mechanism is speculative.

## Stakes

If monotheism is partly a RESPONSE to imperial crisis, then it's not a natural theological evolution but a specific historical reaction. This means monotheism is contingent — under different political circumstances, Israelite religion might have remained polytheistic. It also means monotheism is, at its origins, a theology of RESISTANCE and HOPE, not triumphalism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who date monotheistic developments earlier (pre-8th century) or later (post-exilic). Those who see internal theological logic (not external political pressure) as the driver. Römer emphasizes the Assyrian crisis similarly; Albertz emphasizes the exilic crisis more.

**Alternative reading:** The shift from "each nation has its god" to "only Yahweh is God" might be driven by internal prophetic movements (Elijah, Hosea) rather than imperial politics. The neo-Assyrian correlation might be coincidental — the prophetic critique of other gods predates the Assyrian crisis.

## Assessment

The correlation is real but the causation is underdetermined. Smith needs this claim to bridge the gap between "Israel had a pantheon" (Ch 2-4) and "monotheism emerged as rhetoric" (Ch 8-10). The neo-Assyrian crisis provides a plausible trigger, but Smith doesn't demonstrate mechanism — he asserts correlation. This will need the detailed historical argument of chapters 8-10 to bear weight.
