# schmid-ht-1.1: Traditional OT theology is impossible, but replacing it with religious history is also inadequate

**Source:** [[Schmid — §1 — Is There a Theology of the Hebrew Bible]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §1

## The Claim

The traditional project of OT theology "arose as compensation for the loss of the unity between Scripture and Christian doctrine. Today, such an approach has become impossible and inadvisable. The texts of the Hebrew Bible do not allow for a particular 'biblical' synthesis with regard to doctrine; they are too divergent both historically and conceptually." But "the premature calls...for both the dismissal of the discipline of 'Old Testament theology' as well as the suggestion to replace it with the 'religious history of ancient Israel' prove inadequate."

## Evidence

- The texts are historically and conceptually divergent — no single "biblical" synthesis is possible
- TWO reasons why theology remains necessary:
  1. The canon itself contains "different systemizations and organizations of material, which partially overlap and partially compete with one another. These organizations and systemizations put the theological designs of the biblical authors and redactors on display."
  2. The literary nature of the HB as Fortschreibung (updating) literature — "texts and the books of the Hebrew Bible only reached their current forms over a period of time through inner-biblical commentary and expansion" — constitutes "a unique body of literature that genuinely and prominently includes elements of theological reflection"
- Biblical texts themselves acknowledge being updating literature (Jer 36:32) and theologically reflective literature (Deut 1:5)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is Schmid's core methodological insight and it's well-grounded. The two observations — canon as organized theological statement, and Fortschreibung as inherently theological — are empirical. They don't depend on any particular dating scheme.

## Stakes

If Schmid is right that theology is necessary because the canon IS a theological construct, then purely historical or archaeological approaches are insufficient. Conversely, if Schmid overstates the theological intentionality of the canonical shape, his whole project overreaches. This claim is the justification for the entire book.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The "religious history" camp (broadly the history-of-religions school) would say that describing Israelite religion historically IS sufficient.

**Alternative reading:** One could accept Schmid's two observations but argue they justify literary/redactional history, not theology. The "theological designs" of redactors are still historical data — "theology" might claim more than the evidence warrants.

## Assessment

This is the strongest foundation for a critical theology I've seen. Schmid doesn't claim the texts reveal God — he claims they reveal theological thinking by ancient scribes. That's falsifiable, historical, and genuinely interesting. It's also the move that lets him escape the Barthian trap (theology must be normative) AND the historicist trap (theology is just religion).
