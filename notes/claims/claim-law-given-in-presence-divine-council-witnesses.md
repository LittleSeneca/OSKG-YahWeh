# heiser-ur-21.1: The Law was given at Sinai in the presence of the divine council — they were witnesses, not merely a private revelation

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 5 — Conquest and Failure]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 21

## The Claim

"The link between the law and the heavenly council is noted several times in the New Testament, which uses for the divine council the umbrella term 'angels.'" Acts 7:53 says the law was "delivered by angels." Hebrews 2:2 says it was "declared by angels." This is not a New Testament innovation — it comes from the Old Testament, specifically the Septuagint version of Deuteronomy 33. The law was delivered in a cosmic assembly, witnessed by divine beings, and administered by them.

## Evidence

**The Septuagint's reading of Deut 33:1-4:**
- The Masoretic Text has Yahweh coming from Sinai alone
- The Septuagint has "angels" (divine beings) accompanying him
- The LXX was the Bible of the early church — Acts and Hebrews draw on it
- "There are passages that describe angels at Sinai, but none of them specifically reference the law" — unless you're reading the Septuagint

**Psalm 68:15-18 as additional evidence:**
- "The chariots of God are twice ten thousand, with thousands doubled. The Lord is among them at Sinai, distinctive in victory."
- Tens of thousands of divine chariots. Yahweh among them. At Sinai. This is the setting of the law-giving — a cosmic assembly, not a private mountain meeting.

**The theological implication:**
- "The link between the law and the heavenly council becomes a key part of Paul's theology of the law in Galatians 3:19 ('ordained through angels')."
- The law was mediated by divine beings. It was never meant to be the final word — it was a temporary arrangement administered by the council, later superseded by Christ.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH that the LXX reads divine beings at Deut 33. HIGH that Acts and Hebrews draw on this tradition. MEDIUM on Heiser's specific claim that this was "a key part of Paul's theology" (Paul mentions it once in Galatians).

## Stakes

The Law — the Torah, the foundation of Judaism — was not delivered directly by God in private to Moses. It was delivered in a cosmic assembly, witnessed by divine beings, and administered by them. This demystifies the Law while simultaneously embedding it in a richer supernatural framework.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional readers who see Sinai as a private meeting between God and Moses. Scholars who reject the Septuagint reading of Deut 33 as secondary.

**Alternative reading:** The MT reading is original — Yahweh came alone. The LXX added "angels" under Hellenistic influence. The New Testament authors, reading the LXX, incorporated this secondary tradition.

## Assessment

Heiser's LXX argument is strong — the Septuagint was the Bible of the early church, and if it reads divine beings at Sinai, then Acts and Hebrews are drawing on an authentic textual tradition, not inventing theology. The Ps 68:15-18 parallel strengthens the case independently of the LXX. The Galatians 3:19 connection is weaker — Paul mentions it once, and building a "key part of Paul's theology" on a single verse is a stretch.
