# lewis-ocg-6.5: The Midianite/Kenite hypothesis has genuine strengths but the biblical texts don't unambiguously support it

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 6 — Origin of Yahweh]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 6

## The Claim

The hypothesis "asserts that Moses first learned of Yahweh through contact with his father-in-law Jethro, who was a priest in Midian." But "nowhere is there any explicit reference to Jethro being 'a priest of Yahweh' even in the three explicit references to his priestly office." A straightforward reading of Exodus 18:8 suggests "Moses, as the evangelist, helped bring about Jethro's conversion to Yahwism." J's "Reuel tradition" has Moses' father-in-law bearing an El name — suggesting he worshipped El, not Yahweh.

## Evidence

Exodus 18:11's recognition formula (ʿattâ — "now, as a result") implies Jethro learned about Yahweh FROM Moses, not the reverse. The name Reuel means "friend of El." The E tradition (Exod 3:16) has Yahweh identifying himself as "the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob" — which would be strange if the real origin of Yahwism was Midianite. Moses' mother's name Yochebed contains a Yahwistic element. Yet the archaic poetry (Judg 5, Deut 33, Hab 3) consistently places Yahweh in southern locales, and Jethro does offer sacrifices to Yahweh in Exodus 18:12, which "can hardly be the action of a new initiate."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The strongest evidence is the geographic connection between Yahweh and southern locales, not the Jethro narrative; the biblical texts are genuinely ambiguous.

## Stakes

Where Yahweh came from. If the Midianite hypothesis is right, Yahweh is a foreign import — the god of a neighboring people adopted by Israel. If wrong, we have no good explanation for the southern poetry.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Cross (reformulated version via Reuben), Schloen (caravan hypothesis), van der Toorn (Kenites introduced Yahweh to Saul). Against: Meek (1950) and those who see Moses as the innovator. Kaufmann rejects the hypothesis entirely ([[claim-kenite-midianite-hypothesis-baseless]]).

**Alternative reading:** Both things could be true — Midianite clans worshipped a deity called Yahweh, but Moses (an Israelite by birth, per J) reformulated the cult around liberation from Egypt, creating something genuinely new. The tradition preserves both the foreign origin (southern poetry) and the Mosaic innovation (Exod 3, 6).

## Assessment

Lewis's treatment is masterfully balanced. He presents the evidence for and against without forcing a conclusion. The most honest position is the one he takes: we don't know, but the southern connection is the strongest lead.
