# day-ygc-6.3: Samson is a solar hero behind the Israelite legend

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 6 — Yahweh and the Astral Deities]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 6

## The Claim

"Although certainty is not possible, it seems likely that behind the figure of the judge Samson there lies ultimately a solar hero" (p. 162). "Samson is more than a solar hero and this latter aspect has been overlaid with Israelite features" (p. 163).

## Evidence

1. **Name:** *Šimšôn* = "sunny" (from *šemeš*). The only Hebrew name of this type in the OT
2. **Geography:** Samson's birthplace (Zorah) is 2.5 miles from Beth-Shemesh ("house of the sun"). All locations in the Samson cycle (Eshtaol, Timnah, valley of Sorek) are within 6 miles of Beth-Shemesh (p. 163)
3. **Ps 19:5-6:** The sun "comes forth like a bridegroom... like a strong man (*gibbôr*) runs its course with joy." Samson IS the frolicking strong man (*gibbôr*) of the OT — "a motif which pervades the stories about him" (p. 163)
4. **Seven locks of hair = sun's rays:** Delilah (*delîlâ*) sounds like *laylâ* "night." Helios is depicted with seven rays. The sun's rays are often depicted as hair (p. 163)
5. **Masculine sun deity:** At Ugarit the sun deity Shapash is feminine, but Samson presupposes a masculine sun — consistent with Hebrew *šemeš* being more frequently masculine (p. 164)

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** Day appropriately hedges: "a reasonable case for this can be made" but it's not certain. The name + geography + strong man motif are suggestive but not probative.

## Stakes

If Samson was originally a solar hero, the biblical narrative has demythologized a Canaanite sun myth into an Israelite judge story — the same pattern Day traces for Baal → Hosea → Daniel resurrection.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those who reject mythological readings of the Judges narratives generally. The solar-hero theory was more popular in older scholarship but Day notes it's still found in Martin, Soggin, and Lindars.

**Alternative reading:** Samson is a historical or legendary Israelite figure with no solar-mythological substrate. The name "sunny" and geographic proximity to Beth-Shemesh are coincidence.

## Assessment

This is the weakest claim in the chapter, and Day knows it. The evidence is suggestive but thin. The name *Šimšôn* is genuinely striking, and the Beth-Shemesh proximity is notable, but the seven-locks/Delilah wordplay argument is the kind of cleverness that can create patterns where none exist. Day's caution is appropriate.
