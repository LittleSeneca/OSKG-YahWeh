# sommer-bog-3.5: Genesis 1:26 as P's anti-fluid manifesto

**Source:** [[Sommer — Chapter 3 — Rejection of Fluidity]] — Benjamin Sommer, *The Bodies of God* (2009), Chapter 3

## The Claim

Genesis 1:26 ("Let us make humanity in our form [*ṣelem*], according to our shape [*dĕmût*]") is P's anti-fluid manifesto. P grants that humans share God's "form" and "shape" — but this is a THEOLOGICAL CONCESSION. Humans have God's shape but NOT God's substance. God's body is made of light/energy, not matter. Humans are images of the divine form — but the form itself is transcendent.

## Evidence

**The verse:** "Let us make humanity in our form (*ṣelem*), according to our shape (*dĕmût*)"

**Sommer's reading:** P grants that humans share God's "form" and "shape" — but this is a THEOLOGICAL CONCESSION. Humans have God's shape but NOT God's substance. God's body is made of light/energy, not matter. Humans are images of the divine form — but the form itself is transcendent.

**The word *ṣelem* in context:** In the ANE, kings were called the *ṣelem* of a god because they represented divine rule on earth. P democratizes this: ALL humans, not just kings, are God's image. But the image is a distant representation — not an embodiment.

**P's consistent theology:** P's God has form and shape — this is clear from the text. But P's God does not walk on earth, eat with humans, or appear in multiple locations. The *ṣelem* theology says: humans have God's shape, but God's SUBSTANCE is entirely other. This is how P preserves divine corporeality (God has a form) while rejecting fluidity (God has only one form, in one place — heaven).

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Sommer's reading of *ṣelem* as form/shape is philologically sound; the anti-fluid framing depends on seeing Gen 1:26 in the context of P's broader *kavod* theology.

## Stakes

Genesis 1:26 is one of the most theologically freighted verses in the Bible. Traditional interpretation reads "image of God" as a spiritual or rational capacity. Sommer's reading recovers the physical meaning: humans literally look like God. But P immediately qualifies this: looking like God doesn't mean being made of the same stuff. The *ṣelem* is form without substance — P's theological compromise.

## Disagreement

**Alternative reading:** *Ṣelem* and *dĕmût* are metaphors for spiritual/rational capacities, not physical form. P is NOT acknowledging divine corporeality but deploying ANE royal ideology metaphorically.

## Assessment

This is Sommer's most creative individual exegesis. The *ṣelem* as physical form has strong philological support — it's the primary meaning of the word. But the anti-fluid framing is Sommer's own. Most scholars read Gen 1:26 as P's democratization of royal ideology, not as an anti-fluid manifesto. The reading depends on the full Sommer system: if J/E had fluid corporeality and P rejected fluidity, then Gen 1:26 is where P stakes out its compromise position — yes God has form, no you can't access it.
