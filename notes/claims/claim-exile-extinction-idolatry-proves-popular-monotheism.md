# kaufmann-ri-4.3: Exile extinction of idolatry proves popular monotheism

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch IV — The Religion of the People]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter IV

## The Claim

"The history of religions knows no parallel to such a phenomenon." When other nations lost political independence, their gods were demoted but not abandoned. Israel's idolatry vanished completely — and this "has only one adequate explanation: pre-exilic Israel must have already had a monotheistic faith that was not the property of a narrow elite, but of the entire nation."

## Evidence

- National collapse normally strengthens pagan attachment — a pagan people would interpret exile as their god's defeat by stronger gods and "embrace these foreign gods with doubled intensity"
- The Jerusalem women who worshipped the Queen of Heaven explicitly offered this alternative: Judah's calamity resulted from ABANDONING her cult (Jer 44:17-18) — proving a pagan interpretation was available
- Yet "the overwhelming bulk of the people 'returned' to their God"
- "Even the priests, the nobility, and the royal household... forsake their idols and cleave to YHWH forever"
- Royal reformers (Asa, Jehu, Jehoash, Hezekiah, Josiah) ALL FAILED — "Only in exile, amidst pagan surroundings, without external compulsion, with scarcely a struggle, out of an inner transformation alone, the Jewish people abandon idolatry forever!"
- Later Judaism expressed astonishment at this in the legend of the Men of the Great Synagogue capturing "the Evil Yeser of idolatry" (b. Yoma 69b; Sanhedrin 64a)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The disappearance of Israelite idolatry after the exile IS a genuine historical anomaly. Evolutionary models struggle to explain it. But Kaufmann's inference of POPULAR monotheism goes further than necessary.

## Stakes

Whether Israelite monotheism was an elite achievement gradually imposed or a popular possession suddenly purified. If Kaufmann is right, the exile didn't CREATE monotheism — it merely stripped away vestigial idolatry.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-true-monotheism-emerged-babylonian-exile]] — the critical consensus sees the exile as the crucible where monotheism was FORGED. The Queen of Heaven worshippers (Jer 44) show polytheistic practice persisted even in Egypt. Elephantine community complicates further. [[claim-fall-samaria-catalyst-monotheism]] traces the Assyrian crisis as trigger.

**Alternative reading:** Polytheism was tied to LAND and TEMPLE. Once destroyed, the infrastructure of polytheistic worship (local shrines, high places, territorial deities) was destroyed. Surviving religion was necessarily portable and aniconic — which meant monotheistic YHWH worship. The phenomenon reflects selective survival of a portable religion, not pre-existing popularity. Moreover, those who assimilated and adopted Babylonian gods wouldn't appear in the biblical record — the returning community was self-selected for YHWH-devotion.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's most powerful historical argument. He's identified a genuine anomaly: why did Israel's idolatry die so completely, so quickly, without state enforcement? The critical answer — "the exile produced monotheism" — doesn't explain why the exiles drew THAT conclusion rather than the obvious pagan one (Marduk beat YHWH). But Kaufmann's inference of POPULAR monotheism goes further than the evidence requires. An alternative: the monotheistic interpretation captured the surviving institutions — not "the people were monotheists" but "the monotheistic interpretation won."
