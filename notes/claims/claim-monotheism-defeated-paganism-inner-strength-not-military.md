# kaufmann-ri-ep.5: Monotheism defeated paganism through inner strength, not military power

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Epilogue and Prospect]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Epilogue

## The Claim

"Since the fall of paganism did not come about through a political or military collapse, this triumph of Israelite religion can only have been the product of its inner strength, the strength of a new religious idea. Its only weapons can have been ideological: propaganda, preaching, and missionaries."

## Evidence

- Paganism fell throughout the Roman world without being militarily conquered
- Judaism's missionary activity in the Hellenistic period produced "myriads of converts and 'fearers of God'"
- "An ideological battle rages between Judaism and paganism"
- Four necessary preconditions: (1) elimination of idolatry from within Israel, (2) acceptance of the prophetic idea that idolatry is sinful for nations, (3) cultic forms independent of land (synagogue), (4) religious proselytism instead of ethnic amalgamation

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's picture of active Jewish missionary activity in the Hellenistic period has been challenged by more recent scholarship (some argue Judaism was not actively missionary). But the broader point about ideological rather than military victory is sound.

## Stakes

The nature of Judaism's contribution to world history. Kaufmann presents it as an active, missionary faith that deliberately set out to conquer paganism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who question whether Second Temple Judaism was actively missionary. Martin Goodman's *Mission and Conversion* argues Judaism was not a missionary religion in the way Christianity later became.

**Alternative reading:** Judaism's influence spread through diaspora communities, not active proselytism. Converts came to Judaism; Judaism didn't go to them. The "mission" was passive, cultural, demographic — not organized missionary activity.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's triumphalist tone reflects his historical context (writing after the Holocaust, asserting Judaism's world-historical significance). The actual extent of Jewish missionary activity is debated. But the core observation — that monotheism spread through ideological rather than military means — is obviously correct.
