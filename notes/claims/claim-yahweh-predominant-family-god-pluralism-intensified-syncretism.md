# albertz-hir-3.7.1: Yahweh Became Predominant Family God While Religious Pluralism Intensified

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §3.7 — Family Piety Under the Late Monarchy]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §3.7

## The Claim

"On the one hand the national god Yahweh increasingly naturally took the place of the family god" — Yahweh-name proportion rose from ~25% (early monarchy) to ~80% (late monarchy). "But how little this development in family religion can be interpreted as a sign of an increasingly exclusivist worship of Yahweh is shown... by the fact that internal religious pluralism not only continued in the seventh century but even intensified." Jeremiah (2:27, 28a) accused people of turning to "wood and stone" in private distress while appealing to Yahweh only in national catastrophes. "Internal religious pluralism had even taken on syncretistic dimensions."

## Evidence

- Seals and inscriptions confirm the 80% Yahweh-name pattern (aligns with Tigay's onomastic data)
- Royal family pioneered Yahweh-names from Solomon onward, but Maacah still had an Asherah image (1 Kings 15:13) as late as the 9th century
- Assyrian deportation policy in the North created a genuinely pluralistic religious environment — remaining Israelite families adopted neighbor gods into their personal piety

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The onomastic data is solid. The "pluralism despite Yahwization of names" paradox is a key finding that supports Albertz's model.

## Stakes

This is exactly what Albertz's model predicts (different strata, different logics) and what confuses models that treat "Yahwization of names" as evidence of monotheization. The onomastic data shows Yahweh becoming the default family god; the polemical texts show families worshipping other deities alongside him. Both are true. The name data and the practice data measure different things.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Tigay reads the onomastic evidence as proof of popular monotheism ([[claim-family-piety-uninfluenced-official-yahweh-religion]]). Kaufmann reads the polemical evidence as describing vestigial fetishism, not genuine syncretism ([[claim-popular-cult-independent-non-pagan-outside-silent-temple]]).

**Alternative reading:** The text-evidence of syncretism may be exaggerated — prophetic polemic is not neutral reporting. The 80% Yahweh-name proportion could indicate genuine popular Yahwism, with the polemical texts attacking residual practices that were marginal, not mainstream.

## Assessment

The strongest insight of §3.7. The paradox that Yahweh-name dominance and religious pluralism increased simultaneously is exactly what Albertz's model predicts and what confuses models that treat "Yahwization of names" as evidence of monotheization. The key is Albertz's distinction between strata: the family stratum adopted Yahweh as its default deity while simultaneously absorbing Assyrian practices. "Yahweh" on a seal doesn't mean "Yahweh alone" in the household.
