# romer-inv-3.2: The Midianites were a real historical people in northwestern Arabia who may have mediated Yhwh worship to Israel

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 3 — Moses and the Midianites]] — Thomas Romer, *The Invention of God* (2014), Chapter 3

## The Claim

The Midianites were "nomadic peasants" who combined agriculture, stock raising, and camel domestication. They lived in a confederation in northwestern Arabia/southern Transjordan. They produced distinctive pottery (13th-first centuries BCE), were involved in trade along major caravan routes, and possibly participated in copper and gold mining at Timna. They are the most plausible bridge between the southern Yhwh (Chapter 2) and Israel.

## Evidence

**Geographic location:**
- 1 Kings 11 places Midian south of Edom, on the route from Edom to Egypt through Wadi Feran
- Greco-Roman and Arab geographers knew a town "Midama/Madyan" east of the Gulf of Aqaba, identified with al-Bad in Wadi 'Afal
- Two major commercial routes traversed the region: a coastal route from Aqaba through Wadi 'Afal (archaeological remains from 13th century onward) and an inland route through the Hisma

**Archaeological evidence:**
- Distinctive "Midianite pottery" dated to 13th-11th centuries BCE, decorated with camels, human figures, and ostriches
- Found as far afield as the Levant — evidence of trade networks
- Wadi Sadr probably marks the southern frontier; Wadi Sarma and al-Qurayya were major population centers

**Economic activities:**
- Camel domestication (first domesticated in SE Arabia in 3rd millennium, spread to western Arabia by late 2nd millennium)
- Combined agriculture and stock raising
- Possibly involved in copper and gold mining at Timna under Egyptian control
- A "Midianite holy place" was discovered at the site of an Egyptian sanctuary to Hathor — "the later occupants of this site seem to have tried to erase the Egyptian presence"

**The connection to the Shasu:** "The Midianites, like the Shasu (among whom the Midianites were probably counted by the Egyptians), were perhaps also involved in mining." The Midianites may have been a subgroup of the broader Shasu phenomenon.

**The biblical connection — Jethro:**
- Moses marries Zipporah, daughter of Jethro, "priest of Midian" (Exodus 2-3)
- Jethro already worships Yhwh and offers sacrifice to him (Exodus 18)
- The narrative implies Yhwh was ALREADY known among the Midianites before Moses
- The Kenites (a Midianite subgroup) appear as metalworkers associated with Israel (Judges 1:16, 4:11)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The archaeological evidence for Midianites as a real historical group is solid (distinctive pottery, geographic fix). Their role in transmitting Yhwh worship to Israel is only attested in the biblical Jethro narrative, whose historical value is debated.

## Stakes

If the Midianites already worshipped Yhwh before Moses, then Moses didn't receive a revelation of a previously unknown God. He encountered a god his Midianite father-in-law already knew. Yhwh was not revealed TO Israel through Moses — Yhwh was brought TO Israel FROM Midian. "Revelation" becomes "cultural transmission."

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see the Jethro narrative as a late literary construct (Persian period) with no historical value. Scholars who argue Yhwh was indigenous to Canaan and the Midianite connection is fictional.

**Alternative reading:** The Jethro narrative is a later literary device to explain how a foreign god could become Israel's god — it solves a theological problem, not a historical one.

## Assessment

Romer's archaeological-geographic description of Midian is grounded in material evidence — the strongest section of the chapter. The camel-decorated pottery, the trade routes, the Timna mining context — these are concrete. The leap from "Midianites existed" to "Midianites transmitted Yhwh worship" is the weak link in the chain. Romer acknowledges this but the structure of his argument depends on it.
