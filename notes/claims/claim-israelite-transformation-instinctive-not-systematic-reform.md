# kaufmann-ri-3.10: Israelite transformation was instinctive, not systematic reform

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

"Precisely because it never received a dogmatic formulation which could serve as a standard for the systematic reformation of the old religion, it was unable entirely to eradicate all traces of the pagan heritage." The transformation was "guided by instinct and intuition, not by speculation and systematic formulation." The idea "expressed itself rather in symbols" and "working intuitively, it radically transformed the ancient mythological conceptions of Israel."

## Evidence

- Biblical religion has no systematic theology — no creed, no abstract formulation of monotheism
- The Bible preserves pagan fragments (Rahab, Azazel, serpent, sons of God) that a systematic reform would have excised
- Biblical view of pagan magic/divination is "distorted" — Bible genuinely misunderstands that pagans connect magic with gods. If editors were systematically suppressing pagan theology, they would have polemicized against pagan gods, not ignored them
- Prophets freely use anthropomorphic language ("pleasing odor," "bread of God") that a systematic reformer would have purged

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** High on the observation that biblical religion is non-systematic. Medium on the inference that this proves early, instinctive monotheism rather than gradual development.

## Stakes

Kaufmann is preempting the critical argument that non-mythological features were introduced by late priestly editors. His counter: if it were systematic editing, it would be more thorough and consistent. The messiness of the Bible is evidence of ORGANIC, early, popular transformation, not LATE, elite, editorial one.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Critical scholars can accept the messiness while maintaining late dating. Ancient editing was often conservative, preserving traditions even when they sat uneasily with the editor's theology. The Pentateuch's combination of P (transcendent God) and J (anthropomorphic God) is evidence of conservative editing, not early monotheism.

**Alternative reading:** The non-systematic character reflects monotheism emerging GRADUALLY, with older materials reinterpreted rather than discarded. The "instinct" Kaufmann describes was real — but it was the instinct of a tradition in DEVELOPMENT. The messiness is evidence of evolution, not original monotheism.

## Assessment

Kaufmann makes a legitimate methodological point: if the non-pagan features are the result of late editorial systematization, why was the systematization so incomplete? A genuinely systematic editor would have removed the sons of God, the divine council, God trying to kill Moses. This IS a real problem for models attributing everything to editorial activity. But it's not a problem for gradual development through a living tradition that reinterpreted rather than excised older materials. Kaufmann's "instinct" and the critical "development" may describe the same process from different angles.
