# day-ygc-4.3: Mt Zaphon transferred to Yahweh's dwelling — heaven and Jerusalem

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 4 — Yahwehs Appropriation of Baal Imagery]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 4

## The Claim

The name Zaphon (*ṣāpôn*), originally Baal's mountain in Syria (Jebel el-Aqraʻ), was appropriated in the OT to refer both to "the firmament of heaven as the cosmic mountain on which Yahweh dwells" and to Mt Zion/Jerusalem (pp. 111-112). The transfer to Jerusalem was likely "mediated through the Jebusite cult of El-Elyon, rather than being directly taken over from Baal" (p. 117).

## Evidence

1. **Identification secure:** Ugaritic administrative tablets in Akkadian and Ugaritic "make certain the identification of Mt Hazi (Casius) and Mt ṣpn (Zaphon)" as Jebel el-Aqraʻ in Syria (pp. 108-109)
2. **Ps 48:3 (ET 2):** Jerusalem is "the heights of Zaphon" (*yarketê ṣāpôn*) — prior to Ugarit this made no sense (Jerusalem is not in the far north!). Now understood as applying Baal's sacred mountain name to Zion (pp. 109-110)
3. **Papyrus Amherst 63:** A paganized version of Psalm 20 replaces Zion with Zaphon — "Send your emissary from the temple of Arash, and from Zaphon may Horus sustain us" (lines 13-14) vs. Ps 20:3 "May he send you help from the sanctuary and from Zion may he sustain you" (p. 110)
4. **Isa 14:13:** The Shining One tries to ascend to "the mount of assembly on the heights of Zaphon" (*yarketê ṣāpôn*) — Zaphon as the cosmic mountain/firmament (pp. 110-111)
5. **Job 26:7:** "He stretches out Zaphon over the void" — Zaphon equated with the vault of heaven (p. 111)
6. **Ezek 1:4:** Storm theophany comes "out of Zaphon" — Yahweh's heavenly dwelling (p. 112)
7. **Exod 15:17:** "The mountain of your inheritance... the place for your dwelling" uses "Zaphonic" language derived from descriptions of Baal's dwelling at Ugarit — most naturally refers to Jerusalem (pp. 115-116)

**Against reading Zaphon as simply "north":** The word came to mean "north" in Hebrew BECAUSE the mountain was north of Israel. But context in Ps 48, Isa 14, and Job 26 demands the mountain meaning.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Papyrus Amherst 63 evidence (Zion→Zaphon substitution) is a remarkable confirmation. The pre-Ugaritic translations that rendered Ps 48:3 as "far north" demonstrate the philological problem the discovery solved.

## Stakes

Jerusalem's sacred geography is built on Baal's. Mt Zion inherited the mythological status of Mt Zaphon — the cosmic mountain where deity dwells, where the forces of chaos attack, and where the divine king reigns victorious. The Jerusalem temple ideology is Canaanite at its foundations.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Eissfeldt and others read Ps 89:13 (ET 12) as naming four sacred mountains including Zaphon. Day argues *ṣāpôn* and *yāmîn* more naturally mean "north and south" here (p. 113), with Tabor and Hermon as the center.

**Alternative reading:** Zaphon in Ps 48:3 means simply "the far north" — a geographical description, not a mythological transfer. The psalm describes Jerusalem's beauty using conventional directional language.

## Assessment

The Zaphon → Zion transfer is one of Day's most elegant arguments. The combination of Ugaritic geography, Papyrus Amherst 63, and the Isa 14/Job 26 heavenly Zaphon is mutually reinforcing. The El-Elyon mediation theory fits neatly with Chapter 1's arguments. The counter-reading (Zaphon = simply "north") fails to explain why the psalmist would describe Jerusalem's sacred heights using a word meaning "far north" when Jerusalem is in the south — the mythological reading solves a problem the geographical reading creates.
