# smith-obm-1.2: Sacred mountains and cult sites are exclusive to benevolent deities — cosmic enemies lack them

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch1 — Anthropomorphic Deities and Divine Monsters]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 1

## The Claim

"Within the center or area of human cultivation and civilization, deities are accorded sacred mountains or cult sites, but cosmic enemies are not." Baal's Mount Sapan (*spn*) functions as the cosmic center point. "The pantheon as a whole is marked by the topography of the sacred mountain, for it meets on Mount ll."

## Evidence

- CAT 1.100 snake-bite incantation lists deities with their mountains/cult sites: El on mount *ks* (1.100.9), Baal on *spn* (1.100.9), Anat and Athtart on *'inbb* (1.100.20), Dagan at *ttl* (Tuttul, 1.100.15), Resheph at *bbt* (1.100.31), Athtart at *mr* (Mari, 1.100.78), Mlk at *'ttrt* (Ashtarot, 1.100.41), Yarih at *lrgt* (1.100.26), Zz and KMT at *hryth* (1.100.36).
- Baal's mountain as center point: described as Baal's palace site in the Baal Cycle, heading of one deity list as "the gods of Sapan" (*'il spn* in 1.47.1), superscription of a ritual text as "the feast of Sapan" (*dbh spn* in 1.148.1; 1.91.3).
- Baal's mountain called "pleasant place" (*n'm*) — garden language that, in biblical texts, is a recurring motif for the center point of the cosmos (Genesis 2-3, Ezekiel 28).
- "Yamm, Mot, tnn, and the other cosmic enemies do not have holy mountains."
- Exceptions prove the rule: Mot is associated with mountain *knkny* (1.5 V 13), but it is the entrance to the underworld, not a dwelling on top. El's home at "the channels of the Double-Deeps" (*'apq thmtm*, 1.4 IV 21-22) lies at the edge between near and far. Kothar wa-Hasis has no mountain — he dwells in Memphis and Caphtor (1.100.46), reflecting foreign trade centers.
- The pantheon meets on Mount *ll* (1.2 I 20).

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The CAT 1.100 evidence is explicit and systematic. The exceptions are genuinely instructive rather than undermining.

## Stakes

This claim establishes that spatial location and cultic access are definitional for what counts as a "deity" vs. a "monster" in Ugaritic thought. It also sets up the later argument that Israel's denial of cult sites to cosmic enemies is an intensification of an existing Ugaritic pattern.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Unlikely to attract direct disagreement — this is primarily descriptive. Scholars might quibble about whether Athirat has no mountain or whether Kothar's foreign dwelling counts as a "mountain."

**Alternative reading:** The mountain listings in 1.100 are formulaic and may reflect ritual tradition more than cosmological theory. The absence of mountains for cosmic enemies could be a literary convention of the Baal Cycle's narrative world.
