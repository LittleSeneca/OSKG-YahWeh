# kaufmann-ri-7.4: Israelite Cult and Sanctuaries Were Indigenous, Not Canaanite Borrowings

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch VII — The Conquest and Settlement]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch VII

## The Claim

Despite the proliferation of sanctuaries, high places, pillars, and trees after settlement, "none of the prophets ever reproach Israel with having worshiped YHWH in pagan temples." The sanctity of Israelite cult sites doesn't derive from Canaanite tradition — it originates in patriarchal revelations. Temple legends never antedate the patriarchs; "not one Israelite temple has a history antedating the patriarchs." Agricultural elements in the cult "betray no Canaanite influence" — YHWH blesses produce as universal God, not as a Baal whose life processes are bound to the soil.

## Evidence

- **Josh 22:9-34 reflects the transitional sanctity crisis:** While the ideal boundary is the Jordan, and only Canaan is "the land of YHWH" where "the tabernacle of YHWH stands," the restriction of sacrifice to the tabernacle altar became obsolete once Israel rooted in the land. The land's sanctity "overshadowed that of the tent," and sanctuaries arose everywhere.

- **No takeover of Canaanite temples:** "The particulars of the conquest speak against this notion." The Josephides slaughtered Bethel's entire population (Judg 1:24ff) — "here, then, there was no possibility of continuing the local cultic tradition." The Danites burned Laish and built new (Judg 18:27-28). Jerusalem's temple is on Araunah's threshing floor with a new altar (2 Sam 24:24f).

- **Temple legends differ from pagan pattern:** Pagan myth links sacred sites to gods' lives — "gods built and lived in the temples; in some cases the temples housed their graves." Israelite temple legends "date the origin of the sanctity of the sites to late times." Patriarchs erect altars and pillars but "do not build temples." Local sanctity "has no autochthonous roots."

- **Bethel analyzed:** The ladder connecting heaven and earth is a familiar pagan motif, but "while the pagan idea represents the ladder as a link between men and the gods and a means to apotheosis, this ladder serves the angels and agents of YHWH."

- **Agricultural cult not Baalized:** YHWH as fertility source "does not make him a Canaanite Baal." Pagan Baal's life processes are bound to the soil; his festivals "are designed to revive and reinvigorate him." Such motifs are "entirely absent" from Judges-era materials. "Nor is there a hint of a struggle against such ideas and rituals" — they simply aren't there.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann is right about how the biblical texts *narrate* the origins of sanctuary sanctity — it's always through patriarchal theophany, never through primordial divine presence. This IS a genuine difference from pagan temple legends. But he systematically understates the material continuity. Standing stones, sacred trees, high places — these were the standard cultic furniture of the region, and Israelites used them. The question is whether using the same furniture means worshipping the same way.

## Stakes

Whether Israelite religion was fundamentally transformed by Canaanite agricultural religion (the "Canaanization" thesis) or maintained its distinctive character even as it adopted agricultural forms.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The archaeological and textual evidence for cultic continuity is substantial. Standing stones (*maṣṣebot*), sacred trees (*asherim*), and high places (*bamot*) were standard Canaanite cultic furniture before they were Israelite. Lewis (Ch 4) documents El worship at sites that later became Israelite sanctuaries. The Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions pair "YHWH and his Asherah" — exactly the kind of consort theology Kaufmann claims is absent. The prophets' constant polemic against high places, pillars, and *asherim* suggests these were indigenous Israelite practices the prophets opposed, not foreign imports.

**Alternative reading:** The Israelite cult DID borrow Canaanite forms (sanctuary types, cult objects, festival patterns) but interpreted them within a YHWH-only theological framework. The "absence of struggle" against Baalized agricultural motifs that Kaufmann notes is an argument FROM SILENCE — by the time we have substantial texts (8th century prophets), the struggle against Baalization of YHWH cult IS exactly what we find (Hosea in particular).

## Assessment

Kaufmann is right about how the biblical texts *narrate* the origins of sanctuary sanctity — it's always through patriarchal theophany, never through primordial divine presence. This IS a genuine difference from pagan temple legends. But he systematically understates the material continuity. Standing stones, sacred trees, high places — these were the standard cultic furniture of the region, and Israelites used them. The truth is probably in between: forms were borrowed but gradually reinterpreted, with the reinterpretation being uneven and contested.
