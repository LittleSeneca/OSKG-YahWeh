# schmid-ht-preface.4: Schmid's project is a "Hebrew Bible undertaking" — neither Christian nor Jewish

**Source:** [[Schmid — Preface]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), Preface

## The Claim

"In this respect it is neither a Christian nor a Jewish, but rather a Hebrew Bible undertaking. It is not a project immediately related to present-day application; it instead has an initially historical focus." Schmid frames his approach as "elevating the textually-indicated theological profile of the canon as a whole, divisions of canon, books, texts, and themes."

## Evidence

- He acknowledges that Jewish and Christian canons differ and each "strictly speaking requires its own theology"
- He frames his approach as working with the textual profile of the canon rather than with confessional commitments
- From a Protestant perspective: "the authority of Scripture does not come in the form of imperatives, but that imperatives come through the mediation of encultured propositions of thought that have proven increasingly normative on the basis of the weight of their content"

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** The claim to be "neither Christian nor Jewish" is ambitious but question-begging. The selection of the Hebrew Bible as an object of study is itself a theological choice shaped by Christian and Jewish tradition. Can a scholar in the Christian theological tradition (Zurich is a Protestant faculty) truly produce a "non-Christian" reading?

## Stakes

This framing lets Schmid work with the Jewish Tanakh without claiming to do Jewish theology, and discuss Christian reception without being bound by Christian dogma. It's a strategic move that creates intellectual space. But it may be impossible to actually achieve.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Jewish scholars (Levenson, Sommer) have argued that the very category "Hebrew Bible" is a Christian construct that pretends to neutrality while masking Christian assumptions. Christian theologians would say theology of the OT IS Christian theology by definition.

**Alternative reading:** The claim to neutrality is a rhetorical stance, not a genuine methodological achievement. Schmid is doing Protestant historical-critical theology with a secular veneer. That's fine — but it's better to own it than to claim a neutrality that doesn't exist.

## Assessment

This is the weakest claim in the Preface. Schmid wants to have it both ways: the academic credibility of "neutral" historical study and the relevance of theological inquiry. The "neither Christian nor Jewish" framing is a noble aspiration but probably unachievable. His actual work will inevitably reflect his location in a Protestant theological faculty. That doesn't invalidate his conclusions, but the framing overstates the neutrality.
