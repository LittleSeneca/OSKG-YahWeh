# kaufmann-ri-11.3: Amos's rejection of the cult was not wholesale — it was a conditional rejection of cult divorced from morality

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch XI — Amos and Hosea]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter XI

## The Claim

Amos 5:25 ("Did you offer me sacrifices and oblations forty years in the desert?") is NOT a blanket rejection of sacrifice. The opinion "that Amos unconditionally rejected the cult, especially the cult of sacrifice" is "quite out of the question. A wholesale repudiation of the cult would have meant a rejection of tradition which grounded it on an express command of YHWH." Amos is attacking "the legitimate, national cult of YHWH" — not because cult is intrinsically wrong, but because "it is rejected in favor of morality; God desires righteousness, not sacrifice!" The cult "has no intrinsic value" — its value is conditional on the moral state of the worshipper.

## Evidence

- Amos calls foreign lands "impure" and regards giving wine to Nazirites a sin (2:11) — he has cultic categories
- His condemnation extends "beyond sacrifice, to song and psalm as well" (5:23) — it's not specifically anti-sacrifice
- Isaiah, Jeremiah, Second Isaiah, and Zechariah take comparable positions — and "most of whom express veneration of the Jerusalem temple... did not conceive of a religion without prayer, festival, and Sabbath"
- Amos 5:25 is "hyperbole": the point is that the wilderness period, when Israel couldn't make daily offerings, "was the time of favor and grace" — the contrast is between present abundant empty cult and past needy faithful relationship
- Kaufmann notes that to take 5:25 literally, one must assume Amos was "ignorant of JE" (Exod 3:12, 18; 12:21-27; 24:1-11; Deut 12:8) — unlikely for a prophet steeped in tradition

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Medium-high on the interpretation of Amos 5:25 as hyperbole; medium on the claim that Amos didn't reject cult "in principle." The reading is persuasive and relatively uncontroversial.

## Stakes

If Amos rejected the cult wholesale, he's a radical innovator breaking with the entire sacrificial system. If he rejected only cult-without-morality, he's continuous with Torah values that already prioritized obedience over sacrifice (1 Sam 15:22; Ps 51:16-17).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who read Amos as an anti-cultic prophet. But Kaufmann's reading is actually close to the mainstream on this point — most scholars don't think Amos wanted to abolish the cult, only to subordinate it to ethics. The real disagreement is about whether the Torah's integration of cult and ethics predates Amos (Kaufmann) or develops after him (critical view).

**Alternative reading:** Amos may not have a systematic "theology of cult" at all. He's a poet of doom, not a systematician. His rhetoric is designed to shock, not to establish a nuanced position on sacrifice. Reading 5:25 as hyperbole is reasonable, but the extremity of the rhetoric IS significant — it shows that cult could be imagined as dispensable in a way that morality couldn't.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's reading of Amos on cult is persuasive and relatively uncontroversial. The more interesting question is his framework for WHY the cult has only conditional value — his claim that Israelite religion, "essentially non-mythological and non-magical, could conceive of the relation of God and the world only in terms of free grace," so that "the absolute and autonomous value that paganism placed on the cult thereby vanished." This is Kaufmann's master thesis applied to Amos, and it's more theological than historical.
