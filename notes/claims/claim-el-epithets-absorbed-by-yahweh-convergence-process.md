# cross-cmhe-pre-i.3: El's epithets were absorbed by Yahweh through a convergence process

**Source:** [[Cross — Preface + Part I — Canaanite Religion and El]] — Frank Moore Cross, *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* (1973), Chapter 3

## The Claim

Yahweh was originally NOT identified with El. The identification happened historically — and is visible in the biblical texts. "We can trace the process by which Yahweh came to be identified with El, absorbing El's characteristics and epithets." The systematic application of El's Ugaritic titles to Yahweh in the Hebrew Bible is the primary textual evidence for the convergence of two originally distinct deities.

## Evidence

| El's Ugaritic Title | Hebrew Equivalent | Applied to Yahweh in |
|---------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| "Father of Years" (*'ab šnm*) | "Ancient of Days" | Daniel 7 |
| "Creator of Creatures" (*bny bnwt*) | "Creator of Heaven and Earth" | Genesis 14:19 |
| "The Compassionate One" (*lṭpn 'il dpid*) | "Compassionate and Gracious" | Exodus 34:6 |
| El's divine council | Yahweh's divine council | Psalm 82, 1 Kings 22 |
| El's mountain abode | Yahweh on Sinai/Zion | Exodus 19, Psalm 48 |
| El's epithet *šadday* | *'Ēl Šadday* | Patriarchal narratives |

- The mapping is systematic: six distinct Ugaritic El titles/attributes all find Hebrew equivalents applied to Yahweh.
- The divine council is the most structurally significant parallel — both El and Yahweh preside over a council of divine beings.
- The mountain abode transfer (from El's cosmic mountain to Yahweh's Sinai/Zion) is a spatial-theological transformation.
- The epithet 'Ēl Šadday in the patriarchal narratives preserves the memory of the pre-Yahwistic El worship stage.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The mapping of Ugaritic El epithets to Hebrew equivalents applied to Yahweh is extensive, systematic, and textually demonstrable regardless of one's position on the etymology of "Yahweh."

## Stakes

This is the strongest part of Cross's argument — stronger than his etymology claim (pre-i.4). Even scholars who reject Cross's "Yahweh as El-epithet" thesis accept that El's attributes were absorbed by Yahweh. The table provides a philological foundation for the entire convergence model.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Kaufmann would reject the claim of separate origins, but even Albright acknowledged the epithet transfer. Modern disagreement is mainly about TIMING (when did the transfer happen?) rather than WHETHER it happened.

**Alternative reading:** Some argue the shared epithets reflect common Northwest Semitic religious vocabulary rather than genetic transfer from El to Yahweh. Others (Tigay) argue El's name was already fading in the onomasticon by the monarchic period, so the transfer happened earlier than Cross thought.
