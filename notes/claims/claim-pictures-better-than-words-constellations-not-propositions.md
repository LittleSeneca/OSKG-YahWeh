# keel-ggi-10.1: Pictures are NOT more ambiguous than words — they are BETTER at capturing the complexity of religious concepts, which are constellations, not propositions

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Ch X — Summary and Conclusion]] — Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Chapter X

## The Claim

"People who have never concerned themselves with the unique possibilities of words and pictures usually think that pictures are vague and ambiguous, whereas words are precise and clear. It is easy to demonstrate that this judgment is rash." "When the issue concerns constellations and relationships, pictures are far more potent than words. So constellations of images depict the essence of numerous narrative structures that expand on their themes but can be reduced to the essential narrative once again."

## Evidence

- The passport photo analogy: a photograph captures physiognomic relationships (forehead-to-nose, nose-to-eyes) that words cannot describe with comparable precision
- Names are "artificial" and "rest upon conventions" that can change easily — "Criminals change their names like shirts"
- The Egyptian tree goddess appears with the same form and function across centuries but can be labeled with different names (Nut, Neith, Isis, Ma'at). The NAME tells you almost nothing; the ICONOGRAPHY tells you the function.
- "Yahweh" may have originally meant "he blows" (Knauf) but "no one would want to maintain that this etymology... expresses the dominant aspect of the God of Israel"
- "Asherah" — does the term mean a natural tree, an artificial tree, a pole, an anthropomorphic image, a naked goddess, a clothed enthroned lady, a heavenly personal being, or a mix? The name alone cannot answer; only iconography in context can.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is a philosophical argument, not an empirical one, but it is well-made. The point about names being arbitrary labels while iconography captures functional roles is fundamental to their entire methodology.

## Stakes

This is the theoretical justification for the entire book. If pictures are inherently more ambiguous than words, then the text-priority of biblical scholarship is justified. If pictures are actually BETTER at capturing the "constellation" of divine functions and relationships, then ignoring iconography is not just a gap — it is a systematic distortion.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Text-priority scholars who treat the Hebrew Bible as the primary (or only) source for Israelite religion. The entire guild of biblical theologians who work from texts alone.

**Alternative reading:** Pictures may be better at showing WHAT a deity looks like and what MOTIFS cluster around it, but texts are better at showing what people BELIEVED about the deity — theology requires propositions, not just images.

## Assessment

Their argument is strongest when applied to specific religio-historical questions (like "was Asherah a goddess or a symbol?") and weakest when extended to theology in general. But for their purposes — reconstructing the religious symbol system of ancient Palestine — the iconographic priority is justified.
