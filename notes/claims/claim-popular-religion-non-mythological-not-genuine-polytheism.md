# kaufmann-ri-3.8: Popular religion was also non-mythological

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

"We do find vestiges of Israel's ancient mythology imbedded in the Bible, and there was an 'idolatry' in Israel; yet the biblical God has no mythological features. It would seem that this reflects a level beyond, not before, mythology." The popular religion of biblical times "was non-mythological. The ancient myths disappeared from popular consciousness. Pagan influence was superficial and did not work deeply on folk creativity."

## Evidence

- Gods of Israel's idolatrous cults "failed to bequeath even their names to later Jewish demons, let alone their mythological features"
- Idols consistently mocked as mere wood and stone — prophets never engage with pagan theology as a live option
- Magic, divination, other pagan practices always treated as foreign imports, never native Israelite traditions
- Even wicked kings don't have court magicians or diviners — exception of Manasseh, described as aberration

## Confidence

**Rating:** low

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's weakest claim, most directly falsified by archaeology. Kuntillet Ajrud "YHWH and his Asherah" blessing formula requires a divine realm with at least two powers.

## Stakes

Whether the archaeological evidence for polytheistic practice in Israel represents genuine mythological belief or superficial imitation. If Kaufmann is right, figurines, inscriptions, and high places are "idolatry" in a degraded sense — fetishism without genuine mythological content.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The archaeological record. [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-proves-consort]] — thousands of Judean pillar figurines, standing stones, cult stands. Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom inscriptions bless by "YHWH and his Asherah." Elephantine papyri show Jews worshipping Anat-Yahu. This isn't "superficial" paganism — it's evidence many Israelites conceived of YHWH as having a consort.

**Alternative reading:** Israelite religion was DIVERSE. There was an official, aniconic, exclusivist Yahwism AND a popular religion more continuous with Canaanite practice. Kaufmann's claim that popular religion was non-mythological misreads both the biblical and archaeological evidence. The prophets' polemic against idols as "mere wood and stone" is rhetorical strategy.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's weakest claim, most directly falsified by archaeology. His argument that Israelites who worshipped other gods didn't REALLY believe in them is unfalsifiable special pleading. The Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions pair YHWH with a goddess in a blessing formula — that requires treating Asherah as a divine being who can bless, which is functionally polytheism regardless of whether they had a theogony for her.
