# kaufmann-ri-3.3: Biblical demonology is non-pagan

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

"One of the remarkable aspects of the religion of pre-exilic Israel is that it failed to transmute either its ancient pantheon or the gods of the nations into demons." The old gods became "the desolate haunts of dancing Se'irim who keep company with wild animals. All decisive power, divine and even demonic, has been taken from them and given to the messengers of YHWH." Evil proceeds from YHWH or his angels; even Satan is a member of the divine court (Job 2:1). Later Jewish demonology derives "not from a primordial evil root, but from sin."

## Evidence

- No link of nomenclature between Israelite demons and pagan divinities. Angel names (Gabriel, Michael) have no connection to pagan pantheon
- Destructive agents (negef, reshef) are "messengers of evil" — members of YHWH's suite, not independent powers. Reshef, a known Canaanite god, used "as a bare common noun" (Job 5:7) with "no knowledge of Reshef, the mythological god"
- Shedim and se'irim "are assigned no function in nature or in the life of men" — "below the level of deities"
- "Princes of the nations" in Daniel are not transformed pagan gods; "not one of their names reveals any connection with the gods mentioned in the Bible"
- Even Rahab, dragon, Leviathan are not demons — they're monstrous creatures who rebelled

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The observation is genuine and original. The inference proves either early non-mythological religion OR exclusivist monolatry that never incorporated foreign gods as "former gods" to demote.

## Stakes

One of Kaufmann's most original arguments. If Israelite religion was originally polytheistic and gradually became monotheistic, displaced gods should leave traces as demons, angels, or hostile powers. The standard pattern (Zoroastrianism's daevas = old Indian gods) doesn't appear in Israel. This requires explanation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Critical scholars argue the absence is explained differently: Yahwism was exclusivist from the start and simply didn't incorporate the Canaanite pantheon into its demonology because those gods were always "other gods" — foreign, not defeated predecessors. The Baals and Asheras were never "Israel's old gods" who became demons.

**Alternative reading:** Kaufmann's argument is powerful but cuts both ways. It could mean monotheism was original, OR it could mean Israelite monolatry was so successful at EXCLUDING other gods that they never had "former god" status to be demoted.

## Assessment

Genuinely insightful. The comparison with Zoroastrianism is illuminating. Israel's demonology IS different. But Kaufmann's inference that this proves original non-mythological religion overreaches. It could equally prove exclusivist monolatry that treated other gods as real but forbidden, not as demons to integrate into a dualistic system.
