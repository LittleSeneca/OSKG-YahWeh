# cross-cmhe-pre-i.4: Yahweh originated as an epithet of El

**Source:** [[Cross — Preface + Part I — Canaanite Religion and El]] — Frank Moore Cross, *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* (1973), Chapter 3

## The Claim

Yahweh was not an independent god who later merged with El. Yahweh began as an EPITHET of El — one of El's titles. Over time, the epithet became a proper name, and the proper name displaced the original deity. What scholars call the "merger of Yahweh and El" was actually the elevation of one of El's titles to independent deity status.

Cross reviews the etymology evidence:
- Yahweh is the primitive form, confirmed by 9th-century Mesha Stele and 8th-century seals
- The earliest attestation is Egyptian *yhw3* in 14th-13th century Shasu lists
- The name is a verbal form, probably causative: "He causes to be" / "He creates"
- Cross favors the reading: *yahweh ṣĕbā'ôt* = "He creates the (heavenly) armies"
- This makes Yahweh originally an epithet of El — El who creates the heavenly hosts

"Yahweh is a liturgical title of El, specifically El as creator of the divine council."

## Evidence

- The most common divine title in the Hebrew Bible is "Yahweh of Hosts" (*yhwh ṣĕbā'ôt*) — Cross argues this was the ORIGINAL full form, with "Yahweh" being a shortened epithet meaning "He creates the hosts."
- El as creator of the divine council is well-attested at Ugarit — El presides over the "sons of El" (*bn 'il*). A title meaning "Creator of the (heavenly) armies" would be a natural epithet for El.
- The causative verbal form fits the Northwest Semitic pattern for divine names (cf. *ya'ăqōb-'ēl*, *yiptaḥ-'ēl*).
- The historical trajectory: epithet → proper name → displaced the original deity. The mechanism is linguistic fossilization — a title becomes so dominant it replaces the name.
- Parallels exist in other ANE contexts: Marduk's fifty names in *Enuma Elish* represent a similar process of absorbing other deities' identities through epithet accumulation.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Philologically plausible but depends on a specific causative etymology — and as Romer showed, ALL etymologies of Yahweh are speculative. The stronger part of Cross's argument is the epithet transfer (pre-i.3), not the etymology of the name.

## Stakes

This is a radical claim even within the evolutionary consensus. If Cross is right, "Yahweh" is not a foreign southern god's name at all. It's a TITLE of the Canaanite high god El that eventually became a proper name. The God of Israel is not a newcomer from the desert — he's an aspect of the old Canaanite creator god that broke free and became independent. This directly contradicts the southern origin / Midianite-Kenite hypothesis.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Romer (Yahweh was a southern storm god from Edom/Seir, not an El epithet). Smith (Yahweh merged with El but originated independently). Fleming (Yahweh was a people-group deity from the Shasu). [[claim-yhwh-southern-desert-origin-soleb-poetry-synthesis]] — the southern origin model is the primary alternative.

**Alternative reading:** Yahweh was an independent southern deity (storm/warrior god) who was later identified with El through political-theological convergence — the name reflects an independent origin, not El theology.

## Assessment

Cross's philological argument is elegant but the evidence for "Yahweh as El-epithet" depends heavily on the etymology of the name — and ALL etymologies of Yahweh are speculative. The stronger part of Cross's argument is the El epithets applied to Yahweh (pre-i.3), which is textually verifiable regardless of the etymology.
