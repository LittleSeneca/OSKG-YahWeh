# fleming-ybi-3.1: Cornelis Tiele (1872), not Friedrich Ghillany (1862), was the true creator of the Midianite/Kenite Hypothesis

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 3 — The Midianite Hypothesis — Moses and the Priest]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 3

## The Claim

"Tiele certainly proposes a new way to understand the origin of Yahweh in relation to the peoples of the inland deserts... he advances the idea with consciousness of its novelty: only two alternatives had been considered before him, that Yahweh came from Egypt and that Ya(hweh) was a Canaanite deity acquired after conquest" (Fleming, p. 92). "Tiele should be regarded as the creator of what became the Midianite Hypothesis" (p. 94).

## Evidence

- Ghillany's 1862 work (under pseudonym Richard von der Alm) did NOT propose Yahweh came from the desert — he argued Yahweh came from Phoenicia via the god Yao, in Samuel's time (p. 91)
- Tiele's 1872 Dutch work self-consciously presented the desert-origin idea as novel
- Tiele built his case from Rechabites → Kenites → Moses' father-in-law, with the Rechabite "zeal for Yahweh" as the starting point
- The hypothesis was Kenite, not Midianite: Tiele "has little interest in the story of Jethro as Midianite priest" (p. 94)
- Tiele's spatial reference was "the wider desert" as an alternative way of life, not specifically the south
- German scholars (Stade, Budde) picked up the idea without crediting Tiele, transforming it into a Moses-centered Midianite form
- Blenkinsopp (2008: 132) had attributed priority to Ghillany based on Holzinger (1900), who was apparently mistaken

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is a historiographical claim, verifiable by consulting the primary sources. Fleming has done the legwork in the original Dutch and French editions.

## Stakes

Understanding the hypothesis's origins reveals how much it mutated. Tiele's original version was more modest, less Moses-centric, and not specifically southern. The transformation into a Moses-Midian-Sinai hypothesis was a later development that loaded theological freight onto a more careful comparative-religion argument.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Blenkinsopp (2008) and the scholarly tradition that credits Ghillany.

**Alternative reading:** Priority disputes don't affect the substance of the hypothesis — what matters is the mature form, not the first sketch.

## Assessment

Fleming's historiographical work is meticulous and illuminating. The transformation from Tiele's Kenite/Rechabite argument to the later Midianite/Moses form matters because it shows how biblical scholars progressively centered the hypothesis on the Exodus narrative — the very narrative Fleming argues cannot support it.
