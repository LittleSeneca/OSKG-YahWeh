# tigay-nog-2.10: Biblical onomastic evidence was not systematically censored

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Conclusions

## The Claim

The scribal revisions that changed ba'al names to bosheth names are exceptions that prove the rule. If scribes had systematically purged pagan names, more would have been changed. The fact that the biblical onomasticon matches the epigraphic onomasticon suggests both reflect genuine naming practices, not editorial fabrication.

## Evidence

- 8 individuals with ba'al names underwent deliberate scribal revision: Eshbaal → Ishbosheth, Beeliada → Eliada, Meribaal → Mephibosheth
- These changes prove scribes WERE sensitive to pagan theophorics AND capable of changing them
- But the changes also prove "that the editors did not suppress all pagan names" — some survived (Eshbaal still appears in Chronicles)
- The surviving pagan names (and the fact that most were only partially revised) suggest the biblical onomastic picture is "realistic" — not a tendentious creation
- The convergence with the epigraphic onomasticon (which couldn't have been censored) is the strongest evidence: TWO independent datasets point to the same pattern
- See also [[claim-boshet-scribal-substitution-for-baal]] for the bosheth substitution mechanism

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The convergence between the biblical onomasticon and the epigraphic onomasticon — two independent datasets — is strong evidence against systematic censorship of either.

## Stakes

If the biblical onomasticon was systematically censored, Tigay's entire case collapses — the 89% Yahwistic figure would be an editorial artifact, not a reflection of reality. Tigay's argument that the censorship was partial and unsystematic is essential to his thesis. The bosheth revisions are not evidence of systematic censorship but evidence of its OPPOSITE: scribes could have changed all pagan names but didn't, suggesting the underlying onomasticon was genuinely Yahwistic.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-tigay-onomastics-does-not-disprove-polytheism]] (Smith)

**Alternative reading:** The bosheth revisions show scribal INTENT to suppress pagan names. We can't know how many pagan names were successfully erased. The survivors are accidents of transmission, not evidence of editorial restraint. The convergence with the epigraphic record may reflect that BOTH datasets are biased toward elite/Yahwistic circles.
