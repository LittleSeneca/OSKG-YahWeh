# keel-ggi-5.8: Baal Was the Primary Rival to Yahweh in Iron Age IIB, Not Asherah

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part V — Iron Age IIB — Baal El Yahweh and His Asherah]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part V

## The Claim

"Scholarly discussion concerning the religion of preexilic Israel and Judah has been dominated in recent years... by the question about whether Yahweh had a female partner. The discussion has not given adequate consideration to the fact that the dominant aspects of a symbol system show up in different geographic regions and on different media." Hosea's polemic against Baal is "reiterated repeatedly" (Hos 2:10, 15, 18f.; 9:10; 11:2; 13:1), but "there is no explicit polemic against a female deity" — only possible "veiled allusions" (Hos 4:18; 14:9). The iconographic evidence matches this: Baal-type warrior imagery is prominent; goddess imagery is minimal.

## Evidence

- Hosea explicitly names Baal repeatedly; never names Asherah or any goddess
- The four-winged warrior deity (illus. 210) from Hazor is a Baal-type figure — divine warrior imagery is prevalent
- In Northwest Semitic inscriptions, "Baal and Asherah are never paired" — they appear together only in deuteronomistic redaction of biblical texts
- The real religious competition in Iron Age IIB was between Yahweh and Baal(shamem), and secondarily between Yahweh and El — NOT between Yahweh and a goddess
- "The issue concerning 'Yahweh's asherah' in Samaria and Teman in the early eighth century is independent of the question about a cultic image in seventh-century Jerusalem"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The Baal-as-primary-rival thesis is well-supported by the textual and iconographic evidence. The "goddess obsession" critique of recent scholarship is pointed but justified.

## Stakes

If Baal was the main rival, not Asherah, then the entire "Yahweh and his wife" framework that has dominated popular and scholarly discourse (Dever's *Did God Have a Wife?*, Stavrakopoulou's *God: An Anatomy*) may be answering the wrong question. The real religious conflict in monarchic Israel was not about monotheism vs. polytheism or male god vs. goddess — it was about *which male god* would be supreme.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Dever (whose entire popular book is built around the "wife" frame), Stavrakopoulou, and feminist scholars who emphasize goddess suppression. Also Romer, who argues for Asherah as consort.

**Alternative reading:** The absence of explicit anti-goddess polemic in Hosea could mean goddess worship was so uncontroversial it did not need to be attacked — not that it did not exist. And the "veiled allusions" might be more significant than Keel & Uehlinger allow.

## Assessment

Keel & Uehlinger have a point that the Asherah question has sucked all the oxygen out of the room. But their own evidence shows the asherah WAS significant — it is the *only* mediating entity mentioned in inscriptions alongside Yahweh. It is just that its significance was as a cultic symbol, not as a goddess consort. The "Baal vs. Yahweh" frame and the "asherah as symbol" frame are complementary, not contradictory.
