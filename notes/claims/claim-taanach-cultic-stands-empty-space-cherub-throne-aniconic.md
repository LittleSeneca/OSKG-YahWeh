# keel-ggi-4.4: The Taanach Cultic Stands — Empty Space as Aniconic Yahweh

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part IV — Iron Age IIA]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part IV

## The Claim

The two cultic stands from Taanach (10th century) are the most important Iron IIA cult objects. Stand #1 shows the goddess as stylized tree with caprids (lowest register) and Baal as a subordinate, off-center figure strangling a snake. Stand #2 has an INTENTIONALLY EMPTY space between cherubs — possibly the earliest material evidence for aniconic Yahweh worship. The divine couple has been dissolved: "In contrast to [Middle Bronze Age] iconography, [the stand] does not show male and female deities in any relationship to one another."

## Evidence

*Stand #1 (Sellin):*
- Five vertically arranged zones, alternating winged sphinxes (cherubs) with lions as guardian figures
- Lowest register: stylized palmetto tree flanked by caprids eating from it — the substitute symbol for Asherah
- Baal appears as an appliqué on the RIGHT SIDE — "since the image is off to the side, Baal is not shown as the object of direct veneration by the cult; it suggests rather a certain subordination of that god"
- The divine couple has been dissolved

*Stand #2 (Lapp):*
- Four registers: lowest = naked "Mistress of the Lions" (the goddess directly); third = tree flanked by caprids; second = "only a pair of cherubs and a space and no depiction is missing between them"
- "The space appears to have been left empty intentionally"
- Taylor (1988): the empty space depicts an "invisible deity" — Yahweh enthroned on the cherubs. Keel & Uehlinger are cautious: "yōšēb ha-kĕrûbîm does not mean 'who dwells (between) the cherubim'... but 'who is enthroned on the cherubs' or 'who sits on a cherub throne'"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** HIGH for the iconographic analysis. MEDIUM for the "Yahweh" identification of the empty space — Keel & Uehlinger are appropriately cautious.

## Stakes

If the empty space represents Yahweh, this is the earliest material evidence for aniconic Yahwism — 10th century BCE, centuries before the exile. The cherub throne as aniconic cult object would be the original "seat" of Yahweh, not a later replacement for a lost statue.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue there WAS a statue in the First Temple (Niehr). Also those who interpret the empty space differently (unfinished, damaged, held a lost appliqué).

**Alternative reading:** The empty space could have held a perishable object (wood, textile). It could be a manufacturing flaw. It could be a space for ritual action rather than a depiction of divine absence.

## Assessment

Caution is warranted. But the convergence is striking: empty center between cherubs on a cultic stand from Taanach + empty space between cherubs in the Jerusalem Temple's Holy of Holies + no statue of Yahweh described in any biblical text + the broader archaeological pattern of declining anthropomorphic images. Aniconic Yahwism in the 10th century is not proven — but it is more plausible than the alternative.
