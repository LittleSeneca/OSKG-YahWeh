# schmid-ht-5.2: Duhm used historical criticism to excavate religion beneath theology

**Source:** [[Schmid — §5 — Romantic Devaluation of Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §5

## The Claim

Bernhard Duhm "views his primary task in his work on the Hebrew Bible as 'examining the stage of religion prior to theology, or at least prior to being fully dominated by theology.'" Duhm's commentary on Jeremiah portrays the Deuteronomistic theologians as systematizers who killed the living prophetic impulse: "It is actually a tragedy of religion that the dead prophet kills the living one."

## Evidence

- Duhm, *Das Geheimnis in der Religion* (1896): the task is to find religion "prior to theology"
- Duhm, *Das Buch Jeremia* (1901): Deuteronomistic theology "kills" Jeremiah's living religion
- Schmid's interpretation: "One must unearth the living religion of the Hebrew Bible that lies beneath the theologically shaped and revised form. In the end, this conviction was one of the most important motivations for the success of historical-critical biblical scholarship"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Duhm quotations are direct. Schmid's interpretation (that the religion/theology distinction motivated historical criticism) is plausible and well-argued.

## Stakes

If Schmid is right that historical criticism was originally motivated by the religion/theology distinction, then the discipline's founding impulse was to reach PAST the texts to the "living religion" beneath. This is exactly the opposite of Schmid's own approach, which takes the texts' theological shaping as the primary object of study. Schmid is inverting the founding impulse of his own discipline — and he knows it.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see historical criticism as motivated primarily by Enlightenment rationalism rather than Romantic religion-seeking would narrate this differently. Duhm may be an outlier rather than representative.

**Alternative reading:** The religion/theology distinction was never the PRIMARY motivation for historical criticism. It was a post-hoc justification that appealed to Romantic sensibilities. The real driver was the Enlightenment commitment to treating biblical texts like any other ancient texts — which is methodologically the same move Schmid makes.

## Assessment

Schmid's reading of Duhm is fascinating and important. It shows that the "theology is bad, religion is good" framework is baked into the discipline's origins. Schmid's project — taking "theology" seriously as the texts' own mode of reflection — is a genuine departure from this founding impulse. Whether that departure is correction or regression depends on whether Duhm was right that the theological shaping obscures something valuable, or whether Schmid is right that the theological shaping IS the valuable thing.
