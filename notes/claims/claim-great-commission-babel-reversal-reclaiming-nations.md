# heiser-ur-33.1: The Great Commission and Pentecost are the reversal of Babel

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 7 — The Kingdom Already]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 34

## The Claim

At Babel, the nations were disinherited and given to lesser *elohim* (Deut 32:8-9). At the Great Commission (Matt 28:18-20), Jesus claims "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations." Heiser reads this as the reversal of Babel: Jesus reclaims ALL authority and sends his followers to disciple ALL nations. "The Great Commission is about taking back the nations." Pentecost (Acts 2) confirms the pattern: at Babel, languages were divided and people scattered; at Pentecost, the Spirit enables apostles to speak in all languages and people are gathered. "What Yahweh did at Babel is being undone."

## Evidence

- Matthew 28:18-20: "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations" — the language of universal authority over "all nations" directly echoes the Babel distribution of nations to the *elohim*
- Acts 2:1-11: The apostles speak in the languages of "every nation under heaven" and the list of nations echoes the Table of Nations from Genesis 10
- The structural parallel: Babel scatters nations and divides languages → Pentecost gathers nations and unites languages

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH on the Babel-reversal reading of Pentecost (Acts 2 explicitly echoes the table of nations). The Great Commission reading requires accepting the Deut 32 worldview as the NT authors' operating framework.

## Stakes

If Heiser is right, the Great Commission is not primarily about individual salvation but about cosmic reclamation: taking back the nations that were under the authority of corrupt *elohim*. This reframes Christian mission as participation in the divine council's restoration of proper cosmic order — not merely evangelism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who read the Great Commission as a straightforward missionary mandate and Pentecost as the birth of the church, without the Babel-reversal framework.

**Alternative reading:** The Great Commission is about Jesus's universal authority as the risen Lord, and Pentecost is about the Spirit empowering the church for mission. The Babel echo may be present but is not the primary meaning.

## Assessment

This is one of Heiser's most compelling structural readings. The Babel→Pentecost reversal is textually grounded (Luke explicitly uses the table of nations language in Acts 2). The Great Commission as Babel-reversal is more interpretive but fits the framework. Together they form Heiser's answer to the question: if the *elohim* were given the nations at Babel, how does God get them back? Answer: through the visible Yahweh incarnate, commissioning his followers to disciple those very nations.
