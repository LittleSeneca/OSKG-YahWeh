# fleming-ybi-5.4: Cross's liturgical epithet model is unsustainable against Shasu evidence

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 5 — The Name Yahweh]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 5

## The Claim

"Efforts to explain Yahweh as derived from a liturgical formula for El, as elaborated by Frank Cross from the work of his teacher Albright, bypass the identification of the Shasu group with a human personal name and cannot convince" (p. 203).

## Evidence

- Cross (1973: 65-66) built on Albright's (1924, 1948, 1968) interpretation: Yahweh is a causative verbal form meaning "He brings [the heavenly hosts] into existence" — a liturgical epithet of El
- This interpretation predates full integration of the Soleb Shasu evidence
- The Shasu evidence shows Yhwʒ as a people-name, not a liturgical formula
- Fleming traces the genealogy of the idea: Albright (1924) → Albright (1948, review connecting to Yahweh Seba'ot) → Cross (1973, full elaboration)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Fleming is simply right that Cross's model was developed before the Egyptian evidence was fully integrated and doesn't explain Yhwʒ as a people-name.

## Stakes

Cross's model has been enormously influential in American biblical scholarship. If it fails, one major alternative to the Midianite Hypothesis (still within a southern-origin framework) also fails.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Anyone following Cross's interpretation. But Cross himself (d. 2012) couldn't respond to the Soleb evidence as Fleming reads it.

**Alternative reading:** The Shasu evidence doesn't disprove Cross — Yhwʒ COULD be a place name derived from the divine epithet, and the place gave its name to a Shasu group.

## Assessment

Fleming is right that Cross's model doesn't account for the Egyptian evidence, but Cross's model was never built on the Egyptian evidence in the first place. The two operate in different evidentiary frames. The real question is whether Fleming's reading of the Egyptian evidence is compelling enough to overturn Cross.
