# romer-inv-6-7.2: Yhwh rose to supremacy in Jerusalem gradually; two kingdoms literary framing

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapters 6-7 — Cults of Israel and Judah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapters 6-7 synthesis

## The Claim

Yhwh was not the only god of Jerusalem, and Jerusalem was not the only sanctuary of Yhwh. Multiple Yahwistic temples existed in Judah (Arad, Lachish, Beer-Sheba). In Jerusalem itself, Yhwh initially shared the temple with a solar deity and was identified with El Elyon gradually. The centralization of worship was a late political project — probably Josiah's reform. The south WON the theological war because it survived: after 722 (fall of Israel), Judean scribes wrote the history of the north as apostasy. Judah wasn't a military winner (it fell in 587) but was a literary winner — its scribes outlasted the northern kingdom and shaped the story.

## Evidence

**Multiple Yahwistic sanctuaries in Judah:**
- **Arad:** Yahwistic temple with *maṣṣēbôt* (standing stones), deliberately hidden/buried
- **Lachish:** Assyrian relief shows soldiers carrying large incense burner from a Yahwistic temple
- **Beer-Sheba:** Remains of four-horned altar
- **High places (*bāmôt*):** Yahwistic sanctuaries, NOT pagan sites. Only Hezekiah and Josiah attempted to destroy them — proving they were the NORM

**Yhwh's rise in Jerusalem:**
- Competition with the sun god (Joshua 10:12-13)
- El-Yhwh identification: Genesis 14 (Melchizedek, "priest of El Elyon" at Salem = Jerusalem)
- Regional pattern: Moab, Ammon, and other kingdoms all had national gods who gradually absorbed older pantheons

**The two kingdoms problem:**
- Israel (North): Dominant, wealthy, fertile — but portrayed as apostate
- Judah (South): Small, poor, vassal to Israel — portrayed as sometimes faithful
- Historical reality: Israel had legitimate Yahwism with older El imagery; Judah had gradual Yhwh centralization
- The biblical portrayal is post-722 Judean propaganda

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on multiple sanctuaries (archaeology), HIGH on Yhwh not being Jerusalem's original god. MEDIUM on solar competition (depends on Joshua 10). The two-kingdoms literary analysis is standard critical scholarship.

## Stakes

The Jerusalem temple — the center of biblical religion — was a political construction. Yhwh wasn't always there. He wasn't the only god there. The claim that God's chosen city was always Jerusalem is theological retrojection. And the moral framework of the Bible (Israel = bad, Judah = better) is the product of a literary war the south won by surviving.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional readings of the Deuteronomistic history. Conservatives who see the northern kingdom's fall as divine judgment for apostasy.

**Alternative reading:** The Deuteronomistic history accurately records religious differences between north and south.

## Assessment

The south won the theological war not by military victory but by literary survival. After 722, Judean scribes had a monopoly on the story. They wrote the north as apostasy and Jerusalem as the only legitimate sanctuary. This is the classic case of history written by the winners — except the "winners" lost their kingdom too, just 135 years later. The literary victory outlasted the political defeat.
