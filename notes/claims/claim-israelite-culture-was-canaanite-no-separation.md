# smith-ehg-intro.1: Israelite culture WAS Canaanite culture — there was no radical separation.

**Source:** [[Smith Chapter 0 — Introduction]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Early History of God* (2nd ed., 2002), Introduction

## The Claim

The older scholarly consensus (Albright, Kaufmann, Tigay) treated Israel and Canaan as distinct cultures, with Baal/Asherah worship as foreign "syncretism." This is wrong. The archaeological, linguistic, and epigraphic evidence shows Israelite culture was a subset of Canaanite culture. "One cannot maintain a radical cultural separation between 'Canaanites' and Israelites for the Iron I period."

## Evidence

- Scripts: both linear and cuneiform alphabetic scripts in highlands AND coast/valleys
- Language: Hebrew and Canaanite "cannot be easily distinguished" in Iron I; Isaiah 19:18 calls Hebrew "the language of Canaan"
- Material culture: four-room house, collared-rim jar, hewn cisterns — once thought distinctively Israelite, now attested in coastal and Transjordanian sites
- Burial patterns: continuous from Late Bronze through Iron Age
- Cultic terminology: *zebah*, *šělāmîm*, *minḥah*, *kālîl*, *'ōlāh* all have Ugaritic/Phoenician cognates. *Kōhen* (priest), *qādēš* (cultic functionary), *rb khnm* (chief priest) are Canaanite terms.
- The Merneptah Stele (c. 1208) distinguishes Israel and Canaan — but this shows political distinction, not cultural.

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** This is now archaeological consensus. Even conservative scholars generally accept material culture continuity. The question is what it MEANS for religion, not whether the continuity exists.

## Stakes

This is the foundation under everything else. If Israelite culture IS Canaanite culture, then the biblical narrative of Israel as a distinct people with a distinct God entering a foreign land is historically false. Israel emerged FROM Canaan. Its God emerged from the Canaanite pantheon. You can't maintain "God revealed himself to a separate people" if the people weren't separate.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Almost no one disputes the material continuity. The disagreement is about whether material culture continuity = religious continuity. Conservatives (Hess, Block, Walton) argue shared material culture doesn't prove shared theology. Minimalists (Thompson, Lemche) argue we can't know enough to draw ANY conclusions.

**Alternative reading:** Shared ANE culture doesn't equal shared religion. Modern Americans and Canadians share language, architecture, and consumer goods but have meaningfully different cultures and self-conceptions. The Israelites could have shared pots and houses with Canaanites while worshiping a fundamentally different God.

## Assessment

The material continuity is overwhelming and must be the starting point. But Smith sometimes elides "shared material culture" with "shared religion." These are different claims requiring different evidence. The cultic terminology parallels are stronger evidence for religious continuity than the pottery parallels. Track when Smith shifts between "culture" and "religion" — they're not the same thing.
