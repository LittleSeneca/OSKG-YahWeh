# tigay-nog-2.9: Population estimates contextualize biblical complaints about polytheism

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Conclusions

## The Claim

If Jerusalem's population was 10,600-24,000, then even 5-10% practicing polytheism = 500-2,400 people. Enough to be visible, enough to alarm prophets, but not the majority. "It is the theological significance of the issue, not the statistical picture, which makes it loom so large." The biblical portrayal of rampant national apostasy can be explained by a visible MINORITY whose practices were theologically offensive to the prophets, not by a majority of Israelites worshipping other gods.

## Evidence

- Population estimates for Jerusalem: Broshi gives 10,600; other estimates range up to 24,000
- 5-10% = 500-2,400 polytheists in Jerusalem
- A few hundred visible polytheists in the capital would be ENOUGH to provoke prophetic outrage — especially if some were in the royal court or temple establishment
- The prophets were theologians, not sociologists. Their language of "all Israel" and "the whole nation" reflects theological generalization, not statistical reporting
- Tigay: "It is the theological significance of the issue, not the statistical picture, which makes it loom so large"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The population math is straightforward; the variable is how representative Jerusalem is of Judah as a whole, and what percentage of polytheists would be "visible" to prophets.

## Stakes

This argument reframes the entire debate. If a visible minority of 500-2,400 polytheists in Jerusalem could generate the prophetic indictments we have, then the biblical evidence for "rampant polytheism" is perfectly compatible with Tigay's data showing 90%+ Yahwistic names. The apparent contradiction between Tigay and the prophets disappears — they're describing the same reality from different perspectives (statistical vs. theological).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-dever-folk-polytheism-outlasted-monarchy-monotheism-book-religion]] (Dever)

**Alternative reading:** The prophets weren't just concerned about Jerusalem. Their indictments cover "the land" — rural Judah, the Northern Kingdom, every level of society. And Dever's archaeological evidence (3,000+ figurines) is NOT confined to Jerusalem. The population math may work for Jerusalem but not for the distributed rural population where figurines are found in domestic contexts across the country.
