# kaufmann-ri-4.7: Evolution ran from monotheism to monolatry, not the reverse

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch IV — The Religion of the People]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter IV

## The Claim

"Not from monolatry to monotheism, as is the conventional theory, but from monotheism to monolatry runs the path of Israel's religious evolution." The basic monotheistic IDEA was present from the beginning; the biblical battle was to realize its cultic CONSEQUENCES.

## Evidence

- "The biblical struggle with idolatry restricts itself entirely to the area of cult and ritual. This is decisive proof that from earliest times Israel was no longer a genuinely pagan people"
- "The struggle against idols, calves, pillars, divination, and magic may be said to involve not monotheism but monolatry; at issue is not belief in one God, but the exclusive worship of him"
- "The biblical writers take for granted that faith in one God is common to them and the people; they merely call on the people to consider the implications of this belief, and stop worshiping no-gods"
- "YHWH is never opposed to other living gods, but to other objects of worship"
- "The idols and 'foreign gods' are mythless cult objects" — "Such a polemic obviously presupposes a monotheistic viewpoint"
- The prophets wanted to "concretize and realize in practice what was still an idea"
- "Some stages in the growth of the objection to images are still visible": Gideon's ephod and Micah's image show no absolute ban at first
- The brazen serpent was legitimate for centuries until Hezekiah removed it because "the Israelites were making burnt offerings to it" — the cultic consequence was drawn later than the idea
- "Only because monotheism existed long since could it emerge triumphant and even more purified after the political collapse"

## Confidence

**Rating:** low

**Rationale:** Elegant reversal but unconvincing as history. Requires an entire people to possess sophisticated theological concept (monotheism) while practicing what looks exactly like polytheism — possible in principle, historically implausible.

## Stakes

The entire historical narrative of Israelite religion. If Kaufmann is right, Wellhausen, Smith, Römer, and the critical tradition have the direction backwards.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Effectively the entire field. The archaeological record shows gradual movement from polytheistic practice toward exclusive worship. Biblical texts, read chronologically, show increasing monotheistic clarity. [[claim-romer-narrative-arc-desert-god-to-only-god]] traces a millennium-long development. [[claim-four-stage-model-monotheism-development]] describes the standard evolutionary sequence. [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]] argues monotheism is a late rhetorical development.

**Alternative reading:** The biblical battle IS about CULT rather than THEOLOGY — but this doesn't mean theology preceded cult. It means the final editors were already monotheists who depicted the struggle in monotheistic terms. Actual historical development: polytheistic practice → competing Yahwistic factions → monolatrous Yahwism → monotheistic Yahwism → cultic exclusivism. Kaufmann reverses the first and last steps.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's reversal is elegant but ultimately unconvincing as history. It requires monotheism to exist as an IDEA before it exists as a PRACTICE — historically implausible for an ancient society without philosophical schools or systematic theology. His formulation would work better as a description of the biblical AUTHORS' perspective than of historical reality. From the authors' standpoint, monotheism WAS original and the battle WAS about cultic consequences — because they were writing from within an already-monotheistic framework.
