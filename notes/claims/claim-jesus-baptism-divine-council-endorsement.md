# heiser-ur-32.1: Jesus's baptism was a divine council endorsement

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 7 — The Kingdom Already]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapters 32-33

## The Claim

The baptism scene (Mark 1:9-11) depicts the visible Yahweh (Jesus) being affirmed by the invisible Yahweh (the voice) and the Spirit. This is a divine council scene: the two other persons of the Trinity acting as council witnesses to Jesus's authority to reclaim the nations. The Transfiguration (Chapter 33) repeats this pattern — Moses and Elijah appear as witnesses representing the Law and Prophets, and the voice from the cloud repeats the baptismal endorsement: "This is my beloved Son; listen to him."

## Evidence

- Mark 1:9-11: The voice from heaven ("You are my beloved Son; with you I am well pleased") and the Spirit descending like a dove — two divine persons affirming the third
- Matthew 17:1-8 (Transfiguration): Moses and Elijah appear with Jesus, the voice from the cloud speaks — the same baptismal endorsement repeated with additional witnesses (Law + Prophets)
- In Heiser's Deut 32 worldview framework, the divine council serves as witness to major covenantal events (see Sinai law-giving). The baptism and Transfiguration follow this pattern.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The baptism narrative clearly involves divine presence and voice; the Transfiguration has stronger council imagery (Moses and Elijah as heavenly witnesses). The specific identification as a "divine council scene" is Heiser's interpretive framework rather than a necessary reading of the text.

## Stakes

If Heiser is right, Jesus's public ministry begins and is reaffirmed with divine council witness — the same pattern as the giving of the Law at Sinai. This connects Jesus's mission to the Deut 32 worldview: he is authorized to reclaim the nations by the same council that witnessed the original distribution.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who read the baptism as a purely Trinitarian revelation without any council framework, or who see the Transfiguration as a preview of the resurrection rather than a council scene.

**Alternative reading:** The voice from heaven and the Spirit's descent are a theophany demonstrating Jesus's unique relationship to God — not a formal council proceeding. The Transfiguration is an eschatological preview, not a council endorsement.

## Assessment

The baptism and Transfiguration as council scenes fit Heiser's framework consistently: the same council that witnessed the Law at Sinai and the distribution of nations at Babel now witnesses the commissioning of the one who will reclaim them. The evidence is plausible but the reading depends entirely on accepting the prior divine-council framework.
