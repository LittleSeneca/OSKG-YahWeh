# kaufmann-ri-9.7: National covenant morality is the higher stage, not primitive

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch IX — Some Aspects of the Popular Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch IX

## The Claim

National covenant morality is "not primitive, but rather the highest stage of a morality transcending the individual." The new idea is that morality is an expression of the will of God — his absolute command. "God created not only the realm of the is, but the realm of the ought as well." Human freedom is the root of sin, but also the basis of genuine moral responsibility. "The intense passion of the national morality is the final demonstration that is not primitive... It is no longer satisfied with individual responsibility; morality is not 'a private matter.'" Communal responsibility springs from "the ardent desire to realize the demands of morality — a societal task which lies beyond the power of any individual to accomplish."

## Evidence

- The Sinaitic covenant makes the moral-legal element the very core — "the moral imperatives are the end and purpose of the initial declaration of God's sovereignty"
- "The community to whom God manifested himself becomes corporately responsible, becomes, as it were, a new moral person"
- The Torah addresses not the family, tribe, or state, but "the people of the covenant"
- "The 'you' of the laws shifts imperceptibly from the individual to the people"
- Pagan morality and wisdom counsel in "deliberate, reasonable accents"; Torah and prophets "speak impassionedly of guilt"
- Punishment "is not merely a danger or threat, but a necessary part of the moral order"
- The prophets' moral passion is grounded in the Torah's covenant framework — not their own innovation

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** Arbitrary and driven by chronological need — punishing children for parents' sins is arguably more primitive than individual accountability, not 'higher.'

## Stakes

Whether the prophets were innovators or traditionalists. If the national covenant morality is the mature form, the prophets are its preachers, not its creators. This supports Kaufmann's larger thesis that the Torah precedes prophecy.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Anyone who sees individual moral accountability as a moral advance over collective punishment. Ezekiel 18 is usually read as a breakthrough — the individual is responsible for his own sin. Kaufmann would have to see this as a regression to primitive individualistic wisdom morality, which seems perverse.

**Alternative reading:** Individual and corporate responsibility coexisted throughout Israelite history. Neither is "higher" — they address different moral questions. The passion of prophetic morality comes from its eschatological urgency, not from its corporate framework.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's valorization of the corporate over the individual feels forced — he needs national covenant morality to be the mature form to maintain his Torah-before-prophecy chronology. But the argument collapses if we ask: is punishing children for parents' sins (Exod. 20:5) morally "higher" than holding each individual accountable? Kaufmann's "corporate > individual" moral hierarchy is arbitrary and driven by his chronological needs.
