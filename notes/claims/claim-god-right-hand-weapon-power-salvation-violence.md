# stav-god-12-15.1: God's right hand was his weapon — the source of power, salvation, and violence — and his right-handedness is part of his embodied ANE identity, not incidental imagery.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part IV — Arms and Hands]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 12

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that God's right-handedness is not incidental — it is part of his embodied identity. In ANE culture, the right hand was the hand of honor, strength, and skill. "In the Hebrew Bible, God is gloriously right-handed. His right hand accomplishes his most powerful acts." The phrase "the right hand of Yahweh" (*yĕmîn yhwh*) is practically a divine title in the Psalms and prophets.

## Evidence

- Exodus 15:6: "Your right hand, Yahweh, glorious in power, your right hand, Yahweh, shatters the enemy" — the Song of the Sea, one of the oldest poems in the Bible
- The right hand appears over 100 times in the Hebrew Bible, consistently associated with divine power
- "The right hand of Yahweh" (*yĕmîn yhwh*) functions as a divine title in Psalms (Psalm 118:15-16)
- ANE cultural context: the right hand was the hand of honor, oath-taking, blessing, and combat; kings and deities are depicted with raised right hands in iconography
- God's right hand does specific things: shatters enemies, performs valiant deeds, holds fast, saves, creates

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The right hand appears over 100 times in the Hebrew Bible consistently associated with divine power; this is not poetic flourish but embodied identity.

## Stakes

If God's "right hand" is a real anatomical hand doing real things (grasping, striking, saving), then the Psalms and Exodus are describing a body in action, not employing allegorical language. The right-handedness also connects to the phallic imagery of Part II: the right hand = power = virility = divine masculinity.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those who read the "right hand" as a metaphor for divine power (i.e., most modern readers)

**Alternative reading:** "Right hand" is a Hebrew idiom for power and strength, not a claim about God's anatomical handedness.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou is at her most persuasive here: the right-hand language in the Hebrew Bible is so pervasive, so specific, and so central to Israel's confession (the Song of the Sea) that denying God has an actual hand requires hermeneutical contortion. If the "right hand of Yahweh" that shattered Pharaoh's army is just a figure of speech, then the Exodus confession is built on a figure of speech — which seems like a theological choice, not a linguistic one.
