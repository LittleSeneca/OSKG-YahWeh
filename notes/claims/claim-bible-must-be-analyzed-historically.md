# romer-inv-intro.3: The Bible must be analyzed historically without preconception — Romer's middle position between maximalism and minimalism

**Source:** [[Romer — Introduction]] — Thomas Romer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Introduction

## The Claim

"The authors of the various books of the Bible are obviously not impartial witnesses, but rather are very keen to impose on readers their vision of history and of the god of Israel." Therefore, the Bible must be subjected to the same critical analysis as any other ancient document. Textual evidence must be "compared with the archaeological, epigraphic, and iconographic facts."

## Evidence

### The key distinction
Romer insists the biblical narratives are "not inventions proceeding simply from the minds of intellectuals seated in their comfortable chairs." They are "literature of tradition" — received from predecessors, freely transformed, but resting on "certain archaic kernels of fact" that preserve "traces of memory" of distant events.

### Romer's middle position
- **Against maximalists** (who take the Bible at face value): The texts are ideologically shaped, late, and not objective history
- **Against minimalists** (who say the texts tell us nothing about pre-exilic Israel): The texts preserve genuine historical memories, however distorted

> "It seems to me perfectly legitimate to reconnect to the older tradition of historical interpretation of the Bible, which was still widely cultivated at the start of the twentieth century... but which has been relatively neglected since the 1970s."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Romer's "traces of memory" methodology is defensible but hard to verify. How do you distinguish a genuine archaic kernel from a later invention that LOOKS archaic? He's more optimistic than Smith about what can be recovered from late texts.

## Stakes

If Romer is wrong and the minimalists are right, the entire reconstruction of pre-exilic Israelite religion collapses. We can't say anything about Yhwh before the Persian period. Romer's entire book depends on his middle position being viable.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** European minimalists (Schmid, Kratz, the Copenhagen school) would say Romer is too optimistic. Conservatives would say he's too skeptical — the texts ARE reliable history. Smith occupies a position similar to Romer's but is more cautious.

**Alternative reading:** The minimalist position: the Pentateuchal narratives are Persian-period ideological constructions. Their "archaic kernels" are unfalsifiable — we can't prove they exist, and we can't prove they don't. Any historical reconstruction built on them is speculation disguised as scholarship.

## Assessment

This is the single most important methodological claim in Romer's book, and it's also the most vulnerable. He's trying to have it both ways — the texts are ideologically shaped AND they preserve historical memory. Both things can be true, but proving the second part is extremely difficult. Track whether Romer ever acknowledges when the evidence is insufficient to distinguish memory from invention.
