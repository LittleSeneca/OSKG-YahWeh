# dever-dghw-5.4: Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom inscriptions are evidence of Asherah as Yhwh's consort goddess

**Source:** [[Dever — Chapter V — Archaeological Evidence]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Chapter V

## The Claim

The Kuntillet Ajrud (c. 800 BCE) and Khirbet el-Qom (c. 700 BCE) inscriptions invoke "Yhwh and his Asherah" — and Dever reads this as unambiguous evidence that Asherah was Yhwh's consort goddess, not merely a cult symbol. The grammatical argument that the pronominal suffix prevents reading "Asherah" as a proper name fails when the full archaeological context is considered.

## Evidence

- **Kuntillet Ajrud** (c. 800 BCE): Pithos inscriptions invoking "Yhwh of Samaria and his Asherah" and "Yhwh of Teman and his Asherah" — a caravanserai/way-station in the Sinai, with explicitly Yahwistic religious material
- **Khirbet el-Qom** (c. 700 BCE): Burial inscription invoking blessing "by Yahweh and his Asherah" — a tomb in Judah, the same formula in a funerary context
- Both inscriptions use the same formula: "Yhwh and his Asherah" — suggesting a standardized pairing
- The geographical spread (Sinai + Judah) suggests this was not a local peculiarity but a widespread conception
- Dever argues the "Asherah as cult symbol" reading fails to account for why Asherah — uniquely among cult objects — receives a personal name in these inscriptions. People didn't say "Yhwh and his altar" or "Yhwh and his temple." The pairing is personal.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The inscriptions themselves are unambiguous archaeological finds (HIGH). The goddess-vs-symbol interpretation is debated among scholars, but Dever's reading has strong contextual support (MEDIUM-HIGH).

## Stakes

If Dever is right about the inscriptions, then the textual evidence (biblical + epigraphic) converges with the archaeological evidence (figurines, shrines, standing stones) to establish Asherah as Yhwh's consort. The symbol-only reading (Smith's position) becomes a minority interpretation that must explain away converging lines of evidence.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Mark S. Smith ([[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-symbol-not-goddess]], [[claim-asherah-cult-symbol-not-goddess-monarchic-israel]]) and others who argue the pronominal suffix ("his") prevents reading "Asherah" as a proper name — a deity's proper name cannot take a possessive suffix in Hebrew. Keel and Uehlinger ([[claim-his-asherah-cultic-symbol-stylized-tree-not-goddess-consort]]) read the inscriptions as referring to a cult symbol.

**Alternative reading:** "His asherah" = "his cult object" (the stylized tree/pole that was Yhwh's symbol), not a goddess. The grammar supports this, and it aligns with the biblical evidence where asherah is a wooden object that can be cut down and burned — not a deity.
