# day-ygc-2.1: Asherah appears as a GODDESS (not just a cult object) in several OT passages

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 2 — Yahweh and Asherah]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 2: Yahweh and Asherah

## The Claim

"There can therefore be no doubt that there are several places in the Old Testament where the word 'Asherah' denotes the goddess herself, and not simply the cult symbol of Asherah" (p. 46). The key passages are Judg 3:7; 1 Kgs 15:13; 18:19; 2 Kgs 21:7; 23:4.

## Evidence

### Five Passages Examined

1. **2 Kgs 23:4:** Josiah removes "vessels made for Baal, for Asherah, and for all the host of heaven." Asherah sits between Baal and the host of heaven — both divinities. "It would be extremely forced not to understand Asherah here as the name of a deity" (p. 43). Mark Smith and Judith Hadley read it as a cult object here; Day says their reading is strained.

2. **2 Kgs 21:7:** Manasseh's "graven image of Asherah" (*pesel haʼašerah*). Day argues this means "image of the goddess Asherah" — not "image of the Asherah cult object," which would be "an image of a symbol... an image of what was in effect already a kind of image" (p. 44). The redundancy argument is strong.

3. **1 Kgs 15:13:** Asa removes Maacah "because she had a horrid thing made for Asherah." If Asherah here meant the cult object, "that would imply an idolatrous object made for another idolatrous object, which does not seem very plausible" (p. 44).

4. **1 Kgs 18:19:** Elijah summons "prophets of Baal and prophets of Asherah." The parallelism "can mean only that a divine name is intended" (p. 44). Day concedes this is likely a post-exilic addition (Hexaplaric asterisk, no role in subsequent contest), but "whoever added the reference... must have understood Asherah to be a divine name, which implies continued awareness of this goddess amongst the Jews at this relatively late date" (p. 45).

5. **Judg 3:7:** "Serving the Baals and the Asheroth." The plural parallelism with "the Baals" makes divine reference "undeniable" (p. 45). Some would emend to "Ashtaroth" (Peshitta, Vulgate, two Hebrew mss), but "Asheroth" is *lectio difficilior* — easier to explain a corruption *to* Ashtaroth than *from* it (p. 46).

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The cumulative case from multiple passages, using different arguments in each (parallelism, redundancy, context), is convincing. Day directly engages the counter-arguments from Smith and Hadley.

## Stakes

If Day is right, the OT itself attests that Asherah was worshipped as a goddess — not just a harmless wooden pole. The Deuteronomists weren't just cutting down trees; they were suppressing a goddess cult. This changes how we read every "Asherah" reference in the OT.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Mark S. Smith (*Early History of God*, pp. 88-94) — rejects goddess interpretation, reads "Asherah" as cult object in all cases (see [[claim-biblical-evidence-insufficient-for-goddess]]). Judith Hadley (*The Cult of Asherah*, pp. 62-63) — argues "by the time of dtr... the term had ceased to be used with any knowledge of the goddess." Steve Wiggins emends Judg 3:7 to Ashtaroth.

**Alternative reading:** Smith's position: all OT references are to the cult object. The goddess had been so thoroughly demythologized that even Deuteronomistic authors didn't perceive a divine referent. Day's counter: 1 Kgs 18:19 (even if post-exilic addition) proves awareness of the goddess persisted.

**Depends on:**

- [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]] (day-ygc-1.1) — Day's Ch1 argument that Yahweh and El were separate is foundational: if Yahweh was always El, the goddess = consort reading of OT passages loses some of its force because there's no "transfer" to document

**Supports:**
- [[claim-bible-deliberately-obscures-yhwh-asherah-link]] (romer-inv-9.2) — Day's demonstration that Asherah appears as a goddess in the OT strengthens Römer's claim that the Dtr editors were actively trying to suppress her: the goddess breaks through despite the editorial program

**Contradicts:**
- [[claim-biblical-evidence-insufficient-for-goddess]] (smith-ehg-3.3) — Day and Smith directly examine the SAME passages and reach OPPOSITE conclusions. These two claims cannot both be correct.

**Challenged by:**
- [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-symbol-not-goddess]] (smith-ehg-3.2) — If Smith's grammatical reading of the inscriptions is correct (the asherah was a symbol, not a goddess), Day's goddess reading of the OT passages becomes harder to sustain

**Primary sources:**
- 2 Kgs 23:4; 2 Kgs 21:7; 1 Kgs 15:13; 1 Kgs 18:19; Judg 3:7

## Assessment

Day's strongest argument is the redundancy point (image of a symbol) in 2 Kgs 21:7 and 1 Kgs 15:13. The parallelism arguments are also strong. Smith's counter-position feels like motivated reasoning — he needs "Asherah" to be a cult object to support his "convergence and differentiation" model where Yahweh absorbed attributes without a consort. Day's reading is more natural. The cumulative case from five different passages, each requiring a different argument to neutralize, is persuasive.
