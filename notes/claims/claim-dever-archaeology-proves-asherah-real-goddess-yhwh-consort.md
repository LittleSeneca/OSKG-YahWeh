# dever-dghw-6-9.1: Asherah was a real goddess, Yhwh's consort — and archaeology proves it

**Source:** [[Dever — Chapters VI-IX — Asherah to Conclusions]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Chapter VI

## The Claim

The cumulative archaeological evidence — 3,000+ Judean Pillar Figurines, Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom inscriptions, the Arad temple's two standing stones, the Taanach cult stand — demonstrates that Asherah was actively worshipped as Yhwh's consort throughout the monarchy. The biblical writers' embarrassment about a "Hebrew Goddess" led them to systematically obscure her, but they could not erase her entirely.

## Evidence

- **Ugaritic background:** 14th-13th century texts portray Asherah as "Lady Asherah of the Sea," El's consort, "Mother of the Gods," "Creatress of the Gods." She had seventy divine sons. When El and Yhwh merged, Asherah came with the package.
- **Biblical text:** The term *'asherah* occurs over 40 times. In at least several passages, the term MUST refer to a goddess: 1 Kings 15:13; 1 Kings 18:19; 2 Kings 23:4. "If 'Ba'al' is a deity — as he certainly is — then so is 'Asherah'." The Septuagint and Vulgate understood *'asherim* as connected with trees and sacred groves — preserving a memory of the goddess.
- **Inscriptions:** Kuntillet Ajrud: "Yhwh of Samaria and his Asherah" — deliberately paired. Khirbet el-Qom: "blessed by Yhwh and his Asherah" — a burial inscription, deeply personal.
- **Figurines:** 3,000+ mass-produced nude female figurines in domestic contexts. Overwhelmingly from 8th-6th centuries — the period of the prophets and reforms. "Found in almost every excavated house in Judah."
- **Dever's direct challenge to Smith:** "It is precisely my intent here to demonstrate that a cult of Asherah did flourish in ancient Israel."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The cumulative case from multiple independent archaeological data categories is overwhelming.

## Stakes

"Did God have a wife?" Dever's answer: Yes.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Mark S. Smith (argues for symbol-only reading), Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger (argue "his asherah" = cultic symbol, stylized tree, not goddess), Yehezkel Kaufmann (argues Israel was fundamentally monotheistic from the beginning).

**Alternative reading:** "His asherah" refers to a cultic symbol (stylized tree or wooden pole), not a goddess consort. The figurines may represent generic female figurines for fertility, not a named goddess.

## Assessment

Dever's evidence significantly strengthens the goddess interpretation over Smith's text-based skepticism. The cumulative archaeological case — inscriptions, figurines, temple architecture, cult stands — makes the goddess reading more parsimonious than the symbol-only reading. However, Keel/Uehlinger's counter-argument (paratactic motifs, no unified divine couple scene) remains a live alternative that Dever does not fully engage.
