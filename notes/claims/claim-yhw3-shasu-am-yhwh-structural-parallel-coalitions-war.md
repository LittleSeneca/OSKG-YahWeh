# fleming-ybi-6.6: Yhwʒ of Shasu-land and 'am Yhwh are structurally the same — coalitions of kinship groups united for war

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 6 — The People of Yahweh]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 6

## The Claim

"If by far the oldest attestation of the name attests an inland pastoralist people, not a god, how could a people give its name to the deity who became the focus of worship in the two kingdoms of Israel and Judah?" (p. 242). The link is structural: both Yhwʒ and 'am Yhwh name coalitions of independent groups united for mutual defense.

## Evidence

- Yhwʒ of Shasu-land: one of 4-5 named subgroups within "Shasu-land," encountered by Egypt in a single military confrontation, organized by kinship (mhwt = "families/tribes") (Ch 2)
- 'Am Yhwh of Judges 5: an alliance of ~10 named groups, each making independent decisions about military participation, with no permanent political structure
- Both are coalitions of collectives — groups defined by kinship rather than by cities or kings — uniting against external enemies
- Both are identified by the name Yhw(h): the Shasu subgroup Yhwʒ and the biblical alliance 'am Yhwh
- The Egyptian mhwt category for Shasu subdivisions matches the kinship-based organization visible in the Song of Deborah's list of peoples
- "As an alliance, this people of Yahweh consists of collectives on all levels in a mode that in other contexts would be taken to indicate kinship-based social organization of the sort represented also by the 'families' or 'tribes' (mhwt) of the Shasu in Egyptian conception" (p. 244)
- "The conceptual distance between 'Yahweh-people' and 'people of Yahweh' is not great, and with this use, the Song of Deborah brings us closest to a trace of how one of the ancient Shasu peoples could have given its name to a god" (p. 218)
- The geography is compatible: the Shasu inhabited the inland back country wrapping around the Levantine highlands, which is where Judg 5's peoples lived (p. 249)
- "Both would represent large entities gathered to fight an external foe... Nothing makes the comparison intrinsically out of scale" (p. 229)

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** The structural parallel is real and genuinely suggestive, but the temporal gap (600+ years between Yhwʒ c. 1400 and the composition of Judg 5's battle account, whatever its exact date) makes direct continuity impossible to demonstrate. Fleming acknowledges this: "we confront a chronological gap that resembles in some ways the one presented by Israel in the Merenptah stele" (p. 226).

## Stakes

If the parallel holds, Fleming has identified a genuine historical continuity connecting 14th-century Shasu to first-millennium Israelite tradition. This is the strongest possible evidence for his "Yahweh before Israel" thesis.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Anyone skeptical of identifying Yhwʒ with 'am Yhwh across a 600-year gap with no intermediate evidence.

**Alternative reading:** The similarity is typological (many ancient peoples formed military alliances) rather than genealogical. Yhwʒ and 'am Yhwh happen to share the same name, but the structural similarity is coincidental.

## Assessment

Fleming is honest about the speculative nature of this claim. The structural parallel is intriguing and has not been previously identified. But the evidentiary gap is vast, and the argument depends on accepting multiple prior conclusions (Ch 2: Yhwʒ as people-name; Ch 4: Seir absent from Soleb; Ch 6: 'am Yhwh as pre-Israelite). Each link in the chain is individually plausible but collectively fragile.
