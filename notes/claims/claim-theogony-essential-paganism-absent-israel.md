# kaufmann-ri-2.2: Theogony is essential to all pagan religion and absent from Israel

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch II — Pagan Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter II

## The Claim

"The limitation of divine powers finds its source in the theogonies that are part of every mythology. The gods emerge out of the primordial substance, having been generated by its boundless fertility. It is not the gods and their will that exist at first, but the primordial realm with its inherent forces." What is decisive: gods are born by natural, involuntary procreation. This is entirely absent from the Bible — Yahweh has no birth, parentage, or emergence from primordial substance.

## Evidence

- Sumerian: Nammu "the mother who gave birth to all the gods" → An+Ki → Enlil, Nanna, Utu by successive generation
- Babylonian: Apsu and Tiamat "mingled their waters" → successive generations; Ea begot Marduk
- Egyptian: Atum formed within Nun, engendered Shu and Tefnet upon himself; Re fathers Nut who gives birth to him daily
- Canaanite: Ras Shamra deities are "a community of husbands and wives, fathers and sons" — "their pagan mark is that they are born"
- Greek: Hesiod's Chaos → Gaea → Uranus → Cronus → Zeus — natural sexual reproduction
- Indian: Purusha divided himself, engendered humankind; "from him came the gods also"
- All others: Zoroastrian (Zervan fathers Ormazd/Ahriman), Germanic (Ymir → Buri → Bur → Odin), Japanese (Izanagi/Izanami), Gnostic emanations, Chinese P'an Ku
- "Primitive monotheism" claimants fail: high gods have mythological traits — families, needs, births, magical dependencies

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Theogony is near-universal in surveyed religions; its absence from the biblical corpus is a genuine comparative datum. Kaufmann's strongest empirical point.

## Stakes

Absence of theogony in Israel is one of Kaufmann's strongest comparative observations. The Bible NEVER tells a story of Yahweh's birth. But this doesn't prove absence from EARLY Israelite religion — only from the biblical text. Smith and Cross argue Yahweh's identification with El (who lacks theogony at Ugarit — El just IS, as "Father of Years") is HOW Israel avoided theogony: by absorbing a god who already lacked birth narratives.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-three-stage-convergence-el-yahweh]] — Smith argues El himself lacks a clear theogony; Yahweh absorbed El's identity, so absence of theogony is El's legacy. Cross sees El-Yahweh identification as the mechanism. [[claim-yhwh-originated-southern-deserts-edom-seir]] — Yahweh's southern origins were unknown to Canaanite scribes, so no theogony developed. Kaufmann may be right about the FACT but wrong about the EXPLANATION.

**Alternative reading:** The absence could be secondary. Deut 32:8-9 (LXX/DSS) preserves a theology where Elyon apportions nations to his divine sons — implying divine parent-child relationship without narrating birth. Israel may have HAD a theogonic framework (El as father of Yahweh) that was suppressed as monotheism developed.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's strongest empirical point. The absence of theogony in the Hebrew Bible IS a genuine comparative anomaly. Kaufmann's explanation (Israel was different from the beginning) is one possibility. Smith's explanation (El had no theogony; Yahweh absorbed El) is another. The evidence from Deut 32:8-9 and Psalm 82 favors Smith: Israel HAD a theogonic framework but progressively suppressed it.
