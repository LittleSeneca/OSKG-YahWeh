# heiser-ur-3.1: The divine council is a pervasive biblical reality across multiple genres and books — not an obscure pagan import

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 2 — The Households of God]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 3

## The Claim

The divine council is not a single obscure verse or a pagan concept smuggled into the Bible. It is a recurring theme across narrative, poetry, and prophetic literature. The Hebrew Bible consistently depicts Yahweh presiding over an assembly of divine beings (*elohim*).

## Evidence

**Narrative texts:**
- 1 Kings 22:19-22: Micaiah's vision — "I saw Yahweh sitting on his throne with all the host of heaven standing beside him"
- Job 1-2: "The sons of God came to present themselves before Yahweh" — including *haśśāṭān*, "the adversary"

**Prophetic vision:**
- Isaiah 6: "I saw the Lord sitting on a throne... seraphim were standing above him"

**Poetic texts:**
- Psalm 89:5-7: "Who in the sky is equal to Yahweh? Who is like Yahweh among the sons of God, a God feared greatly in the council of the holy ones?"

**The key terms Heiser catalogues:**

| Hebrew Term | Translation | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| *bĕnê hā'ĕlōhîm* | "sons of God" | Job 1:6, 2:1, 38:7 |
| *bĕnê 'ēlîm* | "sons of gods/God" | Psalm 29:1, 89:7 |
| *sôd qĕdōšîm* | "council of holy ones" | Psalm 89:8 |
| *'ădat 'ēl* | "assembly of El" | Psalm 82:1 |
| *kōl ṣĕbā' haššāmayim* | "all the host of heaven" | 1 Kings 22:19 |

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** The divine council is not a single obscure verse — it is a pervasive concept across multiple genres (narrative, poetry, prophecy) and multiple books (1 Kings, Job, Isaiah, Psalms).

## Stakes

If the divine council is a pervasive biblical reality, then any theology that claims the Bible teaches only one divine being exists is filtering out a massive amount of textual evidence. This doesn't necessarily mean polytheism — Heiser argues for a hierarchical model where Yahweh is supreme over other real *elohim* — but it does mean the "only one God exists" reading is textually unsustainable.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional Jewish and Christian theologians who maintain that the Bible teaches strict monotheism (only one divine being exists). Scholars who read the divine council as evidence of Israel's polytheistic past (Smith, Day) — they agree the council is in the text but disagree about its theological significance.

**Alternative reading:** The divine council passages are poetic metaphors, rhetorical flourishes, or remnants of polytheistic folklore that the biblical editors failed to purge — not a genuine theological commitment of the biblical authors.
