# sommer-bog-6.1: The fluidity model survived in kabbalah and Christianity

**Source:** [[Sommer — The Bodies of God]] — Benjamin D. Sommer, *The Bodies of God and the World of Ancient Israel* (2009), Chapter 6

## The Claim

The fluid God of J and E was suppressed by P and D, but never eliminated. It resurfaced in Jewish mysticism (kabbalah) and Christianity as recoveries of an older, suppressed biblical tradition.

"The Kabbalists recovered a genuinely ancient way of thinking about God that the mainstream of Jewish tradition had rejected."

"Christianity represents, in part, a return to the J and E model of divine fluidity."

## Evidence

**Jewish mysticism (kabbalah):**
- The *sefirot* — ten manifestations or "emanations" of the infinite God (*Ein Sof*)
- Each *sefirah* is a distinct aspect of God, yet all are one — precisely the fluid selfhood model
- The *Shekhinah* — the feminine, indwelling presence of God — is a survival of the old fluidity model, a localized divine manifestation parallel to the *mal'akh*
- Kabbalistic theology formally maintains monotheism while embracing divine multiplicity — exactly the structure Sommer identifies in J and E

**Christianity:**
- The Trinity — Father, Son, and Spirit are distinct yet one God — structurally analogous to a god with multiple overlapping selves
- The incarnation — God taking on a physical body in Jesus — is the ultimate expression of material anthropomorphism
- The Eucharist — God physically present in multiple locations simultaneously — is the cult-statue-as-god concept applied to bread and wine

**The implication:** The Jewish/Christian split is partly about which model of divine presence to embrace. Judaism (following P and D) emphasizes transcendence. Christianity (following older traditions Sommer argues survived underground) embraces fluidity and embodiment. Both are legitimate developments of biblical tradition.

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** LOW-MEDIUM on the direct lineage from J/E to kabbalah/Trinity (Sommer acknowledges this is speculative). HIGH that the conceptual parallels are real. MEDIUM that they reflect influence rather than independent development of similar ideas.

## Stakes

If Sommer is right about the survival of the fluidity model, then kabbalah and Christianity are not "corruptions" of biblical monotheism — they are recoveries of an older, authentic biblical tradition that P and D suppressed. This is a radical claim with implications for Jewish-Christian dialogue: the two religions are not "the original + a corruption" but two different choices about which biblical tradition to privilege.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Mainstream Jewish theologians (for whom kabbalah is a deviation from rabbinic normativity), mainstream Christian theologians (for whom the Trinity is a revelation, not a recovery of J), historians who doubt any direct lineage from J/E to medieval kabbalah.

**Alternative reading:** The parallels between J/E fluidity and kabbalah/Christianity are independent developments of similar ideas, not survivals. The *sefirot* emerged from Neoplatonic philosophy, not from suppressed biblical traditions. The Trinity emerged from Christological debates, not from J/E theology.

## Assessment

This is Sommer's most speculative but also most interesting claim. The conceptual parallels between J/E fluidity and kabbalah are striking — the *sefirot* really do function like the multiple divine manifestations Sommer describes. The Trinity parallel is less precise: the Christian Trinity is three persons, not three localized bodies of the same person. The incarnation is a better parallel — God taking on a physical body is J/E theology taken to its logical extreme. Whether these are survivals or independent developments is ultimately less important than Sommer's demonstration that fluid monotheism is a recurring theological possibility, not a primitive stage to be outgrown.
