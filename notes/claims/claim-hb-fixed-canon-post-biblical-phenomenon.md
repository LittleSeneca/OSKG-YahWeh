# schmid-ht-11.1: The Hebrew Bible as a fixed canon is a post-biblical phenomenon — the texts are pre-biblical and become biblical only through canonization

**Source:** [[Schmid — §11 — The Tanakh as the Hebrew Bible]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §11

## The Claim

"Historically speaking, the Hebrew Bible in the sense of a complete list of holy writings, set in terms of its textual inventory, constitutes a post-biblical phenomenon. Or, to formulate it the other way around, the writings contained in the Bible, are, strictly speaking, pre-biblical and only become biblical through the process of canonization."

## Evidence

- Qumran manuscripts show textual fluidity: "the texts of the Hebrew Bible were not fixed down to the letter at the turn of the era"
- Different versions of the same biblical book existed with "small variations" at Qumran
- The inventory of "biblical" books was not set: at Qumran, Jubilees and Enochic literature "do not appear to have been less important than what later turned out to be included in the standard inventory"
- The term "canon" for the collection of biblical books is first attested for the Christian Bible in the 4th century CE; in Judaism, it only became common in the wake of David Ruhnken's 1768 work
- The acronym "Tanakh" is first attested in the Middle Ages

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Qumran evidence for textual fluidity is well-established. The late dating of canon terminology is not controversial.

## Stakes

If the canon is a post-biblical phenomenon, then the canon's theological authority is a function of reception history, not an inherent property of the texts. This is Schmid's liberation move: he can study the theology OF the texts without being bound by the canon's normative claims ABOUT the texts.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional Christian and Jewish theologians who treat the canon as divinely established rather than historically constructed. Canonical approach scholars (Childs, Seitz) who argue the canon IS the theologically significant framework even if historically it emerged gradually.

**Alternative reading:** The canon's gradual formation doesn't negate its theological significance. The community's recognition of these texts as authoritative is itself a theological act, even if it unfolded over centuries.

## Assessment

This is standard critical scholarship — not Schmid's innovation, but essential groundwork. It establishes that the "Bible" as a fixed entity is a later construction applied to texts that were fluid in both form and status during their composition.
