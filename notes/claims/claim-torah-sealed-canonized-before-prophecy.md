# kaufmann-ri-5.8: Torah Sealed and Canonized Before Prophetic Literature

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch V — The Sources]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch V

## The Claim

"The Torah book was formed out of the desire of a penitent community to know and keep the law of God." The Torah literature "became the first stratum of the Bible, sealed and canonized at a time when prophetic literature was still unordered and still without decisive influence on the life of the people. The entire book is a testimony to the first age of Israelite religion, the age prior to literary prophecy."

## Evidence

**Kaufmann's proposed strata (from earliest to latest):**
1. Genesis 1-11 (Near Eastern materials; folkloristic naïveté)
2. Genesis 12-49 (formative acts/words; no prophecy)
3. JEP — Genesis-Numbers (no trace of Deuteronomic ideas)
4. Deuteronomy-II Kings (centralization influence apparent)
5-7. The three law corpora (JE laws, P laws, D laws)
8. The Torah-group as a whole (Genesis-II Kings; no prophetic influence)
9. Literary prophets (the climax of biblical religion)
10. Hagiographa (post-exilic synthesis)

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-low

**Rationale:** This is the conclusion toward which the entire chapter has been driving — and it's the least supported by the evidence presented. It requires dismissing the linguistic evidence for P's lateness (which Kaufmann doesn't even engage), reinterpreting ALL evidence of institutional development as archaic, treating P's silence about centralization as proof of ignorance rather than programmatic idealism, and ignoring the convergence of multiple independent lines of evidence that point to the Wellhausen sequence.

## Stakes

Everything. If the Torah was "sealed and canonized" before prophecy, then monotheistic law existed before the prophets. The prophets become traditionalists, not innovators. Israelite religion was monotheistic from the beginning. The entire critical reconstruction collapses.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Effectively everyone. Even scholars sympathetic to Kaufmann's overall project (Tigay, Sommer) do not follow him on P's dating. Tigay's onomastic evidence supports EARLY MONOLATRY but he doesn't date P early. Sommer accepts Kaufmann's literary insights about the distinction between Torah and prophetic worldviews but doesn't endorse his chronological reversal. The linguistic evidence for Late Biblical Hebrew in P — which Kaufmann does not address in this chapter — is one of the strongest results of biblical scholarship and directly contradicts his thesis.

**Alternative reading:** The Torah-group's independence from literary prophecy doesn't require chronological priority. It could equally reflect PARALLEL DEVELOPMENT: priestly Torah circles and prophetic circles produced different kinds of literature over the SAME centuries. The Torah was canonized first (which Kaufmann is right about) because it was considered more foundational, not because it was composed first.

## Assessment

This is where Kaufmann's entire project stands or falls — and it's where he's weakest. The claim requires: dismissing the linguistic evidence for P's lateness (which he doesn't even engage), reinterpreting ALL evidence of institutional development as archaic rather than evolved, treating P's silence about centralization as proof of ignorance rather than programmatic idealism, and ignoring the convergence of multiple independent lines of evidence (language, institutions, historical references, literary relationships) that point to the Wellhausen sequence. Kaufmann has identified genuine weaknesses in the Wellhausen model — but these weaknesses don't require his radical reversal. They require a MORE NUANCED version of the critical model — not its abandonment.
