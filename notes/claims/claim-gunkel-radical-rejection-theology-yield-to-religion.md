# schmid-ht-5.4: Gunkel's radical rejection of theology — yield to religion

**Source:** [[Schmid — §5 — Romantic Devaluation of Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §5

## The Claim

Gunkel argued that both "theology" AND "biblical" were problematic terms. "Theology" because Schleiermacher showed religion is more than reflection. "Biblical" because the canon boundary is artificial — "the maturing and blossoming religion from the soil of the Israelite nation is an extraordinarily diverse picture, in which all its forms and orientations, higher and baser, should be recognized."

## Evidence

- Gunkel, RGG (2nd ed.): "the living religion of the heart was quite prominent, while reflection on religion plays a comparatively minor role. Neither the nature of the Israelite people nor the period tended toward reflection"
- "The fence implied in the word 'biblical' continues to fall"
- "If one looks deeper...one notices that [biblical theology] is dominated by the early church doctrine of inspiration"
- Gunkel's Genesis commentary foreword: "Whoever calls himself a theologian must study religion"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Gunkel quotations are direct and unambiguous. Schmid's presentation of the strongest opposing view is intellectually honest.

## Stakes

Gunkel represents the extreme version of the position Schmid is arguing AGAINST. If Gunkel was right — that Israelite religion was primarily "living religion of the heart" with minimal theological reflection — then Schmid's project (a theology of the HB as reflective literature) is fundamentally misguided. Schmid needs to show that Gunkel was wrong about the texts' character, not just about the discipline's method.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Schmid himself — his entire project is a counter-argument to Gunkel. Any scholar who sees the HB as containing genuine theological reflection.

**Alternative reading:** Gunkel was right about the method (historical criticism illuminates religion) but wrong about the texts (they DO contain reflective theology). Schmid can accept Gunkel's historical method while rejecting his characterization of the texts.

## Assessment

Schmid quoted Gunkel at length, and I appreciate his intellectual honesty in presenting the strongest version of the opposing view. But Schmid hasn't yet answered Gunkel's substantive claim: that the HB doesn't contain much "theology" in the reflective sense. Schmid will need Parts E-H to demonstrate that the texts DO contain the kind of reflective, synthesizing theology he claims. Gunkel's challenge hangs over the entire project.
