# albertz-hir-3.3-3.4.2: David's religious policy was deliberate Israelite-Jebusite syncretism; Solomon tipped to the Canaanite side

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §3.3-3.4 — State Cults of Jerusalem and Bethel]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §3.3

## The Claim

David appointed TWO priests: Abiathar (from the old Israelite Shiloh priesthood) and Zadok (the former Jebusite priest of Jerusalem). This was a deliberate balancing act. Solomon exiled Abiathar and gave the priestly monopoly to the Zadokides. "Solomon evidently already saw himself as so much a ruler analogous to the Canaanite sacral monarchy that he no longer felt it necessary to take into account any institutional links... with the pre-state traditions of Yahweh." Consequently, "the official Yahweh religion of the Jerusalem temple shows hardly any awareness of the liberation traditions of the early period."

## Evidence

- 2 Sam 8:17; 20:25: two priests appointed — Abiathar (Eli lineage from Shiloh) and Zadok (Jebusite)
- 1 Kings 2:26f.: Solomon banishes Abiathar to Anathoth; Zadokites gain monopoly (1 Kings 4:2)
- The tradition deliberately obscures the pre-Israelite sanctuary: claims David kept the ark in a tent for 40 years, that the temple site was a threshing floor (2 Sam 24:16ff.), that no sanctuary existed before
- But 2 Sam 12:20 incidentally mentions David visiting "the temple of Yahweh" (bêt YHWH) — proving a pre-existing sanctuary
- "The report of the building of a 'Solomonic temple' in 1 Kings 6 suggests more a rebuilding than a complete new building; it was a former Jebusite temple which Solomon had renovated"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The two-priest tradition and the Abiathar-Zadok conflict are well-attested. The claim that the Solomonic temple was a Jebusite renovation is more speculative but well-argued from the text's own inconsistencies.

## Stakes

If the Jerusalem temple was built on a Jebusite foundation with a Jebusite priesthood, then the central institution of Israelite religion was fundamentally Canaanite in origin. The "Yahweh" worshipped there was substantially fused with El-Elyon and other Canaanite deities from the beginning.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who defend the historicity of the Solomonic temple as a completely new construction, or who see Zadok as an Israelite priest whose Jebusite origin is a later polemical fiction.

**Alternative reading:** Zadok may have been an Israelite (not Jebusite) priest, and the "two priests" may reflect two Israelite priestly families (Mushite vs. Aaronide) rather than Israelite vs. Canaanite. The "temple of Yahweh" in 2 Sam 12:20 could be a later editorial insertion.

## Assessment

The two-priest tradition is one of the most intriguing historical details in the Samuel-Kings narrative. Albertz's reading of it as deliberate syncretistic balancing is plausible, and the textual inconsistencies around the temple's origin (was it new or a renovation?) are real. The argument that the Solomonic temple was a Jebusite renovation is speculative but consistent with the evidence — and if true, has major implications for understanding Jerusalem theology's Canaanite substrate.
