# fleming-ybi-7.6: The early Israelite pantheon cannot be reliably reconstructed

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 7 — The Early Character of the God Yahweh]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 7

## The Claim

"In the decentralized social and political landscape of the early southern Levant, the idea of pantheon is more likely to lead astray than to guide" (p. 292). "When Yahweh came on the scene, was it taken for granted that he had to be fitted into a pantheon with El as head? Perhaps, but I wonder whether we have relied too much on the structures of major kingdoms to reconstruct the religion of small towns and a back country beyond" (p. 293).

## Evidence

- Ugarit had over 100 named deities receiving offerings at the zukru festival (p. 285) — but Ugarit was a wealthy city incorporated into the Hittite empire, not a model for the early Israelite highlands
- Deut 32:8-9 and Psalm 82, the key texts for reconstructing an Israelite pantheon, are late innovations: "the assignment of nations to gods is political and administrative, the act of a world-ruler in patrimonial mode" (p. 290)
- "Both the world oversight and the equation of Yahweh with Elyon may be understood not as prior traditions but as belonging to the setting of the author" (p. 291)
- These texts reflect imperial world-theology (Assyrian, Babylonian, Achaemenid) projected backward — "the novelty of the notion suggests innovation" (p. 290)
- "Where the social settings did not demand strict hierarchy and assignment of relationships, the gods would likewise not have been set in order" (p. 292)
- Schloen's "patrimonial" model assumes too much centralized authority — "the same variety of social configuration would have applied to the gods" (p. 292)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The caution is well-taken: we genuinely don't know how the divine world was structured in pre-monarchic Israel. But Fleming may overstate the case — the divine council imagery in the Bible (1 Kgs 22, Job 1, Isaiah 6) is too pervasive to dismiss entirely as late innovation.

## Stakes

This is a methodological caution, not a positive claim. Fleming is warning against overconfidence in pantheon reconstruction. If he's right, Smith's four-tier model is more speculative than usually acknowledged.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Smith (2001a, 2004) and the Ugaritic-comparative school, who treat the divine council as a fundamental feature of West Semitic religion recoverable from biblical fragments.

**Alternative reading:** The divine council is attested across the ancient Near East (Ugarit, Mesopotamia, Israel) and is too widespread to be a late innovation. The specific form varies, but the concept is ancient.

## Assessment

Fleming's caution is methodologically sound — we should acknowledge what we don't know. But I suspect he overcorrects. The divine council concept is deeply embedded and unlikely to be entirely late. The real question is not whether Israel had a concept of multiple gods in relationship, but what that relationship looked like in different periods and social settings.
