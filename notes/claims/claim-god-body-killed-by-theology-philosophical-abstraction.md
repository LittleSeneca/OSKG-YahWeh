# stav-god-16-21.4: God's body was 'killed' — over centuries, Jewish and Christian intellectuals shaped by Greek philosophy replaced the living, breathing, embodied God of the Bible with an immaterial abstraction, and modern worshippers have been praying to a ghost.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part V — Head + Epilogue]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 21 (Epilogue: An Autopsy)

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou's closing argument: the real God of the Bible is a stranger to modern Judaism and Christianity. "Over centuries, Jewish and Christian intellectuals, shaped by Greek philosophy, killed that body. They replaced the living, breathing God of the Bible with an immaterial abstraction." The book has been performing an anatomical dissection of God's body, part by part. The epilogue is the autopsy report: cause of death = philosophical abstraction; time of death = gradual, over the first millennium CE; the body was never recovered — it was explained away.

## Evidence

- The trajectory from biblical corporeality to rabbinic/Mishnaic abstraction to Maimonidean incorporeality (12th century CE)
- Christian trajectory: biblical embodiment → early church fathers' ambivalence (Tertullian's corporealism vs. Origen's allegorism) → Augustinian spiritualization → medieval Scholastic immaterialism
- The mechanism: Greek philosophical categories (Plato's Forms, Aristotle's Unmoved Mover) provided the conceptual tools to reinterpret biblical body language as metaphor
- The result: "Worshippers have been praying to a ghost" — the abstract God bears no resemblance to the embodied deity who walked in Eden, ate with Abraham, and crushed Egypt with his right hand
- Stavrakopoulou uses the autopsy metaphor: the book has dissected God's body part by part, and the epilogue declares the cause of death

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The historical development from embodied to abstract God is well-established; the moral valence (whether the embodied God is "more authentic") is a value judgment.

## Stakes

If Stavrakopoulou is right, the God worshipped by 4 billion Jews, Christians, and Muslims is not the God of the Bible — it is a philosophical construct that replaced the biblical deity over centuries. This is the most radical claim in the book: not just that the biblical God had a body, but that the God modern worshippers pray to is a different being entirely.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The entire theological traditions of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, which maintain continuity between the biblical God and the God they worship

**Alternative reading:** The development from embodied language to abstract theology is not a "killing" but a maturation — the biblical writers used embodied language because it was the only vocabulary available, but the theological truth they pointed toward was always beyond embodiment. Incorporeality is the fulfillment of biblical revelation, not its betrayal.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou's epilogue is her most rhetorical and least analytical chapter. The autopsy metaphor is effective but overstates: God's body wasn't "killed" so much as progressively spiritualized, and the process began WITHIN the Hebrew Bible itself (as Sommer demonstrates with Priestly and Deuteronomistic anti-fluidity theologies), not as a Greek philosophical imposition from outside. The more precise claim is that post-biblical theology RADICALIZED an anti-corporeal trajectory that had already begun in the Bible's own later strata. Stavrakopoulou's atheist editorializing ("praying to a ghost") undermines what would otherwise be a powerful historical observation: modern abstract theism IS a departure from the biblical God's embodied character.
