# day-ygc-6.1: The sun cult in Judah was native Canaanite, not an Assyrian import

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 6 — Yahweh and the Astral Deities]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 6

## The Claim

"Various pieces of evidence suggest that the sun cult was Canaanite rather than Assyrian" (p. 152). Against the view that Manasseh's astral cults and Josiah's reform targeted Assyrian-influenced worship.

## Evidence

1. **All other cults Josiah removed were Canaanite:** Baal, Asherah, Molech (2 Kgs 23). "Consequently, unless there is strong evidence to the contrary, we should expect the sun and astral cults to be Canaanite as well" (p. 153)
2. **Place names:** Beth-Shemesh "house of the sun" (5 sites across Palestine), En-Shemesh "spring of the sun," Timnath-heres "portion of the sun" (later distorted to Timnath-serah to avoid polytheistic allusion). "A higher number of theophoric place names than those of any deity apart from El and Baal" (p. 153)
3. **Thutmose III's Karnak list** (15th c. BCE): Shamash-Edom — showing sun cult in Palestine predates Assyrian influence by centuries
4. **Taanach cult stand** (10th c. BCE): Horse and sun disc on top tier — well before Assyrian period
5. **Hazor horse's head** (c. 925-905 BCE): Disc symbol on forehead — "the association of the horse with the sun had clearly already been a feature of Syro-Palestinian religion for several centuries" (p. 154). 2 Kgs 23:11 mentions "horses and chariots of the sun" removed from temple
6. **Josh 10:12-13 is NOT evidence of sun/moon cult:** The sun "standing still" at Gibeon reflects a thunderstorm blotting out sun and moon (cf. Hab 3:10-11), not a cult. Day provides an extensive footnote surveying alternatives (p. 155-157)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The pre-Assyrian archaeological evidence (Taanach, Hazor) and place names make the Canaanite origin clear. The argument from Josiah's reform context (all other removed cults were Canaanite) is methodologically sound.

## Stakes

If the sun cult was native Canaanite, Israel's solar worship wasn't late foreign contamination but early indigenous religion. The Deuteronomistic opposition to sun worship wasn't anti-Assyrian polemic — it was anti-Canaanite.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Spieckermann, Oestreicher, Gressmann — Assyrian influence on Manasseh's astral cults.

**Alternative reading:** Manasseh imported Assyrian astral cults as part of vassal obligations, and Josiah's reform was anti-Assyrian nationalist reaction.

## Assessment

The Taanach and Hazor evidence is decisive for the pre-Assyrian date. Day's point that "Shamash" would have been used if Mesopotamian, but the OT always uses *šemeš* — the Canaanite term — supports the argument by implication.
