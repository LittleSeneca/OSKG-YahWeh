# kaufmann-ri-5.7: Torah Compiled Without Revision — Sources Already Fixed

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch V — The Sources]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch V

## The Claim

"The age of the Restoration was an age of compilation, not of edition and revision, let alone of innovation." The compilers transmitted the ancient scrolls intact, unrevised, without harmonizing contradictions — because the texts were already regarded as sacred and unalterable.

## Evidence

1. **Narrative sources were blended, but legal corpora were preserved intact.** The redactors blended parallel narratives into one account, but "the laws, however, have not been so blended. Duplications and contradictions have neither been eliminated nor harmonized." This is evidence that the legal texts were ALREADY FIXED and too sacred to touch.

2. **Embarrassing material was preserved unchanged.** The Torah retains stories "certainly offensive to later religious tastes" — the "sons of God" consorting with women, God walking in the garden at the cool of the day, Reuben with Bilhah, Judah with the "harlot," patriarchs erecting pillars and marrying forbidden relatives. "If the literature was still in flux...how could this embarrassing material have survived unaltered?"

3. **Obsolete laws were retained.** The tithe laws, the Levitical provisions, the home Passover — all "obsolete" by post-exilic times, yet preserved unrevised. The community accepted these laws "as 'found written' in the Torah" and tried to harmonize them midrashically rather than altering them.

4. **No new laws were added.** Despite the pressing need, "no explicit law providing for an obligatory priestly tithe made its way into the Torah." Even when economic realities made the existing law unworkable, the solution was midrashic reinterpretation (Ezra's "penalty"), not textual addition.

5. **Deuteronomy was the first stage, then JE+P were added.** "The nucleus of the Torah book was Deuteronomy, born out of the conviction that Israel could redeem itself only by steadfast adherence to the Torah of God." Later, "the ancient priestly materials transmitted from early times were added to the corpus of Deuteronomy."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** A genuinely valuable observation about the character of the Torah's compilation. Kaufmann is right that the preservation of contradictions, embarrassing stories, and obsolete laws is hard to explain if composition and compilation were happening simultaneously. But this only proves the texts were authoritative AT THE TIME OF COMPILATION (which Kaufmann dates to Ezra-Nehemiah), not that they were composed CENTURIES earlier.

## Stakes

If Kaufmann is right, the development of Torah literature STOPPED long before the exile. The post-exilic community were collectors, not composers. The Torah is a genuine witness to pre-exilic religion.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The critical tradition acknowledges that post-exilic compilers preserved earlier materials — nobody claims they FABRICATED everything. But the selection, arrangement, and framing of materials IS composition. The fact that embarrassing stories were preserved doesn't prove they were already "canonical" — it could reflect the compilers' commitment to preserving tradition even when it was awkward.

**Alternative reading:** A text can be treated as authoritative shortly after composition — especially if it's attributed to Moses. The preservation of embarrassing material and obsolete laws proves the compilers' REVERENCE for the texts they had, not the texts' absolute antiquity. And the lack of new additions to the Torah could simply mean the community considered the Torah CLOSED — a decision that could have been made at any point.

## Assessment

Kaufmann is right that the preservation of contradictions, embarrassing stories, and obsolete laws is hard to explain if composition and compilation were happening simultaneously. The most natural explanation IS that the texts were already considered authoritative when they were gathered. But this only proves the texts were authoritative AT THE TIME OF COMPILATION. A text can be composed, achieve authoritative status, and be preserved without revision — all within a single generation. The dynamic Kaufmann observes (texts treated as fixed and sacred) is compatible with composition in the exile or even early post-exilic period, followed by rapid canonization.
