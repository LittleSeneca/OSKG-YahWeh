# day-ygc-7.2: Resheph is a Canaanite underworld plague god whose arrows bring pestilence, preserved as a demonic personification in the OT

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 7 — Yahweh and the Underworld Deities]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 7

## The Claim

Resheph "was an underworld god, whose arrows brought plague and pestilence" (p. 199). In the OT, *rešep* often retains personalized demonic force rather than being fully demythologized.

## Evidence

1. **Ugaritic identification:** Resheph equated with Nergal (Mesopotamian underworld/plague god) at Ugarit (KTU² 1.47.27; 1.118.26). As sun goddess's gatekeeper (KTU² 1.78.2-4), he guarded the netherworld entrance. Epithets: "the archer Resheph" (*bʻl ḥẓ ršp*) in Ugaritic, "Resheph of the arrow" (*ršp ḥṣ*) in Phoenician (p. 199-200)
2. **Hab 3:5:** "Before him went Pestilence (*deber*) and Plague (*rešep*) followed close behind" — part of Yahweh's storm theophany. This mirrors Ugaritic KTU² 1.82 where "the archer Resheph shot his kidneys and his heart." The pattern of a leading god with two bodyguards is attested elsewhere (Adad with Shullat and Hanish in Gilgamesh) (p. 200-201)
3. **Ps 78:48-49:** "He gave over their cattle to the pestilence and their flocks to the plague (*rešāpîm*)." Day defends the emendation of MT *labbārād* "to the hail" to *laddeber* "to the pestilence" — because Ps 78 parallels the J plague narrative in Exodus and this reading completes all seven J plagues. The "destroying angels" in v. 49 likely allude to the *rešāpîm* (p. 201-202)
4. **Job 5:7:** "The sons of Resheph fly upwards." Day favors the mythological reading (demonic beings bringing pestilence from the underworld) over "sparks" (no certain meaning in Hebrew) or "birds" (the ancient versions' misunderstanding). This coheres with Job 5:6 — *ʻāpār* "dust" can denote the underworld (cf. Job 17:16). "Just as 'the firstborn of death' brings pestilence in Job 18:13... so here in Job 5:7 'the sons of [the underworld demon] Resheph' bring pestilence" (p. 203-204)
5. **Deut 32:24:** "fought by plague (*rešep*) and poisonous pestilence." The verb *lḥm* "fight" used with *rešep* echoes "the mythological archer god Resheph" — not just impersonal plague (p. 204)
6. **Cant 8:6 — demythologized but aware:** "Its arrows (*rešāpeyhā*) are arrows of (*rišpê*) fire." Love's arrows derive from Resheph's pestilential arrows. The juxtaposition with "love is strong as death" (*ʻazzâ kammāwet*) shows the poet was aware of the mythological background (Mot + Resheph both underworld deities) (p. 205)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The convergence of Ugaritic (Nergal equation, archer epithet), Phoenician (Resheph of the arrow), and OT (parallel to *deber*, flying imagery, fighting verb) is strong.

## Stakes

The OT doesn't just have "angels" — it preserves the names, imagery, and functions of specific Canaanite underworld demons. Resheph is the clearest case of a Canaanite demon surviving with his identity intact in biblical literature.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those translating *rešep* as "sparks" or "thunderbolts" in Job 5:7 (KJV, RSV, NRSV). The ancient versions that read "birds."

**Alternative reading:** *Rešep* in the OT is impersonal "plague" or "pestilence," fully demythologized, with no surviving demonic personality.

## Assessment

Day's case is robust. The Hab 3:5 parallel to Ugaritic KTU² 1.82 is particularly impressive — Resheph as divine bodyguard/attendant in both texts. The Ps 78 emendation argument is well-reasoned and solves a genuine textual problem (the missing J plague).
