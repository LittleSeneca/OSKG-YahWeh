# lewis-ocg-4.3: Ugaritic ʾIlu — Fullest El Portrait, Not Deposed by Baal

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 4 — El Worship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"The Late Bronze Age kingdom of Ugarit... has the fullest representation of the deity ʾIlu (= El) both in word and object of any ancient Near Eastern society." Critically: "Both El and Baal are called 'king' though in complementary ways: El remains the executive order of the universe and Baal is the sustainer of the cosmos. El is not deposed in an active struggle with Baal; nor is El degraded or demoted by Baal."

## Evidence

- ʾIlu presides over the divine council; dispenses wise decrees (KTU 1.4.4.41-43)
- Creates the gods Dawn and Dusk through sexual intercourse (KTU 1.23 — with explicit phallic imagery)
- Magically creates an exorcist from clay (KTU 1.16.5.25-28)
- Presides over the marzeaḥ drinking banquet (KTU 1.114, where he drinks to excess and collapses in his own excrement)
- Appears in dreams to Kirta; heals the dying king
- ʾIlu is "father" (ʾab) to gods and humans, "Bull ʾIlu" (virility symbol), "the father of years" (ʾab šnm) indicating eternity
- lṭpn ʾil d pʾid — "the Gracious One, the Benevolent God"
- The deity lists consistently place ʾIlu in prominent position
- He is NOT a deus otiosus — the ribald texts prove vitality, not impotence

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** Very high on the Ugaritic evidence; high that El was not deposed by Baal — now the consensus position (Mullen, Smith, Pitard have all argued against the older Kapelrud/Oldenburg conflict model).

## Stakes

Whether Israelite El was a diminished figure waiting to be replaced. The Ugaritic evidence shows El as fully functional, active, and supreme — just not the primary warrior. This means the Israelite merger of El and Yahweh didn't happen because El was weak; it happened because the functions were complementary.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Van der Toorn (1992a) argued El became a deus otiosus by the first millennium. Oldenburg (1969) saw a conflict between El and Baal. Lewis explicitly refutes both.

**Alternative reading:** The deus otiosus thesis — El was functionally retired in the Iron Age. But Lewis's Iron Age evidence (Claim 4.4) refutes this.

## Assessment

Lewis is correct and his detailed treatment of KTU 1.23 (the "Dawn and Dusk" text) and KTU 1.114 (the drinking binge) is a highlight — he uses texts that seem to show El as weak or ridiculous to argue the opposite. El's very human frailties (drunkenness, lust) are signs of his vitality, not his decline.
