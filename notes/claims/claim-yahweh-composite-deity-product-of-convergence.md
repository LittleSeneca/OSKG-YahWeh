# smith-ehg-7.1: Yahweh became a composite deity whose complexity is the product of the very processes the Bible condemns

**Source:** [[Smith Chapters 5-7 — Cult, Monotheism, Portraits]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Early History of God* (1990), Chapter 7

## The Claim

The Yahweh of the Bible is not a simple, consistent character. He's a composite — built from El's wisdom, Baal's storm, Asherah's nurturing, the sun's radiance, and Anat's ferocity. This wasn't an accident. It was the product of deliberate theological work over centuries.

**The processes that produced the biblical God:**

| Process | Example |
|---------|---------|
| **Addition** | Yahweh receives El's titles, Baal's storm language, Asherah's maternal imagery |
| **Paradox** | Yahweh is manifest IN storm and sun but transcends both; is male but described with female imagery |
| **Polemic as absorption** | Condemning Baal's storm-god status while claiming storm power for Yahweh |
| **Recontextualization** | Solar language moved from "Yahweh IS the sun" to "Yahweh CREATED the sun" (Gen 1:14) |
| **Reduced anthropomorphism** | Divine "name," "glory," and "face" replace direct bodily presence |

## Evidence

Smith synthesizes the evidence from chapters 1-4:

- **El's wisdom and titles:** Yahweh inherits El's role as aged patriarch, wise judge, and "father." The name Israel contains El, not Yahweh.
- **Baal's storm:** Yahweh is described as storm-god with thunder, lightning, cloud-chariot, and defeat of Sea/Leviathan — all Baal's portfolio.
- **Asherah's nurturing:** Female imagery (womb-compassion, nursing, birth-giving) applied to Yahweh reflects absorbed maternal divine roles.
- **Sun's radiance:** Solar language ("Yahweh is a sun and shield," Psalm 84:11; "sun of righteousness," Malachi 4:2) shows absorption of solar deity characteristics.
- **Anat's ferocity:** Martial goddess imagery (the fury of Yahweh drinking the blood of enemies) reflects Anat traditions absorbed into Yahweh.

**What was deliberately excluded from Yahweh:**

Some Canaanite divine roles were deliberately excluded:

- **Sex:** "Yahweh does not have a consort according to any biblical source; nor does he engage in divine sex."
- **Death:** "Yahweh does not die, even figuratively."
- **Drunkenness and carousing:** El's party behavior at Ugarit has no biblical parallel.

**Smith's explanation:** The priesthood, for whom holiness meant separation from sex and death, shaped the final form of Yahweh. "For the priesthood there were several levels of cultic purity, and the deity represented the epitome of this hierarchy." The God who emerged was a priestly God — sexless, deathless, and transcendent.

**Smith's poetic close:**

"Yahweh exercised a variety of roles, even sometimes conflicting ones, to the detriment of the cults of other deities. Yahweh sometimes embodied apparently contradictory capacities. Yahweh was seen as manifest in nature and beyond nature... imaged in the human person yet only partially imaginable... sufficiently powerful both to protect and punish Israel... Yahweh was equally a personal deity, whose pain matched Israel's pain... Yahweh's qualities were often expressed in terms largely shaped by the characteristics of other deities belonging to ancient Israel's heritage that Israel rejected in the course of time."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The evidence for convergence of specific deity profiles (El, Baal, Asherah, Sun) is strong across chapters 1-4, but the priestly-shaping explanation for what was excluded (sex, death) is plausible but speculative.

## Stakes

This is where Smith's project lands. The God you worship is a composite of at least four earlier deities, shaped by priestly editors who systematically removed divine sex and death from his profile. Whether this makes him MORE true (a genuine theological synthesis) or LESS true (a historical accident) depends entirely on your theological priors. Smith provides the history. You provide the theology.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional theologians who maintain Yahweh's character is revealed, not constructed. Scholars who dispute specific convergences (e.g., whether solar language genuinely reflects absorption of a solar deity or is just metaphorical).

**Alternative reading:** The composite portrait could be read as genuine theological development guided by revelation — God gradually revealing more of his character over time, using cultural categories as pedagogical tools. The convergence model could describe the MEANS of revelation rather than its negation.

## Assessment

Smith doesn't resolve the tension between the composite God as historical construction and genuine theological achievement. He doesn't need to. The composite God is BOTH — and Smith's intellectual honesty in presenting the data without forcing a theological resolution is the book's greatest strength.
