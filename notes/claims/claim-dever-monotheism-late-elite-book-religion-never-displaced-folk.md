# dever-dghw-6-9.3: Monotheism was a late, elite construction that never fully displaced folk polytheism

**Source:** [[Dever — Chapters VI-IX — Asherah to Conclusions]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Chapter VIII

## The Claim

"Israelite religion was everywhere and always polytheistic in practice until the very end of the monarchy — and for most people, even beyond." Monotheism was the invention of a small circle of reformers who wrote the Bible. It only became "the religion of Israel" because they won the battle of the books.

## Evidence

- The archaeological evidence of polytheistic practice continues UNABATED through the monarchy. Josiah's reform left no archaeological trace.
- "We have no archaeological evidence whatsoever that Josiah's reform had any lasting effect on folk religion"
- The prophets were a tiny minority — "the 'Yahweh-alone' movement was a minority party until the very end"
- The exile created monotheism not because everyone became monotheistic but because the elite who went into exile wrote the Bible
- "The very fact that the biblical writers condemn folk religion so vehemently is the best proof that it was widespread. You don't rail against things that aren't happening."
- The Canaanite background: Israelite religion grew organically from Canaanite religion. "There was little that was innovative in the biblical sacrificial system." The festivals (Sukkot, Pesach, Shavuot) were originally Canaanite agricultural celebrations later "historicized."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Consensus across Smith, Römer, and Dever; archaeological evidence of persisting polytheistic practice is robust.

## Stakes

The biblical narrative of Israel's journey from pure monotheism to syncretistic corruption and back is exactly backward. The Bible's religion was never the religion of most Israelites. This inverts both traditional Jewish and Christian understandings of Israel's religious history.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Yehezkel Kaufmann (argues Israel was fundamentally monotheistic from the beginning, idolatry was vestigial fetishism), Jeffrey Tigay (onomastic evidence shows minimal polytheism in personal names).

**Alternative reading:** The prophets represent a genuine popular monotheistic consciousness, not an elite minority imposing on a polytheistic majority. The exile extinguished idolatry because monotheism had already taken root among the people (Kaufmann).

## Assessment

This is the consensus position and the evidence is strong. The key vulnerability: Dever, like Römer, may overstate the exile's role as sole catalyst versus longer developmental processes. The archaeological evidence for CONTINUITY of polytheistic practice is robust; the evidence for its complete DISPLACEMENT by exile-era monotheism is more inferential.
