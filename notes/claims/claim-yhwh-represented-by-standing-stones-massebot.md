# romer-inv-8.1: Yhwh was represented by standing stones (maṣṣēbôt) that functioned as divine images

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 8 — Statue of Yhwh]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 8: The Statue of Yhwh in Judah

## The Claim

The traditional view that Israelite religion was aniconic from the beginning is wrong. Standing stones (*maṣṣēbôt*) functioned as representations of Yhwh. "The masseba was a way of representing the god Yhwh." The prohibition on images (Exodus 20:4) is late, not original.

## Evidence

- *Maṣṣēbôt* had four biblical functions: funerary markers, commemorative memorials, contract witnesses, and **divine representations** — the last category is direct evidence that some *maṣṣēbôt* were cult images
- **Genesis 28:** Jacob anoints a *masseba* at Bethel and calls it "a house of God" (*bêt 'ĕlōhîm*) — he treats the stone AS the divine dwelling, not as a marker for where a vision occurred
- **Arad temple:** Two steles in its holy of holies — "representing Yhwh and another deity" (probably Asherah). This is material evidence for a divine couple in an official Israelite sanctuary
- **Mari (2nd millennium):** Betyles and statues COEXIST — "it would be a mistake to oppose aniconism and iconism sharply in this period." Some steles were sculpted with human features and painted
- **Arad steles had traces of paint** — "possibly covered with painted images of the gods who were worshipped there"
- **The prohibition texts are the smoking gun:** Deuteronomy 16:22 and Leviticus 26:1 prohibit BOTH statues (*pesel*) AND standing stones (*maṣṣēbāh*) — proving they were considered equivalent. "In this prohibition the terms *pesel* and *maṣṣēbāh* refer to the cult of Yhwh"

### The Two-Stone Problem at Arad

Two steles in the holy of holies — one certainly representing Yhwh. The other? "Yhwh and his Asherah" is the most natural reading. This is material evidence for a divine couple in an official Judahite sanctuary.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The prohibition texts themselves are the strongest evidence — you don't ban something unless people are doing it. The Arad steles provide material corroboration.

## Stakes

If Yhwh was represented by standing stones that were treated as divine images, the biblical claim that Israel was always aniconic collapses. The second commandment was not a restoration of original purity but an innovation. The invisible, transcendent God is a late construction — earlier Israelites could SEE their God, in stone.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservatives who maintain aniconism was original, treating standing stones as purely commemorative markers. The archaeological interpretation of Arad's two steles is debated — some argue they were purely symbolic without representational function.

**Alternative reading:** The *massebot* were memorial stones or covenant witnesses, not divine representations. The prohibitions in Deuteronomy and Leviticus reflect a later conflation of distinct practices — standing stones weren't originally considered "idols" but were later grouped with them polemically. Arad's steles could represent abstract divine presence rather than the deity itself.

## Assessment

The prohibition texts treating *massebot* and *pesel* as equivalent are the strongest part of Römer's case. The Arad evidence is significant but the "second deity" identification is necessarily speculative without inscriptions. However, the convergence of textual prohibition + archaeological evidence + ANE comparative data makes the case persuasive.
