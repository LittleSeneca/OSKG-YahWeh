# keel-ggi-p1.2: The Naked Goddess was transformed into a uniquely Canaanite "Branch Goddess" for mass-market scarabs

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part I — Middle Bronze Age IIB]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part I

## The Claim

"Fertility and prosperity are accentuated on the scarabs, not the sexuality and eroticism of palace life. The goddess is more patently and directly associated with fertility on the scarabs, especially the fertility of the vegetation, than is the case on the Old Syrian cylinder seals." On cylinder seals, the Naked Goddess disrobes before a male consort (usually the weather god) — emphasis on erotic encounter. On the 44 MB IIB scarabs recovered in Palestine, everything changes: the goddess now appears "in splendid isolation," depicted frontally on two-thirds of specimens, arms hanging down alongside body, genitalia emphasized while breasts not depicted. Crucially, flanked by branches on 36 of 44 specimens (82%) — hence Schroer's designation "Branch Goddess."

## Evidence

- 44 Naked Goddess scarabs total; 22 from controlled excavations or surface finds in Palestine; half purchased (many in Jerusalem)
- Two from Tell el-Yehudiyeh in eastern Nile Delta — either Egyptian exports or Canaanite manufacture
- 36 of 44 (82%) flank the goddess with branches/little trees
- Two-thirds show frontal view — "the direct frontal view is extremely rare in the ancient Near East"
- Exaggeratedly large ears interpreted by Schroer as "willingness of the goddess to hear" worshipers, not a Hathor relic
- Arms hang down alongside body on most specimens — "the real reason... is because of the effect caused by the exaggeratedly long arms as they provide a frame for her genitalia"
- Breasts not depicted; genitalia emphasized
- On cylinder seals, goddess disrobes for copulation; on scarabs, genitalia are "associated with vegetation"
- Goddess heads undergo parallel transformation: cow's ears become large human ears, horns become palm leaves or blossoms (Schroer 1989, 139-185)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The statistical data (82% branch association, two-thirds frontal) is robust. The contrast with cylinder seal conventions is clear and well-documented.

## Stakes

This transformation reveals the democratization of goddess religion. The cylinder seal goddess belongs to palace elites — her sexuality is displayed for a royal consort. The scarab goddess belongs to ordinary people — she faces the worshiper directly, hears their prayers, and guarantees vegetation and fertility. This is the material evidence for the "folk religion" that Dever argues for in Iron Age Israel, but rooted a thousand years earlier. The Judean Pillar Figurines of the 8th-6th centuries are the Iron Age descendants of this MB IIB Branch Goddess tradition. The MB IIB evidence shows that female-centered fertility religion was not a late corruption of "pure" Yahwism but the indigenous religious baseline.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The "Hathor fetish" interpretation of goddess heads — that these are Egyptian imports, not Canaanite transformations. Schroer's arguments for local adaptation challenge this. Scholars who see all nude female iconography as generic "fertility amulets" without specific divine reference.

**Alternative reading:** The branch-flanked goddess might be an entirely separate iconographic type from the cylinder seal goddess — a local Palestinian invention that borrowed some visual vocabulary but represents a different deity or concept.

## Assessment

The transformation argument is convincing because it explains both continuities (figure type, nudity, genital emphasis) and differences (frontal view, branch flanking, isolation from consort) within a single coherent model. The key insight is that the medium shapes the message: a cylinder seal rolls out a narrative scene with multiple figures, but a scarab base is a single static image meant for personal contemplation. The goddess adapted to her new medium by becoming more intimate, more directly present to the worshiper.
