# day-ygc-8.2: Absolute monotheism emerged with Deutero-Isaiah in the exile, preceded by a minority monolatrous movement

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 8 — Conclusion — Rise of Monotheism]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 8

## The Claim

"It is clear that there was indeed a monolatrous party already in the pre-exilic period, though it was not as dominant as Tigay supposes, and absolute monotheism was first given explicit expression by the prophet Deutero-Isaiah in the exile and became fully operative in the post-exilic period" (p. 229). The Albright/Mosaic monotheism view has been "generally rejected in recent decades" (p. 229). Hayman's medieval monotheism claim is "an extreme position" (p. 229).

## Evidence

1. **Deutero-Isaiah as first explicit monotheist:** The tendency "goes with a general understanding, already maintained a century ago by J. Wellhausen, that the achievement of monotheism was a gradual process" (p. 229-230)
2. **Pre-exilic monolatry had deep roots:** The "Yahweh alone movement" (Morton Smith, Bernhard Lang) — "some scholars are willing to grant that this minority monolatrous movement may indeed go all the way back to earliest times, perhaps to Moses" (p. 230). Monolatry (worship of Yahweh alone without denying other gods' existence) preceded monotheism (denial that other gods exist)
3. **Against Hayman:** Hayman's case "hangs on the prominent position given to angels in Second Temple Judaism" — but "throughout history monotheists have not felt belief in angels to be incompatible with monotheism" (p. 229). The category error is obvious: angels are subordinate created beings in monotheistic theology, not rival deities

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is the mainstream scholarly position and Day articulates it clearly. The distinction between monolatry (pre-exilic) and monotheism (exilic/post-exilic) is crucial and widely accepted.

## Stakes

The timing of monotheism determines whether it was always Israel's religion (conservative/confessional), a late Persian-era development (minimalist), or an exilic breakthrough (mainstream critical). Day's position occupies the scholarly center.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Albright — Mosaic monotheism (early). Hayman — medieval monotheism (late). Minimalists — Persian/Hellenistic invention (later than Day).

**Alternative reading:** The seventh-century prophets already exhibit monotheistic thinking (not mere monolatry), and the absence of "other gods" rhetoric in Deutero-Isaiah reflects a shift in genre rather than theology.

## Assessment

Day's central position is well-defended by the evidence throughout the book. The seventh-century prophets (Zephaniah, Jeremiah, the Deuteronomists) are clearly MONOLATROUS (Yahweh-alone) but not yet MONOTHEISTIC (denying other gods' existence). Deutero-Isaiah (Isa 40-55) is the first text to say "I am the LORD, there is no other" without qualification. The Albright position requires reading this back into earlier texts; the Hayman position requires ignoring it.
