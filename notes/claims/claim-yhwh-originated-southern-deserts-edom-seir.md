# romer-inv-2.1: Yhwh originated in the southern deserts (Edom/Seir/Teman), not in Canaan

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 2 — Geographic Origin]] — Thomas Romer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 2: The Geographic Origin of Yhwh

## The Claim

"The oldest attested occurrences of Yhwh are from the south of Palestine, the territory of Edom and Araba." Yhwh was a southern desert/war god associated with the Shasu nomads. The biblical poetry preserves a genuine memory of his southern origin. This is the scholarly consensus.

## Evidence

### The four candidate origins Romer evaluates:

| Site | Proposed Evidence | Romer's Verdict |
|------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Ebla** (Syria, 3rd millennium) | Personal names ending in *-ya* interpreted by Pettinato as short form of Yhwh | **Rejected.** The *-ya* syllable is a hypocoristic ending, not a divine name. "No god Yhwh appears in any of the lists of gods." |
| **Ugarit** (Syria, 14th-13th c.) | Fragmentary text (KTU 1.1) with possible YW — "the name of my son, YW" | **Inconclusive.** "We cannot therefore definitively exclude a link." But the passage is "too fragmentary and unclear." The YW could be Yam (the sea god) or a scribal error for Ym. |
| **Mari** (Euphrates, 2nd millennium) | Proper names like *Yahwi-ilum* | **Rejected.** "These names... cannot be linked with Yhwh, because they contain no name of a divinity, merely a verbal form signifying 'He manifests himself.'" |
| **Egypt/Soleb** (14th c. BCE) | Inscriptions of Amenophis III listing "the Shasu of Yhw(h)" | **The strongest evidence.** Earliest attestation of Yhwh. "In these texts Yhw3 seems to be a geographic term (referring to a mountain?) and perhaps also a divine name." |

### The Soleb evidence in detail:

- **Amenophis III at Soleb, Sudan (c. 1370 BCE):** A list of Shasu nomad territories includes "The country of the Shasu of Yhw(h)"
- **Ramses II at West Amara:** Same designation repeated
- **Papyrus Anastasi VI:** Mentions "the Shasu of Edom" whom Pharaoh Merneptah authorized to sojourn in Egypt with their herds
- **Papyrus Harris I (c. 1150 BCE):** Ramses IV boasts "I destroyed the people of Seir among the Shasu tribes. I razed their tents"
- **Karnak relief (Seti I, c. 1290-1280):** Depicts Shasu with distinctive goatees and hairbands — visual confirmation of the people group

### Archaeological context — the copper connection:

- The Shasu were involved in copper mining in the Araba valley (Timna, about 30 km north of Eilat)
- Mining activity peaked in the 14th-12th centuries BCE
- Punon (Feinan in Jordan), mentioned in Numbers 33:26 and linked with the Shasu in the West Amara list, is also in this area
- This provides an economic context: the Shasu weren't just random nomads — they were connected to a major industry

> Romer's conclusion: "The archaeological, epigraphic, and iconographic evidence all place the Shasu in the territory of Edom or Seir, and in Araba at the time of the transition from the Late Bronze to the Iron Age. And among these Shasu there might have been a group whose tutelary god was called 'Yhw.'"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** HIGH that the Soleb inscriptions are the earliest extrabiblical attestation of Yhwh (the reading is widely accepted). MEDIUM-HIGH that this represents Yhwh's place of origin rather than just one place he was known. MEDIUM on the copper connection as the mechanism of cultural transmission (plausible but not proven).

## Stakes

The God of Israel is geographically foreign to Israel. He came from Edom — the territory Israel would later consider its enemy. The Bible's own poetry (Judges 5, Deuteronomy 33) celebrates Yhwh "coming from Seir/Edom." The God who "chose Israel" was not originally Israel's god at all. He was adopted.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue for a Canaanite origin (the Ugaritic fragment is their best but weak evidence). Scholars who argue Yhwh was always indigenous to the central highlands.

**Alternative reading:** The Soleb inscriptions prove Yhwh was KNOWN in the south, not that he ORIGINATED there. He might have been a Canaanite god whose cult spread south and was later re-imported to Israel. The southern origin poems could be late theological retrojections (Persian period scribes creating a mythic origin story).

## Assessment

Romer's case is well-argued and honest. He discards weak evidence (Ebla, Mari), honestly flags uncertain evidence (Ugarit), and builds around the strongest evidence (Soleb + biblical poetry). His use of the word "might" — "among these Shasu there MIGHT have been a group whose tutelary god was called 'Yhw'" — is proper scholarly caution. The southern origin hypothesis is the best available explanation but not proven beyond doubt.
