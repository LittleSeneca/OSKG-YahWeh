# kaufmann-ri-11.6: The marriage of Hosea was theatrical representation, not biography

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch XI — Amos and Hosea]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter XI

## The Claim

Gomer was not an actual adulteress and the children were not illegitimate. The entire narrative of Hosea 1-3 is "a dramatic representation" — comparable to the prophet's wounds and bandage in 1 Kings 20:35ff, Isaiah's nakedness (20:2), and Jeremiah's bands and bars (27:2; 28:10ff). Gomer plays the ROLE of a harlot, wearing cosmetics and ornaments that signify harlotry. "The 'harlotries' seem to be something material, some cosmetic or face covering, and some whorish ornament on her breast." The children were "born to Hosea legitimately, for there is no hint that Gomer played the harlot after her marriage, or that the children were not Hosea's." They are "children of harlotry" only in their dramatic role. Chapter 3 is also theatrical: Hosea is commanded to "love" (not marry) a woman who represents the nation's forlorn interval without king or cult. "Here, as in all prophetic theatrical acts, the appearance is the essence, not the objective truth."

## Evidence

- Gomer is called "a wife of harlotries" (*ʾēšet zĕnûnîm*), not "an adulterous wife"
- 2:4 says "Let her remove her harlotries from her face and her adulteries from between her breasts" — these are removable items, not behaviors
- The children's history "is complete and unambiguous and does not require imaginative reconstruction" — they were born legitimately
- The names (Jezreel, Lo-ruhamah, Lo-ammi) are symbolic in function, like all prophetic sign-acts
- Chapter 3: Hosea is not commanded to "take" (marry) but to "love" a woman — he plays the lover. She doesn't bear him children. She sits solitary apart from all men — including Hosea

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's reading is textually defensible and avoids the biographical sensationalism that has plagued Hosea interpretation. But it requires assuming the text is a script for a performance rather than a report of events, which is itself an interpretive choice.

## Stakes

Whether Hosea's theology of divine love was born from personal trauma (God told him to marry a prostitute who betrayed him, giving him visceral knowledge of divine heartbreak) or from deliberate symbolic action. The biographical reading makes Hosea's theology more existentially powerful; the theatrical reading makes it more intellectually constructed.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional Jewish and Christian exegesis has generally read the marriage as biographical, though some medieval commentators (Ibn Ezra, Maimonides) read it as a prophetic vision, not an actual event. Modern critics tend toward biography.

**Alternative reading:** The narrative could be a prophetic allegory composed after the fact, neither biography nor theatrical performance. It may never have been "performed" at all — it may be a literary creation designed to convey theological truth through narrative.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's theatrical reading is genuinely illuminating and more textually careful than the standard biographical reading. The detail about "harlotries" being removable items (2:4) is an excellent observation that the biographical reading struggles with. This is one of Kaufmann's better exegetical moves, independent of his larger thesis.
