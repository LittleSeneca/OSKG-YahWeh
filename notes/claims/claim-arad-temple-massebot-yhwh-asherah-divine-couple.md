# dever-dghw-5.3: The Arad temple's two standing stones represent Yhwh and Asherah as a divine couple

**Source:** [[Dever — Chapter V — Archaeological Evidence]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Chapter V

## The Claim

The Arad temple (8th century BCE) — a full Yahwistic temple in southern Judah — had two standing stones (massebot) in its holy of holies. Standing stones represented deities in West Semitic religion. Two stones in the most sacred space of a Yahwistic temple strongly suggests a divine couple: Yhwh and another deity, almost certainly Asherah.

## Evidence

- The Arad temple was a full Yahwistic sanctuary with a holy of holies, two altars, and cultic installations
- Two standing stones (massebot) were found in the holy of holies — "representing Yhwh and another deity" (almost certainly Asherah)
- Standing stones are found at nearly every excavated Israelite site (Hazor, Gezer, etc.) — their function as divine representations is well-established in West Semitic religion
- The temple was deliberately decommissioned (possibly during Hezekiah's reform) — suggesting it was seen as problematic by later centralizers
- The Arad temple is not an outlier — it's evidence that Yahwistic sanctuaries with multiple divine representations existed in monarchic Judah

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The two stones in the holy of holies are unambiguous archaeological finds. The identification of the second stone as Asherah is an inference, but a well-supported one given (a) the West Semitic pattern of divine couples, (b) the Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions naming "Yhwh and his Asherah," and (c) the biblical evidence for Asherah worship in Judah.

## Stakes

If the Arad temple had two stones representing two deities, then goddess worship was not merely folk practice — it was present in OFFICIAL Yahwistic sanctuaries. This blurs the line Dever himself draws between elite "Book religion" and popular "folk religion." The second stone means that even the priests at Arad acknowledged a divine partner for Yhwh.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who interpret the two stones differently — e.g., as Yhwh and his angel, Yhwh and the covenant, or as representing two aspects of Yhwh alone. The interpretation as a divine couple, while plausible, is not the only possible reading.

**Alternative reading:** The two stones represent Yhwh alone — one as the deity, one as a memorial or covenant witness. Or they represent Yhwh and his angel/messenger. Polytheistic interpretation is not required.
