# day-ygc-5.3: Anat-Yahu at Elephantine confirms Anat functioned as Yahweh's consort

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 5 — Yahweh and Astarte and Anat]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 5

## The Claim

"It seems indubitable that the goddess Anat, in the form of Anat-Yahu, did function as Yahweh's wife amongst the Jews at Elephantine in the fifth century BCE" (p. 144). This probably reflects pre-exilic Israelite syncretism going back to the Northern Kingdom.

## Evidence

1. **Anat-Yahu is a deity:** Albright's attempt to read *ʻnt* as "providence" or "sign" was "disproved by the fact that a deity Anat-Bethel is attested c. 675 BCE in the treaty between Esar-haddon of Assyria and Baal, king of Tyre" (p. 144). If Anat-Bethel is a deity, so is Anat-Yahu
2. **Anat as Baal's consort:** Despite challenges from P.L. Day and N. Walls, "other evidence seems to make a good, cumulative case." Baal is constantly paired with Anat and Astarte at Ugarit. In the Egyptian contest of Horus and Seth, Anat and Astarte are "both explicitly stated to be Seth's (i.e. Baal's) wives" (p. 143)
3. **Baal-Yahweh equation permits Anat-Yahweh:** If Yahweh = Baal (Chapter 3), and Anat = Baal's consort, then Anat could serve as Yahweh's consort
4. **Origin of this syncretism:** Day prefers a pre-exilic Northern Kingdom origin, since Bethel (Jer 48:13) is the Northern Kingdom's deity, and Anat-Bethel at Elephantine pairs Anat with Bethel. "I prefer to see the notion of Anat as Yahweh's wife as going back to pre-exilic Israel, rather than as a later accretion" (p. 145)
5. **Hos 14:9 (ET 8):** Day sees a word play on Anat (and Asherah) in Hosea — from the 8th c. Northern prophet — implying Anat was known as a consort of Yahweh then (p. 145)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** HIGH for the Anat-Yahu deity identification at Elephantine; MEDIUM for tracing it to pre-exilic Northern Israel (the Elephantine evidence cannot date the syncretism's origin).

## Stakes

Elephantine is a genuine anomaly — a Jewish community in 5th c. BCE Egypt worshipping Yahweh alongside a goddess explicitly called "Anat of Yahu." This is polytheistic Yahwism surviving CENTURIES after monotheism supposedly emerged in the exile. Either the exile didn't produce uniform monotheism, or Elephantine preserves a pre-reform religion.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Mark Smith and K. van der Toorn argue Anat worship was unknown in pre-exilic Israel and that it arose in Egypt or post-722 Samaria. Day: they "exaggerate the extent to which Anat was absent in the first-millennium BCE Mediterranean world" (p. 145).

**Alternative reading:** Elephantine was an isolated, unrepresentative military colony. Anat-Yahu may reflect syncretism that developed in Egypt among diaspora Jews rather than authentically pre-exilic Israelite religion.

## Assessment

Day's rebuttal of Albright's "providence" reading is decisive. The Esar-haddon treaty evidence for Anat-Bethel as a deity is unimpeachable. But the chain from Elephantine BACK to pre-exilic Israel is speculative — we simply don't know when or where the Anat-Yahu syncretism originated. Day's preference for Northern Kingdom origin is plausible but not proven.
