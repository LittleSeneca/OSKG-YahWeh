# smith-obm-2.1: Ugaritic terminology reveals multiple distinct divine assemblies, not a single unified pantheon

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch2 — The Divine Council]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"The circle of El and the assembly of Baal" (dr 'il wpḫr b'l) in 1.39.7 "seem to refer to the pantheon as a whole as the sum of two parties named according to the two chief gods." This implies "dr 'il does not constitute the pantheon as a whole." The pantheon was not one monolithic body but multiple overlapping assemblies — El's household, Baal's assembly, the Ditanu assembly — each with its own membership and concerns.

## Evidence

- Three categories of assembly language with *pḫr: (1) "assembly of the gods" (pḫr 'ilm, 1.47.29), (2) "assembly of the divine sons" (pḫr bn 'ilm, 1.4 III 14), (3) "assembly of the council" (pḫr m'd, 1.2 I 14, 15, 20, 31).
- God-specific assemblies: "assembly of the sons of El" (mpḫrt bn 'il, 1.65.3), "circle of El" (dr 'il, 1.15 III 19), "circle of El and the assembly of Baal" (dr 'il wpḫr b'l, 1.39.7), "assembly of Baal" (pḫr b'l, 1.162.17), "assembly of the collectivity of Ditanu" (pḫr qbs dtn, 1.15 III 15).
- CAT 1.65 provides a restricted listing of El's household, NOT the general pantheon: El, the sons of El, TKMN and SHNM, El and Athirat, followed by divinized qualities (Mercy, Constancy, Well-being of El) and weapons ("by the spear/axe/weapon of El"), then Baal Sapan and Baal of Ugarit. This "cast of characters... belongs to El's own household, not the company of the pantheon in general."
- Key technical distinction: bn 'il ("sons of El," 1.65) vs. bn 'ilm ("divine sons," elsewhere) — different referents. mpḫrt bn 'il ≠ pḫr 'ilm.
- Cross's wider definition (wherever two or more deities with El are present = divine assembly) is criticized: "there is no reason to assume that the mythological scenes describing El at his abode involve (even as pars pro toto) the general pantheon."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The philological evidence is meticulous — the bn 'il vs. bn 'ilm distinction is genuinely important.

## Stakes

If the Ugaritic pantheon was NOT a single unified body but multiple overlapping assemblies, then the "divine council" is not one thing. This matters for how we read Israelite divine council texts — was there ever an Israelite "pantheon" equivalent to the Ugaritic one, or were there multiple assemblies from the start? Smith's argument: Israel inherited a complex picture that it simplified, not a simple picture it corrupted.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Cross defines the divine assembly more broadly and inclusively. Handy and Mullen tend to treat the pantheon as a more unified body.

**Alternative reading:** The multiple assembly terminology could reflect different literary contexts (myth vs. ritual vs. administrative) rather than genuinely distinct divine groupings. The same deities might appear in different lists under different rubrics without implying structural separation.

## Assessment

Smith's philological case is tight. The distinction between bn 'il and bn 'ilm is real and important. The strongest point is CAT 1.65 — it clearly lists a restricted group that can't be the whole pantheon. But Smith may over-read the organizational implications: the different assembly terms might be functional subsets of a single imagined divine polity rather than evidence of genuinely separate institutions.
