# albertz-hir-2.3-2.4.1: Pre-state Israel was a segmentary, acephalous, deliberately anti-monarchical tribal society

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §2.3-2.4 — Tribal Alliance and Family Piety]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §2.3

## The Claim

Israel was a "counter-model to the Canaanite city-states with their monarchical organization." It was deliberately decentralized and anti-domination — an "acephalous" (headless) and "egalitarian" segmentary society. Noth's amphictyony model is wrong (Israel had no central sanctuary); the African segmentary society model (Crüsemann) is better because "it understands the absence of central political institutions not as a lack of power... but as the expression of a deliberate political concern."

## Evidence

- Political institutions end at the local or tribal level — no permanent bodies above the tribe until the Philistine crisis forced development
- Two bodies existed: elders (heads of families/clans) handled minor business; the assembly of arms-bearing free men made major decisions — "a primitive form of democracy"
- Almost no permanent political offices — commanders reverted to ordinary status after crises (Gideon in Judg 8:22f.)
- Minimal social stratification: Saul ploughed his own field (I Sam 11:5); families were largely self-sufficient; the "ideal of the small farmer" was reality
- Peaceful relations existed with non-monarchical Canaanite cities (Gibeon, Shechem); monarchical cities were conquered
- Tribal structure was partly fictive — tribes were regional-political coalitions, not biological descent groups

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The segmentary society model is well-established in anthropology and fits the biblical data better than the amphictyony model. The deliberate anti-domination character may be slightly idealized.

## Stakes

If Israel's social origins are anti-monarchical by design, the later monarchy represents not evolution but regression — a betrayal of Israel's founding social vision. This sets up the prophetic critique as recovery of original values, not innovation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see more institutional complexity in pre-state Israel, or who argue the anti-monarchical tradition is a later retrojection (the monarchy was more continuous with tribal leadership than the biblical texts admit).

**Alternative reading:** The segmentary model from African ethnography may be a problematic analogue for Iron Age Palestine. The "deliberate" character of Israel's anti-domination stance may be Albertz reading later prophetic ideology back into the pre-state period.

## Assessment

Consistent with Albertz's overall model but feels slightly idealized. The tension between the segmentary model and the later monarchy as "regression" is Albertz's dramatic narrative engine — it works analytically but may overstate the deliberateness of Israel's anti-domination stance.
