# kaufmann-ri-5.3: P Is the Earliest Source — Silence on Centralization Proves Priority

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch V — The Sources]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch V

## The Claim

P must be pre-Deuteronomic because "there is no trace whatever of D's centralization idea in P." This is "the monumental fact" that overshadows all alleged tokens of P's lateness.

## Evidence

**Nine dimensions of P's non-centralized worldview:**

1. **No chosen city.** D's centralization concept involves BOTH a chosen temple AND a chosen city — the latter "figuring in the laws no less than the former." Sacred meals must be eaten in the chosen city; the paschal night must be spent there; the court of highest appeal is there. "Nowhere in P is this basic and peculiarly Deuteronomic idea reflected."

2. **P's spatial theology is different.** P distinguishes three realms: holy place (tent + court), camp, and outside the camp. The camp corresponds to the city but "none of [P's laws] endows the camp with the sanctity of the Deuteronomic chosen city." Sacred meals may be eaten in any "pure place" — inside OR outside the camp.

3. **The camp = any Israelite city with a high place.** P's laws about lepers expelled "outside the camp" parallel the practice of expelling lepers outside cities. The camp represents "at once a temple site as well as any city; D's separation of the two concepts is unknown to it."

4. **No pilgrimage requirement.** P doesn't prescribe the three annual appearances before YHWH at a chosen site — "is such an oversight credible after the Deuteronomic reform had made the pilgrimage an indispensable part of proper celebration?"

5. **Passover is a home sacrifice in P (contra D).** P's Passover (Exod 12:2-20) is "performed in the home with special rites; it belongs to the type of ancient home sacrifice." D forbids celebrating "in your gates."

6. **Festival laws are agricultural and local in P.** P preserves the celebration of first sheaf and dwelling in booths — rural practices that "naturally arose before centralization."

7. **Leviticus 17 assumes multiple legitimate sanctuaries.** The law requiring slaughter at "the door of the tent of meeting" — if post-D, would ban all meat-eating for people far from Jerusalem. "The only plausible interpretation...is that it presupposes the existence of many legitimate local sanctuaries."

8. **P recognizes multiple YHWH temples.** Lev 26:31: "I shall make your temples desolate" — the Holiness Code assumes YHWH has many temples. "Significantly enough, the post-Deuteronomic parallel to this passage in Ezekiel 6 omits 'temples.'"

9. **P's tent is an archetype, not a law.** The desert tent was the only legitimate sanctuary "as a historical necessity." With settlement, "each 'camp' has its own 'tent.'"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The descriptive observation that P genuinely lacks D's centralization concepts is significant and deserves more attention. The inference to P's chronological priority, however, is not the only possible reading — silence could equally be programmatic (P presents an idealized pre-centralization world) rather than ignorant.

## Stakes

This is the linchpin of Kaufmann's entire reversal. If P is pre-centralization, the Wellhausen sequence (D → P) must be thrown out, and with it the entire evolutionary picture of Israelite religion.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The Wellhausen school's counterargument: P doesn't lack centralization; it *presupposes* it. The portable tent of meeting IS the central sanctuary, retrojected into the Mosaic age. P doesn't need to ban high places because — in P's idealized vision — there never WERE any. The single tent in the desert IS the law applied retroactively.

**Alternative reading:** P might be post-D without being *dependent on* D. P represents what priestly circles WISHED Israelite religion had always been — portable, hierarchical, under Aaronic authority — in contrast to D's vision of a chosen city under popular Torah study. Kaufmann needs P to be not just independent of D but *earlier than* D — and his evidence for "earlier" is entirely the silence argument.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's argument about P's non-centralized worldview is genuinely significant and deserves more attention than most critics give it. He's right that P's spatial theology (camp vs. outside camp; "pure places" anywhere) doesn't fit the Deuteronomic paradigm. P is not just "D minus the ban on high places" — it has a DIFFERENT spatial theology entirely. But the inference from "P doesn't know centralization" to "P is EARLIER" is not the only possible inference. The real problem for Kaufmann is that he needs P to be not just independent of D but *earlier than* D — and his evidence for "earlier" is entirely the silence argument plus a few details that could as easily be archaizing as archaic.
