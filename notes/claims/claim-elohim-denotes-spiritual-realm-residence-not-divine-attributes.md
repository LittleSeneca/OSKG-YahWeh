# heiser-ur-4.1: *Elohim* means "disembodied spiritual being" — a "place of residence" term, not a term for divine attributes

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 2 — The Households of God]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 4

## The Claim

When a biblical writer used the word *elohim*, he was not thinking in terms of a specific set of attributes (omniscience, omnipotence, etc.). *Elohim* is a "place of residence" term — it denotes beings whose proper domain is the spiritual world. Yahweh is unique AMONG the *elohim*, not the only *elohim*. The word describes WHAT realm a being inhabits, not WHAT attributes it possesses.

## Evidence

**The six categories of *elohim* in the Hebrew Bible:**

| Category | Example | Shared attributes with Yahweh? |
|---|---|---|
| Yahweh | Deut 4:35 | Unique — incomparable |
| Divine council members | Psalm 82:1 | No — subordinate, can be judged |
| Foreign gods | 1 Kings 11:33 | No — called *shedim* (demons) |
| Demons (*shedim*) | Deut 32:17 | No — malevolent |
| Deceased Samuel | 1 Samuel 28:13 | No — a human ghost |
| Angels | Various | No — messengers |

"Would any Israelite, especially a biblical writer, really believe that the deceased human dead and demons are on the same level as Yahweh? No."

**The implication:** *Elohim* cannot mean "being with all divine attributes" because Samuel's ghost, demons, and angels manifestly do not share Yahweh's attributes. If the word unites such disparate beings, it must be describing something other than a set of attributes — namely, residence in the spiritual realm.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The six-category demonstration is compelling — Samuel's ghost and demons cannot share Yahweh's attributes. But the "place of residence" definition is Heiser's theological innovation, not a standard lexical claim. Standard lexicons (HALOT, BDB) define *elohim* as "god, gods" without this spatial/metaphysical framework.

## Stakes

If Heiser is right, the entire polytheism debate is reframed. The question is not "does the Bible acknowledge other gods?" (it does — the *elohim*) but "what KIND of beings are these other *elohim*?" If they are created spiritual beings subordinate to Yahweh, the biblical worldview is hierarchical monotheism, not polytheism. The existence of other *elohim* poses no threat to Yahweh's uniqueness.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Critical scholars (Smith, Römer, Day) who read the *elohim* as gods in the full ANE sense — deities with independent power, worship, and divine attributes comparable (if inferior) to Yahweh. Kaufmann would reject the entire framework: the other *elohim* are not real beings at all but fetishes — the Bible uses the word polemically.

**Alternative reading:** *Elohim* means "god/gods" in the standard ANE sense, and the application to Samuel's ghost is an anomalous extension, not evidence for a novel lexical category. The biblical writers had a standard concept of divinity and applied it flexibly.

## Assessment

This is Heiser's strongest and most original contribution. The six-category observation is real — the Hebrew Bible does use *elohim* for beings that share no attributes. Whether the "place of residence" definition follows is a separate question, but the data Heiser marshals is undeniable.
