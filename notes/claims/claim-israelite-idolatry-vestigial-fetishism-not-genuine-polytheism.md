# kaufmann-ri-4.5: Israelite idolatry was vestigial fetishism, not genuine polytheism

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch IV — The Religion of the People]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter IV

## The Claim

"Israelite idolatry must have been sufficiently shallow for the shock of the catastrophe to uproot it without a struggle." "The popular idolatry was not authentic polytheism, with mythology, temples, and priesthoods. It was vestigial idolatry, a vulgar superstition of the sort that the ignorant level of monotheistic peoples practices to this day."

## Evidence

- The biblical editorial framework's sweeping indictment is exaggerated: "sins of particular groups are ascribed to the entire people." The historiographer "could not account for what had taken place... without sin"
- Saul's reign "strangely free of taint"; David's reign: "idolatry is nonexistent"
- During Ahab/Jezebel: Baal worshippers all fit in ONE temple (II Kings 10:19, 21) — "the sole temple of Baal, built by Ahab in Samaria"
- After Jehu, "no foreign gods are known by the book of Kings to have been worshiped in the north"
- In Judah, Athaliah's Baal cult consists of "one temple and one priest to Baal"
- Manasseh's reign is the FIRST with "a more general contamination"
- Jeremiah's audience challenges him: "'I have not become defiled, I did not go after the Baals'" (Jer 2:23)
- Popular idolatry in literary prophets is FEATURELESS: "Ephraim is not accused of serving any specified foreign god; he inquires of his wood and staff (4:12), bows down to his handiwork (14:4)... There is no clearly defined pagan cult, no specific mythology, priests, or temples"
- Isaiah and Micah "name no foreign deity as an object of popular worship. Idolatry is always portrayed as the service of images of silver and gold, the handiwork of men"
- Jeremiah's telling phrase: "'other gods' which Israel 'knew not' (7:9; 19:4; 44:3) — the idolatrous cults are obscure, featureless, not really known to Israel"
- Jacob's household surrenders teraphim (Gen 35:2ff.) — treated with "indulgent humor" by the narrator
- David doesn't mind his wife's teraphim (I Sam 19:13) — "neither for the writers nor for their heroes are these teraphim and 'foreign gods' real deities"
- "Paganism was never a creative force in Israelite culture. National poetry, narrative, prophecy, ethics, and politics were all permeated with the belief in YHWH"
- "No prophets of foreign gods ever arose in Israel"
- "Archaeological remains fail to show a distinctive native Israelite iconography... no sculpture developed in Israel... a result of the absence of myth"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Impressive case from biblical texts, but Kaufmann avoids archaeological evidence. His definition of "genuine polytheism" may exclude Israelite practice by fiat.

## Stakes

The entire characterization of Israelite popular religion. If Kaufmann is right, the "idolatry" prophets condemn was shallow superstition — like a monotheist keeping a lucky charm. If wrong, Israel was genuinely polytheistic through most of the monarchy.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-proves-consort]] — Dever's archaeological work: thousands of Asherah figurines in domestic contexts. Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions bless "Yahweh and his Asherah" — consort theology, not mere fetishism. [[claim-asherah-goddess-in-ot-passages]] — Smith documents mythological content Israelites shared with neighbors. [[claim-yhwh-consort-asherah-goddess-baal-link-deuteronomistic-synthesis]] — Römer argues epigraphic evidence shows genuine polytheistic belief.

**Alternative reading:** Kaufmann is right that Israelite idolatry lacked the full mythological apparatus of Mesopotamian religion (no native theogonies, no mythological epics). But this doesn't mean it wasn't polytheism — it was a DIFFERENT KIND of polytheism focused on practice rather than theology. Most ancient polytheism wasn't philosophically elaborated. Kaufmann's requirement that "genuine polytheism" include theogony sets a bar most actual polytheistic practice wouldn't meet.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's signature argument, with genuine force. The prophetic polemic IS consistently against "wood and stone," "gods they knew not," "handiwork of men" — not against named deities with mythologies. This does suggest something different. But Kaufmann defines "genuine polytheism" in a way requiring mythological elaboration, then declares Israelite practice doesn't meet that definition. If your neighbor keeps an Asherah figurine and believes she can bless — does it matter whether they have a theogony? The Kuntillet Ajrud pithos — "I bless you by Yahweh of Samaria and by his Asherah" — is the hardest evidence to explain away.
