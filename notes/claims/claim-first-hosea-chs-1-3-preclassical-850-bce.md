# kaufmann-ri-11.5: "First Hosea" (chs 1-3) is pre-classical prophecy from the time of Jehoram (~850 BCE)

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch XI — Amos and Hosea]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter XI

## The Claim

Hosea 1-3 and 4-14 are "two distinct literary units, whose authors lived at different times." Chapters 1-3 belong to the period BEFORE Jehu's purge of Baal worship (i.e., before 842 BCE), during the reign of Jehoram son of Ahab (853-842). These chapters "were composed, then, before the destruction of Ahab's dynasty." The superscription in 1:1 (Uzziah to Hezekiah) "refer[s] only to chapters 4-14."

## Evidence

- **Style**: 1-3 has "almost mantic" style with an ordered series of events and objective presentation; 4-14 is "a formless aggregation of impassioned reproof, argument, threats, pleading, and hope"
- **Content**: 1-3 deals ONLY with idolatry as national sin — "moral corruption... never figures"; 4-14 focuses on *ḥesed*, knowledge of God, and moral decay
- **Baal worship**: In 1-3, Baal worship is a PRESENT sin; in 4-14, it's a sin of the PAST (9:10; 13:1)
- **Calves**: Samaria's calves are the present sin of 4-14 but "are never mentioned in 1-3"
- **Marriage metaphor**: The marriage/adultery metaphor for God's covenant is unique to 1-3 and "does not recur" in 4-14, where different images are used (grapes, trained calf, adopted son)
- **Historical context**: "We know of only one period of Israel's history when 'days of the Baals' were publicly celebrated: the reign of Jezebel." First Hosea speaks of distinct realms — YHWH's festivals vs. "days of the Baals" (2:13, 15)
- **Textual evidence on "blood of Jezreel"**: 1:4 refers to Naboth's blood, for which Ahab's dynasty was accountable. The MT reading "house of Jehu" is corrupt; LXX reads "Judah," graphically close to "Jehoram" — read "the house of Jehoram" (son of Ahab)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's structural observations are genuinely interesting and deserve engagement. But the argument that 1-3 is pre-classical rests heavily on the "blood of Jezreel" textual emendation and on establishing that Baal worship was exclusively pre-Jehu — both contestable points.

## Stakes

If Hosea 1-3 is pre-classical, the marriage metaphor is NOT an 8th-century prophetic innovation — it's inherited from earlier tradition. This undermines the critical narrative of progressive theological development. It also means the critical association of Hosea's "harlotry" language with syncretistic YHWH-Baal worship is doubly wrong: First Hosea speaks of distinct gods, and Second Hosea doesn't use the metaphor at all.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The critical consensus dates all of Hosea to the 8th century. The stylistic differences Kaufmann observes could reflect different genres or redactional layers within a single author's work. The superscription's dating to Jeroboam II through Hezekiah is taken as controlling.

**Alternative reading:** The differences between 1-3 and 4-14 are real but reflect a SINGLE prophet working in different modes — biographical narrative (1-3) vs. collected oracles (4-14). The shift from present to past Baal worship reflects the prophet's own timeline during a long ministry that spanned Jehu's purge. The marriage metaphor's absence in 4-14 could simply mean the metaphor served its purpose in 1-3 and wasn't reused.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's structural case is compelling — the differences between 1-3 and 4-14 ARE striking. But he needs the two sections to be by DIFFERENT AUTHORS separated by a century, which is a much heavier claim than merely noting they differ. The textual emendation of "Jehu" to "Jehoram" is clever but not decisive. On balance, Kaufmann has identified real features that need explanation, but his specific historical reconstruction goes beyond what the evidence can bear.
