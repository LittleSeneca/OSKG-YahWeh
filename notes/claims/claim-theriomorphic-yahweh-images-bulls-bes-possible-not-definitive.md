# lewis-ocg-7.3: Theriomorphic Yahweh images (bulls, Bes figures) are possible but none is definitive

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 7 — Iconography of Divinity — Yahweh]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 7

## The Claim

Yahweh may have been represented theriomorphically — through bull imagery (Jeroboam's calves, the Bull Site bronze, the Taanach cult stand) and through Bes/Bes-like figures (Kuntillet ʿAjrud pithos A). The Kuntillet ʿAjrud standing Bes figures are particularly intriguing because they appear alongside the "Yahweh of Samaria and his asherah" inscription. But Lewis is cautious: none of these can be definitively identified as Yahweh.

## Evidence

The Kuntillet ʿAjrud pithos A has three figures (two standing Bes-like, one seated lyre player) alongside the inscription. Some scholars identify the Bes figures as representing Yahweh and the seated figure as Asherah. Lewis is skeptical: "There is no need to equate the drawings with the inscription, especially if they come from two separate times, as seems likely." The Bull Site bronze zebu bull is "the best candidate for a theriomorphic representation of Israelite El" — not necessarily Yahweh. Jeroboam's bulls at Bethel and Dan (1 Kgs 12:28-33) are more likely El images than Yahweh images (as argued in Ch5). The Taanach cult stand's bull calf and empty space (for an invisible deity?) is suggestive but inconclusive. The bull-headed warrior stela from Bethsaida is likely the Aramean moon god, not Yahweh.

## Confidence

**Rating:** low

**Rationale:** The iconography is genuinely ambiguous; forcing identifications is methodologically wrong.

## Stakes

The nature of popular Yahwism. If Yahweh was represented by bulls and Bes figures at sites like Kuntillet ʿAjrud, then "aniconic Yahwism" was an elite/Jerusalemite preference, not a universal Israelite practice.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who identify the Kuntillet ʿAjrud Bes figures as Yahweh and the seated lyre player as Asherah (e.g., Dever, Hadley). Scholars who see Jeroboam's bulls as Baal images rather than El or Yahweh.

**Alternative reading:** The Bes figures ARE Yahweh, the seated figure IS Asherah — the iconography and inscription at Kuntillet ʿAjrud were produced together and should be interpreted together. Lewis's two-time-period argument is speculative.

## Assessment

Lewis is appropriately cautious. The Kuntillet ʿAjrud iconography is genuinely ambiguous. His refusal to force identifications is methodologically correct — but also means the theriomorphic evidence remains tantalizing rather than probative.
