# sommer-bog-2.4: J and E are monotheistic — not polytheistic — but their monotheism is fluid

**Source:** [[Sommer — Chapter 2 — Fluidity in Israel]] — Benjamin Sommer, *The Bodies of God* (2009), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"I believe the documents preserved in the Hebrew Bible, when taken as a whole, are monotheistic." J and E do not depict Yhwh as one god among many — they depict Yhwh as the ONE God who has multiple bodies. The multiplicity is not of gods but of embodiments.

## Evidence

**The distinction matters:** If you read Genesis 18 as polytheistic (Yhwh + two subordinate gods), you get the Smith/Römer/Dever narrative: polytheism → monotheism. If you read it as Sommer does (one Yhwh with three bodies), you get fluid monotheism → anti-fluid monotheism. Same text, radically different theological framework.

**Supporting texts from this chapter:**
- Local Yhwh manifestations (2.2): multiple Yhwhs are one Yhwh, not competing local gods
- Mal'akh as avatar (2.3): the messenger IS God in small-scale form, not a subordinate deity

**The burden of proof:** Sommer argues that J/E never name another god as an active, living character. Other gods may be mentioned but they don't act, speak, or receive worship. This is consistent with monotheism, not polytheism.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** This is the most debatable claim in the chapter. It depends on how one defines "monotheism" — as belief in one God (Sommer's definition) or as belief that only one divine being EXISTS in any form (a stricter definition).

## Stakes

What traditional theology considers "pure monotheism" (the transcendent, invisible God) is actually a LATE DEVELOPMENT that rejected the MORE ANCIENT Israelite conception of God. "The God of the philosophers is not the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob."

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-dever-monotheism-late-elite-book-religion-never-displaced-folk]] (Dever dates monotheism late, not early); [[claim-true-monotheism-emerged-babylonian-exile]] (Römer dates true monotheism to exile); [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]] (Smith treats monotheism as rhetoric, not belief)

**Alternative reading:** J/E texts are monolatrous or henotheistic — they worship one God while acknowledging others exist. Calling this "monotheism" retrojects a later theological category onto earlier texts.

## Assessment

This is Sommer's most provocative claim and the one that most divides him from the rest of the field. Smith, Römer, Dever, and Day all treat J/E texts as evidence of polytheism or monolatry. Sommer's reframing depends on accepting that "monotheism" should be defined by the number of gods, not the number of bodies. If you accept his definition, the entire evolutionary narrative collapses: Israel was monotheistic from the start; what changed was not how MANY gods but what KIND of God.
