# heiser-ur-23.1: The conquest of Canaan targeted Nephilim bloodlines, not generic Canaanite populations — it was not ethnic cleansing

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 5 — Conquest and Failure]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 23

## The Claim

"The conquest of Canaan was not about invasion of land belonging to other people. It was about taking back land Yahweh had claimed for his own from the descendants of the Nephilim, the giant clan bloodlines that had survived the flood." The *ḥērem* (total destruction) commands target specific bloodlines — the Anakim, Rephaim, Zamzummim — not generic Canaanite populations.

## Evidence

**The bloodlines the Bible specifically targets:**
- Numbers 13:28-33: "We saw the Nephilim there (the descendants of Anak came from the Nephilim)"
- Deuteronomy 2:10-11: "The Emim... were also regarded as Rephaim, as the Anakim"
- Deuteronomy 2:20-21: "It is also regarded as a land of Rephaim; Rephaim formerly lived there"
- Joshua 11:21-22: "Joshua came at that time and cut off the Anakim from the hill country... No Anakim were left in the land of the people of Israel; only in Gaza, in Gath, and in Ashdod did some remain"

**The Goliath connection:**
- "Goliath was from Gath (1 Sam 17:4). He was a descendant of the Anakim" — survivors of Joshua's campaign
- The Philistine champion wasn't just a tall human. He was a remnant of the divine-human hybrid bloodlines

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The biblical texts DO name specific giant clans. But many *ḥērem* commands target generic "Canaanites." Heiser's reading explains the targeted passages but doesn't fully account for the broader conquest narrative.

## Stakes

Heiser is trying to solve the moral problem of the conquest — God commanding genocide. His solution: it wasn't genocide of humans; it was elimination of non-fully-human hybrid bloodlines corrupted by divine transgression. Whether this actually resolves the moral problem is debatable. Whether it's true to the texts is also debatable — the Bible says "men, women, and children" were killed, not just giants.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Virtually every critic of the conquest narrative would say Heiser is creating a supernatural framework to justify morally indefensible texts. Critical scholars who read the conquest as legendary/theological rather than historical.

**Alternative reading:** The conquest narratives are theological propaganda written centuries after the fact by Deuteronomistic authors — the *ḥērem* commands never happened. The giant traditions are legendary embellishments. Heiser's reading takes both the conquest and the giants literally when neither may be historical.

## Assessment

Heiser's reading of specific giant passages is plausible. His attempt to extend this to a general theory of the conquest is strained. The ethical problem of *ḥērem* remains, regardless of the cosmic framework. The strongest evidence is the Goliath-Anakim connection and the named giant clans. The weakest is the claim that ALL *ḥērem* commands target giants — the text simply does not say this.
