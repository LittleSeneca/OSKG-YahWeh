# schmid-ht-5.1: Schleiermacher's religion/theology distinction restructured biblical scholarship

**Source:** [[Schmid — §5 — Romantic Devaluation of Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §5

## The Claim

The late 18th and early 19th centuries introduced "the distinction between religion and theology that would become definitive for subsequent periods." Schleiermacher discovered religion as "the individual province of the mind" — a unique phenomenon that "cannot be evaluated sufficiently with the traditional tools of theology and philosophy. Religion is always more than what theology and philosophy are able to comprehend."

## Evidence

- Johann Salomo Semler and the Neology movement: theology as reflection ON religion
- Johann Gottfried Herder and Schleiermacher: religion is experience, feeling, sensibility — theology corrupts it
- De Wette explicitly injected Schleiermacher's concept of religion into exegetical disciplines
- The result: "Hebrew Bible studies developed an increasingly negative view of theology"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The intellectual history is well-documented. Schleiermacher's influence on 19th-century theology is standard.

## Stakes

This distinction is the ROOT of the modern disciplinary separation between "theology of the HB" and "history of Israelite religion." Schmid's project aims to heal this split — but to do so, he has to show that the split was a historical contingency, not a necessary development.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** No one disputes the historical facts. The question is whether Schmid is right that the distinction has outlived its usefulness, or whether it still captures something real about the difference between living practice and elite reflection.

**Alternative reading:** The HB IS elite scribal literature — so Schleiermacher's instinct was right that it's "theology" (reflection) not "religion" (experience). Schmid wants to value theological reflection without denigrating it relative to "living religion." The split had real intellectual content, not just Romantic prejudice.

## Assessment

There's an irony here that Schmid doesn't fully address. He insists the HB is elite literature (theology, not folk religion), yet he wants to undo the religion/theology split that was partially motivated by the same recognition. The HB IS theology in Schleiermacher's sense — it's reflective, systematizing, elite. Schmid wants to value that without denigrating it relative to "living religion." Fair enough — but the split had real intellectual content, not just Romantic prejudice.
