# kaufmann-ri-ep.3: The Torah book liberated Israelite religion from ethnic-territorial limitations

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Epilogue and Prospect]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Epilogue

## The Claim

"The Torah was destined to leave the land and the temple and to accompany Israel from exile to exile." Though the compilers still thought of temple and land as necessary, "the Torah book was a self-contained, in itself adequate, embodiment of the religion of YHWH." It "harbored the seed of an ultimate liberation."

## Evidence

- The Torah book could travel. Sacrifice couldn't.
- The temple was stationary. The Torah became portable sanctity — "a fountainhead of sanctity, an eternal expression of the will of God."
- Without this, "the prophetic vision of the universalization of Israel's religion could not have been realized."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is a standard and well-supported observation. The shift from temple-centered to text-centered religion is one of the most consequential developments in religious history.

## Stakes

The mechanism by which a national religion became a world religion. Kaufmann says: the book did it.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Few would disagree with this basic observation. The debate is about timing and causation, not the fact of the shift.

**Alternative reading:** The Torah book was part of a larger package — it wasn't the book alone but the book plus new institutions (synagogue, rabbinic interpretation) that enabled portability.
