# schmid-ht-10-12-13.4: The Septuagint likely drove the CLOSURE of the Hebrew Torah — a translated Torah can't be extended

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§10, 12, 13 — Canon Pluriformity and Relativity]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §12

## The Claim

"The translation was likely one of the decisive factors for the literary closure of the Hebrew Torah; a Torah that has already been translated can no longer be successfully extended."

## Evidence

- Septuagint translation of Torah began ~250 BCE in Alexandria
- The Letter of Aristeas provides the legendary account
- Translation of other books continued into the 2nd century CE
- The Septuagint is a conglomeration of individual translations — varying from "extremely literal to relatively free"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The causal claim (translation → closure) is plausible but speculative. The correlation (translation by 250 BCE, apparent closure by then) is stronger than the causation.

## Stakes

This provides an additional argument for Torah closure by ~250 BCE — consistent with Schmid's Persian-period dating. If the Torah was translated by 250 BCE, it must have been substantially complete. The translation functioned as a kind of textual "freeze."

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue the Torah was already substantially fixed before translation, making translation a consequence rather than cause of closure. Scholars who note that the Septuagint text continued to be revised (Theodotion, Aquila, Symmachus), suggesting translation didn't freeze the text completely.

**Alternative reading:** The Septuagint translation may be evidence of Torah closure but not its cause. The translation was possible BECAUSE the Torah was already a fixed document. The causal arrow may run the other direction.

## Assessment

This is a clever argument that I haven't seen elsewhere. The logic is sound: once a translation exists, any additions to the Hebrew would create discrepancies with the Greek. The translation functioned as a kind of textual "freeze." Whether this is the CAUSE of closure or merely a CONSEQUENCE of an already-closed text is debatable.
