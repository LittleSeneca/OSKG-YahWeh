# lewis-ocg-6.3: P's chronology — the name Yahweh was revealed at Moses' time — is more historically reliable than J's antediluvian Yahwism

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 6 — Origin of Yahweh]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 6

## The Claim

J's "Reuel tradition" (Exod 2) has Moses' father-in-law bearing an El name (Reuel = "friend of El") and being a priest of Midian — suggesting he worshipped El, not Yahweh. P and E both agree that the name Yahweh was revealed at Moses' time. The preponderance of El names in patriarchal narratives (and absence of Yahweh names) supports this. The ninety occurrences of Yahweh in patriarchal narratives are "anachronistic" — the result of later editors retrojecting Yahweh worship backward.

## Evidence

The Ishmael wordplay in Gen 16:11 is the clearest proof: "You shall call his name Ishmael [yišmāʿ-ʾēl, 'May El hear'] because Yahweh has heard your cry." The name presupposes El, yet the explanation uses Yahweh — a telltale sign of editorial updating. Similarly, Abram builds an altar to Yahweh at Bethel (bêt-ʾēl, "house of El"). P's careful structure (Elohim → El Shadday → Yahweh, with three eternal covenants) gives theological motivation but also preserves genuine historical memory.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Ishmael wordplay is hard to explain as anything other than editorial updating of an El tradition; the cumulative evidence (El names, no Yahweh theophorics in patriarchal narratives) supports it.

## Stakes

The entire chronology of Israelite religion. If Yahweh was unknown to the patriarchs, then Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob worshipped El — making El the original god of Israel and Yahweh a later arrival. This aligns with Chapter 4's conclusions.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservative scholars who accept J's chronology at face value. Heiser would argue that El and Yahweh were always the same deity, so the name change is merely progressive revelation, not the arrival of a new god.

**Alternative reading:** P's scheme could be entirely theological — a way to structure covenantal history — with no historical basis. But the cumulative evidence (El names, no Yahweh theophorics in patriarchal narratives, the Ishmael wordplay) suggests otherwise.
