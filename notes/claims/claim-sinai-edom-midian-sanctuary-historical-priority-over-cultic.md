# albertz-hir-2.2.4: Sinai was a mountain sanctuary in Edom/Midian, not the Sinai peninsula; the location was later deliberately obscured

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §2.2 — The Exodus Group and Yahweh's Origins]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §2.2

## The Claim

After liberation from Egypt, Moses led the group to the mountain sanctuary of Yahweh where he had received his original oracle. The Midianites introduced the Exodus group to Yahweh's cult there (Ex 18:12). Sinai was "once a mountain sanctuary in the frontier area between Edom and Midian which was visited by a number of nomadic tribes." The tradition was later deliberately obscured to distance Yahweh from Edomite/Midianite associations. "Israelite religion... maintained the priority of the historical experience of God. The Israelite relationship to God was not grounded in the Sinai theophany but in the Exodus."

## Evidence

- The mountain of God where Moses received his call (Ex 3:1ff.) is explicitly identified as the mountain where the liberated group would worship (Ex 3:12) — later tradition obscured this connection
- Old poetic traditions locate Yahweh's abode in Edom/Seir/Paran, east and west of the Arabah — right next to Midianite territory
- "Sinai" in the old poetry does not denote a specific mountain but a *region* — the name was later applied to a specific mountain
- Deliberate obscuring: Deuteronomists distance Sinai from Edom/Seir (Deut 1:2: "eleven days' journey from Horeb in the direction of Mount Seir to Kadesh Barnea") and use "Horeb" (= "desolate land") as a cipher
- Archaeology: almost no Late Bronze Age settlements on the Sinai peninsula; settlement gap at Kadesh from end of 3rd millennium to 10th century — "today there is more to suggest that the Old Testament Sinai is not to be sought on the present Sinai peninsula but east of the Arabah or the Gulf of Akaba"
- The theophany experience at Sinai added "the experience of the cultic nearness of God" to the "historical experience" — this combination is another "specific feature of Israelite religion"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH on the Edom/Midian location (the old poetry, the archaeological gap, and the deliberate obscuring converge). MEDIUM on the "priority of historical over cultic" as distinctive (comparative claims about ANE religion are hard to verify).

## Stakes

If Sinai was in Midian/Edom, the Kenite hypothesis is further strengthened — Israel first encountered Yahweh AT a Midianite sanctuary. The entire "Mosaic revelation" narrative is reinterpreted as the Exodus group being initiated into an existing cult.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who maintain a traditional Sinai peninsula location. Those who argue the "Edomite" poetic references are anachronistic. Those who see the Exodus-Sinai connection as a literary construct, not historical memory.

**Alternative reading:** The archaeological argument from silence (no LB settlements on Sinai peninsula) has been challenged by Anati's claims about Har Karkom, though these are not widely accepted. The Edomite poetic references (Judg 5; Deut 33) may reflect later geographical knowledge retrojected, not original location.

## Assessment

This is well-argued and aligns with Römer's similar conclusions in *The Invention of God* (Chapters 2-3). The archaeological gap on the Sinai peninsula is a genuine puzzle for the traditional location. Albertz's reconstruction of deliberate obscuring by later tradents is consistent with the textual evidence. The "historical priority over cultic" claim is more theological than historical — it's Albertz's confessional Protestant preference (word over sacrament) showing through.
