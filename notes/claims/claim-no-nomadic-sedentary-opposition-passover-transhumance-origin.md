# albertz-hir-2.1.4: No fundamental nomadic/sedentary opposition; Passover originated as a transhumance ritual later historicized into Exodus

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §2.1 — Patriarchal Religion as Family Piety]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §2.1

## The Claim

The old idea (Alt, et al.) that Israelite religion emerged from a conflict between nomadic religion and sedentary Canaanite religion is wrong. Ethnologically, herders and farmers lived in close symbiosis, and the same clans practiced both. The real opposition was between the hill-country population (farmers and herders) and the city-states of the plains. The Passover festival, despite its later anchoring in the Exodus liberation tradition, probably originated as a family ritual accompanying the spring transhumance.

## Evidence

- Mari texts (c. 1800 BCE) show the same clan engaged in both agriculture and nomadic herding
- The patriarchal tradents depict the patriarchs doing both: Isaac engages in agriculture (Gen 26:12f.) and pastoralism; cattle (baqar) appear alongside sheep and goats
- Passover features suggesting nomadic origin: spring date, eating in haste, prohibition on leaving leftovers, blood rite to ward off the destroyer — all fit the dangers of transhumance
- Despite later connection with maṣṣot (agricultural festival) and centralization under Josiah, Passover "remained a family festival"
- Texts like Gen 32:23-33 and Ex 4:24-26 indicate people felt exposed to demonic attack especially when traveling

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH on the nomad/sedentary symbiosis (ethnologically well-established). LOW-MEDIUM on Passover's transhumance origin (the evidence is suggestive but circumstantial; the Exodus connection is very old and deeply embedded).

## Stakes

If Passover originated as a family-level nomadic ritual before being historicized into the Exodus liberation narrative, it's a perfect example of Albertz's model: family piety (pre-political, concerned with survival) gets absorbed and reinterpreted by official religion (political liberation theology). The two strata don't just coexist — they merge, with official religion reinterpreting family practice.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see the Exodus connection as original to Passover. The biblical tradition is unanimous and ancient that Passover commemorates the Exodus. Separating a hypothetical pre-Yahwistic nomadic Passover from the Exodus Passover requires speculative reconstruction.

**Alternative reading:** Passover's features (blood on doorposts, haste, nocturnal celebration) could equally be explained by its function as an apotropaic ritual within an already-Yahwistic framework, without requiring a pre-Yahwistic nomadic origin. Not everything that looks "primitive" is pre-Yahwistic.

## Assessment

Albertz is probably right about the direction of travel (family practice → official reinterpretation, not the reverse), even if the specific Passover reconstruction is speculative. The broader point — that religious practices moved between the family and official levels, getting reinterpreted in the process — is well-taken and doesn't depend on this one festival.
