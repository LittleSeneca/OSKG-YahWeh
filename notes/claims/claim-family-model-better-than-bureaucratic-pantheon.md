# smith-obm-2.7: The family/patrimonial household model fits the Ugaritic pantheon better than the bureaucratic model

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch2 — The Divine Council]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 2

## The Claim

Against Handy's bureaucratic model (using Weber): "The number of such divinities of bureaucracy in either Ugaritic or Israelite literature is minimal, whereas the number of family members in the pantheon in Ugaritic literature is high."

## Evidence

- Handy: pantheon as bureaucratic system with specialized offices. Satan in Job as "prosecuting attorney" — one of few biblical examples.
- Schloen: monarchy itself was a patrimonial household, not a bureaucracy in Weber's terms. Pantheon reflects patriarchal paradigm.
- Ugaritic evidence against bureaucracy: "there is no typically bureaucratic divine figure such as a scribe in the Ugaritic pantheon nor any other figure apart from a divine craftsman who worked in the royal divine bureaucracy." Kothar (tier 3) is the only possible example, and even he is an outsider/foreigner.
- Familial terminology dominates: "sons of El," "seventy sons of Athirat," divine marriage (Nikkal and Yarih), sibling relationships (Anat and Baal).
- Biblical material "barely supports a general structure of bureaucracy" — Satan, some individuated angels, but few examples.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The absence of bureaucratic figures in Ugaritic texts is striking, but the argument is partly from silence.

## Stakes

This sets up Chapter 3's core argument: the divine FAMILY provided polytheism's conceptual unity. If the pantheon is a family, its "oneness" is familial, not bureaucratic. Monotheism's "oneness" of a single ruler replaces the family's "oneness" of kinship.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Handy (bureaucratic model). Scholars who see Mesopotamian influence (where bureaucratic divine structures ARE more evident) on Israelite rather than Ugaritic models.

**Alternative reading:** Family AND bureaucracy aren't mutually exclusive. A royal court IS both a household and an administrative body. The divine family might function bureaucratically without needing a "divine scribe." The distinction Smith draws may be sharper than the ancient reality.

## Assessment

Smith's critique of Handy is fair — the Ugaritic texts really don't show a bureaucratic pantheon. But the family model has its own problems: if the pantheon is a family, why is there so much internal conflict (Baal vs. Yamm, Anat's violence, El's favoritism)? Smith addresses this in Ch 3 by noting the family includes conflict. The family model works better descriptively than the bureaucratic one, but it's still a model, not the thing itself.
