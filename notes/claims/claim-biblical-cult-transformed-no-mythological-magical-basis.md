# kaufmann-ri-3.7: Biblical cult transformed — no mythological or magical basis

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

"The cult laws of the Bible have no mythological or magic background — that is the heart of the transformation. This was not the product of an artificial reworking, consciously and rationally undertaken... It is the result of an organic development of Israelite religion through the course of generations." Sacrifice is not food for the deity; festivals are historicized, not dramatizations of divine life; impurity is a passive condition, not an active demonic force.

## Evidence

- SACRIFICE: "Expressions such as 'bread of God,' 'a pleasing odor'... must be regarded as petrified linguistic survivals." Prophets use these phrases freely, proving they were innocent formulas. Peace offering eaten "before" — never "with" — YHWH
- PURITY: Impurity is a condition, not a power. "No activity or power is ascribed to impurity in itself." Contact with dead is harmless; contact with holy is deadly. Scapegoat sends sin TO Azazel — doesn't expel Azazel. "Of all known exorcisms... this is unique in being conceived of not as the expulsion of a demon, but as the expulsion of something to a demon"
- FESTIVALS: Passover and Tabernacles are historicized — commemorate Exodus and Wandering, not agricultural/fertility cycles. Dramatization confined to historicized events because "dramatization of a cosmic event could easily have become transformed into a representation of an event in the life of God — a motif that had no place in Israel"
- No spells, no incantations in the cult. Psalms are prayers, not conjurations. Priests are not healers or exorcists
- Sin offering (hattath) offered AFTER recovery, not during illness — purificatory, not therapeutic. "Throughout the Bible healing pertains to the man of God, not the priest"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** High on the transformed character as PRESENTED in texts. Low on the claim that this reflects earliest practice rather than priestly reshaping.

## Stakes

Whether Israel's cultus was fundamentally different from Canaanite cultus from the beginning, or became different through prophetic critique and priestly reform.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The archaeological record — figurines, standing stones, Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions — suggests a popular cult more continuous with Canaanite practice. Within the Bible, golden calves at Dan/Bethel, high places, Asherah in Jerusalem temple suggest a cult not as transformed as Kaufmann claims. Priestly Code purity system has parallels with Hittite and Mesopotamian ritual texts. [[claim-asherah-was-yahwistic-symbol]], [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-symbol-not-goddess]] are attempts to reconcile with archaeological evidence.

**Alternative reading:** The biblical cult represents a REFORMED and IDEALIZED version. The transformation IS real — but it's the product of centuries of priestly systematization and prophetic critique. The Priestly Code (which Kaufmann dates early) is the most demythologized stratum — which is precisely why most scholars date it late.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's description of the biblical cult's non-magical character is generally accurate as a reading of Priestly and Deuteronomic texts. But his attribution to "organic development" over generations just pushes the question back. The more economical explanation: biblical texts represent a LATE stage exported backward. The fact that Kaufmann has to argue that seemingly pagan elements are "vestigial" or "petrified" or "fossils" is telling — there are a LOT of fossils.
