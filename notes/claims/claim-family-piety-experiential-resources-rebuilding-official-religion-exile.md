# albertz-hir-4.8: Family piety provided the experiential and conceptual resources for rebuilding official religion during the exile

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol2 — Chapter 4 — Exilic Period]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume II* (1994), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"The relationship with the God of family piety, conceived of in analogy to the creaturely relationship between parent and child which is ultimately inalienable, is now to help people get over the deep shattering of Israel's relationship with God."

## Evidence

- **Communal laments used personal piety language:** Isa 63.7-64.11 appeals to Yahweh as "our father" and "our potter" — using familial creator imagery to ground hope when the historical relationship (exodus, covenant) seemed broken
- **Thanksgiving festivals went public:** Lam 3 shows an individual's thanksgiving story being told to the whole community — personal deliverance becomes evidence that Yahweh still acts graciously (Lam 3.22-24, 55ff.)
- **Oracles of salvation appropriated for national proclamation:** Deutero-Isaiah uses the genre of the private priestly oracle of salvation (Isa 41.8-13; 43.1-4) to address corporate Israel — "Yahweh according to his purpose has created Israel, like every individual, 'from its mother's womb'" (43.1; 44.2,21,24)
- **Ezekiel's vision of dry bones:** Spun from a typical individual lament ("our bones are dried up, our hope is lost," Ezek 37.11) — personal healing/deliverance from death becomes the template for national restoration
- **Maternal imagery for Yahweh:** Isa 46.3f. compares Yahweh to a mother carrying her child; 49.15 says Yahweh cannot forget Israel any more than a nursing mother; 66.13 describes Yahweh comforting like a mother. This was unprecedented in official religion where feminine divine imagery had been suppressed since Hezekiah's and Josiah's reforms
- **Patriarchal traditions:** The patriarchs offered unconditional promises — unlike conditional Deuteronomic promises annulled by sin. Ezek 33.24 shows those remaining used Abraham to justify land claims; Isa 63.16 appeals to Abraham's fatherhood; Deutero-Isaiah calls Israel "seed of Abraham, my friend" (41.8)
- **The exilic patriarchal narrative:** The literary revision of Gen 12-50 presents the patriarchs as models for exilic families: Abraham's call to leave his homeland becomes a model for return; the promise of becoming a "great people" counters minority anxiety

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is one of Albertz's signature contributions and best-supported claims. The textual evidence for personal piety language infiltrating national/oracular genres is extensive and specific.

## Stakes

This reverses the usual narrative that sees "official religion" as primary and "popular religion" as derivative or corrupt. For Albertz, in the exile, the flow reversed: family religion saved official religion. This also explains why post-exilic Judaism is so family-centered (sabbath at home, passover as family meal, circumcision by the father). The maternal imagery for Yahweh is particularly significant — feminine divine imagery that official religion had suppressed (the asherah, the queen of heaven) resurfaces in a transformed, orthodox form.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars skeptical about reconstructing "family religion" from elite texts. Scholars who see the theological innovations as elite scribal work with no popular basis.

**Alternative reading:** The "personal piety" language in exilic prophecy is a rhetorical strategy by elite theologians, not evidence that family religion was actually thriving or influential.
