# dever-dghw-intro.4: Ancient Israel was a marginal, impoverished backwater — this context is essential for understanding its religion

**Source:** [[Dever — Introduction]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife? Archaeology and Folk Religion in Ancient Israel* (2005), Introduction

## The Claim

"Ancient Israel was a truly marginal economy and society. The country was always poor compared to its prosperous neighbors; always powerless compared to their might; always on the verge of extinction (as finally happened). It may be unsettling to some readers, but the fact is that ancient Israel was an obscure cultural and historical backwater of the ancient Near East. It would have been long forgotten except for its one memorable contribution to civilization: the Hebrew Bible."

## Evidence

| Demographic/Geographic Fact | Implication |
|---------------------------|-------------|
| Total area: size of New Jersey | "Israel looms 'larger than life' in our Western imagination because of its spiritual significance, but it was a tiny country" |
| Total population: never exceeded ~150,000 | Most villages had a few hundred people |
| Jerusalem at its height: ~5,000 people | The "city of God" was a small town |
| 80-90% of population rural | Most Israelites never saw Jerusalem or the Temple |
| Life expectancy: ~30 years | "A man of 50 was an 'elder,' respected for his remarkable longevity" |
| Literacy: below 5% | "Ordinary people could not have read the Bible if they had had it" |
| No natural resources | No gold, silver, precious stones, or useful minerals |
| Unpredictable rainfall | Drought every few years; crops often failed |
| "Fractured geography" | Made political unity nearly impossible |

**On Israel's vulnerability:**
"Because of its exposed position as a 'land bridge' between Africa and Asia, Palestine and later Israel were frequently trampled over, subjugated, and occupied for long periods by foreign powers. Think of the long succession of peoples who have overrun this general area... Amorites, Hittites, Hurrians, Egyptians, Philistines, Assyrians, Babylonians, Persians, Greeks, Romans, Muslims, European Crusaders, Turks."

"No single political entity ever ruled the area in antiquity for more than about 400 years, and that was ironically the rather feeble Israelite-Judean monarchy."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the demographic and geographic facts (archaeological data is solid). HIGH that this context shaped religion (this is basic anthropological reasoning).

## Stakes

The "chosen people" lived in a marginal, impoverished, frequently conquered territory the size of New Jersey. Their capital city had 5,000 people. Their God was the patron of an obscure hill-country chiefdom, not a world empire. The grandeur of biblical theology — God as creator of the universe, Lord of history — was a theological achievement that far outstripped Israel's actual political and economic status. This doesn't make the theology false, but it makes it remarkable.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Few scholars dispute the demographic facts. The debate is over their interpretive significance — some argue population estimates are too low, others that even 150,000 is sufficient to support the institutional religion described in the Bible.

**Alternative reading:** The marginality of Israel is precisely what makes its theological achievements so remarkable — the "provincial backwater" framing understates Israel's cultural creativity.

## Assessment

The demographic facts are solid and their implications are significant. If 95% of Israelites couldn't read and 80-90% never saw Jerusalem, then the religion of the Hebrew Bible was not their religion. This doesn't delegitimize the Bible — but it does mean we need to understand it as the product of a tiny elite, not the voice of a nation.
