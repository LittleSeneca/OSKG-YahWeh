# keel-ggi-5.3: The Bes Figures Are Apotropaic Daemons, NOT Yahweh and Asherah

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part V — Iron Age IIB — Baal El Yahweh and His Asherah]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part V

## The Claim

"An identification of the two Bes images... with 'Yahweh... and his Asherah' is impossible. The iconography and inscriptional evidence known thus far offer no evidence for a connection between Yahweh and Bes, though this has often been assumed, especially not for Yahweh being depicted in the shape of a Bes figure." The two frontal figures on Pithos A (labeled M and N) — the ones the "Yahweh and his Asherah" inscription runs across — are Bes-type Egyptian dwarf deities with grotesque lion faces, protruding ears, arms akimbo, crooked legs, and tails. They are apotropaic protective daemons, not Israelite gods.

## Evidence

- Full iconographic analysis: head ornamentation (blossom/feather crown), grotesque grimacing face, protruding ears, beard or collar, arms akimbo, crooked legs, tail — all standard Bes features
- Gilula (1979) and Margalit (1990) tried to interpret the heads as bovine (connecting to the Bethel bull cult), but this "cannot be correct" — the features are clearly leonine, not bovine
- No evidence for any connection between Bes and Asherah in the entire iconographic record — a few "very hypothetical references" at most
- The solar motifs that accompany Bes in other contexts (Egyptian ntr '3, "Great God") are absent from Kuntillet Ajrud — further evidence these are generic protective daemons, not solarized high gods
- The dots on their bodies are decorative (probably indicating clothing), not apotropaic wedjat-eyes

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** The iconographic identification of Bes figures is among the most secure in ancient Near Eastern art history. The features are diagnostic.

## Stakes

The Bes = Yahweh identification has been one of the most sensational claims in the field — "the only depiction of Yahweh ever found!" If the Bes figures are just protective daemons that happen to share a jar surface with a Yahwistic inscription, the sensationalism evaporates.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Gilula (1979 — bovine interpretation), Meshel (1979 — partly agrees with bovine), Coogan (1987a — "Yahweh and his Asherah"), McCarter (1987), Margalit (1990) who called it a "graphic commentary." Korpel (1990) identified Bes as Yahweh.

**Alternative reading:** Bes figures do sometimes appear in connection with major deities in Egyptian and Phoenician art. Their presence at a Yahwistic site could indicate they were integrated into Yahwistic protective iconography, even if they are not Yahweh himself.

## Assessment

The Bes = Yahweh claim was always a stretch, driven by the coincidence of the inscription's placement. Keel & Uehlinger's systematic iconographic analysis demolishes it. The Bes figures simply happened to be on the jar before the inscription was added — a completely mundane archaeological reality that got turned into a theological bombshell by scholars who did not separate image from text.
