# stav-god-5-8.3: Circumcision marked Israelite males as belonging to a god with a penis — the covenant sign is on the male organ because the deity HAS a male organ, and cutting the foreskin made Israelite males in the image of their circumcised god.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part II — Genitals]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 7

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that circumcision was not merely an arbitrary sign but a bodily mark that identified Israelite men with their male deity. "By cutting the foreskin, Israelite males were made in the image of their circumcised god." The covenant sign is on the penis because the deity HAS a penis.

## Evidence

- Genesis 17: Circumcision is the sign of the covenant — permanent, bodily, on the male organ. The location of the sign is not arbitrary
- Exodus 4:24-26: The bizarre story of Zipporah circumcising her son and touching Moses' "feet" (= genitals) with the foreskin — "a bridegroom of blood." Stavrakopoulou argues "the episode makes sense only if circumcision is understood as marking the male body as belonging to the male deity"
- The covenant sign is on the penis because the deity HAS a penis — the sign mirrors the anatomy of the god

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Circumcision as identification with a male deity is plausible; the specific claim that God was understood to be circumcised is less certain.

## Stakes

The Abrahamic covenant — the foundation of Jewish identity — is literally inscribed on the male penis. It is a bodily mark of belonging to a MALE god. The exclusion of women from the covenant sign (no female circumcision requirement) reflects the gendered nature of the deity. God's masculinity structures the very terms of belonging. This is the most radical implication of Stavrakopoulou's embodied-God thesis: the covenant itself is gendered because God is gendered.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]] (Kaufmann — incorporeal God could not have a penis). Theologians who argue circumcision's location is culturally contingent, not theologically motivated.

**Alternative reading:** Circumcision is an ancient Near Eastern practice adopted by Israel and given covenantal significance — its location on the penis reflects cultural conventions about male identity and fertility, not a claim about God's anatomy.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou's argument is provocative and not without force — the covenant sign IS on the penis, and this IS striking. But the claim that God was understood as circumcised goes beyond the evidence. Circumcision could mark belonging to a male deity without implying the deity himself was circumcised. Stavrakopoulou reads maximum theological significance into anatomical location.
