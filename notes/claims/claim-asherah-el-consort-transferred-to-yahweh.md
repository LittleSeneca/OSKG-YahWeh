# romer-inv-9.1: Asherah was a goddess — El's consort at Ugarit — and became Yhwh's consort in Israel

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 9 — Yhwh and His Asherah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 9: Yhwh and His Asherah

## The Claim

At Ugarit (14th-13th centuries BCE), Asherah (*Aṯiratu*) was El's consort, "Lady Asherah of the Sea," mother of the seventy gods. When El and Yhwh merged, Asherah came with the package. In monarchic Israel, she was worshipped as Yhwh's consort. This is the majority scholarly position — directly contra Smith.

## Evidence

- **Ugaritic texts:** Asherah is "the great goddess, parhedros of the god El and mother of minor gods." The Baal cycle calls them "the seventy sons of Aṯirat." In the Keret legend, the heir "shall suck the milk of Aṯirat" — suggesting connection to fertility and kingship.
- **Mesopotamian attestation:** Asherah appears as Ašratu(m) from the Hammurabi era (18th century BCE) through Seleucid ritual texts.
- **Southern Arabian inscriptions (1st millennium):** *Aṯrt* means either the specific goddess or "goddess" generically.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Very high on Ugaritic Asherah (the texts are unambiguous). High that the El-to-Yhwh merger transferred Asherah.

## Stakes

Yhwh had a wife. The goddess was worshipped alongside the god in the Jerusalem temple. She was removed by reformers, not because she was foreign, but because she was too deeply embedded in Israelite religion. The God of the Bible was once part of a divine couple. This is the single most explosive claim in Römer's book.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Mark S. Smith (see [[claim-asherah-was-yahwistic-symbol]] and [[claim-biblical-evidence-insufficient-for-goddess]]) argues the asherah was a symbol that had lost its goddess connection by the monarchic period. The goddess existed earlier but was absorbed into Yahweh.

**Alternative reading:** Smith's convergence-and-differentiation model: the asherah was a Yahwistic symbol, not a consort goddess. The El-Yhwh merger transferred El's titles and attributes, but Asherah was demythologized in the process. The biblical polemic against the asherah was against a cult object whose functions (divination, healing) offended Deuteronomistic sensibilities.

## Assessment

The Ugaritic evidence is unambiguous — Asherah was El's consort and mother of the gods. The question is whether she survived the El-to-Yhwh merger as a goddess or merely as a symbol. Römer's claim that the transfer happened naturally is the majority view and has strong logic: if Yahweh absorbed El's identity, it would be strange if El's consort simply vanished. The simpler explanation — that she came with the package — is compelling.
