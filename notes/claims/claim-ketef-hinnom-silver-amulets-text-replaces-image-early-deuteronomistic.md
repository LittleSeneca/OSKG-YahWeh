# keel-ggi-6.6: The Ketef Hinnom Silver Amulets — Text Replaces Image as Medium of Divine Presence

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part VI — Iron Age IIC — Astralization and Orthodox Reaction]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part VI

## The Claim

"Not Asherah, and not an 'angel of Yahweh,' but Yahweh himself — mediated by the presence of a text! — accompanies the deceased, with his blessing and protection, into the cold darkness of the grave." The two silver amulets from Ketef Hinnom (Jerusalem, ca. 600 BCE) contain the priestly blessing from Numbers 6:24-26 — the earliest surviving biblical text, predating the Dead Sea Scrolls by centuries. In the context of Keel & Uehlinger's survey, they represent the culmination of the aniconic tendency: the WORD replaces the IMAGE as the medium of divine presence.

## Evidence

- The amulets were found in a burial cave at Ketef Hinnom, Jerusalem, dating to the end of Iron Age IIC (early 6th century)
- Text: "May Yahweh bless you and keep you; may Yahweh make his face shine upon you and be gracious to you; may Yahweh lift up his countenance upon you and give you peace" (Num 6:24-26)
- "The two silver amulets present early evidence for the practice prescribed in Exod 13:9; Deut 6:8; 11:18, which calls for wearing the words from the Torah on the body"
- "In contrast to the evidence discussed in §§189ff., it is worth noting that there are no terra-cottas of doves, goddesses, or riders in the graves of Ketef Hinnom" — COMPLETE ABSENCE of the domestic piety artifacts found everywhere else
- "The motherly Asherah is far removed from the piety documented in this grave"
- The tomb ALSO contained a Phoenician Bes head amulet and a Bastet pendant — showing the family still used small non-Judahite protective amulets alongside their Yahwistic texts
- "The combination that links Deut 7:9 and Num 6:24f. points to a link between priestly and 'early deuteronomistic' thinking"
- The metaphor of Yahweh's "shining countenance" (ya'er panaw) may derive from solar concepts (Korpel 1989) or lunar concepts — in either case, the anthropomorphic divine face replaces the asherah as the source of blessing

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** VERY HIGH for the dating and text identification. MEDIUM for the interpretation that this represents a conscious rejection of goddess piety (the absence of figurines could be specific to this family rather than programmatic).

## Stakes

These amulets show that by 600 BCE, at least some Jerusalemites had made the transition from image-based to text-based religious practice. The divine presence is no longer mediated by a figurine or a cultic symbol — it is mediated by WORDS worn on the body. This is the birth of the "religion of the book" — not in the Persian period, but in the last decades of the Judean monarchy.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Barkay (the excavator) and most scholars accept the dating. Disagreement focuses on whether these amulets represent a widespread movement or an isolated elite practice.

**Alternative reading:** The combination of Yahwistic text amulets WITH Bes and Bastet pendants in the same tomb suggests eclecticism, not orthodoxy. This family was hedging its bets — Yahweh's blessing AND Egyptian protective magic. Not a "reform" but a both/and approach.

## Assessment

The both/and reading is actually MORE historically plausible than the "orthodox reform" reading. Real religious change is messy. The Ketef Hinnom family had Yahwistic texts AND Egyptian amulets. They represent a transitional moment — the text is gaining importance but the old protective magic has not been abandoned. This is exactly what you would expect in a period of genuine religious transformation: people do not flip switches, they accumulate.
