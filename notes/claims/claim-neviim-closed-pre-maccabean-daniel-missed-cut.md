# schmid-ht-11.5: The Nevi'im was closed pre-Maccabean (~200 BCE) — Daniel missed the cut

**Source:** [[Schmid — §11 — The Tanakh as the Hebrew Bible]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §11

## The Claim

"The closing of the Nevi'im section of the canon (Joshua-Malachi) around 200 BCE led to the development of a commentary literature of its own, available especially in the pesharim from Qumran. The fact that the Nevi'im was constructed in the pre-Maccabean period is revealed especially in the book of Daniel's inclusion in the Ketuvim. Daniel is a prophetic book, but it apparently could no longer make it into the Nevi'im because its composition took place in the Maccabean period."

## Evidence

- Daniel was composed in the Maccabean period (167-164 BCE) — too late for the already-closed Nevi'im
- Daniel had to be included in Ketuvim as a prophetic book that missed the prophets' deadline
- Joshua 1:7-8 and Malachi 3:22-24 (ET 4:4-6) form an inclusio around the Nevi'im — "Remember the teaching of my servant Moses" in Malachi mirrors "Be careful to act in accordance with all the law that my servant Moses commanded you" in Joshua 1
- References to the "Torah of Moses" throughout Joshua-2 Kings are anachronistic but show that the Torah was understood as the standard for history
- Malachi was likely separated from Zechariah to create the number TWELVE (the number of completion) — "Malachi" may be an artificial name from Mal 3:1 ("my messenger")

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The Daniel argument is strong: a Maccabean-period prophetic book that ended up in Ketuvim is best explained by the Nevi'im already being closed. The Joshua-Malachi inclusio is a well-recognized literary feature.

## Stakes

The closure of Nevi'im around 200 BCE means the prophetic books (including the Deuteronomistic History in the Former Prophets) were considered authoritative by that date. This provides a TERMINUS AD QUEM for the composition of these books — they must have been substantially complete by ~200 BCE. This doesn't rule out earlier composition but limits how late they can be.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who date Daniel earlier (6th century) would dispute the argument, though this is a minority position. Scholars who argue the Nevi'im section remained somewhat fluid later than 200 BCE.

**Alternative reading:** Daniel may have been excluded from the Nevi'im for theological rather than chronological reasons — its apocalyptic character may not have fit the definition of prophecy recognized by the canonizers.

## Assessment

The Daniel argument is persuasive. The Joshua-Malachi inclusio is a genuine literary feature — Schmid's observation about the Torah-of-Moses references being anachronistic is important. These references were ADDED to make the prophetic books serve as Torah interpretation.
