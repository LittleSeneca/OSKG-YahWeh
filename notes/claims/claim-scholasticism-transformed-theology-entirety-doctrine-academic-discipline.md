# schmid-ht-2-4.2: Scholasticism transformed theology into an academic discipline covering all Christian doctrine

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§2-4 — Concept of Theology from Plato to Gabler]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §2

## The Claim

"Observable contours of the modern conceptualization of 'theology' first arise in the High Middle Ages. These dimensions especially concern the expansion of the concept of theology from the doctrine of God to the entirety of Christian doctrine." The emergence of university *facultas theologica* in the 11th-12th centuries "provided the foundation for the further predominance of the concept of theology."

## Evidence

- Aristotelian reception enabled systematic exposition of Christian doctrine as an academic task
- University theology schools gave theology its academic connotation: "a process involving reason and systematic reflection"
- This scholastic understanding "have largely persisted into the modern period" in English-speaking scholarship — theology = intellectualizing and systematizing
- Schmid notes that criticisms of theology as reductionist or inappropriate for historical inquiry "generally present caricatures of what Scholasticism intended"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The historical development is well-documented. The connection between Aristotle's reception and the rise of scholastic theology is standard medieval intellectual history.

## Stakes

This scholastic equation of theology with systematic doctrine is what later reformers and biblical theologians reacted AGAINST. Schmid is setting up the genealogy: Plato (myth) → Scholasticism (system) → Reformation (existential relationship) → Gabler (historical discipline). Each shift was a response to the previous definition's inadequacies.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Minimal disagreement about the history. The interpretive question is whether scholasticism's systematizing was a corruption (Luther's view) or a legitimate development.

**Alternative reading:** Scholasticism didn't "transform" theology — it developed implications already present in patristic thought. Schmid's genealogy is too linear.

## Assessment

Schmid's genealogy is efficient and accurate, though compressed. The key insight: the "theology = systematics" equation that dominates English-speaking scholarship is a medieval development, not inherent to the term.
