# sommer-bog-3.2: D's *shem* theology rejects the fluidity model

**Source:** [[Sommer — Chapter 3 — Rejection of Fluidity]] — Benjamin Sommer, *The Bodies of God* (2009), Chapter 3

## The Claim

The Deuteronomistic (D) source represents a THEOLOGICAL REVOLUTION — not the invention of monotheism, but the rejection of divine fluidity WITHIN monotheism. D replaced multiple divine bodies with a hypostatic substitute: the *shem* (Name).

## Evidence

**Key text — Deuteronomy 12:5:** Yhwh will choose a place "to cause his Name to dwell there" (*lĕšakkēn šĕmô šām*). NOT "where Yhwh dwells" — the Name dwells; Yhwh does not.

**The revolutionary distinction:** "The Deuteronomic editors wished to make a subtle distinction that the older texts lack: It is not God who dwells in the temple; only God's name does."

**This solves a theological problem:** God dwells in heaven (D's consistent theology), yet God must be accessible to worshippers. The solution: his NAME functions as a localized presence that mediates access without actually embodying God.

**What D rejected:** The older J/E idea that Yhwh himself dwelt in the Temple, walked in the garden, ate with Abraham. D's God is transcendent — in heaven, accessed only through the Name.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Deuteronomic texts consistently use "Name" language where older texts say God dwells — the pattern is systematic and the theological motivation is clear.

## Stakes

D's shem theology is the first step in the anti-fluid revolution that would ultimately produce the God of the philosophers — transcendent, invisible, bodiless. But at THIS stage, D's God still has a body. It's just in heaven, not the Temple. This is anti-fluid, not anti-corporeal.

## Disagreement

**Alternative reading:** The Name theology is not a rejection of fluidity but a protection against idolatry — preventing worshippers from thinking they can contain or manipulate God. It's an apophatic move, not ontological.

## Assessment

This is one of Sommer's strongest arguments because the textual pattern is clear and consistent. D systematically replaces "Yhwh dwells" with "Yhwh's Name dwells." The theological motivation is transparent: if God is in heaven, he can't also be in the Temple — unless you have fluidity. Since D rejects fluidity, the Name solution is the necessary alternative.
