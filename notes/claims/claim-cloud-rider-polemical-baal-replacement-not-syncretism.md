# heiser-ur-27.1: Yahweh absorbed Baal's cloud-rider imagery as polemical replacement, not polytheistic syncretism

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 6 — Thus Says the Lord]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 27

## The Claim

"The image of Yahweh as rider of the clouds is taken directly from Baal language." But rather than this representing polytheistic syncretism, Heiser argues it represents Yahweh's VICTORY over Baal — using Baal's own imagery to demonstrate superiority. "Yahweh is the true rider of the clouds. Baal is a pretender."

## Evidence

- Psalm 68:4: "lift up a song to him who rides through the deserts; his name is Yah" — using Baal's epithet *rkb ʻrpt* (rider of the clouds)
- Daniel 7:13: "one like a son of man... coming with the clouds of heaven" — the divine warrior arrives on clouds
- The "Day of the Lord" in the prophets repeatedly uses storm theophany language
- Where Smith and Cross see absorption of Baal mythology (convergence), Heiser sees polemical replacement — same data, different interpretation

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** HIGH on the Baal-language parallels. MEDIUM on Heiser's "polemical replacement" reading (both readings are possible).

## Stakes

This is the hermeneutical divide in miniature. Same data — Yahweh uses Baal's cloud-rider language. Two interpretations: (1) Yahweh absorbed Baal's traits over time (Smith, Day, Cross — convergence/syncretism). (2) Yahweh always held these traits and the biblical authors weaponized Baal's language to demonstrate Yahweh's superiority (Heiser — polemical replacement). The data doesn't decide between them.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-day-yahweh-cloud-rider-derived-baal-rkb-rpt]] (Day — the cloud-rider epithet derives from Baal; this IS absorption). [[claim-day-psalm-29-yahwistic-baal-hymn-seven-thunders]] (Day — Psalm 29 is a Baal hymn with Yahweh's name inserted; this IS syncretism). Smith and Cross see convergence, not polemical replacement.

**Alternative reading:** The critical reading (Smith, Day, Cross): Yahweh absorbed Baal's traits through a historical process of convergence. The cloud-rider language was originally Baal's. Israel adopted it and applied it to Yahweh. This is polytheistic syncretism, not polemical replacement.

## Assessment

Heiser's reading is theologically motivated: if Yahweh always held storm-god traits, Baal-language is appropriation-for-polemic, not absorption-through-syncretism. The critical reading is historically motivated: Yahweh acquired Baal's traits over time as Israel encountered Canaanite culture. The data — Yahweh using Baal's language — is the same. The interpretation depends on prior commitments about Israel's religious development. Heiser's reading is the minority position in scholarship but consistent within his divine council framework.
