# cross-cmhe-ii-iii.2: Myth was transformed into epic through historicization of Canaanite patterns

**Source:** [[Cross — Parts II-III — Divine Warrior and League]] — Frank Moore Cross, *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* (1973), Parts II-III

## The Claim

Israel's distinctive religious expression — what Cross calls "epic" — was produced by historicizing Canaanite myth. The mythic raw material (divine warrior battles chaos, divine council, sacred mountain) was NOT discarded but TRANSFORMED: cosmic battles became historical battles, mythic deities became historical enemies, the timeless became dated. This is the key mechanism that produced the Hebrew Bible's unique genre — neither pure myth (like Ugarit) nor pure history (like modern historiography) but "epic" that "in interpreting historical events combines mythic and historical features in various ways and proportions."

## Evidence

- **The transformation pattern is systematic:**
  - Ba'l's cosmic battle against Sea (Yamm) → Yahweh's historical victory over Egypt at the Red Sea
  - Ba'l's battle against Death (Mot) → Yahweh's defeat of Pharaoh's army
  - Ba'l's temple on the cosmic mountain Saphon → Yahweh's dwelling on historical mountains (Sinai, then Zion)
  - Ba'l's enthronement as king of the gods → Yahweh's enthronement as king of Israel
  - The divine council's acclamation of Ba'l → the divine council's acclamation of Yahweh

- **The Song of the Sea (Exodus 15) as case study:** "The Song of the Sea is a historicized version of the mythic conflict between the Divine Warrior and the Sea. The historical event (the escape from Egypt) has been shaped and interpreted by mythic patterns. This is not to say the event didn't happen. It is to say that the event was experienced and narrated through inherited mythic categories."

- **The Elijah cycle (1 Kings 17-19) as political-theological application:** Cross reads the conflict between Elijah and the Ba'l prophets as old Yahwistic Divine Warrior theology vs. syncretistic royal cult. "The prophets WON the battle against Ba'l's name, but LOST the battle against Ba'l's attributes. Yahweh became the storm god, the fertility god, the rider of clouds — everything Ba'l had been."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Cross's signature concept — the transformation from myth to epic is demonstrable in specific texts (Exodus 15, Psalm 24, the Elijah cycle) and provides a unified explanatory framework for Israel's literary uniqueness.

## Stakes

This is Cross's most original contribution — the "epic" as a THIRD genre between myth and history. If Cross is right, the Hebrew Bible's theological distinctiveness is not a rejection of myth but a TRANSFORMATION of myth through historicization. The Bible's truth claims are not anti-mythological — they are myth made historical. This reframes the entire debate about biblical uniqueness.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]] — Kaufmann's thesis that Israelite religion was non-mythological from the start is the direct target. [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]] — Smith's rhetoric thesis sees the transformation as later and more deliberate than Cross's organic historicization.

**Alternative reading:** The "historicization" Cross sees is retrojection — later writers imposed historical meaning on originally mythic material, rather than an organic transformation. The Bible's anti-mythological polemic (Deutero-Isaiah, Psalm 82) represents a deliberate BREAK with myth, not a transformation of it.

## Assessment

Cross's myth→epic thesis is his most original and generative contribution. It provides a unified theoretical framework for understanding how Israel produced something genuinely novel without positing cultural isolation or radical uniqueness. The transformation model is more intellectually satisfying than either the "Israel was just like Canaan" or "Israel was nothing like Canaan" poles.
