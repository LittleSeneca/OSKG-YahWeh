# smith-obm-4.7: Cult places and objects were considered divine — divinity adhered to cult

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch4 — Pluralities Pairings and Other Divine Relations]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"By virtue of association with particular deities or their cult sites, particular locales could be regarded as divine." Also cult objects: the lyre (knr) appears in deity lists. "Based on the extension of the rubric of divinity to cult places and objects, one may suggest that divinity was considered to adhere to cult."

## Evidence

- Baal's mountain Sapan is labeled "divine" (1.3 III 29, IV 19) and appears in deity lists (1.47.32).
- The sacred garden and spring appear in ritual contexts.
- 'arṣ w šmm ("Earth and Heaven") and ġrm w['mqt] ("Mountains and Valleys") in deity lists — Cross reads them as "olden gods" from ancient theogony.
- The lyre (knr) in deity list 1.47.32; Jacobsen on Mesopotamian harps: "even the harps were considered deities."
- Consumables of cult (dqt, trt) appear in divine terms.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The specific examples are solid (Sapan, lyre), but the general claim that "divinity adhered to cult" goes beyond the evidence — the mechanism is not specified.

## Stakes

If cult objects were divine, the "aniconic" strain in Israelite religion (no images of Yahweh) was a radical departure, not just from other ANE religions but from the very logic of how divinity works — divinity inhabits objects.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** This is relatively uncontroversial for Ugarit but the degree of divinization is debated. Were these "divine" in the same sense as deities, or merely "sacred/set apart"?

**Alternative reading:** Objects in deity lists might represent the deity's paraphernalia listed for ritual completeness, not because they were thought to BE divine. The lyre might represent the god's instrument, not a god itself.

## Assessment

The evidence for Sapan and the lyre is concrete. The extension to "divinity adheres to cult" is Smith drawing out implications — reasonable but stated more confidently than the evidence strictly warrants. The connection to Israelite aniconism is important.
