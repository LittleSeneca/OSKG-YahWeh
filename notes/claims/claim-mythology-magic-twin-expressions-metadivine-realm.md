# kaufmann-ri-2.3: Mythology and magic are twin expressions of the metadivine realm

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch II — Pagan Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter II

## The Claim

"Just as the fundamental idea of paganism found poetic expression in myth, so it found practical expression in magic." Myth depicts gods not only as actors but as ACTED UPON — there is always "tension between the gods and other forces that shape their destinies." Magic is the practical counterpart: since the metadivine realm contains forces transcending the gods, pagan man "employs magic also, hoping thereby to activate the forces of the metadivine." Even the gods themselves are magicians.

## Evidence

- Myth: "Fate... apportions lots to the gods as well as to men" — myth narrates the subjection of gods to independent forces
- Magic: Pagan man feels subject to both realms — prayer addresses gods; magic addresses the metadivine
- Gods as magicians: Marduk is "great magician of the gods"; Thoth recites magical books over Re; Isis uses sorcery to learn Re's secret name
- Babylonian witches "can weary heaven, bind up the mouths of the gods, tie the knees of goddesses"
- Magical objects confer divine power: Tablets of Destiny, Ishtar's girdle, Aphrodite's girdle, Hermes' wand — all "bespeaking the idea that there is no supreme divine will that governs all"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** High that myth and magic are linked in surveyed religions. Medium that the link is specifically through the metadivine idea. Low that Israel was genuinely free of both.

## Stakes

If myth and magic are structurally linked to the metadivine realm, then Israel's alleged freedom from both becomes evidence for its freedom from the metadivine realm. But this cuts both ways: if we find myth and magic IN Israel (the ark in 1 Samuel 4-6, bronze serpent in Numbers 21, Urim and Thummim, Numbers 5 ordeal), Kaufmann's structural link IMPLIES Israel had a metadivine realm too.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Critical scholars find plenty of magic in Israelite religion. The ark narrative depicts an object of intrinsic power. The priestly ritual of the red heifer has automatic efficacy. Kaufmann will need to argue these are NOT genuinely magical — the same circularity problem at the micro level.

**Alternative reading:** The religion/magic distinction is a modern Western category. Ancient people wouldn't have recognized it. Israelite religion may have INTEGRATED magical and religious elements differently — not eliminated magic but subordinated it to divine will in theological self-presentation.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's structural analysis of the myth-magic link is insightful. But his argument cuts both ways. If myth + magic = metadivine realm, and we find myth + magic in Israel, then Israel had a metadivine realm. The danger of special pleading is acute.
