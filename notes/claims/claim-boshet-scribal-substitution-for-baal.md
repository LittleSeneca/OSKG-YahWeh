# day-ygc-3.4: Bošet ("shame") is a deliberate scribal substitution for Baal in personal names

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 3 — Yahweh Versus Baal]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 3: Yahweh Versus Baal

## The Claim

"*Bošet*, 'shame', has been substituted for *baʻal*, 'Baal', by later scribes in the personal names Ish-bosheth (for Eshbaal), Mephibosheth (for Meribaal), and Jerubbesheth (for Jerubbaal)" (p. 83). This was a targeted theological program: the scribes couldn't change history, but they could change the names of people whose theophoric names honored Baal.

## Evidence

1. **Explicit usage:** Hos 9:10 and Jer 11:13 use *bošet* "shame" to denote Baal explicitly in condemnatory contexts (p. 82-83)
2. **Jer 3:24:** "The shameful thing (*bošet*) has devoured all..." — another clear Baal reference
3. **Pattern:** Individuals with *baʻal* in their names get the *bošet* substitution — and ONLY those individuals. This targeted pattern can't be coincidence (p. 83)
4. **Against Tsevat:** Tsevat's alternative (Akkadian *baštu* "dignity, pride") fails because: (a) it doesn't explain why ONLY individuals with *baʻal* names get this second name; (b) Tsevat ignores the passages where *bošet* clearly means "shame" for Baal, where "dignity" would be contextually absurd (p. 83)
5. **Against Hamilton:** Hamilton's "protective spirit" reading is "forced" — *baštu* means "dignity, pride, vigour," not "protective spirit" (p. 84)

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** This is settled scholarship. The explicit usage in Hos 9:10 and Jer 11:13 proves the word was used as a Baal-substitute. The targeted pattern in personal names (only Baal-names get *bošet* variants) is a pattern that demands explanation, and scribal substitution is the only one that works.

## Stakes

Every time you read "Ish-bosheth" or "Mephibosheth" in Samuel, you're reading a theological edit. The original names honored Baal. The scribes couldn't change history but they could change the names.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** M. Tsevat (Akkadian *baštu* "dignity") and G.J. Hamilton ("protective spirit"). Day's rebuttals are thorough and convincing.

**Alternative reading:** Tsevat's theory would require *bošet* to mean "dignity" in names but "shame" everywhere else — an impossible semantic split that ignores the selective pattern.
