# fleming-ybi-2.4: The Soleb and 'Amarah West lists derive from a single moment of Egyptian contact with the Shasu

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 2 — Yhwʒ of Shasu-Land]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"It is equally important to recognize what lies behind the textual commonality of the Soleb and the 'Amarah West lists: a single moment of Egyptian contact with the Shasu people in circumstances that could generate knowledge of real Shasu group-names" (Fleming, p. 70). "This one list produces the opposite effect [of the Shasu stereotype], acknowledging the known individuality of each named entity. It did not happen again, which suggests all the more that there was some particular purpose to the administrative occasion" (p. 73).

## Evidence

- The Shasu-land names are Semitic — Egyptians wouldn't have made them up
- Their recurrence in two related Nubian temple displays indicates a single source
- The Soleb version is earlier (Amenhotep III), so the contact occurred during or before his reign
- No other Egyptian text names Shasu subdivisions — this list is unique
- The list's specificity (actual named groups) contrasts with the normal Egyptian practice of stereotyping the Shasu as a generic category

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The single-source inference is methodologically sound — the two temple lists are clearly related, and their specificity is unique. But "single military encounter" vs. "ongoing administrative relationship" is acknowledged by Fleming as uncertain (p. 71).

## Stakes

If the names come from a single encounter, they give us a genuine snapshot of Shasu political organization at one moment in the early 14th century. We're not looking at an evolving tradition but at a fixed data point. This makes the evidence both more precious (genuine contact) and more limited (one moment, one Egyptian perspective).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** This is Fleming's own synthesis. The uniqueness of the list hasn't been emphasized this way before.

**Alternative reading:** The names could derive from ongoing Egyptian administration of Shasu territory — not a single battle but accumulated administrative knowledge over decades.

## Assessment

The "single encounter" reading is elegant and fits the evidence well, but it's speculative. The key point — that these are real indigenous names, not Egyptian inventions — is more important than the "single moment" framing.
