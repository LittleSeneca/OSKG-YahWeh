# heiser-ur-10.1: The "sons of God" in Genesis 6:1-4 were divine beings who produced the Nephilim — the Sethite interpretation is a late apologetic invention

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 3 — Divine Transgressions]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapters 10-13

## The Claim

Genesis 6:1-4 describes divine beings (*bĕnê hā'ĕlōhîm*) descending to earth and fathering children with human women. This is NOT about the line of Seth marrying the line of Cain (the standard evangelical reading). The "sons of God" are the same divine council members from Job and Psalms. Their offspring were the Nephilim — giants, demigods. This event was a cosmic transgression that corrupted humanity and prompted the Flood.

## Evidence

**The lexical argument:**
- *Bĕnê hā'ĕlōhîm* ALWAYS refers to divine beings in the Hebrew Bible (Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7)
- Nowhere does the Hebrew Bible use this phrase for human beings
- The Sethite interpretation requires an unprecedented usage

**The Septuagint evidence:**
- The LXX translates "sons of God" as "angels of God" (*hoi angeloi tou theou*)
- This is the earliest Jewish interpretation — pre-Christian

**The Second Temple evidence:**
- 1 Enoch, Jubilees, and other Second Temple literature unanimously read Genesis 6 as angels
- This was the standard Jewish interpretation for centuries

**The New Testament evidence:**
- Jude 6-7 references "angels who sinned" and connects to sexual immorality — linking to Genesis 6
- 2 Peter 2:4-6 references "angels who sinned" in the context of the Flood

**The origin of the Sethite view:**
- Julius Africanus (3rd century CE) originated the Sethite interpretation
- It was motivated by apologetic concerns — discomfort with angels fathering children
- It is late and represents a departure from the earlier interpretive tradition

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The *bĕnê hā'ĕlōhîm* = divine beings reading is the scholarly consensus, even among critical scholars who have no theological stake. It's only conservative evangelicals who insist on the Sethite view. The lexical, Septuagint, and Second Temple evidence is unanimous.

## Stakes

This interpretation connects Israelite religion to a broader ANE pattern of divine-human hybridization myths (Greek gigantomachy, Mesopotamian apkallu traditions). It also explains the persistent biblical theme of giants/Nephilim/Rephaim (Numbers 13:33, Deuteronomy 2-3, Joshua, Goliath). If Heiser is right, the biblical worldview includes a genuine cosmic transgression by divine beings that corrupted humanity's genetic lineage — a far more dramatic narrative than the Sethite "unequally yoked marriages" reading.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservative evangelicals who hold the Sethite view (sons of Seth married daughters of Cain). This is the dominant reading in evangelical seminaries and popular theology. Critical scholars who reject the historical reality of angels/Nephilim — they read Genesis 6 as mythology borrowed from ANE sources.

**Alternative reading:** The "sons of God" are the godly line of Seth, and the "daughters of men" are the ungodly line of Cain. The sin is intermarriage between believers and unbelievers — a moral transgression, not a cosmic one. The Nephilim are simply "mighty men of old, men of renown" — human warriors, not demigods.

## Assessment

This is one of Heiser's strongest claims because it aligns with the scholarly consensus. The Sethite interpretation is a late apologetic innovation with no lexical or historical support. The lexicography is unambiguous: *bĕnê hā'ĕlōhîm* always means divine beings in the Hebrew Bible. The Septuagint and Second Temple literature confirm this. Heiser is not being radical here — he's simply reading the text as the earliest interpreters read it, before apologetic concerns produced the Sethite alternative.
