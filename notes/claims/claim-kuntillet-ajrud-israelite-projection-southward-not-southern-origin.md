# fleming-ybi-4.3: The Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions show Israelite projection southward, not southern origin

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 4 — The Old Poetry]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"Rather, we have here evidence for devotion by Israelites to their own god Yahweh in identification with his southern presence" (Fleming, p. 170). "In either case, Yahweh of Teman was a manifestation of Israelite worship, joining the perception that Yahweh somehow inhabited the wilderness far from Israel (and Judah) to the projection of Israelite, Samaria-based, presence into that very space" (p. 171).

## Evidence

- Kuntillet Ajrud (c. 800 BCE) has three invocations of "Yahweh of Teman" and one of "Yahweh of Samaria"
- The Samaria connection shows the site was Israelite (northern kingdom), not Judahite
- The site is a waystation on a road, not a pilgrimage destination — its purpose is travel-related, not cultic in the temple sense
- Spencer Allen (2015: 271): "no native Temanite community need be assumed"
- The plaster inscription (KA 4.2) contains poetry strikingly similar to Habakkuk 3 — "when El shines forth," mountains melt, earthquake, the Holy One
- The Kuntillet Ajrud poetry shows the same motifs as the biblical old poetry but with completely different wording — demonstrating creative variability, not fixed ancient transmission
- The site's alignment with Samaria means Israel was projecting power/religion southward, not receiving religion from the south

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The archaeological evidence is clear: Kuntillet Ajrud was an Israelite site. The "Yahweh of Teman" inscriptions show Israelites worshipping their own god in his southern manifestation.

## Stakes

Kuntillet Ajrud has often been cited as evidence for Yahweh's southern origins. Fleming flips it: it's evidence for Israelite projection southward. This is a genuinely important reinterpretation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Meshel (the excavator) considered it a religious site. Some interpret "Yahweh of Teman" as evidence for a local Edomite cult of Yahweh that Israelites encountered.

**Alternative reading:** The presence of "Yahweh of Teman" alongside "Yahweh of Samaria" could indicate that two different Yahwehs (local manifestations) met at this crossroads site — one from the north (Samaria), one from the south (Teman).

## Edges

**Supports:**
- [[claim-southern-geography-theophany-not-origin-memory]] — the Kuntillet Ajrud evidence independently confirms that southern geography reflects perceptions of Yahweh's dwelling, not historical origin

**Contradicts:**
- [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-proves-consort]] — Fleming's Israelite-projection reading transforms KA from evidence for southern Yahwism into evidence for Israelite expansion; the same inscriptions that Römer and Dever read as proving Yhwh had a consort, Fleming reads as proving Israelites projected their god southward

## Assessment

Fleming's reading is more parsimonious. The site is clearly Israelite (Samaria connection). The simplest explanation is that Israelites brought their god with them southward, not that they encountered him there. But the possibility of bidirectional influence can't be ruled out entirely.
