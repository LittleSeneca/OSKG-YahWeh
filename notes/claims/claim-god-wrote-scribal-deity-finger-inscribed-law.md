# stav-god-12-15.4: God wrote — he was a literate, scribal deity who produced texts with his finger, kept books, and conformed to the ANE pattern of scribal gods depicted with stylus and tablet.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part IV — Arms and Hands]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 15

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that God was a writer: "His finger inscribed the law. He kept books — the 'book of life,' the 'book of remembrance.'" This is the embodied God as author — a deity with the dexterity and literacy to produce written documents. In ANE iconography, scribal deities are depicted with stylus and tablet — God conforms to this pattern. The tablets of the covenant were not dictated to Moses; they were "written with the finger of God" (Exodus 31:18, Deuteronomy 9:10).

## Evidence

- Exodus 31:18 / Deuteronomy 9:10: The tablets of the covenant "written with the finger of God" — divine handwriting, not dictation
- Exodus 32:32-33 / Psalm 69:28: The "book of life" — God maintains a written register of the living and the blotted-out
- Malachi 3:16: The "book of remembrance" — God records the deeds of the righteous
- Daniel 5:5, 24-28: The hand writes on the wall at Belshazzar's feast — divine fingers producing text visible to humans
- Psalm 139:16: "In your book were written all the days that were formed for me" — divine pre-writing of human life
- ANE iconography: Egyptian god Thoth, Mesopotamian god Nabu — scribal deities depicted with stylus and tablet; Yahweh conforms to this established divine type

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The textual evidence for God as writer is extensive across multiple biblical books; the ANE iconographic context confirms the pattern.

## Stakes

A god who writes has fingers — real fingers capable of the fine motor control required for inscription. Divine writing implies divine literacy and divine manual dexterity, both of which require a body. The tablets of the covenant are the most significant documents in the Hebrew Bible, and they were produced not by divine fiat but by divine finger.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those who read "finger of God" as a metaphor for direct divine causation (as in Exodus 8:19, where the Egyptian magicians say "this is the finger of God")

**Alternative reading:** "Written with the finger of God" means "written by direct divine agency" — not by a literal finger, just as "the hand of God" means divine power.

## Assessment

The "finger of God" gets allegorized because a literal finger seems too anthropomorphic — but the text provides no warrant for this. The same finger that writes the tablets is the finger the Egyptian magicians identify in Exodus 8:19 — and that context is explicitly about divine power manifested in a liminal, physical way. Stavrakopoulou's connection to scribal deities is insightful: Yahweh is doing what Thoth and Nabu do, using the same body part (fingers) and producing the same objects (texts).
