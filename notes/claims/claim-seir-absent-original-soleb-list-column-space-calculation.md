# fleming-ybi-2.2: Seir (S'rr) was probably NOT in the original Amenhotep III Soleb list

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 2 — Yhwʒ of Shasu-Land]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"Considerations of space make it highly unlikely that S'rr could have been part of the Soleb list, which would then require uniquely mismatched α and β sets of six and three names" (Fleming, p. 84). "Aside from the debate over identification of ś'rr from 'Amarah West... the published drawings for the Soleb temple make it difficult to restore 'Seir' to the older Shasu list" (p. 64).

## Evidence

- Column N4 at Soleb has space for exactly 9 prisoners (Fleming's own measurement calculations, done with Elizabeth Knott and Nancy Fleming, pp. 63-64)
- Every other column at Soleb divides names into sets of 4+5 or 5+4 — a 6+3 split would be unique
- The 'Amarah West list has 6 names, the Soleb list has at most 5 (and probably 4: Trbr, Yhw3, Smt, Pyspys)
- Giveon (1964: 245) already doubted room for six cartouches
- The reverse order in the Ramses II copy (S'rr at the head) suggests scribal reorientation
- Seir only becomes associated with Shasu in the 13th century (Ramses II obelisk, Papyrus Harris I)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The physical measurement argument is clever and original but depends on drawings at 1:10 scale with acknowledged imprecision. However, Giveon independently doubted six names would fit. The convergence of physical + historical + pattern arguments (13th-century Seir-Shasu association only) is strong.

## Stakes

Enormous. If Seir was NOT in the original list, the sole geographical anchor for Yhw3 disappears. Without Seir, Yhw3 is somewhere in "the inland back country" — could be south, could be east, could be north. The Midianite Hypothesis's Egyptian pillar collapses.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Giveon (1971), Ahituv (1984), Redford (1992), and essentially everyone who has worked on these texts. They all restore Seir to the Soleb list from the Amarah West copy. Grdseloff (1947) made this identification first and it's been canonical since.

**Alternative reading:** The duplicated -rr is normal Late Egyptian orthography (Redford 1992: 272 n.67), the "careless orthography of the whole list" explains it (Ahituv 1984: 169), and Seir was always there. Fleming's space calculation is based on reconstructed measurements from drawings — a single error in column diameter transforms the conclusion.

## Assessment

This is Fleming's most original and consequential argument. If he's right, the entire geography of Yahweh's origins shifts. The physical measurement is ingenious but vulnerable — I'd want an Egyptologist to verify the column dimensions independently. The historical argument (Seir-Shasu connection only emerges in 13th c.) is independent of the measurement and may be stronger.
