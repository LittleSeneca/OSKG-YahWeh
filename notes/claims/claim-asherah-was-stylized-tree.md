# day-ygc-2.4: The Asherah was a stylized tree, not a living tree, grove, shrine, or simple image

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 2 — Yahweh and Asherah]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 2: Yahweh and Asherah

## The Claim

"There has been growing evidence in recent years that enables us to be more precise and specify that the Asherah was not simply a bare pole but more specifically a stylized tree" (p. 56).

## Evidence

### What It Was NOT

1. **NOT living trees** (LXX "grove," Mishnah trees): OT speaks of "making" (*ʻāśâ*) Asherim, "building" (*bānâ*), "erecting" (*nāṣab*) — verbs you don't use for living trees. Jer 17:2 mentions Asherim "beside every luxuriant tree" — odd if Asherim were themselves living trees (p. 54-55)
2. **NOT groves or shrines** (Lipinski): Both meanings would have to coexist in identical contexts, which makes no sense. "Cutting down" a shrine is incoherent (p. 55)
3. **NOT simply images of the goddess** (Reed): Asherim are listed alongside *pesîlîm* "graven images" as distinct objects in Deut 7:5, 12:3, Mic 5:12-13, 2 Chron 33:7, 19, 34:3-4, 7 (p. 55). However, anthropomorphic images of Asherah DID exist separately: *pesel hāʼăšērâ* in 2 Kgs 21:7
4. **NOT the pillar figurines** (Engle): The verbs indicate wooden objects (not pottery), the Asherim were public/syncretistic (figurines mostly in private houses), and Asherim are distinguished from images per se (p. 56)

### Positive Evidence for Stylized Tree

1. **Kuntillet Ajrud pithos A:** Stylized tree flanked by two ibexes above a lion — Hestrin and Hadley argue this depicts the Asherah mentioned in the inscription (p. 57)
2. **Lachish ewer** (13th c. BCE): Tree flanked by ibexes with inscription *mtn. šy l[rb]ty ʼlt* "Mattan. An offering to my Lady Elat" — AND Elat (= Asherah) appears directly above the tree drawing (p. 57)
3. **Lachish goblet:** Four times depicts ibexes flanking a pubic triangle instead of a tree — "further strengthening the view that the tree symbolizes a fertility goddess" (p. 57)
4. **Late Bronze pendants:** Naked female with tree/branch above pubic triangle, sometimes with Hathor headdress characteristic of Qudshu (= Asherah) (p. 57)
5. **Taanach cult stand** (10th c. BCE): Four tiers — second tier has sacred tree between ibexes, fourth tier has naked female between lions. Same flanking lions = same deity, so tree = Asherah's symbol (p. 57-58)
6. **Pella cult stands:** Nude goddesses on lion's head; stylized trees on sides (p. 58)
7. **Hos 14:9 (ET 8):** Yahweh IS a "luxuriant cypress" — uniquely compared to a tree, in a context condemning idols. Day argues this is Hosea appropriating Asherah's tree symbolism for Yahweh: "It is I who answer and look after him" (*ʼănî ʻānîtî waʼăšûrennû*) = wordplay on Anat and Asherah (p. 58-59)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** High for stylized tree identification. The iconographic convergence (Lachish ewer + Kuntillet Ajrud + Taanach + Pella + pendants + goblet) is extensive. Medium for the Hosea wordplay.

## Stakes

The Asherah was a recognizable religious symbol with consistent iconography across centuries and sites. This wasn't folk improvisation — it was an established cultic tradition with a specific visual vocabulary. That level of consistency across the region implies organized worship, not ad-hoc folk practice.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Lemaire (living trees — but the "making" verbs are decisive against this). Lipinski (grove/shrine — but the cutting-down language kills shrine, and "under every luxuriant tree" kills grove). Olyan and Frevel think Hos 14:9 doesn't necessarily reference Asherah (Day concedes the Isaiah apocalypse dependence doesn't *prove* the Hosea reading was correct, but believes it was).

**Alternative reading:** The asherah was a bare pole (not a stylized tree) — the iconographic evidence shows tree imagery associated with goddess worship generally, but the specific asherah object in Yahwistic contexts was simpler.

## Assessment

The iconographic evidence Day marshals is impressive and multi-site. The Lachish ewer is the closest thing to a labeled depiction — "my Lady Elat" above the tree. The Taanach stand's parallel tiers make the tree-goddess equation explicit. Day's weakest link is the Hosea 14 wordplay (it IS clever and characteristic of Hosea, but the double wordplay on both Anat AND Asherah in a single verb is a lot to hang on one word). The Isaiah apocalypse dependence (Isa 27:9) is actually the stronger argument: a later biblical writer detected what Hosea was doing and pushed back.
