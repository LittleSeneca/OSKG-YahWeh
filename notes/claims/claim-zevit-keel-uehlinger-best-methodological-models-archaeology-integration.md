# dever-dghw-1-2.3: Zevit and Keel/Uehlinger represent the best methodological models for integrating archaeological evidence

**Source:** [[Dever — Introduction and Chapters I-II — Methodology]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Introduction and Chapters I-II

## The Claim

Dever's survey of the history of scholarship ranks Zevit (*The Religions of Ancient Israel*, 2001) and Keel/Uehlinger (*Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God*, 1998) as the best recent work because they treat archaeological and iconographic evidence as primary rather than supplementary. The earlier schools — History of Religions, Myth and Ritual, OT Theology — either ignored material evidence entirely or subordinated it to texts. Even the best recent text-based scholars (Smith, Römer, Day) still under-utilize archaeology. The integration of texts AND artifacts is the methodological gold standard.

## Evidence

**Dever's ranking of schools (Chapter II survey):**

| School | Key Figures | Archaeological Integration |
|--------|------------|---------------------------|
| History of Religions | Wellhausen, Gunkel | None — purely textual |
| Myth and Ritual | Hooke, Engnell | None — theoretical impositions |
| Old Testament Theology | Eichrodt, von Rad, Wright | None — apologetics, not history |
| Sociological approaches | Gottwald, Mendenhall | Minimal — texts still primary |
| Recent revival — Smith | Mark S. Smith | Acknowledges archaeology but doesn't let it change text-based conclusions |
| Recent revival — Zevit | Ziony Zevit | Strong — treats artifacts as independent evidence |
| Recent revival — Keel/Uehlinger | Othmar Keel, Christoph Uehlinger | Best — iconography as primary data, periodized and quantified |

**Dever's praise for Zevit:** He highlights Zevit's "openness to archaeological evidence" and his willingness to let material culture challenge textual reconstructions.

**Dever's praise for Keel/Uehlinger:** Their iconographic approach — working from 8,500+ seal amulets, periodizing the evidence, analyzing "constellations" of motifs — is the model for what archaeological religious history should look like.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Dever's ranking reflects his disciplinary commitments — he praises the scholars who share his methodological values. The claim that text-only approaches are "incomplete" is debatable if the question is about beliefs rather than practices.

## Stakes

If Dever's ranking is correct, then the most reliable scholarship in the field is that which integrates material and textual evidence. This means the Truth Project should weight Keel/Uehlinger's iconographic arguments more heavily than Smith's text-based arguments on questions where material evidence exists — particularly the Asherah question, the aniconism question, and the question of when goddess worship actually disappeared.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Text-based scholars (Smith, Römer, Day) would argue that their work IS informed by archaeology — they read the archaeological literature and incorporate its findings — but that the central questions about Israelite religion (the nature of Yahweh, the development of monotheism, the relationship between Yahweh and other deities) are fundamentally textual questions that artifacts alone cannot answer.

**Alternative reading:** The best approach is neither text-primary nor artifact-primary but genuinely interdisciplinary — with the relative weight of evidence types determined by the specific question being asked.

## Assessment

Dever's ranking is predictable given his disciplinary commitments, but it's not wrong. Keel/Uehlinger's iconographic approach IS the most thorough integration of material evidence with textual analysis in the field. The question is whether this makes their conclusions more reliable or simply different. Iconography can tell us what images people used; it cannot tell us what those images meant to them. For that, we still need texts — and the texts are ambiguous. The best scholarship triangulates: texts + artifacts + comparative ANE data. Keel/Uehlinger do all three.
