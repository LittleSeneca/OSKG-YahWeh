# stav-god-16-21.1: God's face could be seen — sometimes fatally, sometimes safely — and the tension in the Hebrew Bible is not between a God with a face and without one, but between seeing it and surviving it.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part V — Head + Epilogue]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 16

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that God's face is "the most sought-after and the most dangerous part of his body." The tension in the Hebrew Bible is not between "God has a face" and "God doesn't have a face," but between "you can see it" and "seeing it will kill you." Some people see God's face and live (Moses, Jacob); others expect to die (Isaiah, Manoah). The lack of a consistent rule proves the face is real — it's too dangerous in one context and accessible in another because it IS a real face, not a metaphor for revelation.

## Evidence

- Moses sees God "face to face" (*pānîm 'el pānîm*) — Exodus 33:11; Numbers 12:8; Deuteronomy 34:10 — explicitly, repeatedly
- Jacob: "I have seen God face to face, and yet my life is preserved" (Genesis 32:30) — the surprise confirms the danger
- Isaiah 6:1-5: Isaiah sees God's face on the throne and cries "Woe is me! I am lost!" — expects to die; purged by the coal instead
- The Aaronic blessing: "Yahweh make his face shine upon you" (Numbers 6:25) — God's face shining IS blessing
- "Seek my face" (*baqqĕšû pānay*) is a liturgical call to worship (Psalm 27:8) — worshippers are invited to look at God's face
- Exodus 33:20: "You cannot see my face, for no one shall see me and live" — the prohibition proves the face EXISTS; you don't prohibit seeing something that isn't there

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** The face texts are among the most explicit in the Bible — Moses sees God face-to-face multiple times, and the prohibition to not see the face confirms its existence.

## Stakes

The face is the most personal part of the body — seeing someone's face is knowing them. If God has a face, then relationship with God is literally face-to-face. The tension between "you can see it" and "don't look or you'll die" is exactly the tension around a real dangerous object, not an abstraction.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Maimonides (who read all face language as intellectual vision), and the allegorical tradition generally

**Alternative reading:** "Face" (*pānîm*) is a Hebrew idiom for "presence" — to seek God's face is to seek his presence, not his literal visage.

## Assessment

The face texts are Stavrakopoulou's strongest corpus. "Face to face" (*pānîm 'el pānîm*) is the highest relational language in the Bible, and it describes Moses' relationship with God in irreducibly physical terms. The prohibition ("you cannot see my face and live") is the strongest proof the face exists — you don't prohibit looking at something that isn't there. The very danger of the face is bodily danger: proximity to divine radiance that would destroy a human body.
