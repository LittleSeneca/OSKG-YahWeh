# kaufmann-ri-intro.2: The Torah is pre-prophetic — the literary product of the earliest stage of Israelite religion

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Introduction]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Introduction

## The Claim

"But its basic postulate — that the priestly stratum of the Torah was composed in the Babylonian exile, and that the literature of the Torah was still being written and revised in and after the Exile — is untenable. The Torah — it will be shown — is the literary product of the earliest stage of Israelite religion, the stage prior to literary prophecy." The Torah's sources "are demonstrably ancient — not, in part, not in their general content, but in their entirety, even to their language and formulation." Kaufmann accepts source criticism (JE, P, D are distinct) but reverses the sequence: P is EARLIEST, not latest. The Torah was "sealed and canonized" before prophetic literature was even ordered.

## Evidence

This is a promissory note — Chapters V and VI make this case. Kaufmann accepts source criticism (JE, P, D are distinct) but reverses the sequence: P is EARLIEST, not latest.

## Confidence

**Rating:** low

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's most radical historical claim; it contradicts not just Wellhausen but the entire documentary tradition. The linguistic evidence for P's lateness (Late Biblical Hebrew features) is substantial. Kaufmann will need extraordinary evidence to overturn it.

## Stakes

Everything. If P is pre-prophetic, then monotheistic law predates the prophets. The prophets aren't innovators — they're traditionalists calling Israel back to a monotheism it always had. This inverts the entire critical narrative.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Everyone in the critical tradition. Even scholars sympathetic to Kaufmann's overall project (Tigay, Sommer) don't follow him on P's dating. The linguistic and historical arguments for exilic/post-exilic P are among the most robust results of biblical criticism.

**Alternative reading:** Kaufmann may be right that SOME material in P is ancient (ritual texts, priestly traditions) even if the final redaction is late. This is the moderate position many scholars hold — P preserves old material in a late framework. Kaufmann's "entirety, even to language" claim goes far beyond what the evidence can support.

## Assessment

This is the hill Kaufmann is willing to die on, and it's probably where he's weakest. The critical dating of P rests on multiple converging lines (linguistics, institutional development, theological concepts). Kaufmann's alternative would need to explain ALL of those better than the consensus. I'm skeptical he can, but I need to read Chapters V-VI carefully to see if he has arguments I haven't encountered.
