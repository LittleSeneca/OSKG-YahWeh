# schmid-ht-1.6: The Persian period is more important for literary formation than the pre-exilic period

**Source:** [[Schmid — §1 — Is There a Theology of the Hebrew Bible]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §1

## The Claim

Some American and Israeli scholars argue that "due to the absence of Hebrew inscriptions from the Persian period one should primarily place the formation of the Hebrew Bible in the preexilic period." Schmid counters: this "goes against historical probability. Persian period inscriptions were written in the lingua franca of the time, Aramaic. Furthermore, their number is significantly larger than the number of Hebrew inscriptions. In principle, the number of Persian period inscriptions, if anything, confirms the importance of the Persian period for the formation of the literature of the Hebrew Bible rather than constituting an argument against it."

## Evidence

- Persian period inscriptions exist — they're just in Aramaic, not Hebrew
- Hebrew was a literary/religious language in the Persian period, not an administrative one
- The absence of Hebrew inscriptions in the Persian period is exactly what you'd expect if Hebrew had become a scribal language used for literary production, not administration
- The argument from absence of Hebrew inscriptions is therefore invalid

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Schmid's counter-argument is logical and lexically sound. But the claim that Persian period literary formation is "more important" requires additional evidence — it can't rest solely on refuting the counter-argument. Schmid's fuller case would need to be built through the rest of the book.

## Stakes

This is the chronological foundation for Schmid's entire project. If the Persian period is the primary period of literary formation, then the HB's theological content is Persian-period theology, not pre-exilic religion. The entire evolutionary model (polytheism → monolatry → monotheism) might describe the PERSIAN PERIOD's theological imagination, not pre-exilic religious reality.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who date substantial portions of the HB to the monarchy (Cross, Freedman, many American scholars). The "early poetry" school that dates Exod 15, Judg 5, Deut 33, Hab 3 to the pre-monarchic or early monarchic period.

**Alternative reading:** The Persian period is important for the FINAL FORM of the texts, but the texts preserve earlier traditions. Schmid's claim that Persian-period scribes "constructed" Israel's past rather than "preserved" it is a judgment about scribal practice, not a necessary inference from dating.

## Assessment

This is where Schmid is most controversial — and most important for the Truth project. If he's right, the entire consensus edifice is built on sand. But "right" here has degrees: even if substantial material IS Persian-period, the question is whether ANY pre-exilic kernel survives. Schmid tends toward maximal skepticism; Römer, Smith, and Fleming each find more pre-exilic material than Schmid does. The truth probably lies somewhere between Schmid's maximalism and the American school's optimism.
