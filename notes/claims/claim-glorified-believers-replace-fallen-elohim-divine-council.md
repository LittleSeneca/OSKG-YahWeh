# heiser-ur-37.1: Glorified believers will replace the fallen *elohim* in the divine council

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 8 — The Kingdom Not Yet]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapters 37-38

## The Claim

"The idea of believers becoming *elohim* strikes most Christians as bizarre, but that is precisely Paul's point in 1 Corinthians 6:3: 'Do you not know that we are to judge angels?'" Psalm 82 promised the corrupt *elohim* would "die like men." Who takes their place? Glorified humans. God's original plan was for humans to rule creation alongside him (Genesis 1:26-28). The *elohim* were always meant to be ADMINISTRATORS, not replacements for humanity's role. When some *elohim* rebelled, they disrupted that plan. The restoration involves humans FINALLY taking the place God always intended — ruling alongside Yahweh in the divine council. The *elohim* were placeholders. We're the real thing.

## Evidence

- Psalm 8:5: "You have made him a little lower than *elohim*" (reading *elohim*, not "angels" as most translations)
- Revelation 2:26-27: "The one who conquers... I will give authority over the nations" — the same authority the *elohim* had
- Revelation 3:21: "The one who conquers, I will grant him to sit with me on my throne"
- 1 Corinthians 6:2-3: "Do you not know that the saints will judge the world?... Do you not know that we are to judge angels?"
- Hebrews 2:5-10: The "world to come" is not subject to angels but to humans — specifically, to Christ and "the many sons" he brings to glory

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Psalm 8:5 reading *elohim* is textually defensible (MEDIUM-HIGH). The 1 Corinthians 6:3 and Revelation texts are suggestive. The overall framework is internally coherent but depends on accepting Heiser's prior claims about the divine council, Psalm 82, and the Deut 32 worldview.

## Stakes

The cosmic trajectory: God's original plan was for humans to rule creation alongside him. The *elohim* were administrators who went rogue. The restoration isn't just about forgiving human sin — it's about humans finally taking the place in the divine council that God always intended. This reframes salvation as elevation to divine-council status, not just forgiveness.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Virtually all confessional traditions that reject human deification. Eastern Orthodox theology has a concept of *theosis* that bears some resemblance but operates within a very different framework. Most Protestant and Catholic theology would reject the idea that believers become *elohim* in any sense.

**Alternative reading:** The "judging of angels" in 1 Corinthians 6:3 refers to believers' participation in Christ's judgment, not to replacing angels in a divine council. "Authority over the nations" in Revelation is about reigning with Christ in the millennium, not filling vacated divine-council seats.

## Assessment

This is Heiser's most provocative claim — and the one that most clearly separates his framework from traditional Christian theology. If the *elohim* are placeholders, then human destiny is not merely to be forgiven but to be elevated. Heiser's cosmic trajectory (Genesis 1:26-28 → Fall → Babel → *elohim* as administrators → Cross defeats them → Second Coming replaces them with glorified humans) is the most comprehensive single-framework reading available. But it's also the most theologically radical, which is why Heiser spends so much time building the exegetical foundation before arriving here.
