# stav-god-9-11.1: God had a back — Moses saw it — and it was made of divine radiance (kavod), described in precise anatomical terms in Exodus 33.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part III — Torso]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 9

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that Exodus 33:23 — "You will see my back, but my face shall not be seen" — is literal, not metaphorical. God has a back. Moses glimpsed it. The story is not about partial revelation — it describes a physical encounter with a deity whose body was dangerously luminous. The Exodus 33 theophany is the most detailed description of God's body in the Bible.

## Evidence

- Exodus 33:22-23: God's hand covers Moses during the passing; God's back becomes visible as he departs
- God's "glory" (*kavod*) is his bodily radiance — so bright it must be screened by cloud and rock
- The passage anatomizes the divine body in precise, sequential terms: hand → face → back → departure
- "The passage anatomizes the divine body in precise, sequential terms"
- The hand covering Moses is physical contact, not a metaphor for protection

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Exodus 33 text is unambiguously describing a body with sequential anatomical exposure.

## Stakes

If God has a back — a physical, visible back made of dangerous radiance — then the concept of God as pure spirit, invisible, and immaterial is a later theological development, not the biblical baseline. The Exodus 33 theophany is the closest any human gets to full divine exposure, and it's described in irreducibly bodily terms.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Kaufmann, and the entire tradition of Jewish and Christian allegorical interpretation that reads Exodus 33 as a metaphor for the limits of human knowledge

**Alternative reading:** The "back" is a metaphor for partial revelation — God's "after-effects" or "traces" in creation. Maimonides read the entire passage as an intellectual vision, not a physical one.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou's reading of Exodus 33 as literal bodily anatomy is persuasive. The text is sequential and specific: God places his hand, passes by, and Moses sees the back. This is not the language of intellectual vision — it's a physical encounter with a body whose parts are named and whose radiance must be screened. The allegorical tradition that reads this as limited revelation is the later overlay, not the text's own register.
