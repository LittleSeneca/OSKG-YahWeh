# tigay-nog-2.6: Even court circles lacked significant polytheistic following

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter II + Conclusions]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Conclusions

## The Claim

"The inscriptional evidence, part of which reflects the upper classes in the time of Manasseh, does not contradict these charges against the kings, but it indicates that this polytheism must have attracted few adherents even among the circles connected with the court." Even during the reign of Manasseh — portrayed in the Bible as the most polytheistic king of Judah — the seals and bullae of court officials bear overwhelmingly Yahwistic names.

## Evidence

- Seals and bullae from the time of Manasseh (early-mid 7th century) associated with court officials show the same overwhelming YHWH-dominance as other periods
- The biblical charges against the KINGS may be accurate — royal polytheism (sponsored by Ahaz, Manasseh) was real — but it "attracted few adherents even among the circles connected with the court"
- This creates a picture of top-down royal polytheism that failed to penetrate even the immediate court environment, let alone the general population

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The inscriptional evidence from seals and bullae associated with court officials is consistent, but the sample may not be fully representative of court culture.

## Stakes

If even Manasseh's court circles were predominantly Yahwistic in their naming practices, the biblical portrayal of Manasseh as leading the nation into polytheism may be theologically exaggerated. Royal polytheism was real but limited in its reach — the king's personal practices didn't define the religious identity of his officials, let alone the populace.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-dever-folk-polytheism-outlasted-monarchy-monotheism-book-religion]] (Dever)

**Alternative reading:** Names on seals reflect public/official identity, not private belief. Court officials might bear Yahwistic names while participating in polytheistic cultic activities sponsored by the king. The seals show what people CALLED themselves, not what they DID.
