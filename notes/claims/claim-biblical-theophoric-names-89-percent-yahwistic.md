# tigay-nog-1.1: Biblical theophoric names are 89% Yahwistic

**Source:** [[Tigay — Chapter I — The Onomastic Evidence]] — Jeffrey H. Tigay, *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986), Chapter I

## The Claim

Of 466 individuals with theophoric names in the Bible (excluding ambiguous 'el/'eli names), 413 (89%) bear Yahwistic names, only 53 (11%) bear clearly or plausibly pagan names. The pagan names cluster in the early periods (Judges/United Monarchy: 14%); by the Divided Monarchy they drop to 3%.

## Evidence

| Period | Theo. Names | Yahwistic | Pagan |
|--------|------------|-----------|-------|
| Patriarchal | 3 | 0 (0%) | 3 (100%) |
| Exodus-Conquest | 6 | 3 (50%) | 3 (50%) |
| Judges-United Mon. | 163 | 140 (86%) | 23 (14%) |
| Divided Monarchy | 127 | 123 (97%) | 4 (3%) |
| Late Judah | 97 | 92 (95%) | 5 (5%) |
| **Total** | **466** | **413 (89%)** | **53 (11%)** |

- Tigay counted ba'al names among the "plausibly pagan" to give the case for pagan names "every reasonable benefit of the doubt." The real number is probably lower.
- The 8 individuals with ba'al names (7 from Judges/United Monarchy, 1 later) underwent deliberate scribal revision: Eshbaal → Ishbosheth, Beeliada → Eliada, Meribaal → Mephibosheth.
- These changes prove scribes were sensitive to pagan theophorics AND that "the editors did not suppress all pagan names" — those that remain suggest "the essentially non-polytheistic onomastic picture given by the Bible all the way back to the beginning of the Divided Monarchy, and perhaps earlier, is realistic."
- Even non-theophoric elements show YHWH-devotion. The most common predicates in Israelite names are verbs meaning "he gave" (ntn), "he helped" ('zr), "he created" (br'), "he heard" (šm'), "he remembered" (zkr) — all directed toward YHWH.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The name counts are publicly verifiable from the biblical text; Tigay counted ba'al names to give the case for pagan names "every reasonable benefit of the doubt."

## Stakes

This is the direct empirical challenge to the consensus. If 89% of biblical personal names are Yahwistic and pagan names drop to 3% by the Divided Monarchy, the biblical onomasticon itself testifies to overwhelming YHWH-devotion rather than rampant polytheism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-tigay-onomastics-does-not-disprove-polytheism]] (Smith), [[claim-tigay-onomastics-underestimates-pre-exilic-polytheism]] (Day)

**Alternative reading:** Smith argues that (a) Ugarit had few Asherah-names despite Asherah worship (the "Ugarit parallel"); (b) Punic culture had few Tannit-names despite Tannit's popularity; (c) personal names may reflect elite fashion, not popular religion. The 89% figure could indicate YHWH-as-chief-god rather than YHWH-as-only-god.

## Assessment

Tigay's data on biblical names is solid and his counting methodology is honest (he gave ba'al names the benefit of the doubt). The 89% figure is robust. The question is what it PROVES — Tigay himself is careful about this, and Smith's alternative readings (elite fashion, Ugarit parallel) must be weighed.
