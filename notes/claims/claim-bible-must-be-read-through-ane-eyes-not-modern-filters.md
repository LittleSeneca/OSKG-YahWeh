# heiser-ur-2.1: The Bible must be read through ancient Near Eastern eyes, not filtered through modern theological systems

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 1 — First Things]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 2

## The Claim

Most biblical education uses filters — content learned through presumptions and traditions that order material for the modern mind. "Verses that didn't quite work with my tradition were 'problem passages' that were either filtered out or consigned to the periphery." Instead, the Bible should be read like a mosaic: "The pattern in a mosaic often isn't clear up close. It may appear to be just a random assemblage of pieces. Only when you step back can you see the wondrous whole."

## Evidence

**Heiser's three rules of engagement:**

1. **Let the text speak:** Don't filter passages through later theological systems. Read what's actually there.
2. **Read in context:** The ancient Near Eastern context, not modern Western assumptions, is the proper lens.
3. **Trust the biblical writers:** They weren't stupid or primitive. If they used the word *elohim* for multiple beings, they knew what they were doing.

**The filter vs. mosaic metaphors:** The filter removes uncomfortable data. The mosaic assembles all the pieces, even the ones that don't fit initial expectations, and only then reveals the pattern.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Heiser's methodology is essentially the same as Smith, Römer, and other critical scholars — read the texts in their ANE context — but he applies it to defend rather than deconstruct traditional theology. The sincerity is not in question.

## Stakes

This is the methodological foundation for everything Heiser argues. If reading the Bible through ANE eyes is valid, then the divine council, the *elohim* categories, and the Deuteronomy 32 worldview all follow naturally. If ANE contextual reading is rejected, Heiser's entire project collapses. The stakes are whether evangelical theology can incorporate ANE comparative data without abandoning exclusive Yahwism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservative evangelicals who insist the Bible must be read on its own terms without "pagan" comparative context. Also, Kaufmann — he would argue that Israelite religion is fundamentally UNLIKE the ANE and that comparative method reads biblical evidence backwards through a pagan lens.

**Alternative reading:** The Bible is sui generis — uniquely inspired and fundamentally different from its ANE environment. Reading it through ANE parallels distorts rather than illuminates its message.

## Assessment

Heiser's methodology is the same as critical scholars — he just applies it to different conclusions. This makes him an interesting test case: can you accept all the ANE comparative data and still maintain that Israel was not polytheistic? Heiser says yes. The field largely says no.
