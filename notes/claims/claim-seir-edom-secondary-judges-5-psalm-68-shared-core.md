# fleming-ybi-4.2: The shared core of Judges 5:4-5 and Psalm 68:8-9 lacks southern geography

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 4 — The Old Poetry]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"The demonstrably oldest material should be what both have in common. This shared text would look something like the following: *Yahweh, when you went out, *when you walked, *the earth quivered, *the heavens dripped, *from before Yahweh, he of Sinai, *from before Yahweh, god of Israel" (Fleming, p. 153). "Psalm 68:8 lacks Seir and Edom, and instead, it develops Yahweh's movement without reference to the south" (p. 153).

## Evidence

- The two texts share verbatim elements but diverge on geography
- Judges 5 has "from Seir... from the open country of Edom" while Psalm 68 has "from Seir" / "from the open country of Edom" replaced with "in the wasteland" (yĕšîmôn)
- The shared text includes "he of Sinai" and "god of Israel" but NOT Seir or Edom
- "It is likely that Seir and Edom were invoked specifically for the Song of Deborah introduction, and the psalm does not demonstrate the higher antiquity of a shared hymnic fragment" (p. 154)
- The "wasteland" in Psalm 68 can refer to Moab, the wilderness between Babylon and Judah, or just "the back country" (Deut 32:10) — no specific southern location

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The textual-critical argument is clean: what both texts share is older than what only one has. The shared core has "he of Sinai" (a divine dwelling) but not Seir/Edom. This is a genuinely important observation that hasn't received enough attention in the literature.

## Stakes

If Seir and Edom are secondary additions to a fragment that only mentioned Sinai, the oldest recoverable form of the tradition doesn't point to the historical territories of Seir and Edom at all. It points to "Sinai" as a divine dwelling — a sacred mountain, not a people or a political territory.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Smith (2012a: 11) treats the differences as "ancient oral variants" — equally old alternative versions. Cross (1973: 101 n.35) called them "ancient oral variants."

**Alternative reading:** The differences between Judges 5 and Psalm 68 could reflect equally old oral variants of the same tradition. The "wasteland" in Psalm 68 may be a deliberate replacement of Seir/Edom for theological reasons (Jerusalem-focused), not evidence that Seir/Edom weren't original.

## Edges

**Supports:**
- [[claim-old-poetry-theophanic-movement-not-yahweh-origin-ugaritic-analogy]] — the shared-core argument strengthens the theophany-not-origin reading: the oldest form of the tradition has only Sinai (a divine dwelling), not Seir/Edom (historical territories)

**Contradicts:**
- [[claim-four-biblical-poems-preserve-southern-origin-tradition]] — if Seir and Edom are secondary elaborations, the poems don't preserve an ancient tradition of southern origin — they elaborate a divine-dwelling motif with later geographical specifics
- [[claim-yahweh-originated-south-midian-edom]] — Day treats the Seir/Edom geography in Judges 5 as primary evidence for southern origin; Fleming argues it's a secondary elaboration on an older Sinai-only tradition

## Assessment

Fleming's textual-critical approach is methodologically sounder than the "oral variants" approach. Shared = older is a standard principle. His reconstruction is elegant and persuasive. But the "Sinai" in the shared core is still a southern location, so this doesn't eliminate the southern connection — it just narrows it to Sinai specifically.
