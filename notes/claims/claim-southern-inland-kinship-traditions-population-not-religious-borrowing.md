# fleming-ybi-3.5: The Bible preserves genuine tradition about kinship with southern peoples — but this is population affinity, not religious borrowing

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 3 — The Midianite Hypothesis — Moses and the Priest]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 3

## The Claim

"The Midianites and the Kenites themselves are another matter entirely. Jethro — or Reuel, or Hobab — represents just one instance of a recurring theme in biblical accounts of origins and early days, when the predecessors of the biblical peoples manifest diverse lines of affinity and kinship with the inhabitants of the inland back country" (Fleming, p. 128). "This phenomenon constitutes the kind of 'tradition' that is no creation of monarchic or post-monarchic scribes" (p. 128).

## Evidence

- Diverse texts show southern/inland kinship: Esau as Jacob's twin (Gen 25), friendly Kenites (1 Sam 15:6, 27:10, 30:29), Moses' Midianite marriage, Deuteronomy's "your brother" Edomite (Deut 23:8)
- These traditions "creep into widely varied texts with a panoply of particulars" — suggesting genuine tradition, not scribal invention
- But NONE of them suggests Yahweh was borrowed from these peoples
- Tiele's original insight was about Israel AS a desert people, not about borrowing a god FROM desert peoples
- "Tiele's explanation of Yahweh's origin... depended first of all on the notion that Israel began as a desert people" (p. 129)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The observation that kinship traditions are widespread and varied is correct and important. But whether these all point to a single "collective memory" or represent different traditions from different periods is harder to assess.

## Stakes

This is the positive payoff of the chapter. Fleming isn't just destroying the Midianite Hypothesis — he's salvaging what it got right: Israel did have real affinities with inland/southern peoples. The error was interpreting those affinities as evidence for religious borrowing.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The Midianite Hypothesis camp, who see the kinship traditions AS evidence for religious transmission.

**Alternative reading:** Kinship and shared cult typically go together in the ancient Near East. If Israel was genuinely kin to Edom and Midian, shared worship of Yahweh (or a proto-Yahweh) is the most natural inference.

## Assessment

This is Fleming's most constructive move in the chapter. The tradition of southern/inland kinship is real and important. Fleming's interpretation (population affinities, not religious borrowing) is more cautious and probably more defensible than the traditional reading. But he may be drawing too sharp a line between "kinship" and "shared cult" — in the ancient world, these were often inseparable.
