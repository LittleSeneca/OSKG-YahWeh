# albertz-hir-4.7: The Deuteronomistic History was a theological compromise — a "historiography from above" that preserved kingship and temple hope

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol2 — Chapter 4 — Exilic Period]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume II* (1994), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"In addition to Deuteronomic theology, the positive aspects which the Deuteronomists advanced against a totally negative view of history like that of Ezekiel or JerD demonstrably drew on elements from the former official Jerusalem temple and kingship theology, now interpreted in Deuteronomic terms." The DtrG authors were "members of the ruling stratum of the Judahites who had remained at home" and their work reflects "the unique perspective of a 'historiography from above.'"

## Evidence

- DtrG shows clear interest in temple construction, equipment, treasure, and the ark (1 Kings 6-8; Josh 3f.; 1 Sam 4-6; 2 Sam 6)
- The election of Jerusalem is explicitly affirmed (1 Kings 8.16) alongside the election of David — both mitigate judgment (1 Kings 11.13,32,36; 15.4; 2 Kings 8.19)
- David is presented as utterly obedient — his merits and the Nathan promise repeatedly avert disaster
- The Deuteronomists pass over the prophets of judgment "glibly" (Koch's "prophetic silence") — Amos, Hosea, Micah, Isaiah of Jerusalem are never mentioned by name in Kings
- Social legislation of Deuteronomy is completely ignored; only cultic and religious laws are used as criteria
- The criterion for evaluating history is "one-sided restriction... to the religious, ritual, and cult-political level"
- The early monarchy (Saul through Solomon's temple completion) is presented as a time of pure Yahwism with NO syncretism
- The "sin of Jeroboam" (cultic separation from Jerusalem) is the lens through which the entire northern kingdom is condemned

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The literary-critical situation of DtrG is extremely disputed (Cross school vs. Smend school vs. block model vs. others). Albertz's "more open traditio-historical model" is one option among many. His claim about the social location (remaining Jerusalem priests) is plausible but speculative.

## Stakes

If DtrG is a compromise document, it explains its internal tensions without resorting to multiple redactions. It also explains why a work that supposedly learned the lesson of judgment still clings to the institutions (temple, monarchy) that were part of the problem. The Deuteronomistic History isn't a pure "repentance" document but a negotiated settlement between different theological positions.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Cross and his students (Nelson, etc.) posit a pre-exilic Josianic edition. Smend, Veijola, Dietrich posit multiple redactional layers (DtrH, DtrP, DtrN). Noth's original unity theory has few defenders today.

**Alternative reading:** The tensions in DtrG are evidence of multiple editions by different groups, not a single group's internal discussion. The "prophetic silence" may reflect a deliberate theological choice to focus on fulfilled prophecy (Ahijah, Elijah, Elisha) rather than the writing prophets.
