# lewis-ocg-3.3: Epigraphy Has Rewritten the History of Ancient Israelite Religion

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 3 — Methodology]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 3

## The Claim

"The field of epigraphy has rewritten the history of ancient Israelite religion... The simple fact alone that the earliest attestation of the divine name Yahweh comes from the ninth-century BCE Moabite inscription of King Mesha underscores the need for historians of Israelite religion to control the Northwest Semitic epigraphic record." Zevit argues "Israelite religion is most approachable through its manifestations in physical evidence discovered in archaeological excavations [including] inscriptions and drawings."

## Evidence

- Lewis runs through the epigraphic evidence for El (Proto-Sinaitic, Ugarit, Deir ʿAlla, Kuntillet ʿAjrud), El Elyon (Sefire, Karatepe, Philo of Byblos), El Shaddai (Ugarit, Deir ʿAlla), Baal, Yahweh (Shasu, Mesha, Kuntillet ʿAjrud, Khirbet el-Qom, Ketef Hinnom, Arad, Lachish), and Yahweh as Holy One (Kuntillet ʿAjrud)
- Inscriptions can be dated using archaeological context and script typology, unlike biblical texts where dating is fiercely contested
- The Mesha Stele (9th C BCE) provides the earliest attestation of Yahweh outside the Bible
- Epigraphic evidence is contemporary with the period it describes; biblical texts underwent centuries of transmission and editing

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** Very high on the importance of epigraphy; high on specific readings — Lewis himself notes that even leading epigraphists (including his teacher Cross) have made unsustainable readings.

## Stakes

If epigraphy is primary, biblical texts become secondary — contextualized and corrected by inscriptions rather than the other way around. This inverts the traditional hierarchy of evidence.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Biblical maximalists who trust the Hebrew Bible's historical framework. Literary critics who see the biblical text as self-contained and not needing external corroboration.

**Alternative reading:** Epigraphy and biblical texts are complementary, not hierarchical. Each has strengths (epigraphy: date, provenance; biblical texts: narrative context, theological depth). The best scholarship uses both.

## Assessment

Lewis's epigraphic emphasis is one of the book's greatest strengths. Chapters 4-7 will be thick with epigraphic data, and this is where Lewis (as a Cross student with Northwest Semitic training) has a genuine comparative advantage over scholars like Römer or Smith who are more text-centric.
