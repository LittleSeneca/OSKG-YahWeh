# albertz-hir-4.6: The Jeremiah Deuteronomists conducted a "popular missionary work of enlightenment" synthesizing Deuteronomy and Jeremiah

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol2 — Chapter 4 — Exilic Period]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume II* (1994), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"In JerD there is a popular missionary work of enlightenment." These theologians, descendants of the Gedaliah reform wing, combined the Deuteronomic law with Jeremiah's prophecy into a unified divine pedagogy. "Law and the prophets of judgment found a higher union in God's pedagogy; they were just two modes of the divine word (dabar)."

## Evidence

- JerD texts include judgment catechisms (Jer 5.19; 9.11-15; 16.10-13) with question-and-answer format designed for oral instruction
- Gate sermons (7.1-15; 22.1-5; 17.19-27) based on "texts" from prophetic sayings, constructed as admonitions with conditional promises — set in the gate where the legal assembly met, a non-cultic public forum
- JerD's theological synthesis: Yahweh made the covenant at the exodus, Israel was already disobedient, God sent a chain of prophets, Jeremiah was the last, and now the covenant is broken — but a new covenant is possible (Jer 31.31-34)
- Jeremiah is made parallel to Moses (Jer 1.7b,9,17 = Deut 18.18b) and identified as the prophet of Deut 18
- JerD takes Jeremiah's social accusations seriously alongside religious ones — "Mend your ways and your actions" (7.3,5) embraces both
- JerD explicitly criticizes false trust in the temple (7.4) and disputes that the sacrificial cult was ever part of God's original will (7.22f.; cf. Amos 5.25)
- JerD is restrained toward the monarchy: no election of David, no guarantee of dynasty, Jehoiachin remains rejected (Jer 22.24-30)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The JerD hypothesis is well-established in Jeremiah studies (Thiel, etc.), and Albertz's sociological location of the group in the Gedaliah reform wing is plausible. The pedagogical character of the texts is evident.

## Stakes

JerD represents one of the earliest syntheses of "law and prophets" — a canon-consciousness before the canon. It also shows that the exilic period wasn't just about theological reflection but active pastoral and educational work among ordinary people. The tension between JerD's this-worldly reform program and other groups' temple/cult-centered hopes prefigures post-exilic conflicts.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The unity and dating of the Deuteronomistic strata in Jeremiah are disputed. Some see JerD as post-exilic. The degree of actual "popular" reach (vs. literary fiction) is debatable.

**Alternative reading:** JerD was a small scribal circle with limited popular reach. Their "sermons" and "catechisms" are literary compositions, not records of actual pedagogical practice.
