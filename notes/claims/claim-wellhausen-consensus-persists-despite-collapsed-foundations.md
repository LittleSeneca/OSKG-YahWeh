# kaufmann-ri-intro.1: The Wellhausen consensus persists despite collapsed foundations

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Introduction]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Introduction

## The Claim

Biblical scholarship continues to adhere to Wellhausen's evolutionary conclusions even though "both the evidence and the arguments supporting this structure have been called into question and, to some extent, even rejected." Scholars admit the grounds have crumbled but won't abandon the conclusions. Kaufmann references "the critique of Wellhausen's theory which began some forty years ago" (i.e., since roughly the 1920s). The introduction functions as a promissory note — the evidence for this claim is the entire book.

## Evidence

Kaufmann doesn't enumerate the specific critiques here — he's writing for an audience that knows them. He references "the critique of Wellhausen's theory which began some forty years ago." The introduction functions as a promissory note — the evidence for this claim is the entire book.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the observation that Wellhausen's DH has faced serious challenges; LOW on Kaufmann's claim that scholars concede the foundations have crumbled while irrationally clinging to conclusions. Scholars like von Rad and Noth modified and developed the paradigm rather than mindlessly clinging.

## Stakes

Kaufmann's entire project. If Wellhausen's framework is actually robust, Kaufmann's "fundamental critique" is attacking a straw man. If he's right that the paradigm persists on inertia, then the field needs the comprehensive alternative he's offering.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The entire critical establishment of Kaufmann's day. Wellhausen's actual arguments about centralized worship, evolution of sacrifice, and dating of P remain debated on their merits. Römer engages these questions directly and concludes the evolutionary model is substantially correct, even if Wellhausen's specific datings need adjustment.

**Alternative reading:** The persistence of Wellhausen's framework isn't irrational inertia — it's that subsequent modifications (form criticism, tradition history) refined rather than refuted the basic evolutionary insight. The paradigm shifted but the direction of development (from pluralism to monotheism) held.

## Assessment

Kaufmann is partly right — there IS a tendency in biblical scholarship to treat Wellhausen's conclusions as settled even when the arguments for them have been qualified. But he overstates the collapse. The evolutionary model isn't a house with crumbled foundations; it's a house that's been renovated so many times the original blueprint is hard to recognize, but the basic structure (monotheism developed gradually, the Torah reached its final form late) is still standing.
