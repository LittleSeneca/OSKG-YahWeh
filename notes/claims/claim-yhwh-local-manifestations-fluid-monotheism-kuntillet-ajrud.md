# sommer-bog-2.2: Yhwh had local manifestations — "Yhwh of Samaria," "Yhwh of Teman," "Yhwh of Hebron"

**Source:** [[Sommer — Chapter 2 — Fluidity in Israel]] — Benjamin Sommer, *The Bodies of God* (2009), Chapter 2

## The Claim

J and E texts preserve the same fluidity model found in Mesopotamia and Canaan. Yhwh had distinct local manifestations similar to the multiple Ishtars of Assyrian treaties. This is not polytheism — it's fluid monotheism. One God, many embodiments.

## Evidence

**Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions (8th century BCE):**
- Pithos inscriptions mentioning "Yhwh of Samaria" and "Yhwh of Teman"
- "The phrasing... recalls the references to Ishtars of Arbela and Nineveh, and to Baals of Saphon, Ugarit, and Lebanon that were discussed in Chapter 1"
- A bench inscription in the gate shrine also references "Yhwh of Teman"

**Biblical verses:**
- 2 Samuel 15:7: "Yhwh of Hebron" — Absalom says, "Please, I should like to go to fulfill the vow I made to Yhwh in Hebron." Sommer's exegesis: "'In Hebron' is part of the DEITY'S NAME: The prince claims that he made a vow not simply to Yhwh nor to Yhwh of Jerusalem but to Yhwh of Hebron, and thus he had to leave Jerusalem to fulfill it — for in Jerusalem one can pray to Yhwh generally or to Yhwh of Jerusalem, but not to Yhwh of Hebron!"
- Psalm 99:2: "Yhwh at Zion"

**The parallel to Mesopotamia is exact:** Just as treaties listed Ishtar of Arbela and Ishtar of Nineveh as distinct beings with shared identity, so biblical texts recognize Yhwh of Samaria, Yhwh of Teman, Yhwh of Hebron, and Yhwh of Jerusalem as distinct-yet-one.

**A point of methodological honesty from Sommer:** "No single Hebrew text currently known refers to several of these manifestations together. Consequently, one cannot be sure that these terms refer to Yhwhs who were in some way independent of each other, but these texts at least allow one to raise such a possibility." He's careful not to claim more than the evidence allows.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH on the parallel to Mesopotamian practice (the phrasing is identical). MEDIUM that these Yhwhs were "independent" (Sommer acknowledges the evidence is thinner than for Ishtar).

## Stakes

If Sommer is right, references to "Yhwh of Teman" and "Yhwh of Samaria" that scholars like Römer treat as evidence of polytheism are actually evidence of a sophisticated fluid monotheism. The same Kuntillet Ajrud data, opposite interpretation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-yhwh-worshipped-diverse-local-manifestations]] (Römer frames local Yhwh manifestations as polytheistic pattern, not fluid monotheism)

**Alternative reading:** The "Yhwh of X" formula means "Yhwh as worshipped at X" — a geographical origin marker, not evidence of distinct divine manifestations.

## Assessment

This is Sommer's most original biblical argument. The exegesis of 2 Samuel 15:7 is particularly elegant: Absalom's vow to "Yhwh of Hebron" forces him to leave Jerusalem because Yhwh of Jerusalem and Yhwh of Hebron are distinct-yet-one. The parallel to the Ishtar treaties is exact, but Sommer is honest that the biblical evidence is thinner — no single text names multiple Yhwhs together the way Assyrian treaties name multiple Ishtars.
