# kaufmann-ri-3.6: Israelite prophecy is word-centered, not ecstatic

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

In pagan prophecy, "the speech of the rapt prophet is the very utterance of a god who has taken possession of him." In Israel, "prophesying frenzy never had primary mantic value... The primary revelation is the word of God that comes BEFORE the prophetic utterance: 'The prophet repeats the word of God that came to him beforehand.'" The spirit does not cause prophecy; prophecy (the divine word) can cause manifestations of the spirit. "Frenzy never is represented as a preliminary to prophecy." The ideal prophet is Moses, who speaks with God "face to face, as a man speaks with his fellow" (Exod 33:11) — no ecstasy, no spirit, no vision.

## Evidence

- Prophetic speech reported in past tense: "Thus said YHWH," "The word of YHWH came to me" — recounting prior revelatory experience
- Spirit of YHWH operates broadly (judges, kings, Nazirites, poets) without making them prophets. Spirit gives strength, wisdom, poetic ability — but not the word of God
- Primary revelation is not bound up with spirit or ecstasy. Abraham, Jacob, Moses, Joshua, Gideon, Samuel, Elijah, Amos, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Zechariah, Daniel — "no mention of the spirit"
- When spirit acts on seventy elders (Num 11), they "prophesy" but don't speak the word of God
- Ecstatic phenomena (fear, trembling, prostration, paralysis) "are described as results of the prophetic experience, rather than as preconditions to it"
- No external stimuli (wine, vapors, dancing) used to induce prophecy. Elisha's use of music is the sole exception
- Symbolic dreams assimilated to prophecy: God provides the interpretation, not the prophet's oneirocritic skill

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** High on the distinctive biblical conception of prophecy. Low on the claim that this represents the earliest historical form.

## Stakes

Whether Israelite prophecy is a unique phenomenon born from the monotheistic idea, or a local variant of ANE prophecy that developed distinctive features over time.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Mari and Neo-Assyrian prophecy studies find substantial parallels — messenger formula, divine commission, tension between "true" and "false" prophets. The distinctiveness Kaufmann identifies may reflect the BIBLICAL PORTRAYAL shaped by later prophetic self-understanding. Early ecstatic bands (1 Sam 10, 19) look very much like pagan ecstatic groups.

**Alternative reading:** Early Israelite prophecy WAS ecstatic and resembled pagan prophecy. The "word-centered" model is a LATER development — the classical prophets' self-understanding read back onto earlier figures. The ecstatic "bands of prophets" are the EARLIER phenomenon.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's analysis of the biblical CONCEPTION of prophecy is masterful. But he commits the characteristic error of assuming the Bible's theological self-understanding reflects historical origins. The ecstatic bands in Samuel look like the earlier stratum; the non-ecstatic ideal looks like later theological interpretation. Mari prophetic texts show the messenger formula and divine commission were present in prophecy that was ALSO ecstatic — they're not mutually exclusive.
