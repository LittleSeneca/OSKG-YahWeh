# kaufmann-ri-intro.7: Scholarship reads the evidence backwards — "in the light of paganism"

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Introduction]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Introduction

## The Claim

"Yet, as if in stubborn defiance of the very nature of our evidence, biblical scholarship seeks to read it... in the light of paganism!" The critical method assumes Israelite religion must have developed like other ancient Near Eastern religions, and interprets anomalous data as late editorial insertions rather than evidence of genuine distinctiveness.

## Evidence

Kaufmann points to the tendency to dismiss any monotheistic-sounding passage in early texts as a later insertion, creating a self-confirming paradigm where early texts can't be monotheistic because monotheism is late, and texts are dated late because they're monotheistic.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann has identified a genuine circularity in some critical methodology. The argument that "this passage sounds monotheistic, therefore it must be late" DOES assume what it's trying to prove. But Kaufmann has his own mirror-image circularity: assuming early monotheism and dismissing contrary evidence as popular deviance.

## Stakes

The methodological framework within which all the evidence is evaluated. If Kaufmann is right about the circularity, the entire critical dating scheme needs re-examination — not necessarily reversal, but at least reopening.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The critical tradition would say that dating isn't based on theological content alone but on multiple converging lines of evidence (language, institutions, historical references, literary dependencies). The circularity Kaufmann identifies is real but not as damning as he claims, because the dating scheme doesn't rest on a single pillar.

**Alternative reading:** The circularity is a necessary feature of historical reconstruction with limited data. All historical work involves interpretive frameworks that shape how evidence is read. The question is which framework accounts for MORE of the data with FEWER ad hoc adjustments.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's best point in the introduction. There IS a tendency in critical scholarship to assume that anything monotheistic-sounding must be late, and to date texts accordingly. Tigay's onomastic study is powerful precisely because it breaks this circularity — it uses an independent dataset (personal names) to test the question. But critiquing the circle doesn't prove Kaufmann's alternative is correct. It only shows the question should be genuinely open. And on the evidence we've accumulated (Kuntillet Ajrud, the figurines, Ugaritic parallels), the critical framework accounts for MORE data than Kaufmann's revolutionary model does.
