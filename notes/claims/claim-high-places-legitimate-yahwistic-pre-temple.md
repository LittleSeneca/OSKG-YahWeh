# dever-dghw-4.2: High places (bāmôt) were legitimate Yahwistic sanctuaries — not pagan sites

**Source:** [[Dever — Chapter IV — Biblical Reality vs Ideal]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Chapter IV

## The Claim

Before the Jerusalem Temple was built, high places (bāmôt) were legitimate Yahwistic sanctuaries. Their later condemnation as "Canaanite pagan practice" was a retrojection by Deuteronomistic reformers. The biblical writers themselves inadvertently document this shift.

## Evidence

- Saul visited a high place looking for a "seer" with no hint of disapproval (1 Sam 9:5-14)
- David's priest Zadok went to Gibeon's high place "according to all that is written in the law of the Lord" (1 Chron 16:37-42)
- Solomon and "the whole assembly" worshipped at Gibeon's high place (1 Kings 3:3-15)
- **The Deuteronomists' revealing footnote:** "The people were sacrificing at the high places, however, because no house had yet been built for the name of the Lord" (1 Kings 3:2) — the text itself acknowledges that high places were legitimate before the Temple
- "Curiously, polemics against the high places among the idealistic reformers of the late monarchy are conspicuously absent in descriptions of religious life in the United Monarchy"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The biblical texts themselves show the shift — the Deuteronomists' own footnote (1 Kings 3:2) admits the original legitimacy.

## Stakes

If high places were originally legitimate Yahwistic sanctuaries, then the Deuteronomistic condemnation of all worship outside Jerusalem was a late innovation, not the restoration of an original norm. The centralization of worship was a political-theological project, not ancient tradition.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The Deuteronomistic historians themselves. Kaufmann ([[claim-israelite-cult-sanctuaries-indigenous-not-canaanite-borrowing]]) would argue the sanctuaries were indigenous Israelite institutions that the Deuteronomists reformed for legitimate theological reasons.

**Alternative reading:** The high places were always syncretistic and the Deuteronomists were recovering an original aniconic, centralized Yahwism that had been corrupted.
