# kaufmann-ri-intro.6: The Bible's internal ideological consistency is as conclusive as material remains

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Introduction]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Introduction

## The Claim

"Especially significant are the inferences to be drawn from the ideological constants of the literary corpora, from the leading motifs of the various strata, from the several crystallizations of the historical tradition. These are the literary equivalent of geological stratification, and are as conclusive as the material remains of buildings, inscriptions, artifacts, and pottery."

## Evidence

He lists as examples: the biblical polemic against idolatry consistently misrepresenting paganism as fetishism; the peculiar biblical conceptions of sin, impurity, sacrifice, magic, dreams, prophecy; the blanket prohibition of divination; the fact that national collapse led to the COMPLETE extirpation of Israelite idolatry (contrast with other nations that lost political independence but kept their gods).

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** His geological metaphor is rhetorically powerful but methodologically questionable. "Ideological constants" can reflect the perspective of the final redactors, not the original historical reality. The consistency he observes might be the product of deuteronomistic editing, not historical fact.

## Stakes

Whether Kaufmann's method can produce reliable historical conclusions at all. If he's reading the Bible's theological consistency as historical evidence, he's doing theology, not history.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The entire historical-critical method. Source criticism exists precisely because the Bible's surface consistency masks multiple voices and developmental layers. Kaufmann is essentially arguing that the consistency IS the data, which inverts the critical project.

**Alternative reading:** The Bible's "ideological constants" reflect the theological perspective of its final editors, who systematically reshaped older traditions to conform to monotheistic norms. The consistency is real — but it's the consistency of editorial theology, not of historical witness.

## Assessment

This is where Kaufmann's method is most vulnerable. He's treating the Bible's theological coherence as evidence of historical reality. But coherence can be produced by editors as easily as by events. The question isn't whether the Bible is internally consistent on these points — it is. The question is whether that consistency tells us about the period DESCRIBED or the period of the DESCRIBERS. Kaufmann's "geological stratification" metaphor assumes the former; critical scholarship demonstrates the latter.
