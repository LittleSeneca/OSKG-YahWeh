# keel-ggi-p1.8: Erotic couples on MB IIB seals mirror divine intimacy — sexuality integrated into religion

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part I — Middle Bronze Age IIB]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part I

## The Claim

"Perhaps we should treat a series of representations of couples that convey strongly erotic connotations as analogous to the encounter of the weather god with the 'Naked Goddess.'" The divine couple — weather god and goddess — are shown on cylinder seals in explicitly sexual encounter: the goddess pushes aside or lifts up her clothing. The parallel on the human level is a series of couple depictions: an Old Syrian hematite cylinder seal from Tell el-Ajjul shows a man and woman drinking together from a shared vessel through tubes — with palms and an eight-rayed star (symbols of Ishtar/Astarte). Scarabs from late MB IIB "depict a man and a woman being intimate with one another." The authors explicitly note that these erotic representations "are common for the Middle Bronze Age but disappear in the later periods."

## Evidence

- Old Syrian hematite cylinder seal from Tell el-Ajjul: couple drinking together; palms and eight-rayed star (Ishtar/Astarte symbols)
- Krakow cylinder seal: couple drinking together; flying dove replaces star
- Multiple MB IIB scarabs with erotic couples (Keel/Schroer 1985, 94f. illuss. 67-70)
- Megiddo Stratum XII scarab (1750-1700 BCE): fringed-garment ruler + woman with thick braid + flower bud + full bloom
- Divine-level parallel: Megiddo seal — goddess disrobing before weather god
- Hazor fragment — goddess lifting clothing; suckling cow (fertility secondary motif)
- "Such representations are common for the Middle Bronze Age but disappear in the later periods"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The cylinder seals with divine-erotic scenes are well-documented in Winter's (1983) catalog. The scarab couple depictions are more scattered but consistent enough to establish a pattern. The "disappear in later periods" claim requires verification from the rest of the book.

## Stakes

This is evidence that MB IIB Canaanite religion integrated sexuality into its symbolic system at both divine and human levels. The goddess's sexuality — her disrobing, her genitalia, her love-messenger doves — was celebrated, not hidden. Human couples mirrored divine couples in erotic intimacy, and this was considered auspicious enough to place on seal amulets. This stands in stark contrast to the Hebrew Bible's treatment of sexuality in religious contexts: the prophets' metaphorical language of Israel as Yahweh's unfaithful wife treats female sexuality as shameful promiscuity; the Priestly tradition separates sexuality from the sacred; Canaanite religion is condemned for its sexual character. The MB IIB evidence shows what the biblical authors were reacting against.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue that the "sacred marriage" was a literary motif rather than an actual practiced ritual. Those who see the erotic cylinder seals as palace entertainment rather than religious iconography.

**Alternative reading:** The couple scenes could depict wedding celebrations or romantic ideals rather than sexual acts. The drinking-together motif might represent hospitality or alliance rather than eroticism. The "disappearance in later periods" might reflect changes in seal production and use rather than changes in religious attitudes.

## Assessment

The connection between divine and human couple depictions is suggestive but not airtight. The cylinder seals showing the weather god and disrobing goddess are clearly religious — they include divine attributes. The scarabs with human couples are more ambiguous — they could be secular romantic imagery. But the shared vocabulary (branches, flowers, drinking together) and the explicit appearance of Ishtar/Astarte symbols (star, dove, palm) on the couple scenes supports the reading that these are religiously meaningful — human eroticism participating in divine eroticism.
