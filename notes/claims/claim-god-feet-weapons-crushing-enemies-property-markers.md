# stav-god-2-4.3: God used his feet as weapons to crush enemies and as property markers to claim ownership — in the ancient Near East, feet communicated ownership and identity, and Yahweh's feet stamped his authoritative presence onto the world.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part I — Feet and Legs]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 4

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that in the ancient Near East, "whether divine or human, feet communicated ownership and identity." God used his feet for two functions: (1) as weapons to crush enemies in acts of violent domination, and (2) as property markers to claim ownership of land and sacred sites.

## Evidence

**Feet as weapons:**
- Isaiah 63:1-6: God returns from Edom with garments stained red — "I trampled them in my anger, I trod them down in my wrath"
- Psalm 110:1: "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet" — the ultimate image of domination
- Habakkuk 3:12-13: "In fury you strode the earth, in anger you trampled nations"

**Feet as property markers:**
- Mesopotamian legal rituals: "tracts of land were transferred in a series of legal rituals which included references to the seller raising a foot from the land" — lifting the foot = transferring ownership
- Deuteronomy 11:24: "Every place on which the sole of your foot treads shall be yours"
- Joshua 1:3: God to Joshua — "Every place that the sole of your foot will tread upon I have given to you"
- God's feet on temple ground = his ownership of the site

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The textual evidence for feet as weapons is strong; the ANE legal parallels for feet as property markers are well-documented.

## Stakes

God's feet are instruments of violence and ownership. The tenderness of the Eden image (God walking in the garden at the breezy time of day) and the violence of the Isaiah image (God trampling enemies until his garments are stained with blood) are the SAME feet. This is an embodied deity whose physical power is exercised through the most grounded part of his body — the part that touches earth.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those who read the trampling/feet imagery as metaphor for divine sovereignty.

**Alternative reading:** The foot imagery is metaphorical language for God's authority, not a claim about physical feet performing physical actions.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou's ability to connect material culture to biblical texts to ANE social practice is interdisciplinary scholarship at its best. Where Sommer provides the philosophical framework (fluidity vs. non-fluidity), Stavrakopoulou provides the sensory, material, bodily experience: how the feet pressed into stone, how the ground became holy under God's tread, how enemies were crushed underfoot.
