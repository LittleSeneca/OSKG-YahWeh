# smith-obm-2.6: Monotheism does not eliminate divine council imagery — it only reduces the status of council members

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch2 — The Divine Council]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"Divine council language and scenes also appear in the 'priestly work' of the Pentateuch and post-exilic books (Zechariah and Daniel), which assumedly are monotheistic. In other words, monotheism requires that one divine assembly headed by one divine ruler, but it makes little or no impact on the language of assembly in itself."

## Evidence

- Isaiah 40 (opening of Second Isaiah) involves a divine council scene, yet this section contains "the greatest number of monotheistic statements in the Bible."
- Both Enuma Elish and the Hebrew Bible present "a divine ruler surrounded by subservient divinities." Radical divine rulership "polarizes the divine polity into the ruler and his ruled." The difference: in Israelite texts, "all but the highest tier of divinities are reduced in status, no longer regarded as deities in any manner analogous to the head of the council; their power is entirely derivative."
- The contrast with Babylon: "Babylon stands at the height of its political power, and its older deities pay homage to its newly exalted divine ruler. In contrast, Israel stands at the bottom of its political power and it exalts its deity inversely as ruler of the whole universe."
- P.D. Miller: the divine assembly "holds as one reality a monistic impulse in a pluralistic cosmic structure."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Isaiah 6, 1 Kings 22, Daniel 7, Zechariah 3 all have council scenes in indisputably monotheistic contexts — textually undeniable.

## Stakes

This undermines the claim that monotheism is a radical break with polytheism. The STRUCTURE (ruler + court) persists; only the ONTOLOGICAL STATUS of the court members changes. Monotheism is a revaluation within an inherited structure, not a new structure. This is a key piece of Smith's argument that monotheism is "rhetoric" not a "stage."

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Heiser would say the divine council is consistent with "monotheism" properly understood (only one uncreated being; other 'elōhîm are created). Smith and Heiser actually agree that the structure persists — they disagree on what to call it.

**Alternative reading:** The persistence of council imagery could just be literary convention — poetic language inherited from polytheistic tradition but now "dead metaphor." Isaiah 40's "council scene" might not imply any real belief in other divine beings.

## Assessment

Smith is right that the structure persists, and this is a genuinely important observation. But he may understate the ontological shift: going from "other gods exist but are subordinate" to "other heavenly beings exist but are NOT gods" IS a major conceptual change. Smith acknowledges this ("all but the highest tier... are reduced in status") but seems to want the structural continuity to carry more weight than the ontological discontinuity. Both are real.
