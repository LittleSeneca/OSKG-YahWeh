# schmid-ht-2-4.4: Schmid's theology is Protestant only in scholarly lineage, not content

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§2-4 — Concept of Theology from Plato to Gabler]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §3

## The Claim

"The present theology of the Hebrew Bible also participates in the effects of this fundamental decision. Is it then a Protestant theology of the Hebrew Bible? This is true in one sense. It also shares this fate with other subdisciplines of Hebrew Bible studies. Historically speaking, Protestant biblical scholarship bears the primary responsibility for forming and developing the disciplines and methods of Hebrew Bible studies since the eighteenth century."

## Evidence

- "The present volume on the theology of the Hebrew Bible does not set a particularly Protestant accent in terms of its outline or content"
- But "it is Protestant in terms of the history of scholarship"
- The Reformation's definition of theology as interpretation of experiences with God "has broadly permeated Hebrew Bible studies"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The historical claim (Protestant scholarship shaped the discipline) is undeniable. Whether Schmid's work is genuinely non-Protestant in content is more debatable — see the Preface and §1 assessments.

## Stakes

This is Schmid attempting to acknowledge his confessional location while maintaining that his method is not confessionally determined. It's more honest than the "neither Christian nor Jewish" framing but still somewhat evasive.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue that the Protestant shaping of the discipline goes deeper than Schmid admits — that the very categories of "historical-critical" scholarship are Protestant categories.

**Alternative reading:** Schmid IS doing Protestant theology, but a liberal Protestant version that equates "non-confessional" with "not conservative Protestant." Acknowledging this would be more honest than the "history of scholarship" distinction.

## Assessment

This is a better version of the confessional-location claim than the Preface offered. Schmid admits Protestant influence "in terms of the history of scholarship" — that's honest. Whether he can actually do non-confessional theology from within a Protestant faculty is still an open question, but at least he's not claiming neutrality anymore.
