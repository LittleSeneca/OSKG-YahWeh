# romer-inv-5.1: Shiloh was Yhwh's first major sanctuary in Israel

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 5 — Entrance into Jerusalem]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 5

## The Claim

Before Jerusalem and the monarchy, Shiloh in the territory of Ephraim was the primary center of Yhwh worship. Archaeology confirms an important cult site there in the 12th-11th centuries BCE, destroyed by fire around 1050 — probably by the Philistines. The Bible preserves "strong historical memories" of Shiloh as Yhwh's home, memories still alive when Jeremiah wrote in the 6th century.

## Evidence

- **Biblical memory:** "The authors of the Bible seem to have been concerned to emphasize the importance of this site and of its destruction; they were also haunted by the memory that Shiloh had been once the home of a sanctuary of Yhwh." Jeremiah 7 and 26 recall its destruction centuries later.
- **Archaeological confirmation:** Shiloh was occupied in the 2nd millennium, became "very important again in the mid-twelfth to mid-eleventh centuries," and was then destroyed by fire around 1050 — "possibly by the Philistines."
- **The Samuel connection:** Samuel was offered by his mother to the sanctuary at Shiloh, "which is described not as a tent but as a temple built with proper walls." Samuel's name means "El is exalted" — he is a transitional figure: a prophet of the old El-worshipping tradition who anoints Saul as king.
- **Shiloh as the bridge:** "It is perhaps because of this holy place (or because of the prophet Samuel) that Yhwh then became the god of Saul."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on Shiloh as a major cult site (archaeology confirms significant occupation and destruction). MEDIUM-HIGH that it was specifically Yahwistic (the biblical tradition is consistent, but the archaeological evidence for the deity worshipped is indirect). MEDIUM that Samuel is the historical bridge figure (the Samuel narratives are late and idealized).

## Stakes

Yhwh's first home in Israel was NOT Jerusalem. It was Shiloh — a modest hill-country sanctuary in Ephraim. The great temple of Solomon was a late development. Before David, Yhwh was a regional deity of the central highlands, worshipped at a site that was eventually destroyed and abandoned. The "city of God" was not originally God's city.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue Jerusalem was always Yhwh's primary sanctuary. Conservative readings that take the biblical narrative of Jerusalem's eternal election at face value.

**Alternative reading:** The Shiloh tradition is a northern polemic retrojected by Deuteronomistic editors to delegitimize Jerusalem's exclusive claims.

## Assessment

Strong archaeological-biblical convergence. The physical destruction of Shiloh around 1050 matches the biblical memory of its loss. But we cannot prove from archaeology WHICH god was worshipped there. The biblical tradition says Yhwh; Samuel's El-based name suggests the sanctuary may have originally been El's. If so, Shiloh is another site where El-to-Yhwh transition occurred — the pattern Römer traces through the whole book.
