# schmid-ht-11.3: The canon was originally TWO parts ("Law and Prophets"), not three — the tripartite structure developed later

**Source:** [[Schmid — §11 — The Tanakh as the Hebrew Bible]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §11

## The Claim

"The canon of the Hebrew Bible at the turn of the era appears to have been conceived primarily as a two-part textual entity. The three-part structure that later became familiar is not yet recognizable."

## Evidence

- Ben Sira's prologue (ca. 132 BCE): "the Law and the Prophets and the [other writings] that followed them" — "other" is an open, generic category, not a defined "Writings" section
- "So my grandfather Jesus...was himself also led to write something pertaining to instruction and wisdom" — Sirach's grandson could ADD to the "other books" category; it wasn't closed
- Qumran texts: "Moses and the Prophets" (1QS 1.1-2; CD 5.21-6.2; 4QDibHam)
- New Testament: "the Law and the Prophets" (Luke 16:16, 29, 31; 24:27; Acts 26:22; 28:23)
- The Psalms were sometimes included: 4QMMT "the book of Moses [and] the book[s of the pro]phets and into Davi[d's psalms]"; Luke 24:44 "the law of Moses, the prophets, and the psalms"
- But 11QPsa 27.11 identifies David's psalms as "prophecy" — suggesting Psalms were counted as PROPHETS, not a separate third category
- The Ketuvim crystallized AFTER the closure of Nevi'im — a "melting pot of further authoritative literature after the closure of the Nevi'im"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The textual evidence for a two-part canon in the Second Temple period is strong and consistent across multiple sources (Ben Sira, Qumran, NT).

## Stakes

The three-part canon (Torah, Nevi'im, Ketuvim) is often assumed to be original. If it's a later development, then the canonical shape reflects evolving theological judgments, not an original design. The Ketuvim are the "leftovers" — the books that didn't fit the Torah-Prophets framework.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional Jewish readers who treat the tripartite Tanakh structure as original. Scholars who argue the tripartite structure was emerging (even if not fixed) during the Second Temple period.

**Alternative reading:** The tripartite structure may have existed in embryonic form even if the two-part language was conventional shorthand. Luke 24:44 and 4QMMT both attest to a three-part awareness, even if the third part was still fluid.

## Assessment

Solid. The convergence of Ben Sira (pre-Maccabean), Qumran (Hasmonean), and NT (1st century) witnesses to a two-part canon is persuasive. The tripartite structure was a post-70 CE development as rabbinic Judaism formalized its scripture.
