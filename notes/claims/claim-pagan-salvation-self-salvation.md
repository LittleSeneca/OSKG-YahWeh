# kaufmann-ri-2.7: Pagan salvation is self-salvation

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch II — Pagan Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter II

## The Claim

"The most advanced manifestations of paganism show a tendency to regard man as able to save himself by his own devices." Man may be viewed as ally of the gods (Zoroastrianism) or the cult may exalt man to divine rank through magical rites. "Salvation, however, is his own concern, not the gods'; at most, they but help him find the hidden way."

## Evidence

- Buddhism: "it is not the gods who redeem, but knowledge of the secrets of existence"; Buddha is "teacher" of gods and men alike
- Brahmanism: the gods themselves "search, meditate, practice yoga" seeking the cosmic secret
- Gnosticism: "man liberates himself through his mind and spirit from the prison of the body" — knowledge IS salvation
- Plato: "man how to redeem himself through attachment to the realm of ideas"
- Hellenistic mysteries: apotheosis through rites — participant "becomes Osiris" through magical rites; Orphic rites liberate the soul from matter
- Pagan ideal: Apollonius of Tyana, attaining "a rank a little less than the gods" through philosophical discipline — "he who attains it is on a par with Apollo"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Recurrent theme of self-directed salvation in "higher" paganism is real. But Israel also has elements of self-directed righteousness — the grace-works tension exists in BOTH traditions.

## Stakes

Theological heart of Kaufmann's contrast: pagan man saves himself; Israelite man is saved by God. The distinction is real but the question is whether it's original or developmental.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Israelite religion ALSO has elements of self-directed righteousness. Deuteronomic theology makes salvation conditional on human action. Wisdom literature teaches the wise man prospers by his wisdom. Prophetic critique of empty ritual implies some Israelites treated the cult as automatically efficacious — exactly the pagan pattern.

**Alternative reading:** The grace-works tension exists in BOTH traditions. Babylonian penitential prayers cry out for divine mercy; Israelite wisdom advises self-disciplined paths. Works as characterization of IDEAL TYPES but breaks down at the level of actual practice.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's "way to salvation" contrast is theologically powerful but historically vulnerable. Babylonian penitential prayers cry out for divine mercy; Israelite wisdom literature advises self-disciplined paths. The boundary is messier than Kaufmann allows.
