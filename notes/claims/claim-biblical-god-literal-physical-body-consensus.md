# stav-god-pro-1.2: The biblical God had a literal, physical humanoid body with head, hair, face, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, arms, hands, legs, feet, chest, back, heart, tongue, teeth, and genitals — and this is the scholarly consensus, not a fringe position.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Prologue + Chapter 1 — Dissecting the Divine]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 1

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou asserts that the biblical God had a literal, physical body with identifiable body parts. "In academic circles, there is nothing particularly contentious about this paraphrase of the opening chapter of the book of Genesis. Most biblical scholars would agree that when God says, 'Let us make humankind in our image, in our likeness,' he is addressing the other members of the 'divine assembly.'" Humans bearing God's image means they share his physical form — just as Seth shares Adam's "image and likeness," and divine statues share the "image and likeness" of their gods.

## Evidence

Stavrakopoulou provides a comprehensive bodily catalogue drawn from the Bible itself:

- "He had a head, hair and a face; eyes, ears, a nose and a mouth. He had arms, hands, legs and feet, and a chest and a back. He was equipped with a heart, a tongue, teeth and genitals. He was a god who breathed, in and out. This was a deity who not only looked like a human — albeit on a far more impressive, glamorous scale — but who very often behaved like a human."

Behavioral catalogue:
- "He enjoyed evening strolls and hearty meals; he listened to music, wrote books and made lists. He was a god who not only spoke, but whistled, laughed, shouted, wept and talked to himself. He was a god who fell in love and into fights; a god who squabbled with his worshippers and grappled with his enemies; a god who made friends, raised children, took wives and had sex."

Source claim: "This portrait of God has not been lifted from obscure myths inscribed on long-abandoned clay tablets. It is drawn from the Bible itself."

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** The biblical texts describe an embodied God overwhelmingly; this aligns with Sommer's careful Introduction and the data is comprehensive.

## Stakes

Stavrakopoulou is making the same claim as Sommer (God has a body), but with a crucial difference: Sommer argues God had MULTIPLE bodies (fluid embodiment model). Stavrakopoulou argues for ONE physical body. Where Sommer's reading preserves monotheism (one God, many manifestations), Stavrakopoulou's reading is more radical: God is literally a physical being with a body located in space. The difference between "God HAS bodies" (Sommer) and "God IS a body" (Stavrakopoulou) is the difference between a complex monotheism and a radically physical theism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]] (Kaufmann — Israelite religion was fundamentally non-mythological and God incorporeal). The entire Jewish and Christian theological tradition that reads anthropomorphic language as metaphor or accommodation.

**Alternative reading:** Biblical anthropomorphism is metaphorical — God accommodates human understanding by describing himself in human terms, but is not literally embodied.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou's confidence is VERY HIGH on the specific catalogue of body parts (each is textually attested). MEDIUM on "took wives and had sex" — this is more inferential and depends on reading Asherah as a literal consort/wife. Her claim that this is the consensus is correct for critical biblical scholars but overstated for the field as a whole.
