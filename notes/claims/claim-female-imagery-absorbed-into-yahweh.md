# smith-ehg-3.4: Female imagery from the asherah was eventually absorbed into descriptions of Yahweh

**Source:** [[Smith Chapter 3 — Yahweh and Asherah]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Early History of God* (1990, rev. 2002), Chapter 3: Yahweh and Asherah

## The Claim

While the asherah as a distinct cult symbol was eliminated, its imagery — particularly maternal, nurturing, life-giving language — was absorbed into Yahweh's profile. The figure of Wisdom in Proverbs 1-9 is the clearest example. Yahweh was described in female imagery without being considered female.

## Evidence

- **Wisdom as transformed asherah:** Proverbs 3:13-18 describes Wisdom as a "tree of life" — the same imagery associated with the asherah. The passage forms a chiasm using the root *'šr* ("happy"), echoing the root of Asherah's name.
- **Deuteronomy 32:18:** "The Rock who begot you... the God who gave you birth" (*mĕhōlĕlekā*) uses birth imagery for Yahweh. This is the Yahwistic transformation of the maternal stone/tree symbolism condemned in Jeremiah 2:27.
- **Hosea 14:9:** "I am like an evergreen cypress, from me comes your fruit" — possibly transforms asherah tree imagery into a Yahwistic symbol of life.
- **Female imagery for Yahweh:** *Rḥm* (compassion/womb) language (Isa 49:13, Jer 31:20, Hos 2:21), mother imagery (Isa 42:14, 46:3, 49:15, 66:9, 13). "It is precisely these features that belong to Asherah in Canaanite literature."
- **ANE parallels:** Mesopotamian and Hittite prayers address gods with both male and female parental imagery. "Female metaphors do not imply a female status for a god." Yahweh could receive female imagery without being considered female.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Medium on direct Asherah-to-Wisdom connection (interesting but speculative). High that female imagery was applied to Yahweh. Medium that this imagery specifically derives from Asherah rather than general ANE conventions.

## Stakes

Moderate. The female dimension of biblical God-language has a history — and that history may trace back to a goddess whose cult was suppressed. The nurturing, life-giving aspects of God that many Christians find most meaningful may be inherited from Asherah.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** J.W. Miller argues female imagery for Yahweh is metaphorical and rare; Yahweh was understood as male. The female language doesn't derive from a goddess — it's normal ANE divine rhetoric.

**Alternative reading:** The female imagery for Yahweh is genuinely innovative (especially in Second Isaiah) and represents theological expansion, not absorption of a suppressed goddess. The Wisdom figure draws on ANE wisdom traditions, not Asherah specifically.

## Assessment

The Wisdom-asherah connection is one of Smith's more speculative arguments, but it's intriguing. The broader point — that Yahwism absorbed and transformed imagery from suppressed cults — is well-supported elsewhere (Baal's storm imagery, El's titles) and applying it to Asherah is reasonable even if the specific examples are debatable. The Wisdom connection is the most speculative element; the female imagery for Yahweh is well-attested regardless of its origin.
