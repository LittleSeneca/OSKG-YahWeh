# kaufmann-ri-7.5: Judges-Era "Idolatry" Was Vestigial Fetishism, Not Genuine Polytheism

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch VII — The Conquest and Settlement]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch VII

## The Claim

Biblical historiography divides Israelite history into alternating periods of loyalty and idolatry — the period of the judges being the first "idolatrous period." But "the artificiality of this period division is apparent." The schematic framework of Judges "knows only of a generalized worship of Baals and Ashtoreths, without being able to supply further details." This "idolatry" was "a vestigial fetishistic idolatry that was found among the ignorant masses throughout the entire pre-exilic period, and to which women seem to have been particularly addicted."

## Evidence

- **The schematic framework is contentless:** Judges knows only "a generalized worship of Baals and Ashtoreths" — no specifics about myths, rituals, or theology.

- **Joshua's generation wasn't pure:** Josh 24:23 shows that even the "non-idolatrous" conquest generation harbored foreign gods — the division into idolatrous and non-idolatrous periods is artificial. "Since this age enjoyed victory and success, the biblical historiographer could not regard it as guilty of idolatry."

- **No creative pagan influence detectable:** "No trace of any creative influence of paganism can be found in our sources for this period. There is no battle against mythological beliefs. There are no temples or priests to any foreign god." The judges come "in the name of YHWH only; no other god raises prophets or judges to challenge the sole dominion of YHWH over the national life of Israel."

- **Wafer-thin syncretism:** The idolatry Kaufmann concedes existed was the worship of physical objects by the ignorant — not engagement with Canaanite mythology.

## Confidence

**Rating:** low

**Rationale:** This is Kaufmann's most strained argument. He has defined "genuine polytheism" so that it requires elaborate mythology (theogonies, divine biographies, cosmic dramas). By this definition, almost no popular religion in history would qualify — most people throughout history have worshipped gods without knowing the elite mythological literature about them. The Judges narratives describe community-wide worship of named foreign gods with dedicated altars — exactly what genuine polytheism looks like. The Gideon story alone (Judg 6:25-32: Gideon's father maintains a Baal altar and Asherah pole, and the community rallies to defend them) is fatal to Kaufmann's thesis.

## Stakes

Whether the biblical portrayal of the Judges as a period of cyclical apostasy reflects historical reality (in which case Kaufmann's "original monotheism" thesis is in trouble) or a schematic theological overlay (in which case it's compatible with Kaufmann's view that the underlying reality was monotheistic).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The biblical texts themselves, read straightforwardly. Judges 2:11-13: "The Israelites did evil in the eyes of YHWH and served the Baals... They forsook YHWH and served Baal and the Ashtoreths." Judges 6:25-32: Gideon's father has an altar to Baal and an Asherah pole — and Gideon tears them down at YHWH's command, nearly getting killed by the townspeople. Judges 8:33: After Gideon's death, "the Israelites again prostituted themselves to the Baals and set up Baal-Berith as their god." This is not "ignorant masses" worshipping objects — it involves named deities, dedicated altars, and community identity.

**Alternative reading:** The Judges narratives preserve genuine memories of a period when YHWH worship competed with — and often lost to — Canaanite cults. The Deuteronomistic framework *systematizes* this into a cycle of apostasy-punishment-deliverance, but the underlying reality of religious competition is historical.

## Assessment

This is where the Kaufmannian method shows its deepest cracks. He has defined "genuine polytheism" so that it requires elaborate mythology. By this definition, almost no popular religion in history would qualify. The fact that Judges describes Israelites "serving the Baals" without reciting Baal mythology doesn't mean they weren't genuinely polytheistic; it means the biblical authors weren't interested in reporting Canaanite theology. The Gideon story alone is devastating to Kaufmann's thesis: Gideon's own father maintains a Baal altar and Asherah pole in Ophrah, and the townspeople want to kill Gideon for destroying them. That's not "ignorant masses"; that's community-level polytheistic practice.
