# kaufmann-ri-6.1: Apostolic Prophecy Is Uniquely Israelite and Coeval with Monotheism

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch VI — The Origins of Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch VI

## The Claim

The "apostolic prophet" — a messenger SENT by God to a people, bearing divine commands, not answering human inquiries — is an Israelite creation with no true pagan parallel. This institution arose simultaneously with monotheism. Moses is the archetype; the "send" verb (שלח) is the heart of the prophetic call. Pagan analogues (Mari prophets, ecstatics) are fundamentally different: they lack ideological continuity, moral passion, and the sense of being commissioned by a single absolute divine will rather than embodying a personal charism.

## Evidence

Kaufmann surveys the prophetic call narratives (Moses, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel), noting the dominance of "send" language. He contrasts with pagan manticism where the prophet answers men's inquiries rather than bearing God's unsolicited word. He engages the Mari texts in a lengthy footnote (pp. 215-216) and dismisses Noth's claim that they level the qualitative difference — pagan messenger-prophets are "peripheral," lacking the ideological and moral content of Israelite prophecy.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The descriptive observation that Israelite prophetic literature emphasizes divine sending in a distinctive way has merit. The claim of absolute uniqueness is overstated — the Mari texts DO show messengers sent by gods with unsolicited messages, and Kaufmann's dismissal is too quick.

## Stakes

If apostolic prophecy is unique to Israel and coeval with monotheism, then the monotheistic revolution happened early and was institutionalized from the start. If the institution developed gradually from Canaanite/ANE models, the evolutionary picture holds.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Noth and subsequent scholarship on Mari prophecy see genuine continuity. The Mari messengers of Dagan and Adad deliver unsolicited divine messages with moral content (rebuke for neglecting offerings and building projects). Kaufmann's distinction between "peripheral" pagan messenger-prophecy and "central" Israelite apostolic prophecy may reflect the Bible's own self-presentation more than historical reality.

**Alternative reading:** Israelite prophecy DID develop distinctive features — ideological continuity, moral passion, historical consciousness — but these developed OVER TIME, not from a single Mosaic revolution. The shift from clairvoyant/seer to messenger-prophet was gradual, and early Israelite prophecy (the bands of ecstatics, the "sons of the prophets") looked much more like its Canaanite environment than the classical prophets did.

## Assessment

Kaufmann is right that Israelite prophecy developed something genuinely novel. But his insistence that this novelty was present "from the very beginning" in Moses flattens the developmental evidence. The earliest Israelite prophets (Deborah as judge, the ecstatic bands in 1 Samuel) look much more like their ANE environment than like Isaiah or Jeremiah. Kaufmann's Moses is a retrojection of later prophetic ideals onto a founding figure.
