# fleming-ybi-1.6: Tiele (1872), not Ghillany, was the true creator of the Midianite/Kenite Hypothesis

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 1 — Introduction]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 1

## The Claim

"I found that its creator was not Friedrich Ghillany but Cornelis Tiele (1872) and that Tiele's interpretation was much less concerned to validate the accounts of Moses as founding figure than the work of many who followed" (p. 39).

## Evidence

1. **Tiele (1872) proposed a new direction:** Against two earlier failed alternatives — Yahweh from Egypt via Moses, and Yahweh as a Canaanite god (p. 29). Tiele charted a third path that became the Midianite/Kenite Hypothesis
2. **Tiele focused on Rechabites and Kenites:** Midian became central only later, through the focus on Moses and Jethro (pp. 29, 39). The hypothesis was originally about marginal Yahwistic groups (Kenites, Rechabites) rather than about Moses's father-in-law
3. **Intellectual context matters:** The hypothesis was born in the context of emerging Egyptology and Assyriology — Tiele was motivated by the new comparative history of religions, not by confessional apologetics (pp. 29-30). The historical-critical context was fresh and exploratory
4. **Later distortion:** The mature form of the hypothesis became attached to Moses and the Exodus narrative in ways Tiele did not intend. The Moses-Midian centering may have distorted the original Kenite/Rechabite insight

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is a historiographical claim, verifiable by consulting Tiele's 1872 publication. Fleming's archival work on this point is careful and detailed.

## Stakes

Low for the historical argument about Yahweh, but significant for understanding how the hypothesis evolved. Tiele's version was more modest and less Moses-centric than what followed. The later Moses-Midian centering may have distorted the original insight — turning a hypothesis about marginal Yahwistic groups into a hypothesis about Moses's father-in-law.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those who credit Ghillany with the hypothesis. The priority dispute is minor for the substantive argument — what matters is the mature form of the hypothesis — but Fleming's correction is important for intellectual history.

**Alternative reading:** The priority dispute is minor; what matters is the mature form of the hypothesis, not its earliest expression. The hypothesis stands or falls on evidence, not on who first proposed it.

## Assessment

Fleming's historiographical precision is characteristic. He wants to show that the hypothesis changed substantially over time, becoming more attached to the Exodus narrative than its creator intended. This supports his larger argument that the hypothesis has been adapted to serve changing intellectual needs rather than being continuously confirmed by evidence. The Rechabite/Kenite original is genuinely more modest — and more interesting — than the Moses-centric version that dominated the 20th century.
