# day-ygc-1.2: Yahweh originated outside Canaan in the south (Midian/Edom)

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 1 — Yahweh and El]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 1: Yahweh and El

## The Claim

"Most scholars who have written on the subject during recent decades support the idea that Yahweh had his origins outside the land of Israel to the south, in the area of Midian" (p. 16). Evidence points to N.W. Arabia rather than the Sinai peninsula.

## Evidence

### 1. Biblical Poetry
- Judg 5:4-5: Yahweh "marches from" Edom/Seir
- Deut 33:2: Yahweh "came from Sinai and dawned from Seir"
- Hab 3:3, 7: God comes from Teman, Paran — all southern locations

### 2. Kuntillet Ajrud Inscription
"Yahweh of Teman" (*yhwh tmn*), Teman being a southern region in Edom (p. 17). This epithet independently confirms the southern association found in the poetry.

### 3. Egyptian Soleb Inscription (14th c. BCE)
Egyptian texts reference "the Shasu of Yhw" alongside "the Shasu of Seir" — a listing of Yahweh-land among Shasu (nomadic) territories in the Edom/Seir region (p. 17). This antedates any Israelite presence by centuries — it's the strongest single piece of evidence.

### 4. Archaeological Evidence
Civilization in the Hejaz area (N.W. Arabia/Midian) in the Late Bronze Age/Early Iron Age, contrasted with "general lack of this in this period in the Sinai peninsula" (p. 17).

### 5. Scholarly Consensus
Even "arch-'minimalist' N.P. Lemche feels confident about this" (p. 16 n. 12). The southern origin is one of the few claims that enjoys consensus across the maximalist-minimalist spectrum.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The convergence of Egyptian onomastics (Soleb), biblical poetry, and the Kuntillet Ajrud epithet is unusually strong. The Soleb inscription (14th c. BCE) antedates any Israelite presence by centuries.

## Stakes

If Yahweh came from the south, then Israel's god was NOT indigenous to Canaan. This undercuts any claim that Israelite religion was simply "Canaanite religion." It also complicates the Kenite/Midianite hypothesis — was Moses historical or just a narrative vehicle for a genuine cultural memory of southern origins?

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** M.C. Astour questioned the reading of *Seir* in the Egyptian texts, claiming the reference was to Sarara in Syria, not Seir in Edom (p. 17). Daniel Fleming (*Yahweh Before Israel*, 2021) argues Yahweh was NOT taken from outsiders but the name reflects Israel's own diverse origins. Christian Frevel also complicates the southern origins thesis.

**Alternative reading:** Fleming's view that Yahweh's name emerged from within the diverse herding peoples who became Israel, rather than being imported from Midian. This preserves non-Canaanite origins without requiring the Kenite hypothesis.

## Assessment

The Soleb evidence is genuinely difficult to dismiss. But Day concedes Astour's objection only with "on balance" — he doesn't demolish it. The strongest point is convergence: Soleb + biblical poetry + Kuntillet Ajrud + archaeology of Hejaz all point south. Fleming's 2021 challenge is important but doesn't explain the Egyptian data.
