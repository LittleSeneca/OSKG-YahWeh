# lewis-ocg-9.3: Psalm 82 depicts Yahweh prosecuting the gods for injustice

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 9 — King and Judge]] — Theodore Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 9

## The Claim

"Psalm 82 is one of the most visionary poems in the entire Hebrew Bible, as it applies Yahweh's judicial authority to the wrongdoings of the gods." If Yahweh is absolute judge, "his legal authority must transcend human society. It must extend to the realm of the gods."

## Evidence

- The psalm is saturated with legal vocabulary: four occurrences of špṭ plus haṣdîq, pallĕṭû
- Mythopoeically, one can read it as Yahweh bringing an accusation of injustice against the gods in El's divine council — with El as the presiding judge
- The gods are charged with failing to protect the poor, orphan, and destitute
- The sentence is death: "Like humans you shall die, like one of the officials you shall fall"
- The psalm ends with a plea for Yahweh to "rise up" and judge the earth because he inherits all nations — a dramatic expansion of jurisdiction
- Lewis presents BOTH the mythopoeic reading (Yahweh subordinate to El in El's court) and the mythopoetic reading (Yahweh = El = Elyon, gods are rebelling subordinates)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the legal interpretation and visionary character. MEDIUM on the mythopoeic/mythopoetic resolution — Lewis honors the ambiguity of the text.

## Stakes

Psalm 82 is ground zero for the debate about Israelite polytheism vs. monotheism. If the mythopoeic reading is right, Yahweh is NOT the high god — El is, and Yahweh is prosecuting in El's court. If the mythopoetic reading is right, Yahweh IS Elyon and the "gods" are created divine beings (Heiser's position). The legal framing — Yahweh as prosecutor — is more original and penetrating than the standard "divine council" reading.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Heiser: the gods are rebellious elohim, created divine beings, not independent deities. Sommer: Psalm 82 is about the fluidity of divine identity. Frankel: El is the speaker in 82:6-8, condemning the gods and appointing Yahweh.

**Alternative reading:** The psalm may preserve an older tradition (Yahweh subordinate to El) that has been editorially reframed by the Elohistic Psalter to identify Yahweh with Elyon.

## Assessment

Lewis handles this brilliantly. By presenting BOTH the mythopoeic and mythopoetic readings without forcing a choice, he honors the ambiguity of the text. The legal framing — Yahweh as prosecutor — is more original and penetrating than the standard "divine council" reading.
