# kaufmann-ri-2.5: Pagan divine wisdom is external and acquired

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch II — Pagan Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter II

## The Claim

"The wisdom of a deity does not consist in self-awareness, in knowledge of his will and its effect on a world dependent upon it, but rather in a knowledge of the world and its mysterious properties. Its object lies outside the gods; it is knowledge of an order of which they are but a part." The god "acquires his wisdom along with his other divine powers from the seed out of which he was born, or by some magical means."

## Evidence

- Wisdom is a specific property of certain gods, not universal: Thoth (Egypt), Ea (Babylon), Metis (Greece) are "lords of wisdom" — but not necessarily heads of pantheons
- Connection of wisdom with primeval waters: Ea is god of waters AND lord of wisdom; Thetis, Nereus, Proteus are water deities who are wise and oracular
- Gods ACQUIRE wisdom: Marduk learns medicine from Ea; Cronus learns tricks from Gaea; Apollo learns divination from Pan; Odin learns runes by falling from the world tree; Izanami and Izanagi learn copulation from a bird
- Mortals/lesser gods can surpass chief gods: Lugalbanda solves the Zu bird problem when gods cannot; Kothar-wa-hasis builds Baal's palace; Hephaestus crafts Hercules' shield
- Zeus swallows Metis because she is "wiser than all the gods and men" — "a striking expression of the pagan concept that wisdom is something external to the gods and has to be acquired by them"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The pattern of wisdom as specific, acquirable, external attribute is well-documented. The interpretive claim that this reflects the metadivine realm is less certain.

## Stakes

If pagan wisdom is external and acquired while Israelite wisdom is internal to God, this is another axis of difference. But Proverbs 8 depicts Wisdom as a created being "beside" Yahweh "as a master workman" — remarkably like the external-wisdom pattern Kaufmann attributes to paganism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see Israelite wisdom literature as heavily indebted to ANE wisdom traditions (Egyptian, Mesopotamian). Proverbs shares substantial material with the Instruction of Amenemope. If Israelite wisdom is borrowed from "pagan" sources, the absolute distinction looks artificial.

**Alternative reading:** The distinction may be about LITERARY GENRE rather than theology. Pagan myths narrate how gods acquired wisdom; Israelite texts attribute wisdom to Yahweh as inherent. Different kinds of texts making different kinds of claims.

## Assessment

One of Kaufmann's most subtle observations. The Metis story (Zeus swallows Wisdom to possess it) really IS a vivid expression of the idea. But the claim that Israelite divine wisdom is fundamentally different needs more evidence — Proverbs 8, the Wisdom of Solomon, and apocalyptic hidden knowledge all complicate the picture.
