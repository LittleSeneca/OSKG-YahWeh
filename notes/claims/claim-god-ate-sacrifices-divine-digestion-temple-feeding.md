# stav-god-9-11.3: God ate the sacrifices offered in the temple — the sacrificial system was 'the care and feeding of the gods,' with God consuming offerings through fire and smelling the pleasing odor.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part III — Torso]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 11

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that the biblical sacrificial system operated on the logic of divine feeding: God consumed the offerings through fire. He is a consuming fire — literally, a digestive fire. The phrase "a pleasing odor to Yahweh" (Leviticus 1:9 and repeatedly throughout the priestly legislation) describes God SMELLING the sacrifices. The "bread of the Presence" in the temple was literal food set before the deity. Ezekiel's vision of the temple (Ezekiel 46) includes kitchens for preparing God's meals. This is "the care and feeding of the gods" (William Dever's phrase).

## Evidence

- "A pleasing odor to Yahweh" (*rêaḥ nîḥōaḥ layhwh*) — the priestly refrain, appearing dozens of times in Leviticus and Numbers
- The "bread of the Presence" (*leḥem pānîm*) — twelve loaves set before Yahweh weekly (Leviticus 24:5-9)
- Ezekiel's temple vision includes sacrificial kitchens (Ezekiel 46:24) for preparing God's meals
- The fire on the altar is God consuming the offering — the deity eats through flame
- Psalm 50:12-13: "If I were hungry, I would not tell you... Do I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats?" — Yahweh's denial that he NEEDS to eat implies that other deities DO expect to be fed this way
- Comparative ANE: Mesopotamian and Ugaritic ritual texts describe gods eating and drinking sacrifices

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The sacrificial system as divine feeding is a standard scholarly reading; the more provocative claims about defecation push further than the evidence clearly supports.

## Stakes

If Israel's sacrificial system was literally feeding Yahweh, then Israelite worship was not categorically different from ANE temple worship — it operated on the same bodily logic. The later prophetic critique ("I do not eat the flesh of bulls") represents a theological innovation, not the original logic of the cult.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Kaufmann (who argued Israelite sacrifice was non-magical, non-pagan, purely about atonement), and the priestly theology itself (which spiritualizes the offering)

**Alternative reading:** The "pleasing odor" is a metaphor for divine acceptance; the bread of the Presence is a symbolic offering; Yahweh does not actually eat — Psalm 50:12-13 explicitly denies it.

## Assessment

The "pleasing odor" refrain is so pervasive in the priestly material that it cannot be dismissed — it genuinely describes God's sensory reception of the offering. The bread of the Presence is harder to allegorize: twelve loaves, replaced weekly, displayed in the sanctuary. This IS food for the deity. Stavrakopoulou's more provocative extension — that God therefore digested and defecated — is inferential and pushes beyond what the texts support. But the core claim that the sacrificial system was divine feeding is solid.
