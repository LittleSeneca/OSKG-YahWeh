# smith-obm-1.1: The center-periphery spatial scheme provides the fundamental organizational structure for Ugaritic divinity

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch1 — Anthropomorphic Deities and Divine Monsters]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 1

## The Claim

Smith applies Wiggermann's anthropological distinction between center and periphery to Ugaritic divine geography, proposing three zones: center (home + foreign), periphery (outback), and beyond the periphery (underworld, cosmic waters). "Deities inhabit 'near' places whereas 'monsters' or 'demonic forces' do not. The division of divinity between deities and demons, so to speak, corresponds to the mapping of divine space."

## Evidence

- CAT 1.23.65-69 expresses center vs. periphery in agrarian terms: the "sown" (*mdr'*) versus the "outback, steppe" (*mdbr*). The sown contains plenty of food and wine (1.23.70-76).
- The "sown" appears in administrative lists: royal workers (CAT 4.141 III 16; 4.618.6), wine records (4.149.16), and cultic devotion — "five (jars of) wine for the sacrifice of the queen in the sown" (*hms yn bdbh mlkt bmdr'*, 4.149.14-16).
- Schloen's description of center-periphery at Ugarit: "the social center was focused in the physical center of the kingdom at Ras Shamra... 'urban-rural' is more-or-less synonymous with 'center-periphery.'"
- The periphery: "rocks and brush" (*l'abnm wl'shm*, 1.23.66), site of grazing and hunting (1.12 I 34-35, 1.92.3), the locale where Baal meets Mot at the edge of the underworld (1.6 II 20; cf. 1.5 VI 6, 29).
- Beyond the periphery: the underworld (*'ars*), netherworld (Mot's realm), and the cosmic waters beyond (*thmtm* — e.g., Yamm).

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The spatial mapping is well-supported by indigenous Ugaritic terminology and consistent across multiple genres.

## Stakes

If the spatial scheme is valid, Ugaritic religion was not a chaotic collection of deities but a coherent conceptual system organized around the fundamental human experience of home vs. wilderness. This supports Smith's broader thesis that West Semitic polytheism had its own conceptual unity.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The scheme is Smith's synthesis of Wiggermann (who applied it to Mesopotamia) with Ugaritic data. Scholars who see Ugaritic religion as primarily practical/cultic rather than conceptual might resist this level of systematization. The three-zone refinement (center, periphery, beyond-periphery) is Smith's own.

**Alternative reading:** The *mdr'*/ *mdbr* contrast in CAT 1.23 could be a narrative device specific to that text rather than a systematic cosmological principle. The three-zone model may over-structure data that the Ugaritians themselves experienced more fluidly.

## Assessment

Smith is largely convincing. The multiple attestations of *mdr'* across genres and the consistent mapping of deities to mountains and monsters to wilderness make the spatial thesis strong. The refinement into three zones (home, foreign, far) does useful analytical work without overclaiming.
