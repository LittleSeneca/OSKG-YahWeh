# kaufmann-ri-5.2: Three Law Corpora Are Independent Crystallizations

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch V — The Sources]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch V

## The Claim

"The evolutionary sequence and literary dependence assumed by Wellhausen has no foundation." Each of the three codes is "an independent crystallization of Israel's ancient juristic-moral literature." They are not revisions of each other but parallel developments from a common ancient Near Eastern legal tradition.

## Evidence

- **D doesn't derive from the Covenant Code (JE).** D omits many JE laws inexplicably (laws about striking parents, bailees, goring ox, slave manumission for injury) while including others unchanged. "Such haphazard divergences can be accounted for only on the assumption that D and the Covenant Code are independent crystallizations."

- **P doesn't derive from JE.** P omits JE laws about indemnity for lost time, physician's costs, injury to pregnant women, goring ox, the three annual appearances, the ban on boiling a kid. These omissions "cannot be explained on the basis of P's peculiar character."

- **P doesn't derive from D.** P's priestly gifts "do not supplement, but differ completely from, those of D." P omits D's priestly portions (Deut 18:3), first shearings (18:4), the ban on working/shearing firstlings. P lacks D's social-moral laws about bribes, boundary removal, kidnapping, restoration of lost property.

- **No cross-contamination.** "Not a single peculiarity of one legal corpus has insinuated itself into either of the others." D's style nowhere appears in P; P's style nowhere appears in D.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The DESCRIPTIVE observation (the codes are more independent than simple linear models allow) is strong. The INFERENCE that this disproves all genetic relationship is weaker — independence of formulation doesn't equal independence of chronological development.

## Stakes

If Kaufmann is right, the Wellhausenian sequence (JE → D → P, each modifying the previous) collapses. The codes become parallel witnesses to a common tradition rather than successive stages. P could be as old as JE.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The critical tradition acknowledges the codes are more complex than simple linear derivation but maintains that thematic development (especially around centralization and the priesthood) reveals a chronological sequence. Even if D didn't "copy" JE, D's treatment of the same topics shows later development. Kaufmann's "inexplicable omissions" are explained by critics as selective use — later codes didn't need to repeat everything in earlier ones; they supplemented and modified where they disagreed.

**Alternative reading:** The independence Kaufmann documents is real and important. But it doesn't require his conclusion. Codes can develop independently of each other while still being chronologically sequential. D could be later than JE in its *ideas* (centralization) while independently formulating its *laws*. P could be later than D in its *priestly hierarchy* while independently formulating its *priestly dues*.

## Assessment

Kaufmann makes a strong case that the law corpora are more independent than Wellhausen's model allowed. The omission patterns he documents are real and rhetorically effective. But he overreads his evidence. Independence of formulation does not equal independence of development. Kaufmann conflates "these codes weren't directly copied" with "these codes are all from the same period" — a leap the evidence doesn't justify.
