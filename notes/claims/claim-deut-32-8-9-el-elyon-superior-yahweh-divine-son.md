# lewis-ocg-4.5: Deuteronomy 32:8-9 — El Elyon Superior to Yahweh, Who Is a Divine Son

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 4 — El Worship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"According to the reconstruction, which restores Deuteronomy 32:8 based on the Qumran readings from 4QDeutj and the Septuagint, (El) Elyon as god most high demonstrated his supremacy by establishing national boundaries and assigning land and people to various divine beings... Yahweh starts out in the telling of this story as one of the divine sons (bĕnê ʾĕlōhîm), and yet he ends up preeminent, ruling over Elyon's people and bearing the creator's epithets."

## Evidence

- The MT reads "sons of Israel" (bĕnê yiśrāʾēl) — clearly a theological correction
- 4QDeutj and LXX read "sons of God" (bĕnê ʾĕlōhîm) or "angels of God"
- El Elyon apportions the nations to divine beings; Yahweh receives Jacob/Israel as his portion
- "The poem celebrates how Israel is especially fortunate because not only were they (El) Elyon's own special people, but they were assigned to Yahweh, who is presented as the divine heir apparent"
- Psalm 82 tells a similar story: Yahweh condemns the unjust gods in "the council of El" (ʿădat ʾēl), and the "sons of Elyon" are stripped of immortality (Ps 82:7-8)
- The Qumran evidence (4QDeutj) is decisive for the textual reconstruction — "sons of God" is the original reading
- The MT's "sons of Israel" is a theological correction that resolves the uncomfortable implication that Yahweh was originally subordinate

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** Very high on the textual reconstruction (4QDeutj/Qumran evidence is decisive); high on the theological interpretation — Yahweh begins as subordinate to El Elyon.

## Stakes

This is the smoking gun for El's original supremacy. Even the Bible itself — when the textual corrections are peeled back — preserves a tradition where Yahweh is not the Most High. Heiser reads this differently (as Yahweh = Elyon, distributing nations to the divine council), but the text as reconstructed doesn't support that reading.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Heiser argues that Elyon IS Yahweh and the "sons of God" are created divine beings receiving administrative assignments, not independent deities. The MT's "sons of Israel" reflects an authentic tradition. The confessional approach harmonizes El and Yahweh from the start.

**Alternative reading:** Even if the Qumran/LXX reading is textually superior, the poem presents a STAGE in tradition, not necessarily historical reality. It's a theological narrative, not a historical report.

## Assessment

Lewis is right on the text. Deuteronomy 32:8-9 is the single most important biblical passage for establishing El's original supremacy over Yahweh. Combined with Psalm 82, the pattern is clear and cannot be explained away as mere literary convention. This is the passage that Cross, Smith, and now Lewis all build their cases on.
