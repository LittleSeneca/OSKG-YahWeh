# stav-god-pro-1.3: The incorporeal, immaterial God of Western theism is a Greek philosophical invention (Plato, Aristotle, Xenophanes) imported into Judaism and Christianity, not a biblical idea.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Prologue + Chapter 1 — Dissecting the Divine]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 1

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that the God of Western theism — invisible, transcendent, immaterial, incorporeal — is not the God of the Bible but a Greek philosophical construct. "It is the broadly Platonic notion of the otherness and unlikeness of the divine to anything in or beyond the universe that has shaped the more formal theological constructions of God in the Western religious imagination. And yet these constructions are built on a conceptual framework very much at odds with the Bible itself."

## Evidence

The historical development:
1. Xenophanes (6th-5th c. BCE): Argued the gods shouldn't be imagined with human bodies or behaviors — "Homer and Hesiod have attributed to the gods everything that leads to blame and abuse"
2. Plato (c. 429-347 BCE): God is incorporeal, invisible, abstract — an intellect, not a body
3. Aristotle (c. 384-322 BCE): The divine as "unmoved mover" — pure thought thinking itself
4. These ideas "gradually came to shape the thinking of certain Jewish and Christian intellectuals" toward the close of the first millennium BCE
5. By the early centuries CE, Jewish and Christian elites were "drawing ever-sharper distinctions between the heavenly and the earthly, the divine and the human, and the spiritual and the bodily"

The irony: The God of the philosophers (incorporeal, immaterial, abstract) replaced the God of the Bible (embodied, physical, present). Jews and Christians now worship a Greek philosophical construct, not the deity described in their own scriptures.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The historical development from Xenophanes through Plato and Aristotle into Jewish and Christian intellectual circles is standard intellectual history; Stavrakopoulou's framing is deliberately provocative but substantively accurate.

## Stakes

The "God" of Western theism — the invisible, transcendent, immaterial being of creed and catechism — is a Greek import. The biblical God walked, ate, fought, and had a body you could see. The two are radically different beings with the same name. This claim undermines the entire theological tradition that reads biblical anthropomorphism as metaphor.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]] (Kaufmann — argues that Israelite monotheism was fundamentally non-mythological and God incorporeal from the beginning, not a Greek import). Jewish and Christian theologians who argue that the incorporeal God is authentically biblical (or at least authentically Jewish/Christian) and not a Greek import.

**Alternative reading:** The incorporeal God is a legitimate development OF biblical ideas — the Bible itself contains anti-corporeal trajectories (Deuteronomic Name theology, Priestly kavod theology, Second Isaiah's incomparability rhetoric) that were later synthesized with Greek philosophy, not replaced by it.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou is correct that Greek philosophy transformed Jewish and Christian concepts of God. But she understates the extent to which anti-corporeal trajectories already existed WITHIN the biblical tradition (documented by Sommer). The synthesis of Greek philosophy with biblical anti-fluid traditions was a merger of compatible ideas, not a hostile takeover. Her framing is polemically effective but historically oversimplified.
