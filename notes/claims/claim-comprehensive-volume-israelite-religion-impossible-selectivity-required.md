# lewis-ocg-1.2: A truly comprehensive volume on ancient Israelite religion is impossible in a single book

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 1 — Introductory Matters]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 1

## The Claim

"The subject of the present volume, ancient Israelite religion, is far too large to be covered in a single volume. This is especially true if one desires to probe its various topics with the depth of research they deserve." Lewis walks through what comprehensiveness would require: full treatment of every male and female deity, sacred time and space, the full range of cultic practice, all religious personnel, incantation specialists, wisdom literature, and aesthetic dimensions.

## Evidence

The list itself is the evidence. Lewis names roughly 22 male deities, 11+ female deities, dozens of festival types, at least 12 varieties of offerings, 12+ types of incantation specialists, multiple priestly groups (Aaronids, Levites, Mushites, Zadokites, Shilohnites, etc.), and multiple literary corpora. Zevit's 2001 volume, which focuses primarily on sacred architecture and epigraphy, already runs to 700+ pages. Albertz's two-volume history covers 600+ pages.

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** This is publicly verifiable — no single volume has ever achieved comprehensive coverage.

## Stakes

Lewis is preemptively defending himself against the criticism that his book is incomplete. By openly acknowledging what he's NOT covering (goddesses, cultic calendars, incantation, wisdom literature in depth), he makes his selectivity explicit rather than implicit.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** No one seriously disagrees with the claim that comprehensiveness is impossible. The debate is about whether Lewis's chosen focus (divinity, especially El and Yahweh) is the *right* selection — or whether it overweights elite/theological concerns at the expense of popular religion.

**Alternative reading:** The "impossibility" claim is partly rhetorical. Other scholars have produced synthetic volumes (Römer, Smith, Albertz) that achieve reasonable coverage. Lewis's choice to focus on divinity reflects his interests and expertise, not an objective necessity.

## Assessment

This is honest framing. Lewis tells you exactly what he's doing and why. The real question is whether his seven "tastings" are representative enough — and that can only be judged after reading them all.
