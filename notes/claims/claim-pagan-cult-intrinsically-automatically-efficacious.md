# kaufmann-ri-2.6: Pagan cult is intrinsically and automatically efficacious

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch II — Pagan Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter II

## The Claim

"The characteristic mark of the pagan cult is not its plurality of worshipped beings, but its view of ritual as automatically efficient and intrinsically significant. The cult is not ordained by the supreme, free will of the deity... It is rather a system of rites capable in themselves of working good and evil, whose potency derives from the realm above the gods." The cult serves the gods' NEEDS — it "not only invokes the blessings of the gods, it also supports them and strengthens them through its rites."

## Evidence

- Sacrifice provides food for the gods: Mesopotamian gods "cluster around the sacrifice of Utnapishtim 'like flies'"; daily temple rites include "arraying of banquet tables before the images"
- The cult's higher form: ritual brings "mysterious powers to bear on the cosmos" — man is "a partner of mighty gods"
- Aztecs revitalize aging gods through festival; rites of death and resurrection of gods; slaying the god to keep him from aging
- Hinduism: sacrifice is the "navel of the world," "above the gods," through which "the gods live, create, and govern"
- Zoroastrianism: man who keeps purity "strengthens Ormazd" — the battle of good and evil is fought THROUGH the cult
- **Gods as priests** — the ultimate expression: Marduk is "priest of the gods"; Egyptian ritual imitates what Horus/Isis did for Osiris; Hindu gods "sacrifice, use incantations, practice ascetic rites"; Zervan "made libations one thousand years"; Greek gods purify themselves after murder and sexual relations
- "In no other realm is the subservience of the pagan gods so evident as here."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Descriptive evidence is genuinely widespread. But Israelite sacrifice also has automatic features (scapegoat Lev 16, red heifer Num 19, ordeal Num 5).

## Stakes

If Israelite sacrifice is petitionary (asking God) while pagan sacrifice is manipulative (feeding God or activating metadivine forces), the contrast holds. But Israelite sacrifice has BOTH elements. Psalm 50:12-13 explicitly REJECTS that God needs sacrifice — which suggests the idea was present enough to need refutation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who note Israelite sacrifice shares substantial features with ANE practice. Scapegoat ritual sends sin to Azazel with automatic efficacy. Priestly consecration involves blood manipulation that sanctifies through contact. Functional similarity beneath theological self-presentation.

**Alternative reading:** The difference between pagan and Israelite cult may be about THEOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION rather than ritual practice. Similar acts were reinterpreted within a monotheistic framework. The acts didn't change; the meaning did.

## Assessment

The "gods as priests" section is Kaufmann at his most brilliant — the image of deities dependent on cult for purification, strength, and sustenance is a genuinely powerful contrast to the biblical picture of a God who needs nothing. But the power of the contrast doesn't make it historically accurate for early Israel.
