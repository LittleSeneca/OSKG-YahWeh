# fleming-ybi-7.1: Yahweh was not originally a storm god or young warrior — these are secondary traits

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 7 — The Early Character of the God Yahweh]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 7

## The Claim

"All of these associations, however archaic, came afterward" (p. 294). "I do not find Yahweh's associations with rain or storm to be demonstrated as original to his worship" (p. 255). "Every likeness to a major type is likely to be secondary, attached to Yahweh... as an extrapolation from his basic centrality to his own people" (p. 265).

## Evidence

- The storm imagery in Judg 5:4-5 (dripping clouds, quaking earth) belongs to the monarchic hymn, not the older battle account (Ch 4 conclusion)
- Psalm 29 is clearly a Baal hymn adapted for Yahweh — "Yahweh thus replaces Hadad or Baal, although it is not obvious when and against what religious background" (p. 281)
- The Mesha inscription shows Kamosh fighting for Moab without storm imagery — being a people's primary deity doesn't require storm god attributes
- The Kurkh Monolith shows Assur granting victory without storm imagery — "fighting for a people" doesn't make a "war god" (p. 282)
- The South Arabian political gods (Almaqah, 'Amm) lack nature-god profiles — they're defined by their identification with a people, not by cosmological function
- Yahweh's "young warrior" profile (Smith's second tier) is an artifact of measuring Yahweh against the Ugaritic pantheon, not independent evidence

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The claim is plausible and fits Fleming's overall model, but it's hard to prove a negative. The absence of storm/warrior features in the earliest evidence doesn't prove they weren't original — it could reflect the fragmentary nature of the evidence.

## Stakes

If Yahweh was never originally a storm god, the entire Berlin hypothesis (Yahweh as local highland storm deity) loses its foundation, and the Midianite-southern-storm-god variant (van der Toorn, Smith, Römer) also falls.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Van der Toorn (1999), Smith (2001a, 2004), Müller (2008), Pfeiffer (2017), Römer (2015) — all identify Yahweh's original character with storm or warrior features. This is close to a consensus position.

**Alternative reading:** Yahweh's storm features are too deeply embedded in the earliest poetry to be secondary. Psalm 29 adapts a Baal hymn precisely BECAUSE Yahweh was already a storm god — you don't borrow hymns for gods of completely different types.

## Assessment

Fleming is pushing against a strong consensus here. His South Arabian analogies are genuinely illuminating and underutilized in the field, but they're analogies, not evidence about Yahweh specifically. The claim that storm features are "secondary" is plausible but would require a much fuller argument than this chapter provides.
