# schmid-ht-preface.2: A theology of the Hebrew Bible must first clarify what "theology" means and what "Hebrew Bible" designates

**Source:** [[Schmid — Preface]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), Preface

## The Claim

"The project of a 'theology of the Hebrew Bible' is in need of clarification both from the side of theology as well as from the side of the Hebrew Bible/Old Testament." Schmid notes a "lack of orientation" about what "theology" means, coupled with the problem that "there is no such thing as the Hebrew Bible/Old Testament" — we have Jewish and Christian versions requiring different theologies.

## Evidence

- Ludwig Köhler's classic definition ("opinions, thoughts, and concepts of the OT brought into the correct contextual configuration") is "little more than a tautology"
- The plural and "almost inflationary" use of "theology" has "virtually supplanted the classical conception"
- Multiple canonical forms exist: the Jewish Tanakh and various Christian Old Testaments

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** These are definitional problems, not empirical claims. The canonical diversity is indisputable. The "inflationary" use of "theology" is a fair observation — if everything is "theology," nothing is.

## Stakes

Schmid's definition of theology as "reflexive and synthesizing efforts" will determine what counts as theological data. If theology is too broadly defined, the project collapses into general description. If too narrow, it misses the phenomena Schmid wants to capture.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who favor the plural "theologies of the Hebrew Bible" approach (Gerstenberger, Fischer). They would say the "inflationary" use IS the correct approach — the Hebrew Bible contains multiple theologies, not one.

**Alternative reading:** The plurality of canonical forms and theological conceptions is not a problem to be solved but a feature to be celebrated. A single "theology of the Hebrew Bible" is an impossibility — we should only speak of "theologies."
