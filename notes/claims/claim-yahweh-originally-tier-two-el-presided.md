# smith-obm-2.4: Psalm 82 and Deuteronomy 32:8-9 preserve an older theology where Yahweh was originally in the second tier

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch2 — The Divine Council]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"In early Israel the god of Israel apparently belonged to the second tier of the pantheon; he was not the presider god, but one of his sons. Accordingly, what is at work is not a loss of the second tier of a pantheon headed by Yahweh. Instead, the collapse of the first and second tiers in the early Israelite pantheon likely was caused by an identification of El, the head of this pantheon, with Yahweh, a member of its second tier."

## Evidence

- Psalm 82:1 — "God ('elōhîm) stands in the divine assembly/assembly of El ('ădat 'ēl)." Yahweh takes his stand in El's assembly, presumably as a prosecutor, not the presider. The presiding figure is either El or undescribed. The name El "was understood in the tradition... to be none other than Yahweh."
- Psalm 82:6 — the other gods are "sons of the Most High (Elyon)" — Elyon being a title of El (cf. El Elyon in Genesis 14:18-20). Yahweh is implicitly one of these sons, differentiated only by his prosecutorial role.
- Psalm 82:8 — Yahweh is called to "inherit all the nations" — to arrogate what belonged to the other divine sons.
- Deuteronomy 32:8-9 (LXX/DSS): "When the Most High (Elyon) allotted peoples for inheritance... He fixed the boundaries for peoples, according to the number of the divine sons: For Yahweh's portion is his people, Jacob His own inheritance." The MT reads "sons of Israel" — "an anti-polytheistic alteration" (Tov). The older text has Elyon distributing nations to divine sons, with Yahweh receiving Israel.
- The theology: each nation has its own patron god; Yahweh is Israel's patron. The head god (Elyon/El) presides over all.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The MT alteration of Deut 32:8 is one of the most famous text-critical arguments in biblical studies, widely accepted.

## Stakes

This is the single most important claim in the chapter for Smith's overall argument. If Yahweh was originally tier 2, then biblical monotheism is not the survival of an original revelation but the result of a structural merger (El + Yahweh → Yahweh-El). This inverts the traditional narrative completely. It also explains why Yahweh has BOTH El characteristics (aged, wise, fatherly, council-head) and Baal characteristics (young warrior, storm-god, dragon-slayer).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Evangelical scholars who maintain Mosaic monotheism (Heiser's "divine council" model keeps Yahweh as supreme from the beginning — the 'elōhîm in Psalm 82 are subordinate beings, not fellow tier-2 gods). Scholars who identify Elyon as Yahweh from the start (reading Psalm 82 as Yahweh judging lesser divine beings, not a separate El). Cross argues El and Yahweh were identified very early — so early that Psalm 82 already reflects the identification.

**Alternative reading:** Psalm 82 could be read as Yahweh (already identified with El/Elyon) judging lesser divine beings who have failed in their duties. In this reading, the "assembly of El" IS Yahweh's assembly, and 'ēl in verse 1 already refers to Yahweh. The reference to "sons of Elyon" in v. 6 would then refer to Yahweh's sons, not El's. This reading is possible but requires ignoring the clear differentiation between the presider and the prosecutor.

## Assessment

Smith's reading is the most natural one. The MT alteration of Deut 32:8 is the smoking gun — scribes changed the text BECAUSE it implied polytheism. That change only makes sense if the original reading was theologically problematic. Psalm 82's structure (Yahweh as prosecutor, not judge) is harder to explain away. The cumulative case is strong: early Israel knew a pantheon headed by El/Elyon with Yahweh as one member. The identification happened later, and the texts that preserve the older view survived because later readers re-interpreted El as Yahweh.
