# smith-obm-9.3: Genesis 1:26 inverts Ezekiel 1:26 — humanity elevated to divine image as reversal of reduced anthropomorphism

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch9 — Formation of Monotheistic Theologies]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 9

## The Claim

"Genesis 1:26 inverts Ezekiel 1:26, which itself represents a deliberate attempt to stress the transcendent character of Yahweh by reducing the anthropomorphic presentation of Yahweh in the heavenly divine council... Ezekiel 1:26 conveys the prophet's vision of the divine with the language of 'image' of the human person... whereas Genesis 1:26-28 presents a vision of the human person in the likeness of the divine."

## Evidence

- Ezekiel 1:26 uses *demut kemar'eh 'adam* ("an image like the appearance of a human") — the divine is reduced to human terms, a deliberately limited anthropomorphism compared to earlier prophetic visions (e.g., Isaiah 6).
- Genesis 1:26 uses the same vocabulary (*selem*, "image"; *demut*, "likeness") but reverses the direction: "Unlike Ezekiel 1:26, which reduces Yahweh to human terms in an anthropomorphic portrait... Genesis 1:26-28 magnifies the human person in divine (or perhaps mythic) terms."
- The vocabulary of *selem* and *demut* is also used for statues — "this vocabulary may represent an implicit polemic aimed against the making of images. Clearly humanity serves as the living image of the Israelite deity, and it is perhaps implicit then that images of man-made objects constitute lifeless symbols of dead gods."
- This anti-idol polemic becomes explicit in Second Isaiah (Ch10).

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The verbal parallel between Ezekiel 1:26 and Genesis 1:26 is striking and the inversion reading is elegant. But Smith's claim of direct literary dependence rather than shared priestly vocabulary is harder to prove.

## Stakes

If Genesis 1:26 is responding to Ezekiel 1:26, the Priestly creation account is later than Ezekiel (early 6th century) and represents a further theological development. The sequence would be: Isaiah 6 (full anthropomorphic council vision) -> Ezekiel 1 (qualified/reduced anthropomorphism) -> Genesis 1 (humanity elevated to divine image instead). This is a plausible developmental arc.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who date P earlier than Ezekiel. Those who see the parallel as coincidental or as both drawing on common priestly language rather than direct inversion.

**Alternative reading:** Both Ezekiel 1 and Genesis 1 could be independent developments from the same priestly tradition, using *selem*/*demut* language for different purposes, without one being a response to the other.

## Assessment

The inversion reading is elegant and fits Smith's larger argument about priestly transformation of earlier traditions. But the evidence for direct dependence is thin — shared vocabulary in priestly circles is expected. The anti-idol polemic reading is stronger and well-supported by the use of *selem* for statues elsewhere.
