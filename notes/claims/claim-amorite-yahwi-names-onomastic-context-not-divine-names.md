# fleming-ybi-5.2: The Amorite Yahwi- personal names provide onomastic context but are not divine names

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 5 — The Name Yahweh]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 5

## The Claim

"Study of the Shasu name generally moves directly to the divine, assuming reference to the god later attached to Israel and leapfrogging the question of how to understand a human political entity on its own terms. This risks anachronism" (pp. 195-196). "The element never indicates a god, and it cannot be invoked as direct evidence for what would become the god of Israel" (p. 199).

## Evidence

- Extensive listing of Mari personal names with Ya-wi- and Ya-ah-wi- elements (pp. 197-198): Yawi-DINGIR, Yawi-ila, Yawi-Dagan, Yawi-Addu, Yawi-Erah, Yahwi-asar, Yahwi-nasi, Yawium, Yawiya
- ARM XXIII 448-51 proves that Ya-wi- and Ya-ah-wi- spellings can represent the same name/verb — no fixed separation between "to live" and "to be" (pp. 198-199)
- The element occurs with multiple deities (Dagan, Addu, Erah), not only with "the god" (ilu/El) — the element is not specifically tied to El (p. 199)
- Both Yawi- and Yahwi- forms appear with the storm god Addu (p. 199)
- Streck (1999, 2000) argued all these names use ḥwy ("to live"), but the spelling variation in ARM XXIII doesn't settle which verb is intended
- Semantic parallels exist for hwy ("to be") in Akkadian (ibašši- names), Ugaritic (ykn- names), and Phoenician (ykn- names) (pp. 200-201)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Fleming's Mari expertise is on display — his own gathering of names from post-1965 publications adds substantially to Huffmon. But the key question (hwy "to be" vs. ḥwy "to live") remains unresolved. Fleming acknowledges Streck's objection has force.

## Stakes

If the Amorite names all use "to live," they're less relevant to Yhwʒ. But Fleming's point is that even if the etymology is "to live," the naming TYPE (people named like persons) is what matters, not the specific root.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Streck (1999, 2000) argues all Yahwi- names use ḥwy "to live" — removing the Amorite evidence from relevance to Yahweh. Tropper (2017) argues Yhwʒ is a noun, not a verb at all.

**Alternative reading:** Streck is right — all the Amorite names mean "May DN live," and they have nothing to do with the divine name Yahweh, which has a different etymology entirely.

## Assessment

Fleming is honest about the uncertainty. The key insight is methodological: stop treating Yhwʒ as a divine name and the onomastic question becomes simpler. Whether the root is "to be" or "to live" or something else, a people named after a verbal personal name is a common phenomenon. The divine-name question is secondary.
