# romer-inv-8-9.2: Yhwh had a consort; Asherah was paired with Yhwh, not Baal

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapters 8-9 — Statue and Asherah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapters 8-9 synthesis

## The Claim

This is where Römer directly contradicts Smith. Where Smith argued the asherah was a YAHWISTIC SYMBOL that had lost its goddess connection, Römer argues the asherah WAS the goddess Asherah and she was Yhwh's consort. The biblical association of Asherah with Baal is a Deuteronomistic invention designed to break the Yhwh-Asherah link. When Yhwh absorbed El's identity, he inherited El's consort. The Deuteronomistic reformers' strategy: associate her with BAAL (the enemy god) rather than Yhwh — making her seem foreign and idolatrous.

## Evidence

**Asherah in the ancient Levant:**
- Origins in western Semitic culture; earliest references in Mesopotamia (Hammurabi era, 18th century BCE)
- At Ugarit (14th-13th centuries): Asherah (*Aṯiratu*) is El's consort, "mother of the gods," called "the seventy sons of Aṯirat"
- She is associated with fertility and kingship — the heir to Keret's throne "shall suck the milk of Aṯirat"
- In southern Arabian inscriptions (1st millennium), *aṯrt* means either the goddess or "goddess" generically

**Asherah in the Hebrew Bible:**
- 40 occurrences, mostly with the article ("the asherah")
- Four categories: (a) plural in stereotypical destruction formulas, (b) associated with Baal, (c) with standing stones, (d) near the altar/house of Yhwh
- "The biblical texts make no direct link between Asherah and Yhwh" — BUT they also don't link her with Baal except in Deuteronomistic passages. The Baal-Asherah link is a "Deuteronomistic invention."

**The inscriptions — the smoking gun:**
- **Kuntillet Ajrud** (c. 800 BCE): "Yhwh of Samaria/Teman and his Asherah"
- **Khirbet el-Qom** (c. 700 BCE): a Judean burial inscription invoking "Yhwh and his Asherah"
- These inscriptions directly link Yhwh and Asherah — something the Bible never does. The Bible's silence is censorship, not absence.
- Kuntillet Ajrud was a caravanserai/way-station, not a fringe site — the religious practices there reflect mainstream Yahwism

**What happened to Asherah?**
- When Yhwh absorbed El's identity, he inherited El's consort
- The Deuteronomistic reformers, pursuing Yhwh-alone theology, had to eliminate Asherah
- Their strategy: associate her with BAAL (the enemy god) rather than Yhwh
- Josiah's reform removed the asherah from the Jerusalem temple (2 Kings 23:6-7)
- The goddess was suppressed; her symbol was destroyed; the memory was rewritten

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH that Asherah was a goddess in the ANE (Ugaritic unambiguous). HIGH that Kuntillet Ajrud links her with Yhwh. MEDIUM that she was Yhwh's consort specifically (versus a subordinate deity or hypostasis). The grammar remains ambiguous.

## Stakes

If Römer is right, the biblical God had a wife. The goddess was worshipped alongside Yhwh in the Jerusalem temple itself. She was removed by reformers, not because she was foreign, but because she was TOO indigenous — too deeply woven into Israelite religion. The God of the Bible was part of a divine couple. This is the single most explosive claim in Römer's book.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-asherah-was-yahwistic-symbol]] — Smith reads the evidence as referring to the symbol, not the goddess (smith-ehg-3.1). Frevel and Korpel agree with Smith.

**Alternative reading:** Smith's: the asherah was a Yahwistic cult symbol that had lost its goddess connection by the monarchic period. The Deuteronomists suppressed it because of its divinatory/healing functions, not because it represented a goddess.

## Assessment

This is the sharpest disagreement between Smith and Römer. Smith says symbol; Römer says goddess. I previously assessed Smith as "probably wrong" on this point. Römer strengthens that assessment. The Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptional evidence is more naturally read as a divine couple than as a deity and his cult object. The Deuteronomistic invention of a Baal-Asherah link to suppress the Yhwh-Asherah link is a compelling argument. The Ugaritic background (Asherah as El's consort → Yhwh inherits El's consort) provides a coherent developmental narrative. I'm now at: Römer is probably right, Smith is probably wrong. Yhwh probably had a consort.
