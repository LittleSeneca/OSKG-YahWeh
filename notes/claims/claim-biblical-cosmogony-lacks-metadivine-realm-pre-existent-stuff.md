# kaufmann-ri-3.4: Biblical cosmogony lacks the metadivine realm

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

Even where the Bible seems to depict creation from pre-existent matter (Genesis 1's tohu wabohu, Genesis 2's dust and rib), "the role of the tohu wabohu is quite unlike the part played by the primeval matter of pagan cosmogonies. God creates the cosmic phenomena of light, firmament, sun, moon, and host of heaven by fiat alone, with no recourse to primeval stuff." The notion of pre-existent stuff is "a vestigial idea which has no meaningful role in the accounts themselves." Lacking a theogony, the Bible has no need of a pre-existent realm.

## Evidence

- Creation by fiat (Genesis 1): God speaks and it is. No divine substance, no sexual procreation, no emanations
- Where matter is used (dust for man, rib for woman), "considerations of etiology motivated the writer" — explains natural connections, not metaphysical necessity
- Gen 6:1-4 (sons of God mating with women) is perhaps "a genuine mythical fragment," but survived because "its protagonists on both sides are creatures of God, not God himself"
- Job 26:7: God stretching out the north "upon the void" and suspending the earth "on nothingness" — "creation out of nothing is here given clear poetic expression"
- Isa 45:7: YHWH created darkness; Prov 3:20: created the watery abyss; Ps 95:5: created sea and dry land

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's reading of Genesis 1 as essentially creation ex nihilo is defensible. But his dismissal of tohu wabohu as "vestigial" is an interpretive move, not a demonstration.

## Stakes

Whether the Bible's creation accounts are fundamentally different from or continuous with ANE cosmogonies. If Kaufmann is right, the Bible's cosmology was already non-mythological in its earliest strata.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see Genesis 1 as polemical transformation of Enuma Elish. Tohu wabohu corresponds to Tiamat; division of waters to Marduk's splitting of Tiamat; creation by word as deliberate rejection of creation through combat. Genesis 1 is sophisticated theological polemic — late, not early. Cross would see Chaoskampf in earliest poetry (Exodus 15) later demythologized in Genesis 1.

**Alternative reading:** The biblical cosmogony IS different — no theogony, no divine combat as creation mechanism. But these differences may be the product of theological development, not original intuition. Old poetry (Exodus 15, Psalm 29, Habakkuk 3) contains combat imagery Genesis 1 stripped away. Direction: FROM mythological combat TO serene creation by fiat.

## Assessment

Kaufmann is right about the DIFFERENCES but probably wrong about the SEQUENCE. Genesis 1's non-mythological character is more plausibly the ENDPOINT of demythologization, not its starting point. The combat mythology in biblical poetry looks like the EARLIER stratum, with Genesis 1 representing a later, more abstract theology.
