# heiser-ur-1.1: Psalm 82 describes a divine council where Yahweh judges other elohim — the evangelical "human judges" reading fails exegetically

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 1 — First Things]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 1

## The Claim

Psalm 82:1 reads: "God [*elohim*] stands in the divine assembly; he administers judgment in the midst of the gods [*elohim*]." The second instance of *elohim* must be translated as plural — the God of the Old Testament was part of an assembly, a pantheon, of other gods. This was Heiser's watershed moment: "My friend handed me his Hebrew Bible, open to Psalm 82. He said simply, 'Here, read that... look at it closely.' The first verse hit me like a bolt of lightning."

## Evidence

**The evangelical explanations and why they fail:**
- The *elohim* are human judges — but "the Bible nowhere teaches that God appointed a council of Jewish elders to rule over foreign nations, and God certainly wouldn't be railing against the rest of the Trinity, Jesus and the Spirit, for being corrupt"
- The *elohim* are Trinity references — absurd given the context of judgment for corruption
- The *elohim* are Jewish elders — no biblical basis for elders ruling foreign nations

**The critical scholarship:**
- Dozens of articles showing "parallels between the psalm and its ideas and the literature of other civilizations of the biblical world — in some cases, matching the psalm's phrases word for word"
- Psalm 82:1 uses *'ădat 'ēl* — "assembly of El" — directly paralleling Ugaritic divine council terminology

**The decision point:** "Was my loyalty to the text or to Christian tradition? Did I really have to choose between the two? I wasn't sure, but I knew that what I was reading in Psalm 82, taken at face value, simply didn't fit the theological patterns I had always been taught."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** This is Heiser's genuine experience, and the plural elohim reading is the scholarly consensus — even critical scholars agree.

## Stakes

Heiser is not deconstructing — he's RECONSTRUCTING. "I still believe in the uniqueness of the God of the Bible. I still embrace the deity of Christ." Psalm 82 didn't destroy his faith. It forced him to build a better theology that takes the text seriously. If Heiser is right, evangelical theology has been filtering out a major biblical theme for centuries.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservative evangelicals who read *elohim* as human judges (the Sethite/hermeneutical tradition since Julius Africanus, 3rd century CE). Also, scholars who read the divine council as evidence of Israelite polytheism (Smith, Day, Römer) — Heiser accepts the council data but rejects the polytheism label.

**Alternative reading:** The human judges reading: Psalm 82 addresses corrupt human rulers whom God calls "gods" sarcastically. Or the Trinity reading: the *elohim* are persons of the Trinity.

## Assessment

Heiser positions himself between two camps. Against evangelicals: stop filtering out the divine council. Against critical scholars: stop calling the divine council polytheism. Accept the data. Reject both traditional and critical interpretations. Build something new. The question is whether this middle position is coherent or whether accepting the divine council data inevitably leads to accepting Israelite polytheism.
