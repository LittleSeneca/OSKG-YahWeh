# smith-obm-7.2: El was the original deity at Israelite sanctuaries, later absorbed into Yahwistic cult

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch7 — El Yahweh and the Original God of Israel]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 7: El, Yahweh, and the Original God of Israel

## The Claim

"At a variety of sites, Yahweh was incorporated into the older figure of El, who belonged to Israel's original West Semitic religious heritage" (p. 140). Specific sites — Shiloh, Shechem, and Jerusalem — show El language and characteristics that predate Yahwistic appropriation. This inverts the traditional picture: it wasn't that El was "added" to Yahwism but that Yahweh was "added" to El worship.

## Evidence

### Shiloh
- C. L. Seow: El language and characteristics reflected in the cult of Shiloh
- Tent tradition at Shiloh (Psalm 78:60; Joshua 18:1; 1 Samuel 2:22) conforms to Ugaritic descriptions of El's abode as a tent
- Samuel's incubation-dream — divine appearance in dreams is an El characteristic
- Hannah's gift of a child — El as granter of progeny (Kirta parallel)
- Elqanah's name — suggests an El worshipper
- Shiloh called bêt 'ĕlōhîm ("house of God/El") in Judges 18:31
- Psalm 78 repeatedly uses El names and epithets in describing the rise and fall of the Shiloh sanctuary

### Shechem
- Local god was 'ēl bĕrît ("El of the covenant") — Judges 9:46; cf. 8:33, 9:4
- The word 'ilbrt apparently appears as a Late Bronze Age title for El (CAT 1.128.14–15)
- In patriarchal narratives, the god of Shechem is called 'ĕlōhê yiśrā'ēl ("the god of Israel") and presumed to be Yahweh
- Process of reinterpretation: when Shechem's cult became Yahwistic, it continued El traditions; Yahweh received the title 'ēl bĕrît, the old title of El

### Jerusalem
- Possible connection of El Elyon and El "creator of the earth" in Genesis 14:8–22 to Jerusalem
- "Israelite knowledge of the religious traditions about other deities did not only reflect contact between Israel and her Phoenician neighbors in the Iron Age... the old religious lore of El was inherited by the priesthood in Israel"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Shiloh evidence is particularly strong — the tent tradition, incubation-dream, and Elqanah's name form a coherent cluster. The Shechem case is also solid because the title 'ilbrt is attested at Late Bronze Age Ugarit.

## Stakes

If El was the original god at Israel's major sanctuaries, then Yahweh's presence there is secondary — a southern import that took over existing El cult sites. This inverts the traditional picture: it wasn't that El was "added" to Yahwism but that Yahweh was "added" to El worship.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who maintain Yahweh was always the god of Israel from Sinai onward (Albright, de Moor, Propp). Those who read the patriarchal narratives as straightforward history rather than layered tradition.

**Alternative reading:** The El characteristics at these sites could reflect a shared West Semitic divine typology that Israelites applied to Yahweh FROM THE START — not evidence of an earlier El cult later Yahwized. The tent and dream traditions could be Israelite innovations borrowing from common stock.

## Assessment

Smith's cumulative case is persuasive. The convergence of (1) Ugaritic parallels, (2) site-specific traditions, and (3) the name Israel itself (Claim 3) makes the "Yahweh was always there" position increasingly difficult to maintain. The Shiloh evidence is the strongest — Seow's study shows too many El-specific traits in one place for coincidence.
