# day-ygc-3.1: "The Baals" are local manifestations of one cosmic deity (Hadad/Baal), not distinct gods

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 3 — Yahweh Versus Baal]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 3: Yahweh Versus Baal

## The Claim

Prior to Ugarit, scholars (like W. Robertson Smith) thought "the Baals" referred to "quite distinct Canaanite deities, each Baal having its separate local identity." The Ugaritic texts revealed that Baal "was the epithet (though becoming a personal name) of one great cosmic deity, Hadad, so that the local Baals were, in fact, simply local manifestations of this particular deity" — "analogous to the local manifestations of the Virgin Mary in the Roman Catholic Church" (p. 69).

## Evidence

1. Ugaritic pantheon list (KTU² 1.47.5-11) repeats the name Baal seven times, suggesting "for the Ugaritians he could appear in various manifestations" (p. 69)
2. OT place names reflect local Baal cults: Baal-Gad, Baal-Hamon, Baal-Hazor, Baal-Hermon, Baal-Meon, Baal-Peor, etc. — at least 20+ such places (pp. 69-70)
3. Extra-biblical attestations: Baal-Zaphon at Ugarit, Baal of Lebanon (KAI 31.1, 2), Baal-Meon on the Moabite stone (lines 9, 30) (p. 70)
4. Baal epithets independent of specific sanctuaries: Baal-Shamem ("Baal of the heavens"), Baal-zebul ("Prince Baal") (pp. 70-71)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Ugaritic evidence is decisive, and Day's Catholic analogy is both precise and accessible. The distinction between the god and his local manifestations is now standard in the field.

## Stakes

The OT wasn't fighting a diffuse polytheism with dozens of unrelated Baals — it was fighting ONE god who had successfully established himself at many sanctuaries across Palestine. This makes the Yahweh-vs-Baal conflict more coherent: it's a battle between two cosmic deities, not Yahweh against an undifferentiated swarm.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Pre-Ugaritic scholars (W. Robertson Smith); the view is now essentially uncontested.

**Alternative reading:** N/A — this is now a settled factual question in the field.
