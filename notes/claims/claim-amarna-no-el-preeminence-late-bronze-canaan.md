# lewis-ocg-4.2: Amarna Shows No El Preeminence in Late Bronze Age Canaan

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 4 — El Worship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"There is no indication of any superiority of the deity El from this catalogue of names... the majority of those living in Canaan at this time would beg to differ, as they looked to deities other than El for personal protection... To be fair, we should not weigh the Amarna letters very heavily. They are straightforward correspondence of a fairly stereotypical nature... they have precious little comment on religion in general."

## Evidence

- Hess's catalogue shows a pluralistic environment: Egyptian (Amon, Ptah, Re, Seth), Mesopotamian (Anu, Assur, Ninurta), Kassite, Hurrian (Hebat, Tessub), Indo-Aryan, and West Semitic (Anat, Asherah, Astart, Baal, El, Milku, Yamm) deities appear in personal names
- El names like Ildayyi ("El is my sufficiency") and Ilumilku ("El is king") exist alongside many non-El names
- The name Jacob-el appears in toponym lists of Thutmose III, Ramses II, and Ramses III — tantalizing but too meager for reconstruction
- The Amarna letters are diplomatic correspondence between Canaanite vassals and the Egyptian court — formulaic, stereotypical, not religious texts
- Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — the genre limits what we can infer

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** High on the Amarna evidence; high that El wasn't preeminent in Late Bronze Canaan based on available sources; medium on whether this accurately reflects reality — the Amarna letters are diplomatic correspondence, not religious texts.

## Stakes

Whether El's prominence in Israel was a local development or inherited from broader Canaanite culture. If El wasn't dominant in Late Bronze Canaan, then Israelite El worship is a distinctive development, not a simple inheritance.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Cross argued for an older, more widespread El cult based on Amorite onomastics and Proto-Sinaitic inscriptions. But Lewis shows those readings can't be sustained (see Claim 3).

**Alternative reading:** The Amarna letters are the wrong source for this question. Ugarit (where ʾIlu is clearly head of the pantheon) is a better comparator. The Amarna evidence may simply reflect the nature of diplomatic correspondence, not religious reality.
