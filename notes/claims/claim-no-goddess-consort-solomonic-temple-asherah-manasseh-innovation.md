# keel-ggi-4.7: No Goddess Consort in the Solomonic Temple — Asherah Was a Late Introduction Under Manasseh

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part IV — Iron Age IIA]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part IV

## The Claim

The Temple's iconographic program is systematically gender-neutral. Motifs associated with goddesses in earlier periods (doves, caprids at trees, suckling animals, the naked goddess herself) are "conspicuously absent." The goddess had already receded into gender-neutral symbols of royal blessing. The evidence for Asherah in the Temple comes from later biblical texts — 1 Kgs 15:13 (Asa removes Maacah's "abominable image") and 2 Kgs 21:7 (Manasseh installs an asherah image) — both postdating Solomon's Temple by centuries. The goddess came LATER (Manasseh, 7th century) — she was an innovation, not a survival.

## Evidence

- The Temple decorations: cherubs, palm trees, open flowers, lions, oxen, pomegranates, lilies — all attested in Iron IIA Syro-Phoenician royal iconography. None specifically associated with a goddess
- Doves: associated with the goddess in LB iconography (illuss. 16, 19-21) but absent from Temple decoration
- Caprids at trees: the substitute symbol for the goddess — absent
- Suckling mother animals: associated with Anat/Astarte — absent
- The "Mistress of the Animals" — absent
- "The tree no longer represents the goddess but stands for the kingdom" — the goddess's symbols have been re-semanticized as royal symbols
- The goddess has been "demoted" from temple to household — she appears on domestic cultic stands (Taanach) and terra-cotta figurines, not in monumental temple decoration

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The absence of goddess motifs from the Temple description is striking. But the description is textual, not archaeological — we do not have the actual Temple to excavate.

## Stakes

If the Solomonic Temple was goddess-free, then the "Yahweh had a consort in his own Temple" narrative is wrong for the early monarchy. This inverts the usual narrative: the goddess was not suppressed; she was INTRODUCED, then suppressed. The goddess's absence from the early Temple and her introduction under Assyrian influence is a coherent narrative that fits the archaeological evidence better than the "original consort" model.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Dever, Römer, and others who see Asherah as an original partner of Yahweh from the beginning of the monarchy. Also scholars who argue the Temple description is a post-exilic idealization.

**Alternative reading:** The biblical description of the Temple may be a post-exilic text that deliberately omits goddess elements that were actually present. The deuteronomistic editors had every reason to erase Asherah from the Temple's history.

## Assessment

We cannot excavate the Temple. But the iconographic pattern Keel & Uehlinger document — the goddess's retreat into substitute symbols during Iron IIA — is consistent with a Temple that was goddess-free from the start. If Asherah was introduced under Manasseh, that would represent the SAME goddess revival we see in Iron IIC (Part VI), just at the official Temple level. The goddess's absence from the early Temple and her introduction under Assyrian influence is a coherent narrative that fits the archaeological evidence better than the "original consort" model.
