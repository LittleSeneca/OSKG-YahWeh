# day-ygc-3.5: "Abomination of desolation" is a wordplay on Baal-Shamem

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 3 — Yahweh Versus Baal]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 3: Yahweh Versus Baal

## The Claim

Nestle's 1884 recognition that *šiqqûṣ šōmēm* ("abomination of desolation") is a wordplay on Baal-Shamem ("Lord of the heavens") is correct. *Šiqqûṣ* "abomination" replaces Baal, and *šōmēm* "desolating" replaces *šāmēm* "heavens" (pp. 84-85). This continues the OT tradition of polemical name-distortion into apocalyptic literature — Daniel isn't using mysterious code but a specific insult directed at the god Antiochus installed in the Temple.

## Evidence

1. **Phonetic wordplay:** The substitution of *šōmēm* for *šāmēm* is a near-homophone — classic Hebrew wordplay
2. **Pattern consistency:** Substituting *šiqqûṣ* for Baal's name fits other OT usages where *šiqqûṣ* denotes detested pagan gods (1 Kgs 11:5, 7) (p. 85)
3. **Historical fit:** Antiochus IV dedicated the Jerusalem temple to Zeus Olympios. Zeus Olympios = Baal-Shamem. The Peshitta of 2 Macc 6:2 refers to the deity as *bʻl šmyn* (p. 85)
4. **Zeus-Baal equation:** "Baal and Zeus were commonly equated in the ancient world" and "Mt Olympus was frequently equated with heaven" — so Zeus Olympios → Baal-Shamem is natural (p. 85)

### Against Keel's objection

Keel claims there's no evidence Zeus Olympios = Baal-Shamem and that "abomination of desolation" refers to swine offered on the altar, not the altar itself. Day: 1 Macc 1:54, 59 "clearly indicates that the term refers to the altar" (p. 85). And Zeus Olympios for a Hellenized Syrian "must have constituted some form of the Syrian god Baal."

### Against Bickerman(n)/Hengel

They argue this was "extreme syncretistic Yahwism" (Yahweh equated with Zeus Olympios/Baal-Shamem by the Hellenizing party). Day: "It is more likely... that Antiochus' cult should rather be envisaged as pure paganism" — both earliest Jewish and gentile sources say this, and 2 Macc 6:7 mentions enforced Dionysus worship too, which doesn't fit monotheistic syncretism (p. 85-86)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Nestle's wordplay theory is widely accepted and phonetically elegant. The historical equation (Zeus Olympios = Baal-Shamem) is well-supported.

## Stakes

Daniel isn't just using apocalyptic code — he's continuing the OT tradition of polemical name-distortion. The "abomination of desolation" is not a mysterious symbol but a specific insult directed at Baal-Shamem, the god Antiochus installed in the Temple.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** O. Keel (claims no Zeus-Baal Shamem equation; argues "abomination" refers to swine, not altar). E. Bickerman(n) and M. Hengel (see it as syncretistic Yahwism, not pure paganism).

**Alternative reading:** Bickerman/Hengel: the "abomination" represents a Hellenizing Jewish faction's attempt to syncretize Yahweh with Zeus/Baal-Shamem. But the earliest sources call it pagan imposition, and 2 Macc 6:7 (Dionysus worship) doesn't fit the syncretistic model.
