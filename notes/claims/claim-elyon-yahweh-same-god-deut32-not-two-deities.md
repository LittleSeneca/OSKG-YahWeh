# heiser-ur-16.1: Elyon and Yahweh are the same God in Deuteronomy 32:8-9 — one God, two titles, not two deities

**Source:** [[Heiser — Part 4 — Yahweh and His Portion]] — Michael Heiser, *The Unseen Realm* (2015), Chapter 16

## The Claim

"When Elyon divided the nations... he fixed the boundaries according to the number of the sons of God. Yahweh's portion is his people." Elyon IS Yahweh. One God, two titles, one action: distributing nations to subordinate divine beings. The two names describe ONE being performing TWO functions. Elyon = the distributor. Yahweh = the receiver of Israel. Same God, different roles. "To a biblical writer, the Most High (*elyon*) was the God of Israel. The Old Testament refers to him as Most High in several places."

## Evidence

- Psalm 82 is the parallel: "God (*elohim*) stands in the assembly of El (*'ădat 'ēl*); among the *elohim* he judges." One God judging the council — NOT two separate deities
- Genesis 14:18-22: Melchizedek is priest of "El Elyon" — and Abraham identifies El Elyon with Yahweh
- Numbers 24:16; Psalms 7:17; 18:13; 47:2: all identify Elyon with Yahweh
- "The whole point of Deut 32:8-9 is that Israel is uniquely blessed because they are Yahweh's DIRECT portion — the other nations got lesser beings. There is no hint in the passage that Israel's God is a lesser being."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Heiser's reading is textually possible but requires collapsing the distinction between Elyon and Yahweh that Deut 32:8-9 seems to PRESERVE. Why use two names for one God doing one action unless there was a historical distinction being acknowledged?

## Stakes

If Heiser is right about Elyon=Yahweh, the entire critical edifice — Yahweh as junior deity rising to supremacy — collapses. If he's wrong, Deut 32 is the smoking gun for Israel's polytheistic past. This single claim carries the weight of the entire debate.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-deut-32-8-9-el-elyon-superior-yahweh-divine-son]] (Lewis — the Qumran/LXX text shows El Elyon as superior to Yahweh, who is one of the divine sons). Smith and Römer also read two distinct deities: Elyon (El, the high god) distributes the nations, and Yahweh (a junior god) receives Israel. Later, the two were identified.

**Alternative reading:** The critical reading: two distinct deities. Elyon (El, the high god) distributes the nations. Yahweh (a junior god) receives Israel. Later tradition merged them. This is the scholarly consensus (Smith, Römer, Lewis, Day, Cross).
