# dever-dghw-6-9.2: The suppression of Asherah = the suppression of women's religion

**Source:** [[Dever — Chapters VI-IX — Asherah to Conclusions]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Chapter VII

## The Claim

Folk religion was centered in the household, and the household was women's domain. Women were the primary practitioners of domestic religion — tending shrines, using figurines for fertility and healing, maintaining family rituals. "Official" Yahwism — the Temple, the priesthood, the written law — was men's domain. The Deuteronomistic reform was, in effect, a male clerical coup against women's traditional religious authority.

## Evidence

- Women's work included "tending to the family shrine and all of the religious rituals, rites of passage, and so forth"
- The figurines are found in domestic contexts — women's spaces — and relate to women's concerns (fertility, childbirth, nursing)
- Men had leisure time for "talking religion and politics" — women did the religious WORK
- "Official religion was a largely male preserve: priests, Temple functionaries, scribes, royal officials — all men. Women were excluded from the priesthood, from writing, from public office."
- "It is women's cults, centering on the veneration of Asherah, that the reformers set out to destroy."
- Dever distinguishes between "mainstream feminists" (competent scholars focusing on women's issues) and "doctrinaire feminists" (ideology trumps scholarship). He identifies himself as "politically speaking, a feminist" but not a "feminist scholar."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The domestic locus and women's role in household religion is well-supported by ethnographic parallels and archaeological context. The gendered dimension of the reform is real but hard to prove was the PRIMARY motivation.

## Stakes

The Bible's religion — monotheistic, aniconic, male-priest-led — is the religion of the winners. The religion it suppressed — polytheistic, image-rich, woman-led — was the religion of most Israelites. Recovering Asherah is recovering women's history.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see the Deuteronomistic reform as primarily theological rather than gendered; those who argue women's religious role was not as central as Dever claims.

**Alternative reading:** The reformers targeted polytheism wherever they found it — the domestic cults happened to be women's domain, but the motivation was theological purity, not gender politics.

## Assessment

The gendered dimension is real — the domestic/private sphere WAS women's domain and the reform DID target domestic cults. But "suppression of women's religion" may overstate the case: the reformers suppressed EVERYONE'S folk religion (men worshipped at high places too), and some women may have supported the reform. The more precise claim is that the reform disproportionately affected women because women's religious authority was concentrated in the domestic sphere that the reform abolished.
