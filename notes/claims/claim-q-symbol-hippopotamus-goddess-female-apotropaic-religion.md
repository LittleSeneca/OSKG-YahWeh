# keel-ggi-p1.3: The Q-symbol and Hippopotamus Goddess reveal female-centered apotropaic religion

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part I — Middle Bronze Age IIB]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part I

## The Claim

The Q-shaped mark "originated in Babylonia and may have symbolized a mother's womb. This sign was associated with ancient mother goddesses such as Ninhursag or Nintu." The Hippopotamus Goddess (Thoeris) was a hybrid creature whose "principal duty was to keep all types of evil from mothers and small children." Together, these represent a specifically female-oriented religious practice addressing pregnancy, childbirth, and infant mortality. The Q-symbol persisted for over a millennium: from MB IIB seal amulets through Late Bronze Age pendants to Iron Age IIC Judean graves, where "its function might be to characterize the grave as a mother's womb."

## Evidence

- **Hippopotamus Goddess:** Magic knife fragment from Tell el-Ajjul (~1750 BCE, Altenmuller 1965); two examples on bone inlay from tomb south of Jaffa (~1650-1550); scarab from late MB IIB necropolis at Azor
- **Q-symbol seal amulets:** Oval plaque with ring handle in Anatolia, rudimentary scarab shape in southern Palestine — produced at faience export facility ~1750 BCE
- **Old Babylonian plaque:** Mother goddess with Q-signs above miscarried infants
- **Continued use:** Late Bronze Age mother goddess pendant with Q-symbol; Iron Age IIC Judean graves with Q-symbol amulets
- **Function:** "its function might be to characterize the grave as a mother's womb" (Keel et al. 1989, 39-87 for full study)
- **Timespan:** ~1200 years from MB IIB through Iron Age IIC

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The Hippopotamus Goddess material is well-dated and clearly Egyptian in origin — the identification is secure. The Q-symbol's womb interpretation is more inferential, supported by the Old Babylonian plaque and grave context but ultimately an interpretation of an abstract symbol.

## Stakes

This shows that MB IIB religion had a distinctly female sphere of concern — pregnancy, childbirth, infant mortality — addressed by female-coded religious objects. The persistence of the Q-symbol from MB IIB through Iron Age IIC (~1200 years) demonstrates extraordinary religious continuity at the level of personal/family piety. This continuity is invisible in texts, which are concerned with official cult and national deities. If we only had the Hebrew Bible, we would know nothing of this tradition. The iconographic evidence reveals a layer of religion the biblical authors either didn't know about or chose not to record.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who interpret the Q-symbol differently — as a potter's mark, a decorative element, or an abstract sign without specific religious meaning.

**Alternative reading:** The Q-symbol might have multiple meanings depending on context — womb symbolism in one setting, something else in another. The 1200-year continuity might reflect reuse of older objects (heirlooms) rather than continuous religious practice.

## Assessment

The magic knife/Hippopotamus Goddess evidence is strong — these are well-understood Egyptian apotropaic objects with clear function. The Q-symbol interpretation is plausible but less certain. The most important contribution is methodological: by connecting objects found in children's graves to iconography of mother goddesses and womb symbolism, Keel & Uehlinger demonstrate how to extract religious meaning from archaeological context without texts. This is exactly the methodology the Introduction promised.
