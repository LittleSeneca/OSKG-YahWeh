# kaufmann-ri-13.7: Ezekiel is more priest than prophet — his ideology is pre-prophetic Torah religion

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch XIII — The Prophecy of the Fall]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter XIII

## The Claim

"Ezekiel's outlook is not so much that of classical prophecy, as of the Torah literature." He is "the prophet of the early religion, especially of the ideology of P." Unlike Amos, Hosea, Isaiah, Micah — who emphasized morality over cult — Ezekiel "alone among the classical prophets threatens national collapse for ceremonial sins."

## Evidence

- Ezekiel never enunciates "the contrast of morality versus cult" — the hallmark of classical prophecy
- He threatens destruction for ritual/ceremonial sins (chap. 22) alongside moral ones
- "He alone is concerned with prescribing cult laws"
- Murder, sexual immorality, idolatry — these "Sodomic" sins are his focus, echoing P's categories (Lev 18, 20)
- Ezekiel is "the only prophet who utilizes these legends of rebellion" from the Torah; other prophets view the Wandering as Israel's betrothal
- His dependence on P is pervasive and detailed: the forty years, the Levites, the tabernacle vision, Shaddai epithet
- He views the monarchy as "a supreme expression of God's grace" (Gen 17) — a Priestly rather than prophetic perspective
- "Ezekiel is fond of describing Israel's sin in 'Sodomic' terms" — "the land has become full of violence" echoes the Flood story (Gen 6:11)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The contrast between Ezekiel's categories and those of the 8th-century prophets is real and well-observed. Ezekiel IS different — more ritual, more priestly, less focused on social justice. Kaufmann's explanation (Ezekiel is drawing on P) is plausible but not the only possible one.

## Stakes

Whether Ezekiel represents a DEVELOPMENT beyond classical prophecy (the critical view) or a RETURN to pre-prophetic Torah religion (Kaufmann's view). If Kaufmann is right, Ezekiel isn't a late evolution but an archaic survival.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Critical scholarship typically sees Ezekiel as reflecting exilic or post-exilic priestly concerns. The dependence on P is read as evidence that P already existed in some form by Ezekiel's time (exilic), not that P is pre-prophetic.

**Alternative reading:** Ezekiel's priestly character reflects his social location (Zadokite priest) and his exilic context (where ritual/identity markers become more important), not the antiquity of his ideology. He draws on P because P is the literature of his priestly milieu — but P itself may be roughly contemporary with him.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's observation that Ezekiel differs from the 8th-century prophets in systematic ways is sound. His explanation (Ezekiel = pre-prophetic Torah religion) requires P to be substantially earlier than Ezekiel, which is the dating question Chapter V must answer. The evidence here is consistent with Kaufmann's thesis but doesn't independently prove it — Ezekiel's use of P is equally compatible with P being roughly contemporary (standard critical dating).
