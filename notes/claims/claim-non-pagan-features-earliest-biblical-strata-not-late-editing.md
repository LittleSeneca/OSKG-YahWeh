# kaufmann-ri-3.9: Non-pagan features present in earliest biblical strata

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter III

## The Claim

"The monotheism of these stories is, thus, not the outcome of an artificial adaptation of pagan materials. It permeates their every aspect and finds expression even in passages of artless naivete." "That any conscious censorship has been at work here to purge these stories of pagan features is improbable in view of the folk naivete that permeates the legends of Genesis 2-11. It was rather that the religion of YHWH instinctively excluded such motifs." The non-mythological features are "visible in the earliest strata, permeating even the 'magical' legends."

## Evidence

Kaufmann's methodological argument from candor: the biblical texts are CANDID about embarrassing details (God walking in garden, God's regret at creating humanity, God trying to kill Moses, sons of God mating with women, Jacob wrestling with a divine being, ecstatic frenzy, witch of Endor). If editors were systematically purging mythology, they did a terrible job — they left in material that could be read mythologically. What's missing (theogony, divine sexuality, God's death/resurrection) must be missing because it was never there.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The argument from candor is genuinely powerful. But it can only push non-mythological character back to the earliest SOURCES, not to historical ORIGINS. Between the events and the earliest written sources, centuries of oral tradition could have transformed the material.

## Stakes

The entire question of directionality. If non-pagan features are early, Kaufmann is fundamentally right. If they're late, the critical model stands.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The documentary tradition. The argument from candor cuts both ways: the Bible's frankness doesn't prove everything is historically reliable — it proves editors had criteria that weren't simply "make everything look pious." The absence of theogony could reflect that, by the time traditions were written down, theogony was no longer thinkable. [[claim-bible-preserves-polytheistic-traces]] argues the opposite: the texts preserve genuine early polytheism.

**Alternative reading:** The candor Kaufmann observes is genuine, but doesn't prove earliness. It proves Israelite religion, BY THE TIME OF ITS EARLIEST LITERARY SOURCES (10th-8th century BCE), was already non-mythological. But religion during the settlement/Judges period could have been more mythological. Archaeological evidence from that earlier period suggests continuity with Canaanite practice.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's argument from candor is genuinely powerful, and one critical scholars don't take seriously enough. If the non-mythological character were product of systematic editing, editors would have been more thorough. They left in Genesis 6:1-4, divine council scenes, Chaoskampf allusions — NOT the work of careful theological censors. BUT: this can only push non-mythological character back to the earliest SOURCES, not to ORIGINS. Centuries of oral tradition separate the two.
