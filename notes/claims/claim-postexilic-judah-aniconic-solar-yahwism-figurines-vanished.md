# keel-ggi-7.5: Postexilic Judah consolidated aniconic solar-Yahwism — lion stamp seals potentially symbolize Yahweh, figurine production vanished, and sharp cultic boundaries now coincided with political boundaries

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Part VII — Iron Age III]] — Keel & Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Part VII, §223–227

## The Claim

"Generally, there are now 'clear cultural and cultic boundaries within Palestine that coincide with those political boundaries known from historical tradition.' The contrast is striking between the discoveries on the coastal plain and in the Edomite-Arabic south (Hurvat Qitmit!)." The material record of postexilic Judah shows what monotheism looked like on the ground: no figurines, no goddess imagery, and administrative seals that used animal symbols (lion) rather than anthropomorphic deities — possibly with solar-Yahwistic connotations. The lion with solar disk and winged sun bulla from En Gedi suggest that "the God of Jerusalem was conceived primarily in terms of a 'being of light,' related to the sun." The solar dimension "should be seen as a constant element — even though flexible and adaptable — in Israelite and Judean worship of Yahweh."

## Evidence

- **Lion stamp seals** (§223): About 70 anepigraphic seal impressions on vessel handles from Jerusalem, Ramat Rahel, Gibeon, Tell en-Nasbeh, Jericho, and En Gedi (6th–5th centuries). The lion appears in two postures: standing on all fours (~60 times, illus. 380a–b) and standing on hind legs (3 times, illus. 381a–c). The lion "is to be treated as a symbol for Judah (Gen 49:9; cf. Ezek 19:2–9)" — and would later be replaced after 450 BCE by aniconic *yh(w)d* seals. An incense stand next to the lion on some impressions (illus. 381) "suggests that the latter interpretation cannot be excluded" — the lion may represent Yahweh symbolically "roaring from Zion."
- **Solar disk on lion** (§224): A vessel-handle impression from Ramat Rahel (illus. 382a–b) is more likely "an attacking lion, and the head of the lion... can be interpreted as having an open mouth." The solar disk "makes it probable that the aggressive animal... may represent a solar connotation of Yahweh of Zion." An En Gedi bulla (illus. 383) with a winged sun and Aramaic inscription *lmr* ("for the lord" or "belonging to the lord") "can be hardly any doubt that the winged sun represents the solar god of heaven." Isaiah 60:19f. and Malachi 3:20 show that even in postexilic times "the God of Jerusalem was conceived primarily in terms of a 'being of light,' related to the sun."
- **Disappearance of figurines** (§226): "There are hardly any terra-cotta or bronze figurines at all in postexilic Judah." Pillar figurines and horse-and-rider types vanish from Judah after the 5th century. Isolated statuettes in border areas like Lachish may indicate the settlement "did not belong to Judah/Yehud." "There was apparently no market for the inexpensive and once popular terra-cotta images in postexilic Judah, whether because religious development had advanced so far in the direction of monotheism that the need was no longer that great or because so-called 'orthodox' circles in postexilic Judah had effectively halted the production or import of these goods."
- **Aniconic name seals persist and intensify** (§225): Seventy bullae from an early postexilic Judean site show impressions that are "exclusively simple name seals." This contrasts with the 4th-century Wadi ed-Daliyeh archive in Samaria, whose bullae display "a colorful mixture of Greek and Persian images."
- **The solar dimension as constant** (§224): "In retrospect, this solar dimension should be seen as a constant element — even though flexible and adaptable — in Israelite and Judean worship of Yahweh."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the archaeological pattern. MEDIUM on the lion = Yahweh interpretation (the authors flag it as uncertain). HIGH on solar Yahwism as a persistent element — this is one of the book's central arguments.

## Stakes

If the lion stamps *do* represent Yahweh symbolically, then postexilic Yahwism wasn't fully aniconic — it used animal symbolism that later tradition would classify as idolatrous. The transition from lion stamps to aniconic *yh(w)d* stamps around 450 BCE marks the final step toward the imageless monotheism of rabbinic Judaism. The solar dimension is also significant: the Yahweh of postexilic Jerusalem was a *god of light*, not just a lawgiver.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Aharoni and many others read the Ramat Rahel impression as a bull with solar disk (alluding to the golden calf/Bethel tradition). Keel & Uehlinger argue persuasively against this on iconographic grounds (bulls' tails hang down; lions' tails rise over the back). Stern (1982) reads the upright lion as wounded/agony from the Persian king-hunt motif, which Keel & Uehlinger also dispute.

**Alternative reading:** The lion stamps could be purely administrative symbols with no theological significance — just as the rosette stamps probably were. But the solar disk and incense stand make a purely secular reading harder to sustain.

## Assessment

The disappearance of figurines from Judah is the single most important material fact in this chapter. It's not that figurines declined — they *vanished*. Meanwhile, Phoenicia and Edom continued producing them in quantity. This is a controlled experiment: same period, same region, but radically different archaeological profiles on either side of political borders. The orthodox campaign wasn't just literary polemic — it changed what people made, bought, and used. The solar-Yahwism theme connects the entire book's arc: from Egyptian solar imagery in Iron Age IIB, through the winged sun disk on royal seals in IIC, to the solar lion of postexilic Yehud. Yahweh absorbs the sun god's attributes.
