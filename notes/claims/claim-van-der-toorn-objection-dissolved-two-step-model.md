# fleming-ybi-5.3: Van der Toorn's objection dissolves with the two-step model

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 5 — The Name Yahweh]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 5

## The Claim

"When we shift the object of inquiry to Yhwʒ of Shasu-land in the Soleb inscription of Amenhotep III, and we set aside any consideration of deity in that name as reading back from a later phenomenon... the problem is not whether a major deity could be identified by a human ancestor but rather whether a people could be identified by a human personal name" (p. 203).

## Evidence

- Van der Toorn (1999: 914) acknowledged the theoretical possibility that Yahweh began as a deified ancestor but rejected it: "it is difficult to believe that the major Israelite deity... was originally a deified ancestor"
- Stol (1991) showed that Old Babylonian personal names like Ikrub-El, Ikšudum, and Ikūnum originated as deified ancestors — but these were minor family gods, not major pantheon deities
- Van der Toorn's objection is valid IF Yhwʒ was always a divine name. But if Yhwʒ was a people-name first, the objection doesn't apply
- "The difficulty shifts to explaining how the name for a people could come to be attached to a deity" (p. 203) — which is Ch 6's task

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The logical point is sound: van der Toorn's objection only matters if you're trying to derive a major deity directly from a personal name. Fleming's two-step model (personal name → people-name → divine name) avoids the objection.

## Stakes

This removes one of the main objections to deriving Yahweh from the Amorite onomasticon.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Van der Toorn and anyone who treats Yahweh as always having been a divine name.

**Alternative reading:** The two-step model just pushes the problem back: how does a people-name become the name of a major deity? That's no easier than deriving a deity from an ancestor directly.

## Assessment

Fleming is right that the two-step model changes the problem. A people giving its name to its god is a different phenomenon from a deified ancestor becoming a pantheon head. But he hasn't yet solved the problem — he's just relocated it. Ch 6 needs to deliver.
