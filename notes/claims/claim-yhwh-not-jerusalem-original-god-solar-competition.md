# romer-inv-7.2: Yhwh was not Jerusalem's original god; he rose to supremacy gradually

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 7 — Cult of Yhwh in Judah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 7

## The Claim

"Even in Jerusalem Yhwh was not the only god worshipped. He at first occupied the temple together with a solar god, to whom he was perhaps even subordinated." Yhwh rose to supremacy through a gradual process paralleled in other Levantine kingdoms (Moab, Ammon), where national gods absorbed older pantheons.

## Evidence

- **The solar competition:** Joshua 10:12-13 ("Sun, stand still over Gibeon") may preserve a memory of Yhwh's competition with Jerusalem's older solar deity. Either the text "insists on the superiority of Yhwh over solar and lunar gods" (7th century) or "shows traces of the competition between Yhwh, god of the army of Israel, and the tutelary deities of Jerusalem" (older).
- **The El-Yhwh identification at Jerusalem:** Genesis 14 (Melchizedek, "priest of El Elyon" at Salem = Jerusalem) preserves a tradition of Jerusalem as an El city. The Masoretic text identifies El Elyon with Yhwh — the identification was accomplished textually.
- **Regional parallel:** "In comparably sized kingdoms to the east of the Jordan, such as Moab and possibly also Ammon... the members of the Canaanite pantheon began to some extent to fade away behind the dynastic god who took up more and more space." Yhwh's rise fits a regional pattern.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH that Yhwh was not Jerusalem's original god (the El Elyon tradition is strong). MEDIUM on the solar competition (Joshua 10 is a difficult text to interpret historically).

## Stakes

Jerusalem — the "city of David," the place where God chose to dwell — originally belonged to OTHER gods. Yhwh was a latecomer who displaced both El (the older high god) and a solar deity (the older city god). The biblical narrative of Jerusalem as Yhwh's eternal city is theological retrojection.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional readings that see Jerusalem as always belonging to Yhwh. Scholars who argue Joshua 10 does not preserve genuine solar competition.

**Alternative reading:** Yhwh was always identified with El in Jerusalem; the Melchizedek episode simply narrates what was always the case.

## Assessment

The rise of Yhwh in Jerusalem is the local instantiation of the broader pattern Römer traces: a southern desert god, adopted by a northern people, imported to a Jebusite city, gradually absorbing the identities of the gods he displaced. Jerusalem was never "his" city in any original sense.
