# day-ygc-6.5: Isaiah 14:12-15 ("Lucifer") reflects a Canaanite myth of Athtar/Venus attempting to usurp Baal's throne, applied to Nebuchadnezzar's destruction of Jerusalem

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 6 — Yahweh and the Astral Deities]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 6

## The Claim

The "Shining One, son of the dawn" (*hêlēl ben šāḥar*) = the morning star Venus = the Canaanite god Athtar. The myth: Venus/Athtar attempts to ascend to Baal's throne on Mt Zaphon but is cast down. This is a VARIANT (not identical to) the Ugaritic Athtar myth (KTU² 1.6.I.43-67) where Athtar is APPOINTED to Baal's vacant throne but proves too small. The historical referent is specifically Nebuchadnezzar's capture of Jerusalem and destruction of the temple in 586 BCE — Day's own original proposal. The myth was appropriated through Jebusite Jerusalem: Elyon (Jebusite god), Zaphon applied to Zion (Ps 48:3), Shalem (god of dusk) = Jerusalem's name, and Shahar (dawn, Shalem's brother at Ugarit) all pointing to Jebusite mediation (p. 180-181).

## Evidence

**Venus = Athtar:**

1. LXX, Vulgate, Targum all understand the figure as the morning star (p. 168-169)
2. Classical parallel: Eos (Dawn) brought forth Eosphoros (Morning Star) — Hesiod, *Theogony* 381 (p. 169)
3. Against alternatives: Jupiter-Marduk and Enlil — both already supreme, no need to usurp. Moon — no known moon usurpation myth. Sun — doesn't match astral rivalry pattern. Halley's Comet — wrong date (before 550 BCE required) (p. 169-171)
4. South Arabian Athtar = Venus; Mesopotamian Ishtar = Venus; Astarte (Athtart) = Aphrodite (= Venus). Athtar and Astarte likely represent Venus as morning and evening star respectively (p. 172-173)

**Canaanite background:**

- Zaphon (v. 13) = Baal's mountain
- "Above the clouds" (v. 14a) = Baal as *rkb ʻrpt*
- "Mount of Assembly" (v. 13) = Ugaritic *phr mʻd*
- Elyon (v. 14) = Canaanite god attested at Ugarit, Philo, Sefire, and as Jebusite god of Jerusalem

**NOT the same as Ugaritic Athtar myth:**

- Differences too great: (a) Shining One acts from pride; Athtar is appointed by El and Athirat; (b) Shining One is cast down to Sheol; Athtar meekly descends to rule earth; (c) Shining One is deprived of power; Athtar "ruled on earth, god of it all" (p. 174-175)
- But both provide "variations on the theme of Athtar's inability to ascend the divine throne on Mt Zaphon, and so there is probably some ultimate connection" (p. 175)

**NOT the same as Ezek 28 or Psalm 82:**

- Ezek 28: figure is HUMAN (first man in Eden), not a star; figure is initially PERFECT; God PLACED him on the mountain. "Stones of fire" = lightning, not stars (p. 177-179)
- Psalm 82: all seventy sons of Elyon die collectively — not one figure's hubris
- Gen 6:1-4: sons of God descend to earth for sex — opposite movement

**Nebuchadnezzar and 586 BCE:**

1. "Zaphon was the name of both Yahweh's heavenly dwelling place (Isa 14:13; Job 26:7) and his earthly dwelling place on Mt Zion (Ps 48:3). Any attempted usurpation of the earthly throne naturally had repercussions in the heavenly sphere" (p. 184)
2. The most flagrant act of hubris by Nebuchadnezzar was destroying the temple — Isa 14:12-15 "is a heavenly reflection of this"
3. Daniel 8:9-14 (little horn rising above stars to "Prince of the host") also reflects temple violation (Antiochus IV) — same pattern
4. Jeremiah 50-51 (dependent on Isa 13-14) has the recurring theme that Babylon's downfall = revenge for violating Zion's temple (Jer 50:28-29; 51:11, 24, 34-37, 49-51). Jer 51:53 alludes to Isa 14:12-15

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** HIGH for Venus identification; MEDIUM-HIGH for Athtar connection; MEDIUM for the specific Nebuchadnezzar/586 BCE proposal (Day's own original suggestion, well-argued but novel).

## Stakes

The "Lucifer" passage that became foundational for Christian demonology (Satan as fallen angel) was originally a Canaanite astral myth about the morning star, applied by Isaiah to a historical Babylonian king. The entire Lucifer/Satan tradition is a remythologization of a polemical reuse of a Canaanite myth.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those identifying Helel with Jupiter-Marduk (Langdon, G.R. Driver), Enlil (Gallagher), the moon (Winckler, Snaith), the sun (May, Spronk), Halley's Comet (Etz). Those equating the myth with Ezek 28 (Clements, Barth, Page). Those seeing Greek Phaethon influence (Gruppe, Duhm, Grelot).

**Alternative reading:** The passage is about Jupiter-Marduk, or Phaethon from Greek myth, or a general hubris motif without specific mythological background.

## Assessment

This is Day at his best — philological precision, mythological comparison, historical contextualization, and an original thesis all in one section. The Venus/Athtar identification is the scholarly consensus and well-supported. The distinction from Ezek 28 is important and underappreciated. The Nebuchadnezzar/586 BCE proposal is elegant — it explains why a myth about Zaphon would be applied to a Babylonian king (because Zaphon = Zion, and Babylon destroyed Zion). The Jeremiah 50-51 parallel strengthens it considerably.
