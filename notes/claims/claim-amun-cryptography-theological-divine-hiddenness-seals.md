# keel-ggi-5.6: The Amun cryptography in Iron Age I seals reveals a sophisticated theology of divine hiddenness that predates and may have influenced Israelite aniconism

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Ch V — The Hidden God Victorious Gods Fertility Iron I]] — Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Chapter V

## The Claim

Building on the Gaza temple claim, the authors argue that the specific MECHANISM of Amun's hiddenness — cryptographic writing — was itself a theological practice. "The name Ymn-R' 'Amun-Re' recurs on numerous mass-produced pieces from the Ramesses Period... The name is frequently written cryptographically; for example, the sun disk instead of y, mn and the beetle (ntry 'divine') for n, which can be read simultaneously as the throne name of Tuthmosis III, Mn-hpr-r." The Tel Gerisa seal writes Ymn twice on the base: "partly explicit, partly cryptographic, we read the name Ymn twice (reed stalk = y + m3y 'lion' = m + nb 'Lord' = n; ytn 'sun disk' = y + mn)."

The lion plays a dual role in this cryptography: it writes the name AND expresses the god's nature. The Wenamun story quotes Amun's spokesman: "The sea is his, and Lebanon is his... Truly, a lion loves his possessions." The lion "both show[s] him as the 'hidden one' and as the one who had the power of a lion." The cryptography does not just encode the name — it encodes a THEOLOGY in which the god is simultaneously hidden (requiring decoding) and powerful (the lion).

## Evidence

- Multiple seal amulets with Amun cryptography: Tel Qasile, Tel Gerisa, Jaffa piece, Megiddo hoard pieces
- Cryptographic techniques: reed stalk = y, lion = m3y/m, nb = n, sun disk = ytn/y+mn, red crown = y, Horus falcon + flagellum = r'
- Uraeus used acrophonically from y'r.t for y
- Wenamun II 24, 34: Amun's spokesman claims universal dominion with lion metaphor
- Amun praised as "king of the gods" and "lion" in other contemporary texts
- Mass-produced goods concentrated on the coastal plain but also found in Shephelah and hill country

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The cryptographic readings are philologically sound (Jaeger 1982, Drioton's earlier work on Egyptian cryptography). The theological interpretation of the cryptography as expressing hiddenness is supported by Amun's very name. The dissemination into the hill country is archaeologically documented.

## Stakes

This claim bridges Egyptian theology and Israelite aniconism in a very specific way. If the "hidden god" concept was circulating in the hill country during Iron Age I — the period when Israelite identity was forming — then the eventual Israelite prohibition of divine images may have drawn on a pre-existing theological vocabulary of divine hiddenness. This is NOT the same as saying "Yahweh IS Amun" — but it does suggest that the CONCEPT of a god whose true form is inaccessible was available in the cultural environment.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who insist on the uniqueness of Israelite aniconism (e.g., Mettinger) would resist any Egyptian genealogy. The "hidden god" of Amun theology is not truly aniconic — Amun HAS a cult image, it is just hidden in a sanctuary no one can enter. Israelite aniconism involves the ABSENCE of a cult image, not its concealment.

**Alternative reading:** The cryptography may reflect Egyptian scribal playfulness rather than disseminated theology. The presence of Amun seals in the hill country could indicate trade, not religious influence. People may have worn Amun seals for their apotropaic value without understanding the cryptographic theology.

## Assessment

The distinction between "hidden image" and "no image" is real and important — Keel and Uehlinger do not fully address it. But their broader point stands: Iron Age I Palestine had a LIVED EXPERIENCE of a god whose form was inaccessible, whose name was concealed in puzzles, and whose sanctuary could not be entered. Whether this directly influenced Israelite aniconism or merely created a cultural environment in which aniconism was THINKABLE, it complicates any narrative of aniconism as a sui generis Israelite invention.
