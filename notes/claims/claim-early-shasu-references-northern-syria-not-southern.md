# fleming-ybi-2.5: Early Shasu references (15th-14th centuries BCE) point north toward Syria, not south

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 2 — Yhwʒ of Shasu-Land]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 2

## The Claim

"This evidence for the geography of the Shasu in the 15th and 14th centuries does not settle the location of 'Shasu-land'... The point is that in the earlier phases of Egyptian involvement in Asia... the geographical associations of mobile Shasu people tended to be more northern" (Fleming, p. 81).

## Evidence

- Thutmose IV's chariot list places Shasu among Syrian sites: Naharin, Babylon, Tunip, Kadesh, Takhsi — all in the Orontes region (p. 80)
- Amenhotep II's prisoner list (15,200 "living Shasu") appears alongside Nuhašše (northern Levant, east of the Orontes, south of Aleppo) — "the Amenhotep II list offers only a land far north of Palestine for its one point of reference" (p. 78)
- Amenhotep III's Document 5a lists "Shasu Spring" alongside Dothan and Šamhuna (Jezreel Valley, northern Palestine) — not southern (p. 79)
- The Soleb column N4 is on the north side of the hypostyle hall — Asian sphere, not African (south side has African peoples) (p. 53)
- Ramses II fought Shasu at Kadesh on the Orontes (p. 80)
- Only in the 13th century do Seir, Edom, and Moab appear in connection with Shasu

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** This is data-driven and verifiable from Giveon's corpus. The early texts genuinely point north. But "pointing north" doesn't mean Yhw3 specifically was northern — Shasu were everywhere in the inland back country. The argument establishes that there's no default southern location for early Shasu, not that Yhw3 was northern.

## Stakes

If early Shasu generally appear in northern/Syrian contexts, the default assumption that Yhw3 must be southern (driven by the Bible) loses its Egyptian support. The southern Shasu association is a 13th-century development, not an original feature.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Giveon (1971) read the early texts through a southern lens, assuming the Shasu were always associated with Edom/Seir. Most biblical scholars have followed Giveon.

**Alternative reading:** The Shasu category was applied to mobile pastoralists across the entire inland Levant. Southern Shasu existed alongside northern Shasu. The fact that Egyptian military interests were in Syria in the 15th century doesn't mean Shasu weren't also in the south — it means Egypt wasn't campaigning there yet.

## Assessment

Fleming's point is more modest than it seems. He's not proving Yhw3 was northern; he's deconstructing the assumption that it must be southern. The geographical agnosticism is methodologically sound: we simply don't know where Amenhotep III's Shasu-land was, and the default should be the entire inland back country, not the deep south.
