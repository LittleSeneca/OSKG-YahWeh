# smith-obm-2.2: The Ugaritic pantheon had four tiers: presiding couple, major deities, craftsman gods, and messenger/servant gods

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch2 — The Divine Council]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 2

## The Claim

The four-tier structure (following Handy, Mullen) provides a "heuristically helpful approach to the pantheon in Mesopotamia and Syria-Palestine." The tiers are: (1) El and Athirat as presiding couple, (2) the seventy sons — major active deities like Anat, Athtart, Baal, (3) Kothar wa-Hasis as craftsman, (4) messenger-gods and divine workers.

## Evidence

- **Tier 1:** El presides; Athirat is consort who may influence decisions (petitions El for Baal's palace, 1.4 IV-V; participates in selecting Baal's successor, 1.6 I). Her title rbt ("Lady") parallels Akkadian rabîtu for royal wives.
- **Tier 2:** The seventy sons of Athirat (1.4 VI 46) — possibly designated 'ilm rbm ("great gods"). Major figures: Anat, Athtart, Athtar, YD'-YLHN, Shapshu, Yarih, Shahar, Shalim. Baal is an outsider but can call El "father" (1.3 V 35; 1.4 IV 47). Combat and natural phenomena are concentrated at this tier. Important caveat: nature is "rarely stressed in either proper names, prayers, or incantations" — Baal's warrior role is more prominent across genres than his storm-god role.
- **Tier 3:** Kothar wa-Hasis — craftsman, serves upper tiers, homes in Egypt/Crete (foreign), "general factotum" of the great gods. The only deity clearly at this level. Represents Ugarit's trade connections.
- **Tier 4:** Messenger-gods, military retinues, divine workers ('inš 'lm, literally "men of the gods," 1.41.27).
- Mesopotamian council may have covered fewer levels: Mullen notes lower tiers like workers/servants may not have been included in the Mesopotamian council proper.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Tiers 1-2 are well-supported; tier 3 is thin (Kothar alone); tier 4 is residual.

## Stakes

The four-tier model becomes the template for understanding what Israelite monotheism ELIMINATED. When Smith says Israel "collapsed and telescoped" the pantheon, he means specifically: tiers 1-2 merged (El→Yahweh), tier 2 deities became either demons or Yahweh's servants, tier 3 disappeared, tier 4 became "angels." The model gives precision to what "convergence" actually means structurally.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Handy, whose work Smith builds on, sees the model as more structurally rigid (bureaucratic). Schloen prefers a patrimonial household model. Scholars who see Mesopotamian influence rather than Ugaritic may find different tier structures.

**Alternative reading:** The four-tier model may impose an artificial order on data that is messier. Is Kothar really a "tier" by himself or just a unique figure? Are the "seventy sons" a fixed number or a literary convention? The model works descriptively but may over-systematize.

## Assessment

The model is useful for what Smith needs it for: showing what Israelite monotheism eliminated. But it's clearly a modern scholarly construct. The strongest element is the tier 1/2 distinction — the presiding couple vs. the active warrior/nature deities. Tiers 3-4 are fuzzier. Smith acknowledges this implicitly by noting how little data supports tier 3.
