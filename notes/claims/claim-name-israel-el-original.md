# smith-obm-7.3: The name "Israel" proves El was the original god

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch7 — El Yahweh and the Original God of Israel]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 7: El, Yahweh, and the Original God of Israel

## The Claim

"If Yahweh had been the original god of Israel, then its name might have been *yiśrâ-yahweh, or perhaps better *yiśrâ-yāh in accordance with other Hebrew proper names containing the divine name. This fact would suggest that El not Yahweh was the original chief god of the group named Israel." The divine element in "Israel" is *'ēl — unambiguous. This is the simplest, most elegant evidence in Smith's entire argument: you don't need Ugaritic, epigraphy, or source criticism — just look at the name.

## Evidence

- The divine element in "Israel" is *'ēl — unambiguous
- Israel is a very old name, apparently known both at Ebla and Ugarit — thus predates the historical phenomenon of the Iron I highlands people
- "When the name began to refer to the historical phenomenon of a people in the Iron I highlands, perhaps it no longer referred to the god to whom it was devoted" — Smith acknowledges the name may have become detached from its original referent, but its very form is evidence of origins
- The distribution of El vs. Yahweh in personal names in early poems "likewise points in this direction"
- Smith concedes proper names "pose difficulties when used to reconstruct religious history, yet when used in conjunction with other evidence, proper names offer admissible evidence"
- Israelite inscriptions: 557 names with Yahweh, 77 with *'l — Tigay argues 'l is a title for Yahweh, not a separate deity. Smith doesn't deny this for the Iron II period but argues the NAME "Israel" preserves an earlier stage
- The Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BCE) attests the name — but this doesn't tell us which god was referenced

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH as a suggestive datum, MEDIUM as a stand-alone proof. The name is a powerful piece of the cumulative case but not by itself dispositive. Smith explicitly notes the name may have become detached from its original referent.

## Stakes

This is the simplest, most elegant evidence in Smith's entire argument. You don't need Ugaritic, epigraphy, or source criticism — just look at the name. It's the kind of argument that makes you wonder why it isn't foregrounded more often in discussions of Israelite origins.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Those who argue the name could be a sentence name with El as an appellative ("God strives") rather than the proper name of the deity El. Those who date the name "Israel" to a period AFTER El = Yahweh identification.

**Alternative reading:** The name "Israel" could have been coined when El and Yahweh were already identified — i.e., the name-bearers thought of 'ēl as a title for Yahweh. The etymology doesn't have to preserve the earliest stage of religion.

## Assessment

Smith uses the name as one piece of a cumulative argument, not as a silver bullet. He explicitly notes that "when the name began to refer to the historical phenomenon of a people in the Iron I highlands, perhaps it no longer referred to the god to whom it was devoted." This is honest. But combined with the sanctuary evidence (Claim 2) and Deut 32:8-9 (Claim 4), the name becomes part of a triangulation that's hard to dismiss.
