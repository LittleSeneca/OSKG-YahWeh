# schmid-ht-1.7: The Hebrew Bible's theology is neither Christian nor Jewish

**Source:** [[Schmid — §1 — Is There a Theology of the Hebrew Bible]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §1

## The Claim

"The theology of the Hebrew Bible and, at the same time, the promotion of the theology of the Hebrew Bible as historical description means that the results of such a project can neither be a Christian theology of the Old Testament, nor — with qualifications — a Jewish theology of the Hebrew Bible." However, "there is some difference in content between what a historically descriptive approach...will reconstruct...and what is to be expected in Christian and Jewish tradition. However, it will not be completely alien, as these traditions have developed from, among other sources, the Hebrew Bible."

## Evidence

- "The Hebrew Bible is older than Christianity, and this is also the case for the relationship of the Hebrew Bible to Judaism with regard to the preexilic texts"
- Both traditions developed FROM the HB, so historical description should not be entirely foreign to either
- The differences and proximities will be addressed in Part I at the book's end

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** This is the same claim from the Preface, slightly elaborated. The "older than" argument is historically true but hermeneutically naive — it assumes that "older" means "independent of," when in fact the HB AS A CANON is a product of both Jewish and Christian reception decisions.

## Stakes

Same as in the Preface. Schmid wants to claim a neutral academic space. Whether that space exists is debatable. If Schmid's claim fails, his project is exposed as Protestant historical-critical scholarship pretending to be neutral — which doesn't invalidate his results but undermines his methodological self-presentation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Levenson, Sommer, and others who argue "Hebrew Bible" is a Christian construct. Jewish scholars who maintain that the Tanakh's theology IS Jewish theology and cannot be separated from the tradition that preserved it.

**Alternative reading:** Schmid IS doing Christian (Protestant) theology — he's just doing a liberal Protestant version that doesn't require normativity. Admitting this would be more intellectually honest than claiming neutrality.

## Assessment

This is Schmid's most vulnerable claim. He's doing Protestant historical-critical scholarship and calling it neutral. That doesn't invalidate his results — but pretending he's neither Christian nor Jewish is less honest than admitting his location and arguing for its methodological advantages.
