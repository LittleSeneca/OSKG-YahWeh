# romer-inv-7.3: Centralization of worship in Jerusalem was a late political project

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 7 — Cult of Yhwh in Judah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 7

## The Claim

The requirement that all worship be centralized at Jerusalem (Deuteronomy 12) was a 7th-century innovation, not the original norm. The kings who enforced it (Hezekiah, Josiah) were pursuing political consolidation as much as religious reform. "The irony of history: these high places were probably typically 'Israelite' or 'Judean,' but they had finally to cede their places to the Temple of Jerusalem."

## Evidence

- Before the Temple was built, high places were legitimate — even Solomon worshipped at Gibeon (1 Kings 3:3-15)
- The Deuteronomists rationalized this with a footnote: "the people were sacrificing at the high places, however, because no house had yet been built for the name of the Lord" (1 Kings 3:2)
- This footnote reveals their awareness that centralization was an INNOVATION that needed explaining
- Only two kings (Hezekiah and Josiah) attempted to eliminate the high places — proving they were the NORM, not the exception
- Hezekiah's centralization (late 8th century) was partial and probably politically motivated — consolidating control after the fall of Israel in 722
- Josiah's reform (late 7th century) was more thorough — destroying high places, removing solar worship from the temple, centralizing sacrifice

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The biblical texts themselves acknowledge the shift. The historical development is clear.

## Stakes

One capital, one temple, one god — the centralization of worship was the political arm of the monotheizing project. The theological claim that God chose Jerusalem as his dwelling place was a political ideology that served the interests of the Davidic dynasty. The destruction of the high places was not purification; it was consolidation of power.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Traditional readings that see the temple as God's original intent from Sinai. Some archaeologists who argue the high places really were syncretistic.

**Alternative reading:** The temple was always the intended center of worship; the high places were tolerated only until the temple was built. The 1 Kings 3:2 footnote is merely chronological clarification, not admission of innovation.

## Assessment

Centralization was the lever by which monotheism was enforced. You can't have one god if people are worshipping him in ten different places with ten different local manifestations. The temple monopoly on worship was the infrastructure of monotheism. It was political, not theological, in origin — and it was theology only after the fact.
