# romer-inv-9.2: The Bible deliberately obscures the Yhwh-Asherah link by associating her with Baal

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 9 — Yhwh and His Asherah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 9: Yhwh and His Asherah

## The Claim

"The biblical texts make no direct link between Asherah and Yhwh." Instead, they associate her with Baal. This is a deliberate Deuteronomistic strategy: "The fact that all the cited passages come from editors of the Deuteronomistic school strongly suggests that this association was invented to break the link between Yhwh and Asherah."

## Evidence

- *'Ăšērāh* occurs 40 times in the Bible (18 singular, 19 masculine plural, 3 feminine plural)
- The masculine plural is surprising — "an artificial creation of the editors of the Bible in order to avoid any possible allusion to the goddess Asherah"
- Four categories of biblical reference: (a) destruction formulas, (b) associated with Baal, (c) with standing stones, (d) near Yhwh's altar/house
- The Baal-Asherah link appears ONLY in Deuteronomistic passages — a polemical invention
- "In sharp contrast to the biblical texts, a close link between Yhwh and Asherah is attested in the inscriptions"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** High that the Bible obscures the Yhwh-Asherah link. Medium-high that the Baal association is a deliberate Deuteronomistic invention — the restriction to Dtr passages is suspicious.

## Stakes

If the Bible deliberately rewrote the Asherah-Yhwh relationship into an Asherah-Baal relationship, then the biblical text is actively misleading on this point. The "idolatry" it condemns isn't foreign importation — it's displaced domestic tradition. This changes how we read every Deuteronomistic polemic: they weren't just condemning, they were erasing and rewriting.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who read the Baal-Asherah pairing as authentic tradition rather than polemical invention. If Baal and Asherah were genuinely paired in Israelite religion (perhaps in northern circles), the Dtr association isn't fabrication but accurate reporting of a rival tradition.

**Alternative reading:** The Baal-Asherah pairing reflects genuine northern Israelite religion where Baal, not Yhwh, was Asherah's partner. The Deuteronomists accurately reported this and used it polemically — but didn't fabricate it. The absence of Baal-Asherah pairing in extra-biblical texts could simply reflect the accidents of preservation.

**Supports:**

**Primary sources:**

## Assessment

The restriction of Baal-Asherah pairing to Dtr passages is genuinely suspicious. If Baal and Asherah were authentically paired in Israelite religion, we'd expect to see it outside Dtr circles. The fact that the inscriptions pair Yhwh with Asherah while the Bible pairs Baal with Asherah suggests one of these pairings is secondary — and the biblical one has the clearer editorial fingerprints. Day independently reaches the same conclusion (Olyan's "guilt by association" theory). This is one of the more elegant arguments in the field.
