# lewis-ocg-4.6: El Shadday Is Genuinely Archaic, Not a Priestly Invention

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 4 — El Worship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"The extra-biblical attestations of Shadday together with these three biblical passages make it unwise to view P's and Job's use of Shadday as fictitious creations from a late (exilic/post-exilic) period." He then quotes Cross (1998): "The Pentateuchal tradents... were more successful in their reconstructions of early Israel than we critical historians have supposed."

## Evidence

- (1) Ugaritic KTU 1.108 attests a deity called šd/Shadday in Late Bronze Syria
- (2) Cross and Knauf argue the name appears in a Late Bronze Egyptian name: ś3-d-ï-ʿ-m-y = "Shadday is my paternal relative"
- (3) The 8th C BCE Deir ʿAlla texts mention the Shaddayyin (šdyn) meeting in divine council — independent of the biblical tradition and centuries earlier than P
- (4) Three biblical passages seem archaic: Genesis 49:24-25 (El//Shadday with womb/breast imagery), Numbers 24:16 (El//Elyon//Shadday triplet), and Numbers 1:5-15 (list of names with El and Shadday theophorics but NO Yahweh or Baal names)
- Cross: "these lists actually reflect characteristic formations of the onomasticon of the second millennium"
- Lewis refuses to pick among the three etymologies (mountain, steppe, destruction) — the antiquity of the name is solid regardless of etymology

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The convergence of Ugaritic, Egyptian, Deir ʿAlla, and biblical evidence is strong; medium on the Num 1:5-15 list — it could be genuinely archaic OR brilliant P archaizing.

## Stakes

Whether P (Exod 6:3: "I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as El Shadday") preserves genuine historical memory or is a late theological construct. If Shadday is archaic, P is a reliable tradent, not just an inventor.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Minimalist historians see P as a Persian/Hellenistic period creation with no access to genuine archaic traditions. The names in Num 1:5-15 would be deliberate archaisms.

**Alternative reading:** P may have used genuinely archaic names (Shadday) while creating a fictional theological framework around them. Archaism does not equal historicity.

## Assessment

Lewis makes a strong case. The Deir ʿAlla Shaddayyin alone are enough to prove Shadday is not a Priestly invention — they're 8th C BCE and independent of the biblical tradition. The question of whether Shadday was associated with mountains, steppe, or destruction is genuinely unresolved (Lewis refuses to pick among the three etymologies), but the antiquity of the name is solid.
