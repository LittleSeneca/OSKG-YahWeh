# kaufmann-ri-11.7: "Second Hosea" (chs 4-14) knows nothing of syncretistic YHWH-Baal worship — the critical reading is wrong

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch XI — Amos and Hosea]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter XI

## The Claim

"Baal worship is mentioned explicitly only twice (9:10; 13:1), both times as a sin of the past. The current sin of Ephraim... is that of the calves (13:2). Of a continuous historical sin of YHWH-Baal syncretism Hosea knows nothing." The "harlotry" of Hosea 4-14 is literal sexual license, NOT a metaphor for Baal worship. "The harlotry spoken of in these passages is no more symbolic than the drunkenness with which it is linked; it has nothing to do with Baal worship (a misconception which has been furthered by the failure to separate the two Hoseas)." The mention of "the Baals" in 11:2 is "no more than a derogatory reference to idol-worship in general, not to actual Baal worship."

## Evidence

- 9:10 and 13:1 both frame Baal worship in the past tense
- 13:2 identifies the CURRENT sin as the calves
- 4-14 makes "no reference at all to worship of 'other gods' as a sin of the present"
- Hosea's depiction of Israel's history: "At first Israel was loyal to YHWH; apostasy set in only when Israel became fat and full (13:6; cf. Deut. 31:20; 32:15)"
- "Hosea knows of no punishment for past periods of sin; the past furnishes him only with sporadic examples of sin: Gibeah (9:9; 10:9) and Baal Peor (9:10)"
- The harlotry condemned in 4-14 is described alongside drunkenness, linked to "a spirit of harlotry" (5:4), and associated with threshing-floor festivities (9:1ff) — it is actual sexual behavior

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann is making a characteristically bold claim: that the entire critical reading of Hosea as a witness to syncretism is built on a category error (conflating First and Second Hosea). But the claim that "harlotry" in 4-14 has "nothing to do with Baal worship" is hard to square with the prophetic use of sexual imagery for idolatry throughout the Hebrew Bible (Ezekiel 16, 23; Jeremiah 2-3).

## Stakes

Whether Hosea 4-14 depicts a syncretistic popular religion or a morally decayed but theologically monotheistic one. If Kaufmann is right, the critical narrative of Israelite syncretism loses one of its key textual witnesses.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The critical consensus reads Hosea's harlotry language as a metaphor for Baal worship, rooted in the actual sexual rites associated with Canaanite fertility cults. Day (Chapter 3) documents the reality of Baal cult in Israel. Smith traces the Yahweh-Baal conflict as a central dynamic of Israelite religion.

**Alternative reading:** Kaufmann is partly right that the "harlotry" language has been over-read as ALWAYS referring to cultic prostitution. Some passages in Hosea DO refer to literal sexual license. But the prophetic use of marriage/adultery as a root metaphor for covenant faithfulness/idolatry is so pervasive that it's perverse to claim Hosea 4-14 uses it only literally. The "spirit of harlotry" (5:4) that prevents knowledge of God is theological language, not a commentary on sexual mores.

## Assessment

Kaufmann is making a real and valuable point about over-reading "harlotry = Baal cult" in every instance. But he overcorrects. The prophetic tradition consistently uses sexual unfaithfulness as a metaphor for religious unfaithfulness. To claim that Second Hosea somehow doesn't participate in this — while First Hosea DOES (the marriage metaphor) — requires Kaufmann's prior conclusion that the two are separate authors. The argument is circular: they're separate because Second Hosea doesn't use the marriage metaphor, and Second Hosea doesn't use the marriage metaphor because they're separate.
