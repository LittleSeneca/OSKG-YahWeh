# smith-obm-4.8: Lineage erosion drove the shift from divine family to individual divine accountability

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch4 — Pluralities Pairings and Other Divine Relations]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"For ancient Ugarit, conceptual religious unity was expressed most strongly in the identification of the divine council as a divine family." Israel's later monotheism, by contrast, "was denuded of the divine family, a development perhaps intelligible in light of Israel's weakening family lineages and patrimonies."

## Evidence

- At Ugarit, the patronym was "the most important element...when identifying a person" (Pardee). Land transfers were from "sons of X" to "sons of Y." The family was the strongest form of social identity.
- "The polytheistic family may have provided what would have been in Ugaritic culture the most 'natural' means to express singleness and coherence in 'divine society.'"
- In Israel, by the 7th-6th centuries: prophetic and deuteronomic texts proclaim individual accountability (Jer 31:29-30; Ezek 18; Deut 24:16) over inherited family guilt.
- The post-exilic bêt 'ābôt ("fathers' house") replaces the older bêt 'āb — a shift possibly reflecting decline of traditional family structure.
- "A culture with a diminished lineage system...might be more predisposed both to hold to individual human accountability for behavior and to see an individual deity accountable for the cosmos."

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** Smith himself calls this a "working hypothesis" — the correlation between lineage erosion and monotheism is real but proving causation requires more evidence.

## Stakes

If Smith is right, monotheism is not just a theological development but a SOCIAL one — it emerges when the old family-based social structures that made the divine family intelligible break down. This is a materialist explanation for what is usually treated as an intellectual/spiritual development.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see monotheism driven by theological factors (the prophets' ethical vision), political factors (Assyrian/Babylonian imperial pressure), or literary factors (scribal redaction). Anyone who thinks the timeline of lineage erosion doesn't match the timeline of monotheistic emergence.

**Alternative reading:** The correlation could be reversed: monotheism's emphasis on individual accountability might have DRIVEN changes in family structure, not the other way around. Or both could be effects of a third cause (urbanization, imperial disruption).

## Assessment

This is Smith's boldest claim in Part I and the least well-supported. It's a fascinating hypothesis that ties the theological and the social together, but as Smith admits, it's a "working hypothesis." The timeline needs more precision: when exactly did lineage systems erode in Judah, and can we date the earliest monotheistic expressions with enough confidence to establish sequence? I'm skeptical but intrigued.
