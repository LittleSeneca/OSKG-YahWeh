# kaufmann-ri-4.2: Creation legends prove folk monotheism

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch IV — The Religion of the People]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter IV

## The Claim

"The folk character of the creation legends signifies that the people among which they were formed was monotheistic." These stories "could not have arisen among a polytheistic people, who conceived of YHWH as one of the gods, who knew and believed in pagan myths." "Too naive and crude for the prophets, these can only have been creations of the people." They "lack all traces of ideas peculiar to later times" so "must be early creations."

## Evidence

- Gunkel's error: assuming literary adaptations of pagan materials. "In antiquity adaptation implies an acceptance of and a belief in the material adapted"
- "A later adapter would hardly have left so many anthropomorphisms and dangerous ambiguities in his story"
- Stories have a "primitive, idyllic — one might almost say provincial — character... so different from the grand pagan myths of universal forces clashing in primeval times"
- "The homely image of the serpent who is far removed from the later symbol of cosmic evil, of a God who strolls in the garden and sews shirts for Adam and Eve point to a literary genre nearer the folk tale"
- "And yet with all their primitiveness there is no real myth here: no theogony, no sexual differentiation or desire in the deity, no theomachy, no magic, no demonic realm"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The observation that Genesis 2-3 lacks theogony, theomachy, and divine sexuality IS correct. But the conclusion that these stories "could not have arisen" in a polytheistic environment assumes what needs proving.

## Stakes

Whether Israel's creation traditions are evidence of early monotheism or later monotheistic editing of originally polytheistic material. If Kaufmann is right, Genesis 2-3 was never myth in the first place.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see Genesis 1-3 as Israelite transformations of Mesopotamian material (Enuma Elish, Atrahasis). The parallels — primordial chaos, divine rest, the garden, the serpent, the tree of life — suggest Israel didn't invent these motifs ex nihilo. [[claim-genesis-1-monotheistic-poetics]] sees Genesis 1 as late theological polemic.

**Alternative reading:** Kaufmann's own evidence cuts both ways. If stories are "too naive and crude for the prophets," their monotheism may be folkloric simplicity rather than theological conviction. The absence of polytheism could equally be evidence of theological naivete — a simple story that hasn't yet asked the question of other gods.

## Assessment

Kaufmann's reading of Genesis stories as genuinely folkloric rather than literarily adapted is appealing. The homely quality of Genesis 2-3 IS different from Enuma Elish's cosmic scope. But the absence of other gods proves at most that the STORIES are non-polytheistic, not that the CULTURE was monotheistic. A polytheistic culture can produce stories focused on a single deity — it just implies narrative focus.
