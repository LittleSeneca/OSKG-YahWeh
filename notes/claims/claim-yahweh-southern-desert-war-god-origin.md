# romer-inv-intro.2: Yhwh was originally a southern desert/war god who gradually became Israel's god and then the only God

**Source:** [[Romer — Introduction]] — Thomas Romer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Introduction

## The Claim

Yhwh "probably had his origin somewhere in the 'South,' between the Negev and Egypt. Originally he was a god of the wilderness, of war and storms, but gradually through a series of small steps he became the god of Israel and Jerusalem." After the catastrophe of 587, he became "the one god, creator of heaven and earth, invisible and transcendent."

## Evidence (in the Introduction)

This is a programmatic claim — Romer previews the argument the whole book will make. The specific evidence is promised for later chapters (2-3 on the southern origin, 4-5 on becoming Israel's god, 12 on becoming THE only God).

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Preview only — cannot assess until the evidence chapters are evaluated.

## Stakes

If Yhwh's journey went: desert war god -> national god of Israel -> the only God, then monotheism is not a revelation FROM God but a human CONSTRUCTION about God. The God of Jewish, Christian, and Islamic theology is the end product of a historical process. Whether that process was guided by God or was purely human is a theological question Romer doesn't answer — but he makes it impossible to claim that monotheism dropped from heaven fully formed.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who trace Yhwh's origins to the north (Canaan, Ugarit) or see him as originally a manifestation of El. Those who argue the southern origin hypothesis lacks sufficient evidence.

## Assessment

This is the book's thesis previewed. Romer's confidence is striking and will need to be earned. The southern origin is widely accepted in critical scholarship, but the claim that we can trace the specific steps of Yhwh's journey from desert god to sole God requires evidence that the Introduction does not (and cannot) provide. Track whether Romer's later chapters deliver on this ambitious program.
