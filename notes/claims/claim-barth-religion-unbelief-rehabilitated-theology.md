# schmid-ht-6-9.2: Barth's "religion is unbelief" reversed the Romantic devaluation — now RELIGION was the problem and THEOLOGY was rehabilitated

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§6-9 — Jewish Reception to Implicit Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §7

## The Claim

Karl Barth declared "religion is unbelief. It is a concern, indeed, we must say that it is the one great concern, of godless man." Religion, as human work, stands in opposition to revelation. This "stigmatization of the concept of religion" had the effect of rehabilitating "theology" — the term that 19th-century scholarship had treated as pejorative.

## Evidence

- Barth, *Church Dogmatics* I/2: "Religion is never true in itself and as such"
- The history-of-religions approach had treated theology as dead systematization; Barth reversed this: religion is dead human work, theology is faithful response to revelation
- "Accompanying the gradual success of neo-orthodox theology...skepticism towards 'religion' increased. On the flip side, the concept of theology...bloomed again"
- The result in HB studies: calls for an independent, non-historical theology of the OT
- Emil Brunner and Rafael Gyllenberg went further: "there can be no independent theology of the Hebrew Bible" — because the HB doesn't attest Christian revelation

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Barth's "religion is unbelief" is one of the most famous theological declarations of the 20th century. Schmid's analysis of its effect on HB studies is historically accurate.

## Stakes

The Barthian rehabilitation of "theology" is what made the 20th-century discipline of OT theology possible — but it saddled it with a fatal ambiguity. Is OT theology describing the HB's own religious thought (descriptive)? Or is it articulating Christian doctrine from OT texts (normative)? The Barthian inheritance tends toward the latter, which is exactly what Schmid resists. Schmid is doing theology in the wake of Barth — but against Barth's normative grain.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** George Fohrer critiqued neo-orthodoxy harshly: it "did OT studies great harm through its renunciation of any religio-historical perspective, its disregard for an appropriate understanding of the uniqueness of Israelite religion, and its revival of allegorical and typological interpretation."

**Alternative reading:** The Barthian turn was necessary to rescue the theological significance of the HB from being dissolved into mere history-of-religion. Schmid's attempt to reclaim "theology" while rejecting Barth's normative framework may be extracting the term without its animating logic.

## Assessment

This is the crucial pendulum swing in Schmid's genealogy. Barth saved "theology" as a term but at the cost of disconnecting it from historical scholarship. Schmid wants to keep the term "theology" (Barth's gift) while restoring its connection to historical description (the 19th century's gift). Whether this synthesis is stable is the question.
