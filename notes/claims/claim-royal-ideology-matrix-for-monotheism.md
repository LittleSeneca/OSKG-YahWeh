# smith-obm-8.5: Royal ideology provided the conceptual matrix for monotheism

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch8 — Emergence of Monotheistic Rhetoric]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 8

## The Claim

"The royal theology and religious practice served as a basic matrix to support the national god." Three overlapping types of royal theology drew on West Semitic myth: (a) action parallel between Yahweh and the king, (b) metaphorical granting of divine power to the king in the language of the Baal-Yamm conflict myth, and (c) possibly the king as `'ĕlōhîm` (divine).

## Evidence

**(a) Action parallel — divine and human kings mirror each other:**
- Royal psalms present Yahweh and his "anointed" ruling together from Jerusalem (Ps 2), with Zion as cosmic center (Pss 46, 48, 87)
- "In contrast, all the other kings and their nations, ideally, are to submit to the authority of Yahweh and his human regent on earth."
- 1 Sam 25:28: the king fights "Yahweh's wars"
- Ps 18 ∥ 2 Sam 22: Yahweh described in Baal's storm-battle terms (vv. 8-19), enabling the king to conquer (vv. 29-45)
- Habakkuk 3: Yahweh's storm-theophany tramples enemy-nations to save the "anointed" (v. 13); includes Resheph (v. 5) as attendant destructive divinity
- "This poem bears a further importance, as it illustrates divine powers subservient to Yahweh, the warrior-king, in a context supportive of the monarchy."

**(b) The king receives Baal's power:**
- Ps 89:26 — "I will set his hand on Sea and his right hand on Rivers." The human king's power extends to the cosmic enemy of Baal. "It crosses the boundaries of this parallelism by making the king into a figure of his patron storm-god."
- Nur-Sin letter from Aleppo to Zimri-Lim of Mari: Adad (Baal) says "when you ascended the throne of your father, I gave you the weapon with which I slew Sea." — same motif at Mari centuries before Israel.
- Egyptian texts (Tutmoses III stele) dress the king in Baal's storm imagery.
- This is not merely figurative: "the human king was perhaps thought to 'participate in' the power of the divine king."

**(c) The king as `'ĕlōhîm` (divine):**
- Ps 45:7: "your throne, O divine one (`'ĕlōhîm`), (is) forever and ever." The versions generally support this reading.
- Isaiah 9:5: the "messianic" figure is called `'ēl gibbôr` ("warrior-god") and `'ăbî 'ad` ("eternal father") — titles drawn from Yahweh's own titles (Isa 10:21; Deut 32:6)
- Holladay's counter-argument (theophoric throne-names) fails: `*gibbôr` is not productive in Israelite names
- Zech 12:8: "the house of David shall be like God" — "muted reflex" of older high royal theology
- Porter: 2 Sam 14:17 (David as "angel of God" knowing good and evil) parallels Gen 3:22, placing David among the `'ĕlōhîm`

**Key concession:** "The use of `'ĕlōhîm` for the monarch may represent not an ontological claim but a description intended to heighten the power of the king by rhetorically raising him to divine status."

**The hērem-warfare connection to Anat:**
- The West Semitic conflict myth also underlies Israelite `hērem` (ban) warfare
- Ugaritic Anat conducts `hērem` warfare: "Destroy under the ban for two days" (CAT 1.13)
- 1 Kings 20:42: Ahab is condemned for NOT executing `hērem` on Ben-hadad
- "As the major deployers of warfare, Iron Age monarchs were well-served by the mythos of hērem-warfare."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH on the royal theology framework; MEDIUM on the king-as-`'ĕlōhîm` reading (Smith himself concedes it's "debatable but plausible"). The broader framework doesn't depend on Ps 45:7.

## Stakes

If the king was called `'ĕlōhîm`, divinity was on a spectrum — not a binary. The same rhetorical heightening that made the king "divine" would later make Yahweh "the only god." Monotheistic rhetoric may be the same kind of move applied to a different object. When the monarchy collapsed, the cosmic exaltation remained but the earthly parallel vanished, creating the conditions for monotheism.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Emerton and Mosca on Ps 45:7 (lack of clear parallels to king as `'ĕlōhîm`). Holladay on Isa 9:5 (theophoric names). Anyone who reads Ps 45:7 as "Your throne is God's throne forever."

**Alternative reading:** Even if the king wasn't literally called `'ĕlōhîm`, the broader royal framework (king as Yahweh's earthly parallel, empowered by divine weapons) is independently attested and doesn't depend on the Ps 45:7 debate.

## Assessment

This is the longest and most technical section of the chapter, and the arguments are somewhat esoteric for the larger thesis. But the PAYOFF is important: it shows that the conceptual TOOLKIT for monotheism (absolute language, cosmic scope) was already present in royal ideology. Monotheism didn't invent new concepts — it repurposed old ones under new conditions. The hērem-Anat connection is a bonus insight — it shows how deeply royal warfare drew on West Semitic divine mythology.
