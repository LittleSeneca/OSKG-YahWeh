# romer-inv-6.1: Biblical pure Yahwism is Deuteronomistic propaganda

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapter 6 — Cult of Yhwh in Israel]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapter 6

## The Claim

The Deuteronomistic editors of Kings (6th century BCE) retrojected their theology of exclusive Yhwh worship and centralized Jerusalem cult backward onto the entire monarchy. This picture "does not correspond to historical reality." Before Josiah's reform in the late 7th century, Yhwh worship was diverse, regional, and tolerant of other deities and symbols. The Bible's presentation of northern Israel as apostate from the beginning is Judean propaganda written after the north had fallen.

## Evidence

- **The editorial bias:** The Deuteronomists judged kings not by political success but by theological purity. Manasseh — presented as the worst king — reigned 55 prosperous years as a loyal Assyrian vassal. Hezekiah — praised as the best — pursued a suicidal anti-Assyrian policy that reduced Judah's territory. The theological evaluation inverts the political reality.
- **Israel was dominant, not apostate:** "During the two centuries when the two kingdoms coexisted, Israel was the geopolitically dominant one, while Judah was a tiny monarchy that seems to have often been in the position of a vassal to its 'big brother' in the north."
- **The united monarchy is fiction:** "The idea of a great kingdom, united under David and Solomon, comes more from the imagination of the authors... than from any historical reality."
- **History written by the survivor:** Northern Yahwism was just as legitimate as southern — just different. But Judah survived the Assyrian conquest. Its scribes wrote the history.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on Deuteronomistic bias (well-established in scholarship since Noth). HIGH that Israel was more powerful than Judah (archaeological and textual evidence converge). The united monarchy as ideological fiction is broadly accepted in critical scholarship.

## Stakes

The entire biblical history of the monarchy — the good kings and bad kings, the faithful north and apostate north — is a theological construction. The real history of Israelite religion was diverse, regional, and politically complex. The Deuteronomists flattened it into a single evaluative framework: centralized worship in Jerusalem = good; everything else = bad. This framework still shapes how many people read the Bible today.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who defend the essential historicity of the Deuteronomistic History. Maximalist positions that accept the biblical evaluative framework as reliable.

**Alternative reading:** The Deuteronomists preserved genuine historical traditions even while evaluating them theologically. The northern kingdom DID introduce non-Yahwistic cults (Baal worship under Ahab and Jezebel). The theological critique has a historical basis.

## Assessment

This is the foundation claim for all of Römer's Chapter 6-7 analysis. If you accept the Deuteronomistic bias — and nearly all critical scholars do — then the biblical portrait of Israelite religion must be read against the grain. The question is not WHETHER the Deuteronomists shaped the history, but HOW MUCH genuine historical memory survives their editing. Römer leans toward "not much." Other scholars would say "more than he admits." But the basic point is unassailable: the Bible's evaluative framework is a late Judean construction.
