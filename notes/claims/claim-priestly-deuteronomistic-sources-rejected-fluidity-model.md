# sommer-bog-3.1: P and D sources rejected the fluidity model — creating the transcendent God

**Source:** [[Sommer — The Bodies of God]] — Benjamin D. Sommer, *The Bodies of God and the World of Ancient Israel* (2009), Chapter 3

## The Claim

The theological revolution was not from polytheism to monotheism. It was from a FLUID monotheism (J and E) to a DISCRETE, transcendent monotheism (P and D). P and D rejected the idea that God has multiple bodies, that God can be physically present on earth, and that God's self is fluid. They replaced the fluid God with a God accessible only through mediated forms: the *kavod* (glory), the *shem* (name), and the *torah*.

The P and D sources are not "more monotheistic" than J and E. J and E were already monotheistic. What P and D did was change the NATURE of the one God — from fluid, multi-bodied, and physically present to discrete, single-bodied (or bodiless), and transcendent. The God of later Judaism and Christianity is the P/D God, not the J/E God.

## Evidence

**The P source's anti-fluid moves:**
- Genesis 1: Humanity is made in God's *ṣelem* (image) — but P never describes God walking, eating, or being physically present. The *ṣelem* is abstracted.
- P's Tabernacle: God's *kavod* (glory) dwells in the Tabernacle, but it's a luminous cloud — not a body. It's "the physical manifestation of the divine presence that does not quite amount to an actual body."
- Exodus 33: P weaves together traditions about seeing God, then insists "you cannot see my face" — the chapter is "an anthology of conflicting traditions regarding the presence of God and how humans relate to it"
- For P, God dwells in heaven. What dwells on earth is God's *kavod* — not God himself.

**The D source's anti-fluid moves:**
- Deuteronomy insists God dwells in heaven, not on earth: "Yhwh your God is God in heaven above and on earth below" (Deut 4:39)
- The Temple is not God's house where he lives — it's "the place where Yhwh your God will choose to CAUSE HIS NAME TO DWELL" (*lĕšakkēn šĕmô šām*)
- God himself remains in heaven. Only his "name" (*shem*) dwells in the Temple
- This is a deliberate rejection of the older idea that God literally lived in the Temple

**The anti-anthropomorphic trend:**
- Both P and D reduce anthropomorphic descriptions of God
- God doesn't walk, eat, or appear in human form
- God's presence is mediated through non-anthropomorphic entities: *kavod* (glory), *shem* (name), *ruach* (spirit)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH that P and D reduce anthropomorphism (well-established in scholarship). MEDIUM that this represents a deliberate "rejection" of fluidity rather than a different tradition that developed independently. Sommer's claim that P and D were REACTING to J and E is harder to prove than his claim that they DIFFER from J and E.

## Stakes

If Sommer is right, then the God of later Judaism and Christianity (transcendent, noncorporeal, accessible only through mediated forms) is not the "original" biblical God. It's the result of a theological revolution within the Bible itself — P and D's rejection of the older J/E fluidity model. This means the tradition ITSELF contains multiple legitimate theologies of divine presence. The task of faith is not to recover "the" biblical God but to choose among the biblical Gods.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see P and D as independent traditions rather than as reactions to J and E; scholars who argue J and E were also anti-anthropomorphic in their own way.

**Alternative reading:** P and D do not "reject" J and E's theology — they represent independent priestly and scribal traditions that developed their own theological vocabulary. The reduction of anthropomorphism is a natural development within monotheism, not a polemical rejection.

## Assessment

Sommer's strongest contribution here is the *shem* theology observation: D's insistence that only God's NAME dwells in the Temple, not God himself, is a deliberate theological choice. Whether this represents a "rejection" of J/E or an independent development is less clear. The evidence that P and D reduce anthropomorphism is solid; the evidence that they were reacting AGAINST something is more speculative. The *kavod* as "a body that is not quite a body" is a fascinating concept — it preserves the idea of divine presence while stripping it of the problematic implications of full embodiment.
