# kaufmann-ri-7.2: The Ḥerem Historically Destroyed the Canaanite Population

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch VII — The Conquest and Settlement]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Ch VII

## The Claim

The biblical tradition of Canaanite annihilation through *ḥerem* is essentially historical. "The view everywhere is that the Canaanites within the Israelite area of settlement were destroyed; Israel was seduced by those who remained outside and surrounded them." To understand the evidence, one must distinguish the "real land" (Dan to Beersheba, the area actually occupied) from the "ideal land" (including Philistia, Tyre, Sidon, Lebanon, Hermon).

## Evidence

- **Earliest records know no peaceful relations:** Josh 15:63; 16:10; 17:12-18; Judg 1 "know nothing of alliances and intermarriage with the Canaanites or their influence on the religion of Israel." Later sources that DO mention intermarriage actually refer to nations *surrounding* Israel, not Canaanites *within*.

- **No Canaanites blamed for apostasy:** "Nowhere are Canaanites within the real land blamed for Israel's apostasies." Prophets attribute idolatrous influence to Philistines and surrounding nations, never to domestic Canaanites. Even Judg 6:8ff and 10:6 refer to "the peoples that were round about them" — Aram, Sidon, Moab, Ammon, Philistines.

- **The Gibeonite exception proves the rule:** The Gibeonites survive only because of an oath; they become hierodules (Nethinim) and remain a distinct, inferior caste through the Second Temple period. "Can we suppose... that the Israelites intermingled amicably with the other Canaanites who are never reported to have made peace with Israel?" The *ḥerem* status is precisely what kept them segregated.

- **No Canaanite military factor after Deborah:** Only one Judges war is with Canaanites (Barak/Deborah, on the disputed boundary). After this, all Israel's enemies are non-Canaanite. "Never did the Canaanites rise from within against their conquerors; never did they unite with their brethren on the boundaries."

- **No class of Canaanite private slaves:** Judges 1 records mass impressment into forced labor (whole communities, for royal service), but "there is no trace of the formation of a class of Canaanite private slaves." Biblical foreign slaves are *purchased*, not captured in war — inconsistent with a surviving Canaanite population.

- **Disappearance of war horse and chariotry:** The Canaanite military technology (horse, iron chariot) "disappears" with Israel's occupation. Joshua hamstrings horses and burns chariots; David does the same. Not until Solomon do these reappear. "Why was this indispensable weapon not adopted by Israel from the Canaanites, if not because the *ḥerem* had destroyed the only class that could teach Israel its use?"

- **Disappearance of the Canaanite city-state:** The city-state political form disappears from within Israelite settlement. Israelite kingship "owes nothing to Canaanite predecessors" — it rules over tribes, not cities.

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** Kaufmann identifies genuinely interesting data that deserves more attention (the Gibeonite/Nethinim argument is striking; the absence of Canaanites as a military factor after Deborah is a real datum). But the archaeological record shows substantial cultural continuity between Late Bronze and Iron I highland settlements. The "real land"/"ideal land" distinction lets Kaufmann quarantine all counterevidence by definition. The *ḥerem* tradition is more likely a theological construct — the language of total destruction expresses the ideal of separation from Canaanite religion, not a historical report of genocide.

## Stakes

Whether early Israelite religion developed in cultural isolation (as Kaufmann claims) or in continuity and conflict with Canaanite culture (the consensus view). If the Canaanites were largely annihilated, the "Canaanization" model loses its demographic basis.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The archaeological record shows substantial cultural continuity between Late Bronze and Iron I highland settlements — the same pottery traditions, settlement patterns, and agricultural practices. The "peasant revolt" and "gradual emergence" models (Mendenhall, Gottwald, Dever, Finkelstein) see early Israelites as largely indigenous Canaanites who withdrew from the city-state system, not as external conquerors who annihilated the population. Smith (Ch 1) documents that YHWH was worshipped alongside Canaanite deities in the Judges period.

**Alternative reading:** The *ḥerem* tradition is a theological construct — the language of total destruction serves to express the ideal of separation from Canaanite religion, not a historical report of genocide. The fact that the Bible continually *commands* destruction of Canaanites and *complains* that Israel failed to do so suggests the destruction didn't actually happen. The archaeological evidence supports a model of gradual highland settlement by populations that included both incoming groups and indigenous elements.

## Assessment

This is the most historically consequential claim in the chapter, and also the weakest. Kaufmann is arguing for a genocide that the archaeological record doesn't support. His strongest evidence is negative — the absence of Canaanites as a named class, the disappearance of the city-state, the absence of chariot warfare — but all of these can be explained by the collapse of the Late Bronze city-state system (a regional phenomenon, not uniquely Israelite) and the nature of highland settlement. The Gibeonite argument is genuinely interesting and underappreciated: if Canaanites were peacefully absorbed, why do the Gibeonites alone survive as a segregated caste for a millennium? But this proves only that the Gibeonites had a special status, not that all other Canaanites were annihilated.
