# lewis-ocg-10.3: The Holiness Code democratized holiness to the entire community

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 10 — Holy One]] — Theodore Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 10

## The Claim

"Even more striking is the Holiness Code's (H's) comprehensive notion of holiness that includes the entire community (not only priests) and covering a wide array of cultic, economic, judicial, moral, and social parameters." H's command "You shall be holy, for I Yahweh your God am holy" (Lev 19:2) is addressed to "all the congregation of the Israelites" — not to priests.

## Evidence

- H expands holiness beyond the sanctuary to everyday life:
  - Harvesting practices (leaving gleanings for the poor)
  - Labor relations (paying workers promptly)
  - Justice (impartial judgment)
  - Social ethics (love of neighbor, love of the ger/foreigner)
  - Sexual ethics
  - Economic justice (Jubilee)
- This is a DEMOCRATIZATION of holiness: P restricts holiness to Aaronids; H extends it to everyone
- The theological basis: imitatio Dei — "to be holy as he is holy"
- H's command is addressed to "all the congregation of the Israelites" (Lev 19:2), not to priests
- The P vs. H tension means the Hebrew Bible preserves genuine debate about the nature of holiness, not a single uniform doctrine

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** VERY HIGH. H's expansion of holiness is well-documented in the scholarship (Knohl, Milgrom, Schwartz).

## Stakes

Whether Israelite religion is fundamentally hierarchical (priests mediate holiness) or democratic (everyone participates in holiness). The answer is both — P and H represent competing visions preserved within the same canon. The democratization of holiness in H is one of the most ethically significant developments in Israelite religion — and it grows directly from the concept of imitatio Dei.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who see H as an organic extension of P rather than a counter-tradition. The debate is about the relationship between P and H, not about H's content.

**Alternative reading:** H's democratization may be rhetorical rather than practical — everyone is CALLED to be holy, but priests still manage the cult.
