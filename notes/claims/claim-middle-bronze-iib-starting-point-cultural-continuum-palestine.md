# keel-ggi-1.6: The survey begins with Middle Bronze IIB because that's when a continuous cultural tradition begins in Palestine

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Ch I — Starting Point and Points of Departure]] — Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Chapter I

## The Claim

"One can justify beginning our survey at this point, when the urban culture in Palestine begins to flourish, because from this point on we can deal with a cultural continuum in Palestine that extends all the way to the time of the emergence of the Hebrew Bible."

## Evidence

- Early Bronze IV/MB I (2200-2000): "Iconographic evidence is virtually nonexistent"
- MB IIA (2000-1750): "limited generally to Egyptian imports"
- MB IIB (from 1750): Syrian influences arrive, indigenous production begins, Hyksos period creates Canaanite-Egyptian interchange
- Finkelstein's argument: the hill country population didn't disappear during Late Bronze — they shifted to nomadism and resettled in Iron I

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The archaeological rationale is sound. The "cultural continuum" claim about ethnic continuity is more debatable.

## Stakes

This justifies the entire chronological scope of the book and implies Israelite religion didn't emerge from nowhere — it developed within a Canaanite matrix.
