# fleming-ybi-1.4: The "people of Yahweh" ('am Yhwh) in Judges 5:13 represents a pre-Israelite identity

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 1 — Introduction]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 1

## The Claim

"The central battle narrative evokes the god first to identify the 'people of Yahweh' who will win victory and then to curse those who stay at home (Judg 5:13, 23). This 'people of Yahweh' name the alliance of groups to follow without reference to Israel, which is only linked to the battle by addition of the opening hymn in verses 2-11" (p. 40).

## Evidence

- All eight references to Israel in Judges 5 appear in the opening hymn (vv. 2-11), per Sara Milstein's (2016) "revision through introduction" framework (p. 27)
- Mark Smith (2014) affirmed and built on this observation
- Smith does not prefer Fleming's reading that the battle's participants shared identity as "the people of Yahweh" — Smith sees the 'am Yhwh as also belonging to the introductory revision (p. 27, n.11)
- The groups named (Ephraim, Benjamin, Machir, Zebulun, Issachar, Naphtali) are not called "Israel" in the core narrative

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The redaction-critical observation (Israel only in the frame) is strong and attested by multiple scholars. But whether the core narrative's participants identified as "people of Yahweh" rather than by some other collective name is more contested — Smith disagrees with Fleming on this point. The 'am Yhwh phrase in v. 13 is itself textually and interpretively disputed.

## Stakes

This is one of Fleming's two "principal items" of evidence (alongside the Egyptian texts). If 'am Yhwh is a pre-Israelite identity, then Yahweh defined a people before Israel did — and some of those people became Israel. This inverts the Midianite Hypothesis: Yahweh wasn't imported TO Israel; Israel was partly constructed FROM Yahweh-people.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Mark Smith (2014: 245-46) considers the 'am Yhwh passages part of the introductory revision, not the original battle account. Fritz (2006) similarly sees the battle as not involving Yahweh originally.

**Alternative reading:** The 'am Yhwh is an Israelite self-designation added by the same redactor who added the "Israel" frame. The original battle poem celebrated human warriors without divine involvement or collective identity labels.

## Assessment

This is Fleming's most creative and most vulnerable claim. He's honest that Smith disagrees. The claim depends on (a) the redaction-critical separation holding up and (b) the 'am Yhwh belonging to the older layer. If Smith is right that 'am Yhwh is also secondary, this pillar weakens substantially.
