# keel-ggi-5.4: Seth-Baal and Reshef transition from warrior gods who FIGHT to gods of domination who STAND — a shift from conflict to command

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Ch V — The Hidden God Victorious Gods Fertility Iron I]] — Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Chapter V

## The Claim

"Images of Reshef wielding a weapon and Baal-Seth in battle are not typical of the Iron Age I. Images showing them in command and standing on animals become the norm." Seth-Baal is shown as "a winged deity with horned cap, occasionally standing on a lion." Reshef appears "on top of a horned animal" — carefully executed pieces identify the animal as a gazelle. The lion under Seth-Baal's feet "is not his attribute animal, but his opponent, known from Late Bronze Age glyptic art and probably to be identified as Mot, the Canaanite god of summer drought and death."

This shift from active combat to static domination has a theological dimension: "the weather and fertility aspects of Baal that had been emphasized in Middle Bronze Age IIB receded in importance, in deference to military themes, when Baal was associated with Seth in the Late Bronze Age" — and in Iron Age I, even the military action recedes in favor of a posture of achieved victory. The god no longer needs to FIGHT; he stands on his defeated enemy.

## Evidence

- Seth-Baal on truncated pyramid seals: winged with horned cap and long tassel, standing on lion, flanked by uraei
- Seth-Baal on Ramesses Period mass-produced scarabs
- Reshef on scarabs: god on gazelle
- Bronze Reshef figurine from Megiddo Area BB Str. V B (c. 1050-975): "appears with a hand weapon in his raised hand and holds a shield" — but even this may be an heirloom from Late Bronze Age
- The Baal-Seth / Amun relationship in Wenamun II 19: Amun thunders, sends Seth
- Cryptographic seals combining Amun and Seth names

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The iconographic pattern is consistent across multiple media and sites. The shift from combat to standing-on-animal is well-documented.

## Stakes

This provides an iconographic parallel to the biblical shift from Yahweh as divine warrior who fights FOR Israel (Exodus 15, Judges 5) to Yahweh as enthroned king who rules OVER the nations (Psalms 93-99). If Seth-Baal's iconography shifted from combat to command during Iron Age I, this represents a broader West Semitic theological development that Israelite religion participated in, not a uniquely Israelite innovation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who emphasize the distinctiveness of Yahweh's kingship would resist the implication that it derives from Canaanite models. The Baal cycle at Ugarit (Late Bronze) already shows Baal enthroned after defeating Yamm — the "standing on" motif may simply be a new ICONOGRAPHIC convention for an old theological concept.

**Alternative reading:** The shift from combat to standing may reflect changes in seal-carving fashion (static poses are easier to execute on small stamp seals) rather than theological change. The "standing on" motif could simply be a compositional convenience.

## Assessment

The authors' reading is more persuasive than the "artistic convenience" alternative because the motif appears consistently across multiple media (pyramid seals, scarabs, scaraboids) and the same shift affects MULTIPLE deities (Seth-Baal, Reshef, Horus-as-crocodile-master). A fashion change might affect one figure; a systematic pattern across the entire pantheon suggests a conceptual shift.
