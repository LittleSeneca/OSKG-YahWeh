# smith-obm-4.1: Divine military retinues fit the patrimonial household model

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch4 — Pluralities Pairings and Other Divine Relations]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 4

## The Claim

Baal has a meteorological retinue (CAT 1.5 V 6-9) and "divine helpers" ('il t'dr b'l in 1.47.26). Resheph appears as both singular god and plural "Reshephs" (ršpm). "As members of the second tier of the divine assembly and sons of the divine patriarch, these two gods were in a position to have retainers work for them. Accordingly, these retinues are well within the paradigm of the patrimonial household."

## Evidence

- Baal's meteorological retinue in CAT 1.5 V 6-9.
- 'il t'dr b'l, "Baal's divine helpers" in pantheon lists (1.47.26, 1.118.25).
- mhr b'l wmhr 'nt, "Baal's and Anat's warriors" in 1.22 I 8-9.
- The plural ršpm in CAT 1.91.11 (entering the royal palace/sanctuary).
- Egyptian comparison: Ramses III's army "as mighty as Rashaps."
- Sidonian inscriptions: 'rṣ ršpm, "land of Reshephs" (KAI 15:2).
- BH rešep as part of Yahweh's theophanic vanguard in Habakkuk 3:5 and Deuteronomy 32:23-24.
- Philo of Byblos (PE 1.10.20): allies of Elos called "Eloim" — the pattern of naming a group after its patron deity.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The Ugaritic evidence for divine retinues is fragmentary but consistent; Philo of Byblos parallel strengthens the model.

## Stakes

If divine retinues are structurally similar to human household retainers, the family model extends even to military organization — the pantheon is a household, not a bureaucracy. This reinforces Smith's core argument about the family as root metaphor.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Few directly dispute this; the evidence is simply sparse. Alternative models might emphasize bureaucratic/administrative hierarchy (as in Mesopotamia) over household retainers.

**Alternative reading:** The plural forms (ršpm, 'eloim) could be honorific plurals or abstract collectives rather than literal "retinues named after the patron god."

## Assessment

The data is suggestive but thin. The strongest parallel is Philo of Byblos, and his reliability is debated. The Israelite reflex (Resheph in Yahweh's retinue in Hab 3:5) is a good data point — it shows the concept traveled.
