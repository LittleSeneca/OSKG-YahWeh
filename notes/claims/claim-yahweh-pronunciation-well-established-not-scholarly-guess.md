# lewis-ocg-6.1: The pronunciation "Yahweh" is well-established — not a scholarly guess

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 6 — Origin of Yahweh]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 6

## The Claim

Statements that the pronunciation is "a scholarly guess" (Thompson), "uncertain" (Baker), or "unknown" (Grabbe) are "overly circumspect and reflect a long-standing agnosticism born out of the post-rabbinic tradition that one ought not to pronounce the divine name at all. In contrast, historians of Israelite religion have confidently vocalized yhwh as Yahweh for years, and rightly so."

## Evidence

(1) The abbreviated form Yah occurs independently (Exod 15:2; Ps 68:5) and in theophoric names (Nērî-yāh, Nĕtan-yāh) and hallelujah — establishing the first vowel as a-class. (2) The second vowel is i-class due to regular formation of third-he verbs in Hebrew. (3) Amorite personal names show yaqtil forms: yawi-ila, yahwi-Dagan. (4) Greek transcriptions give Iaoue and Iabe. (5) "Jehovah" is an artificial hybrid — the consonants of YHWH pointed with the vowels of ʾădōnay, never intended to be pronounced. The Masoretes were creating a reading indicator, not a new name.

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** This is a settled philological conclusion that introductory textbooks have been slow to adopt.

## Stakes

Surprisingly little for religion — but a lot for scholarly credibility. The persistence of "Jehovah" in popular discourse and the agnosticism about "Yahweh" in textbooks reflects the gap between specialist knowledge and public understanding.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Jehovah's Witnesses and other groups that use "Jehovah" as the divine name. Some scholars maintain agnosticism as a gesture of respect for Jewish tradition.

**Alternative reading:** The agnosticism is a feature, not a bug — the divine name's pronunciation was deliberately lost, and scholarly reconstruction, however confident, is still reconstruction.
