# kaufmann-ri-4.6: No genuine syncretism occurred — YHWH was never merged with Baal

**Source:** [[Kaufmann — Ch IV — The Religion of the People]] — Yehezkel Kaufmann, *The Religion of Israel* (1960), Chapter IV

## The Claim

"The conventional opinion that the popular religion was Canaanized and worshiped a syncretistic YHWH-Baal is groundless. Syncretism (the combining of two gods) could have arisen only out of the mutual influence of two cults. But there is no record of an independent, thriving cult of Baal in the period of the judges, nor during the monarchy before the time of Omri."

## Evidence

- Baal names (Eshbaal, Meribbaal, Beeliada) only show "titular" use — Baal means "lord"
- Baal worship of Ahab's time was "altogether new. Its votaries were Jezebel's Sidonian entourage. It was a special phenomenon, entirely apart from the worship of YHWH"
- "The worshipers of Baal and YHWH are two separate groups (II Kings 10:23). Jezebel kills the prophets of YHWH... But Jezebel does not sacrifice to Baal on YHWH's altars nor erect symbols of the Baal cult in the temples of YHWH"
- Elijah's challenge: "If YHWH is God, follow him; but if Baal, follow him" (I Kings 18:21) — "The underlying assumption is that only one is God, and that a decision must be made. The audience is assumed to have a monotheistic, not a syncretistic, outlook"
- "The only attested instance of syncretism is to be found in Manasseh's association of pagan deities with YHWH in his own temple. But his was a royal idiosyncrasy which had no popular roots"
- "For genuine syncretism presupposes an essential parity between the deities to be amalgamated... But when, as in Israel, there is a belief that only one being is supreme... no genuine syncretism is possible"
- Hosea's supposed syncretism is imaginary: "There is no commingling of YHWH and Baal; on the contrary, the people are blamed for having forgotten YHWH"

## Confidence

**Rating:** low

**Rationale:** Kaufmann's reading of the Elijah narrative is interesting but the Kuntillet Ajrud evidence shows YHWH paired with Asherah in a way that IS syncretistic. He's attacking a straw man — the critical claim isn't about formal amalgamation but gradual absorption.

## Stakes

Whether Israelite religion was a hybrid of Canaanite and Yahwistic elements or a fundamentally distinct religion that occasionally suffered foreign incursions.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** [[claim-convergence-differentiation-framework-israelite-religion]] — Smith argues YHWH absorbed characteristics of El, Baal, and other deities through convergence and appropriation, not formal syncretism but genuine borrowing. [[claim-yahweh-absorbed-baal-storm-warrior-imagery]] is the specific mechanism. [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-proves-consort]] shows YHWH paired with Asherah.

**Alternative reading:** Kaufmann is attacking a straw man. The critical claim isn't that Israelites consciously merged YHWH and Baal into one hybrid deity. It's that Israelite religion gradually absorbed Canaanite elements — divine council imagery from El, storm theophany from Baal, possibly consort from Asherah — while maintaining YHWH's distinct identity. The Kuntillet Ajrud "Yahweh and his Asherah" isn't formal syncretism (merging two gods) but giving YHWH a consort — which IS religious borrowing that complicates Kaufmann's absolute-difference thesis.

## Assessment

Kaufmann is right that biblical texts don't depict formal syncretism (YHWH-Baal hybrids). The Baal cult under Ahab IS depicted as alien and opposed. But he defines syncretism too narrowly. Religious borrowing doesn't require formal amalgamation — adoption of imagery, epithets, rituals, and divine relationships is borrowing. "Yahweh and his Asherah" at Kuntillet Ajrud looks like syncretism in a form Kaufmann doesn't adequately address: not merging YHWH with another god, but giving him a consort.
