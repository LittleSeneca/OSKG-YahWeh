# smith-obm-1.5: El's "beloved" epithets for cosmic enemies carry legal force — designated heir status and vassal subservience

**Source:** [[Smith Origins — Ch1 — Anthropomorphic Deities and Divine Monsters]] — Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001), Chapter 1

## The Claim

The cosmic enemies are not merely El's favored creatures — they are his legally designated successors and beloved servants. The term *mdd 'il* ("Beloved of El") carries legal force comparable to Solomon's name Jedidiah ("beloved of the Lord"), marking public designation as heir. The animal terms *'gl* ("calf") and *klbt* ("dog") connote subservience and service, not merely affection.

## Evidence

- The *locus classicus*: Anat's speech to Gpn w-Ugr in CAT 1.3 III 36-IV 1 lists cosmic enemies by their El-relationship terms: Yamm = *mdd 'il* ("Beloved of El"), River = *'il rbm* ("Great God"), Arsh = *mdd 'ilm* ("Beloved of El"), 'tk = *'gl 'il* ("Calf of El"), 'isht = *klbt 'ilm* ("Dog of El"), Flame = *bt 'il* ("Daughter of El").
- Mot also bears the title *mdd 'il* elsewhere. Yamm is called *ydd 'il*.
- **Szubin on biblical *yadid/yedidyah*:** "This status invested the chosen 'beloved' designee with power, authority and title and bestowed upon him special rights and privileges... in adoption, matrimony, inheritance and succession." The designation functioned to "silence also claims... which challenged the legitimacy of the lawfully chosen 'righteous' son."
- **Amarna letters:** *kalbu* ("dog") used to express vassalage to Pharaoh. "What is Abdi-Ashirta, servant and dog, that he takes the land of the king for himself?" (EA 71:16-19; cf. 60:1-9; 88:9-11). Same usage in Lachish letters (KAI 192:3-4) and 2 Kings 8:13.
- **Akkadian personal names:** *A-ga-al-dMarduk* ("calf of Marduk") and names using *buru* ("calf") plus divine name — indicating subservience.
- **Ugaritic, Hebrew, Phoenician names:** containing *klb* ("dog") understood as "servant, slave."
- **Narrative context:** In CAT 1.1 IV, El selects Yamm as champion of the gods — at Baal's expense. "These epithets mark not only El's preference for them over the other gods but also their status as his publicly designated successors."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The Szubin legal analysis and Amarna parallels are persuasive, but we lack an explicit Ugaritic legal text confirming the "designated successor" reading of *mdd*. The animal terms as subservience markers are probable but not certain.

## Stakes

If correct, this transforms our understanding of the Baal Cycle's central conflict. Baal is not merely fighting monsters — he is fighting El's legally designated heirs, which makes his eventual kingship a usurpation of the divine succession. This also explains why El initially supports Yamm against Baal.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who read *mdd* simply as "beloved" in an affective sense, without legal overtones. The legal reading depends on Szubin's biblical analysis and Amarna parallels — neither is Ugaritic. Some may argue the animal terms are purely descriptive.

**Alternative reading:** The "legal" and "affectionate" readings are not mutually exclusive — *mdd* could be the standard term for a favored subordinate without specific inheritance implications. The animal terms could reflect both form AND status simultaneously.

## Assessment

This is one of the most original and interesting arguments in the chapter. The Szubin parallel is genuinely illuminating — it explains why El's "love" for Yamm has political-narrative consequences rather than being mere sentiment. The Amarna/Lachish evidence for "dog" as vassal terminology is strong. The "calf" evidence is thinner (mostly onomastic) but consistent. I find the legal reading of *mdd* persuasive; the animal terms as subservience markers are probable but not certain.
