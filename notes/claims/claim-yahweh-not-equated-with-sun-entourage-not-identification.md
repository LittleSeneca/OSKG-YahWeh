# day-ygc-6.2: Yahweh was NOT equated with the sun; the sun was part of Yahweh's entourage

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 6 — Yahweh and the Astral Deities]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 6

## The Claim

Despite a growing body of scholarship arguing for solar Yahwism, "it is clear that Yahweh is not here being seen as the sun, but rather as one greater than, and transcending, the sun" (p. 161). The sun was part of Yahweh's entourage (the host of heaven = sons of God), not Yahweh himself.

## Evidence

1. **East-west temple orientation:** Non-solar ANE temples also had this. The Solomonic temple was "completely covered over, which is not suggestive of a solar temple" (p. 158). Van Dyke Parunak's astronomical calculations: the sun did NOT penetrate the Holy of Holies at equinoxes
2. **1 Kgs 8:12 (LXX):** "The Lord has set the sun in the heavens, but he has said that he would dwell in thick darkness." Yahweh is CONTRASTED with the sun, not identified with it. "Thick darkness" (*ʻărāpel*) is storm-god imagery (clouds), not solar (p. 158-159)
3. **Sun cult in temple (2 Kgs 23:5, 11; Ezek 8:16):** The sun was worshipped alongside moon and host of heaven. "Hardly anyone seriously believes that Yahweh was equated with them." The host of heaven = Yahweh's heavenly court (sons of God). The sun "most naturally belonged in the same category" — subordinate, part of the entourage (p. 159)
4. **Ps 84:12 (ET 11):** "The Lord is a sun (*šemeš*) and a shield." But *šemeš* can also mean "rampart" (attested in Isa 54:12). The parallelism with "shield" (*māgēn*) supports "rampart," not "sun." Elsewhere "shield" parallels "fortress" (*meṣûdâ*) and "stronghold" (*miśgāb*) — never "sun" (p. 159-160)
5. **Theophany language (*zrḥ* and *ypʻ*):** Used of Yahweh at Sinai — but Sinai theophany is lightning/thunder, not solar (Exod 19:16). Isa 60:1-2 uses *zrḥ* of Yahweh but vv. 19-20 explicitly say the sun will no longer be needed — Yahweh transcends the sun (p. 160-161)
6. **No Yahweh-Šemeš theophoric names:** "If Yahweh had been equated with the sun, it is surprising that no Hebrew theophoric names combining Yahweh and Šemeš are attested" — unlike Yahweh-Baal (Bealiah, Yehobaal) and Yahweh-El (Elijah, Joel) compounds (p. 161)
7. **J.G. Taylor's book-length argument:** Day dismisses most of Taylor's arguments as "very weak." Examples: (a) Taylor claims Josh 10:12 implies sun-Yahweh equation because the Deuteronomistic framework says Joshua "spoke to the Lord" — but Joshua addressed the moon too; (b) Hezekiah's sun miracle implies solar equation — unlikely given Deuteronomistic esteem for Hezekiah; (c) "Lord of hosts" implies equation with chief member of host (sun) — "by no means obvious" (p. 161-162)
8. **Taanach cult stand:** Taylor's identification of the third tier (cherubim) as Yahweh's throne is "not certain" — cherubim are elsewhere guardians, not necessarily a throne (p. 162)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** Day makes a strong negative case (Yahweh was NOT the sun), but the positive case for sun AS entourage (subordinate, not equated) relies on the "host of heaven = sons of God" framework which he established in Chapter 1.

## Stakes

If Yahweh WAS identified with the sun, then solar monotheism (like Akhenaten's Aten) rather than Canaanite polytheism would be the relevant comparative framework. Day's position preserves the Canaanite paradigm while acknowledging genuine solar influence.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Morton Smith, H.-P. Stähli, H. Niehr, O. Keel & C. Uehlinger, E. Lipinski, and especially J.G. Taylor (*Yahweh and the Sun*, 1993). Mark Smith argues for solar INFLUENCE without full equation.

**Alternative reading:** Yahweh was identified with or heavily influenced by the sun deity — solar language, temple orientation, and theophany imagery all point to a solar Yahwism, especially in the Jerusalem temple cult.

## Assessment

Day's strongest point is the absence of Yahweh-Šemeš names. If people equated Yahweh with the sun, why did no one name their child "Yahweh-is-sun" or "Sun-of-Yahweh"? The presence of Yahweh-Baal names proves the principle. The "rampart" reading of Ps 84:12 is philologically defensible but not certain. The overall case is solid: the sun was worshipped IN Yahweh's temple as part of his court, not AS Yahweh.
