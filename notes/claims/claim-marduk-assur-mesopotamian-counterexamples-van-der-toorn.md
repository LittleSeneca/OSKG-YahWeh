# fleming-ybi-7.3: Marduk and Assur disprove van der Toorn's objection — local gods CAN become great gods

**Source:** [[Fleming — Ch 7 — The Early Character of the God Yahweh]] — Daniel Fleming, *Yahweh Before Israel* (2021), Chapter 7

## The Claim

"The most successful of great gods from Mesopotamia counter the objection that a deity rooted in identification with a limited people could not emerge as a major god. The actual histories of gods like Marduk and Assur demonstrate otherwise" (p. 265).

## Evidence

- Marduk: originally the god of Babylon — a single city — yet became "one of the great gods of ancient Mesopotamia" with the rise of the Old Babylonian kingdom (p. 256)
- Assur: god of the city Assur, sharing its name. "Deity and city share the name" (p. 257). "Already in the earliest second millennium... Assur was still just an important merchant town and its god a divine embodiment of its population" (p. 257)
- Itur-Mer at Mari: a god whose name takes the form of a full personal name, yet was "king of Mari" and presided over oaths — "this does not represent a type that prevented such prominence" (p. 256)
- Van der Toorn (1999: 914) had argued a major deity couldn't originate as a deified ancestor because "their worship tends to remain local." Fleming counters: Marduk, Assur, and Itur-Mer all began locally and became major
- "With growth of social scale and developing political structures it is natural for a god identified with a particular people to be transformed in the process" (p. 265)

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The Mesopotamian counter-examples are well-documented and effectively refute van der Toorn's objection. Fleming doesn't need to prove Yahweh followed the same trajectory — only that the trajectory is possible.

## Stakes

This clears a major objection to Fleming's model. If Marduk could go from city-god to pantheon-head, Yahweh could go from people-god to sole God.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Van der Toorn (1999) — but his objection was specifically about DEIFIED ANCESTORS, not about gods identified with peoples. Fleming somewhat blurs this distinction.

**Alternative reading:** Marduk and Assur became great through imperial conquest, not through inherent qualities. Yahweh had no empire — the comparison may not transfer.

## Assessment

The Marduk/Assur analogies are effective against van der Toorn's specific objection, but they don't prove Fleming's positive model. They show that local gods CAN become great — not that Yahweh DID follow this path. The objection is cleared; the positive case still needs to be made.
