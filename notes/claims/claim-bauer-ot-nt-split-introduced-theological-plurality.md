# schmid-ht-2-4.7: Bauer's OT/NT split introduced theological plurality into the concept of theology

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§2-4 — Concept of Theology from Plato to Gabler]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §4

## The Claim

Georg Lorenz Bauer's *Theologie des A.T.* (1796) is "usually seen as the first standalone representative of the new subdiscipline." This sundering "results in the first conception of theology that contains a notion of plurality. The Bible does not have a single theology, but...at least two theologies." After this, "no fundamental obstacles stood in the way of the further pluralization of theology that unfolded over the course of the twentieth and the beginning of the twenty-first centuries."

## Evidence

- Bauer 1796: first independent OT theology
- Subsequent separate treatments: Gramberg, Bruno Bauer, Hävernick, Hitzig, Kayser, Dillmann
- De Wette's *Biblische Dogmatik* (1813) and Vatke's *Die biblische Theologie* (1835) still attempted whole-Bible approaches but through historical-developmental frameworks
- The Old and New Testaments "do not even fit together in terms of a progressive linear sequence"

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** The historical facts are undisputed. The interpretive claim (that this opened the door to theological pluralism) is a logical inference: if the Bible has TWO theologies, why not more?

## Stakes

This is the crucial moment in Schmid's genealogy. Once "theology" can be plural, the door opens to "theologies of the Hebrew Bible" — the position Schmid partially endorses but partially resists. His book is a theology (singular) of the HB, but one that acknowledges internal plurality. The tension between singular and plural is baked into the discipline's history.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Minimal disagreement about the history. The debate is whether theological plurality is a problem to be solved or a reality to be embraced.

**Alternative reading:** The recognition of theological plurality is a feature, not a bug. It accurately reflects the HB's internal diversity and prevents the imposition of artificial unity.

## Assessment

Schmid's genealogy of theological pluralism is compelling. The move from one Bible → two Testaments → two theologies → many theologies is a logical unfolding of implications already present in Gabler and Bauer. Schmid's own position (theology in the singular, but with acknowledged internal plurality) tries to hold the tension rather than resolve it. Whether that's coherent or evasive depends on the execution.
