# schmid-ht-6-9.4: Von Rad's Old Testament Theology was epoch-making but genre-confused — it collapsed literary history into theology

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§6-9 — Jewish Reception to Implicit Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §8

## The Claim

Von Rad's decision to present OT theology as "retelling" was genuinely innovative, grounded in form criticism's discovery of "ancient creedal formulas." But his distinction between "the world of Israel's faith" (history of religion) and "Israel's confession of faith" (theology) was untenable. Carl A. Keller: "Von Rad's book is basically not a theology, but rather an introduction." Schmid agrees: von Rad's work "is many things, but it is not true to its genre."

## Evidence

- Von Rad's program: theology = "Israel's own explicit assertions about Yahweh" — specifically as "a continuing divine activity in history"
- Friedrich Baumgärtel's critique: the divergence between historical reconstruction and confessional portrayal is "a pseudo problem" — "the 'confessional portrayal of history' that comes from faith is a part of the religious history of Israel and as such can be investigated with the current historical-critical research methodology"
- Keller: von Rad's book is "not a theology, but rather an introduction"
- Von Rad HIMSELF admitted: "the surprising convergence — indeed the mutual intersection — which has come about during the last twenty or thirty years between introductory studies and Biblical theology"
- Von Rad ALSO acknowledged (outside his *Theology*) that the OT "contains not only one, but a number of theologies that diverge greatly"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Von Rad's genre confusion is widely acknowledged. Schmid's analysis is sharp and well-supported by both von Rad's own statements and contemporary critics.

## Stakes

Von Rad is the giant whose shoulders (or shadow) all subsequent OT theologies stand in. By showing that von Rad's project was conceptually unstable, Schmid clears space for his own alternative. But von Rad's instinct — that the HB's narrative character demands narrative theological treatment — remains powerful. Schmid's alternative (historical-descriptive theology organized by canon, theme, and literary history) is more analytical but may lose the narrative power that made von Rad's work compelling.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Von Rad's defenders who argue that the genre ambiguity IS the point — that theology of the HB necessarily straddles history and faith. Canonical approach scholars (Childs, Seitz) who build on von Rad's instincts.

**Alternative reading:** Von Rad's genre confusion may be a feature, not a bug. The HB itself resists the history/theology distinction that Schmid wants to maintain. Von Rad's "retelling" approach may authentically reflect the HB's own character as narrative theology.

## Assessment

Schmid's critique of von Rad is fair and well-argued. Von Rad couldn't decide whether he was doing history or theology, so he did something in between and called it both. Schmid's solution — being clear that he's doing historical description — is more honest. But honesty may not be enough. Von Rad's work compelled because it took theological risks. Schmid's work may be more rigorous but less powerful.
