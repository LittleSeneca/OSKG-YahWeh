# schmid-ht-6-9.7: "Stereometric theology" — the HB conveys theological meaning through literary juxtaposition, not explicit statement

**Source:** [[Schmid — §§6-9 — Jewish Reception to Implicit Theology]] — Konrad Schmid, *A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible* (2019), §9

## The Claim

"Implicit theologies can become more explicit over time through the development of a text's redactional history. However, the explication can also be functionally transferred to the audience's imagination." The HB uses "stereometric reading": theological meanings emerge through the juxtaposition of texts in the reader's mind, not through explicit theological propositions.

## Evidence

- Jer 6:1 ("Flee for safety, Benjaminites!") — political advice with no theological interpretation
- Jer 4:6-7 — reuses Jer 6:1's language but explicitly attributes the evil to God: "I am bringing evil from the north"
- Jer 1:13-14 — the "boiling pot" vision preemptively synthesizes Jer 4 and 6 but remains silent on whether God himself acts. The theological meaning emerges through the reader's synthesis of all three texts.
- Proverbs 11:25-27 — parallelismus membrorum evokes theological dimensions without naming God. "A generous person will be enriched" — the divine passive implies God's action but doesn't state it. The parallelism "swings" the theological dimension between cola.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The Jeremiah example is well-chosen — it genuinely shows texts in theological dialogue without any explicit theological proposition. The Proverbs example is more speculative but plausible. The concept of "stereometric" reading is Schmid's most original contribution to theological method.

## Stakes

If Schmid is right that the HB's theology is fundamentally stereometric — conveyed through literary juxtaposition rather than explicit doctrine — then traditional theologies that abstract "teachings" from the texts are methodologically wrong. You can't extract "biblical teaching on X" because the HB doesn't teach that way. It teaches by making you hold multiple texts in tension. This has massive implications for how the HB can be used in theological argument.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Systematic theologians who extract propositional content from biblical texts. Canonical approach scholars who might argue the stereometric effect IS the canon's theological strategy (converging with Schmid rather than contradicting him).

**Alternative reading:** The stereometric effect may be an artifact of the canon's historical formation rather than a deliberate theological strategy. The juxtaposition of Jer 1, 4, and 6 may reflect editorial accident, not theological intention.

## Assessment

This is the most exciting idea in Part B. "Stereometric theology" captures something real about how the HB works: meaning emerges from the space BETWEEN texts, not from individual texts in isolation. The Jeremiah example is compelling because it shows theological development through redaction without ever stating a theological proposition. The Proverbs example is more debatable (is the theological dimension really "stereometric" or just implicit?) but the concept is productive. If Schmid can sustain this reading across the whole canon, he's made a genuine methodological contribution.
