# day-ygc-1.4: The Garden of Eden story derives from El's dwelling place

**Source:** [[Day — Chapter 1 — Yahweh and El]] — John Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000), Chapter 1: Yahweh and El

## The Claim

"The thesis I shall defend... is that the garden of Eden — or garden of God, as it is sometimes called — derives from the dwelling place of El" (p. 33). El dwelt at the source of the rivers in Armenia, and this is exactly where Genesis places Eden.

## Evidence

### 1. Geographic Precision — Armenia, Not Dilmun
- Gen 2:10-14 places Eden at the source of the Tigris and Euphrates — this points to Armenia, not the Persian Gulf (where Sumerian Dilmun was located)
- Albright: Hebrew *rāšîm* "refers solely to the headwaters of a river" — Eden is at the rivers' source, not their mouth (p. 30-31)

### 2. Mountain Location
- Ezek 28:14, 16 places Eden on a mountain — fits Armenia (mountainous), not the Persian Gulf (flat)
- El characteristically dwells on a mountain in Ugaritic tradition

### 3. Hittite-Canaanite Elkunirsa Myth
- El (= Elkunirsa, "El, creator of the earth") "lives in a tent at the source of the Mala river (Euphrates)" — which "must also be in Armenia, since El characteristically dwells on a mountain" (p. 31-32)
- This is direct textual evidence for El at the Euphrates headwaters, the same location as Genesis's Eden

### 4. Flood Story Connection
- Noah's Ark landed on Ararat (Urartu/Armenia, Gen 8:4)
- In the Mesopotamian flood story, Utnapishtim was translated to Paradise at *pî nārāti* "the mouth/source of the rivers" — naturally the Armenian source, not the Gulf mouth (p. 31)

### 5. Eden-El Theme Convergence
- Job 15:7-8: The first man's dwelling is "in the council of God" — the divine council meets on El's mountain at Ugarit
- Wisdom emphasis in Eden (Gen 3:6, 22; Ezek 28:12, 17) connects to El, who was particularly noted for wisdom
- Eden associated with creation — consonant with El as creator god
- Gihon (one of Eden's rivers) shares its name with Jerusalem's spring, associated with El/Elyon in Ps 46:5 (ET 4)

### 6. Ezekiel 28 Confirms El Traditions
Both oracles (vv. 2-10 and vv. 12-19) reflect El traditions:
- God called *ʼel* (rare in Ezekiel — only here and Ezek 10:5)
- "Seat of God in the heart of the seas" reflects El's dwelling at the source of rivers/deeps (Ugaritic *mtb ʼil*)
- Emphasis on divine wisdom mirrors El
- Ezek 28:12-19 is a variant of the Eden story (cherub, casting out, Eden)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The geographic argument (rivers + mountain = Armenia) is strong and the Elkunirsa myth is direct evidence. But multiple sacred mountains competed in tradition (Hermon, Lebanon, Amanus — see Day's footnote on pp. 32-33).

## Stakes

The Eden story is not a Hebrew original — it's a transformed Canaanite myth about El's cosmic mountain. This means one of the most foundational biblical narratives (creation, fall, paradise) has a pre-Israelite Canaanite source. Theologically significant for doctrines of revelation and biblical uniqueness.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Speiser (locating Eden in the Persian Gulf, following Sumerian Dilmun tradition). Westermann (thinks the Yahwist had no particular location in mind — Day says the "precise geographical indications" refute this). For competing locations of El's mountain: Pope (Khirbet Afqa), Cross (Mt Amanus), Naccache (Mt Lebanon).

**Alternative reading:** The Eden story could draw from MULTIPLE ANE paradise traditions (Sumerian Dilmun, Canaanite El's mountain, Jerusalem temple symbolism) without being reducible to any single one. Day's derivation is plausible but too monocausal.

## Assessment

Day makes a strong cumulative case. The convergence of geography (Armenia = rivers + mountain), the Elkunirsa myth (direct textual evidence), and the El-specific themes (wisdom, divine council) is compelling. But the confidence should be slightly tempered — paradise myths were widespread, and Genesis may fuse multiple sources. The Ezekiel 28 connection to El is the most persuasive single link.
