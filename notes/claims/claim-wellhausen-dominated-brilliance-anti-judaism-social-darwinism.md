# lewis-ocg-2.2: Wellhausen's synthesis was philosophically loaded with anti-Judaism and social Darwinism

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 2 — History of Scholarship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 2

## The Claim

Wellhausen's reconstruction was "unilinear and evolutionary in nature, progressing in positive fashion from primitive, animistic origins to the ethical monotheism of the prophets" — but then "once it reached such heights, it would lose its ability to grow and became a static, mechanical abstraction in post-exilic Judaism." Lewis emphasizes that "it is no mere coincidence that 1859 saw the publication of Charles Darwin's *The Origin of Species*" and quotes Geller: Wellhausen refracted his view through "the optimistic evolutionary lenses of social Darwinism."

## Evidence

- de Wette's caricature of "Judaism" as "the unfortunate reinterpretation of Hebraism"
- Vatke's Hegelian dialectic (thesis → antithesis → synthesis mapped onto pre-prophetic → prophetic → post-prophetic)
- Wellhausen's famous line: "One God one sanctuary, that is the idea"
- Wellhausen's description of post-exilic Judaism: "the warm pulse of life no longer throbbed in it... the soul was fled, the shell remained"

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** VERY HIGH that Wellhausen's work was philosophically shaped. HIGH that anti-Judaism was a significant factor. The anti-Jewish element has been documented extensively (Levenson, Heschel, Geller).

## Stakes

If Wellhausen's framework is contaminated by anti-Judaism, the entire documentary hypothesis needs reexamination — not necessarily rejection, but cleansing of its value judgments. Many scholars accept the literary sources (J, E, D, P) while rejecting the evolutionary schema.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Morgan and Barton (1988) argue Wellhausen worked in the spirit of "research free of presuppositions." More conservative scholars might dispute the anti-Judaism charge as anachronistic.

**Alternative reading:** Wellhausen's literary observations can be separated from his philosophical framework. The four-source model is an empirical hypothesis; the evolutionary valuation is a separate ideological layer.

## Assessment

Lewis is right. Wellhausen's anti-Judaism isn't subtle. The question is whether it invalidates his literary analysis or just his historical reconstruction. Most critical scholars today accept the four-source model while rejecting his valuation of post-exilic Judaism as "lifeless."
