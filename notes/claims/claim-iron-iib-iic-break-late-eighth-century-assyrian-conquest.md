# keel-ggi-1.5: The standard archaeological periodization is wrong — Iron IIB/C break at late 8th century (Assyrian conquest), not c. 850

**Source:** [[Keel Uehlinger — Ch I — Starting Point and Points of Departure]] — Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God in Ancient Israel* (1998), Chapter I

## The Claim

"If one considers the artistic works, as will be done here, it will seem not only plausible but unavoidable that the break between Iron Age IIB and C must be dated to the second half of the eighth century, when Palestine came increasingly under the direct political influence of the great Assyrian superpower." They critique H. Weippert's periodization, which ends Iron IIB at c. 850 (end of Omri dynasty), based solely on architectural evidence.

## Evidence

- H. Weippert's 1988 Handbuch uses ONLY architectural evidence, producing an Iron IIB (c. 925-850) that leaves almost no small artifacts
- Assyrian conquest introduced massive cultural changes: pottery types shift, settlement patterns change, iconography transforms
- Their alternative: Iron IIB = 925-700 (Phoenician/Egyptian orientation), Iron IIC = 700-600 (Assyrian/Aramean influence)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The argument from iconographic evidence makes intuitive sense but depends on data presented in chapters VII-VIII.

## Stakes

If they're right, then religious developments of the 8th century must be understood against an ASSYRIAN backdrop, not just internal Israelite politics.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** H. Weippert explicitly. The traditional scheme (Iron IIA = United Monarchy, IIB = Divided Monarchy, IIC = Assyrian period) has the advantage of aligning with the biblical narrative.
