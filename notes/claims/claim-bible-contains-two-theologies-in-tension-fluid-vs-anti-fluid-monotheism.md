# sommer-bog-4-6.1: The Bible contains two theologies in permanent tension — fluid and anti-fluid monotheism

**Source:** [[Sommer — Chapters 4-6 + Appendix]] — Benjamin Sommer, *The Bodies of God* (2009), Chapters 4-6 + Appendix

## The Claim

The Bible preserves two competing theologies that exist in unresolved tension, not a single consistent doctrine of God:

1. **Fluid monotheism** (J, E, Psalms): One God, many bodies. God walks, eats, fights, can be in multiple places at once — the Tent of Meeting, the Ark, heaven, and earthly manifestations simultaneously. This is the ANE inheritance.

2. **Anti-fluid monotheism** (P, D, later tradition): One God, no body (or only the *kavod*/*shem* as mediated presence). God transcends space and is accessed through hypostases — Glory, Name, Wisdom — rather than direct embodiment. This is the theological innovation — NOT monotheism itself, but the disembodiment of the one God.

The resolution: Neither theology won completely. Judaism developed toward anti-fluid monotheism (Maimonides' "no body, no form") while preserving fluidity underground in mysticism (kabbalah, the *sefirot*). Christianity split the difference — God the Father is transcendent (anti-fluid); God the Son is embodied (fluid). The canon deliberately preserved BOTH traditions, creating an anthology that preserves the DEBATE rather than delivering a single resolution.

## Evidence

**Fluid theology in the canon:**
- The Tent of Meeting (Exodus 33): Moses speaks with Yhwh "face to face, as a man speaks with his friend" — a localized divine body outside the camp
- The Ark as throne/footstool for a divine body
- J/E texts where Yhwh walks in the garden, eats with Abraham, wrestles with Jacob
- Psalms that speak of God's body parts (hand, arm, face, mouth)

**Anti-fluid theology in the canon:**
- 1 Kings 8 (Solomon's prayer): "The heavens and the highest heaven cannot contain You; how much less this house" — D theology, God does NOT dwell in the Temple
- P's *kavod* theology: God's presence is fire/cloud, not a body
- D's *shem* theology: God dwells in heaven; only his Name is in the Temple
- Genesis 1:26 — divine image without divine embodiment

**Canonical preservation of both:**
- The Pentateuch preserves J/E (fluid) AND P/D (anti-fluid) side by side
- The Psalms move between fluid and anti-fluid language in the same poem
- The editorial process did not harmonize the two traditions

**Later developments:**
- Rabbinic Judaism: increasingly anti-fluid (Maimonides' incorporeality principle)
- Kabbalah: rediscovery of fluidity via the *sefirot*
- Christianity: Trinity as fluid monotheism (three persons), incarnation as divine embodiment

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** The textual evidence for two competing theologies within the canon is clear and Sommer documents both traditions extensively.

## Stakes

This claim transforms how we read the Bible. Instead of trying to harmonize contradictory portraits of God into a single systematic theology, Sommer argues the contradictions ARE the theology. The Bible is not a book of answers about God's nature — it's a book that preserves an unresolved debate about God's nature. This makes the canon more interesting, not less authoritative: it shows the biblical writers wrestling with the same tensions later theologians would systematize.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Systematic theologians in both Jewish and Christian traditions who read the canon as doctrinally unified. Kaufmann would reject the claim that the Bible contains contradictory theologies — for Kaufmann, the non-mythological, non-pagan character of Israelite religion is consistent throughout.

**Alternative reading:** The standard theological harmonization reads the fluid texts as anthropomorphic metaphor, not genuine embodiment. The anti-fluid texts represent the "mature" theology toward which the canon was developing. Sommer rejects this as anachronistic — reading later theological preferences back into earlier texts.

## Assessment

This is Sommer's most important contribution to the Truth Project. If the canon preserves a debate rather than a resolution, then the entire project of tracing "the evolution of monotheism" is asking the wrong question. The evolution happened WITHIN the canon — and the canon preserves all stages of it simultaneously. The question isn't "when did Israel become monotheistic?" but "what were the competing versions of monotheism that the canon refused to choose between?"
