# dever-dghw-3.1: The biblical texts are late, elitist, and theologically biased — unreliable for reconstructing folk religion

**Source:** [[Dever — Chapter III — Sources and Methods]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife?* (2005), Chapter III

## The Claim

The textual sources for Israelite religion are all late (8th century BCE at earliest for composition, heavily edited through the post-exilic period), produced by a tiny literate elite (less than 1% of the population), and shaped by theological agendas that make them unreliable as direct historical sources. The Hebrew Bible is not history in the modern sense — it is "historicized fiction": stories based on some genuine historical events but always told in such a way as to advance the ideological agenda of the writers. For reconstructing what ordinary Israelites actually practiced, the Bible is a secondary source at best — and a misleading one at worst.

## Evidence

**The sources and their problems:**

| Source | Dating | Problem for Folk Religion |
|--------|--------|--------------------------|
| **J** (Yahwist) | Originally 10th c., now 9th-8th | "If the date of J were indeed post-exilic... this source would tell us nothing whatsoever about supposed 'ancestral religion.'" |
| **E** (Elohist) | 8th century, northern | Prophetic interests; tells us about prophetic theology, not popular practice |
| **P** (Priestly) | Late monarchy or post-exilic | "Irrelevant for folk religion during the monarchy." |
| **Dtr** (Deuteronomistic History) | 7th-century core, exile-era editing | "A call to repentance... a demand for the wayward people of Israel to restore the pure, monotheistic ideals." — precisely the agenda that distorts the picture of actual practice |

**On the prophetic books:**
> "It would not be misleading to say that the real religions of ancient Israel consisted precisely of all the things that the prophets condemned, all of them incidentally illustrated by archaeological discoveries."

**On the Bible's historical reliability:**
> "The Hebrew Bible is not history in the modern sense... I shall often regard the Hebrew Bible as 'historicized fiction' — stories that are based on some genuine historical events, but always told in such a way as to advance the ideological agenda of the writers."

**On the writers:**
> "They were mostly elites, literati attached to court and priestly circles in Jerusalem. Taken together, they can hardly have constituted more than a fraction of one percent of the population."

**Chronological gap:** The earliest biblical compositions (8th century) postdate the events they describe by centuries. The final editing (post-exilic, 5th-4th centuries) postdates the monarchy by centuries. The Bible is a late retrospection, not a contemporary record.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH on the late dating (critical consensus). HIGH on the elitist character of the texts. MEDIUM on "historicized fiction" as the right characterization (some texts contain more reliable historical memory than this phrase suggests).

## Stakes

If the Bible is a late, elite, ideologically-driven text, then using it as the primary source for Israelite religion is like reconstructing medieval European religion from Thomas Aquinas alone. You would miss the saints' cults, the pilgrimage sites, the folk magic, the women's piety — everything that most people actually did. This is Dever's fundamental objection to the entire text-based scholarly tradition.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who maintain the historical reliability of the biblical narratives (more conservative positions) or who argue that even late, edited texts can preserve accurate historical memory (more moderate positions). Also scholars like Kaufmann who date the sources much earlier.

**Alternative reading:** The Bible's theological agenda does not make it useless for historical reconstruction — it makes it a source that must be read critically, like all ancient texts. And the prophetic condemnations, precisely because they attack popular practice, are excellent evidence FOR what people were actually doing.
