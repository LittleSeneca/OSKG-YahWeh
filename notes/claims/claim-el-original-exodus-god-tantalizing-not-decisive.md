# lewis-ocg-4.8: El as Original Exodus God — Tantalizing, Not Decisive

**Source:** [[Lewis — Chapter 4 — El Worship]] — Theodore J. Lewis, *The Origin and Character of God* (2020), Chapter 4

## The Claim

"What should we make of such tantalizing evidence? Its fragmentary character recommends caution. The fusion of earlier El traditions with those of Yahweh makes it likely that El's divine warrior motifs are, if one may use a linguistic term, back-formations."

## Evidence

- Numbers 23:22 and 24:8 read "El brought him out of Egypt" (ʾēl môṣîʾām mimmiṣrāyîm) with bull imagery suggesting invincibility in battle
- Mettinger, Toews, Levine (the "El repertoire"), and Wyatt have all argued for El as the original Exodus deity
- Habakkuk 3:3 mentions "Eloah marching forth from Teman" in a militaristic passage
- Counter-arguments: Exodus 32 (golden calf) requires textual emendation to read "El" for "these"; Psalm 106:21 is in a thoroughly Yahwistic context; Numbers 23-24 already contains Yahweh alongside El
- Lewis suggests El's warrior motifs may be "back-formations" — created retroactively when El and Yahweh were identified

## Confidence

**Rating:** low-medium

**Rationale:** The evidence exists but is too fragmentary to build a strong case on. Lewis is appropriately cautious.

## Stakes

Whether the Exodus was originally an El tradition later appropriated by Yahweh. If so, the foundational event of Israelite religion belongs to El, not Yahweh. This would be a major revision. If not, Yahweh brought the Exodus with him when he arrived from the south.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Most scholars see the Exodus as a Yahweh tradition. Smith (2001b) sees Yahweh's original profile as a warrior-storm god from the south — exactly the kind of deity who would lead a military liberation. Römer likewise places Yahweh's origin in the southern wilderness.

**Alternative reading:** Lewis's suggestion that El's warrior motifs are "back-formations" — created retroactively when El and Yahweh were identified — is the most parsimonious explanation. If El had strong warrior traditions, he would have become the national deity.

## Assessment

Lewis is right to be cautious. The El-Exodus connection is a minority position and the evidence is thin. Lewis's more important contribution is explaining WHY it's unlikely: if El was already the warrior-liberator god, what need was there for Yahweh? The sociological explanation (family deity → national deity, with different functional profiles) makes more sense.
