# albertz-hir-2.3-2.4.2: The name "Israel" proves the tribal alliance originally worshipped El, not Yahweh

**Source:** [[Albertz Vol1 — §2.3-2.4 — Tribal Alliance and Family Piety]] — Rainer Albertz, *A History of Israelite Religion in the Old Testament Period, Volume I* (1994), §2.3

## The Claim

"The conclusion is unavoidable that the element 'el in Israel originally points to a stage in its religious history in which El and not yet Yahweh was the god of the tribal alliance of Israel." The Merenptah stele (1219 BCE) mentions "Israel" before the Exodus group could have arrived — "the Egyptian mention is probably to be related to a still pre-Yahwistic Israel." Yahweh was brought by the Exodus group in a "second phase" and was adopted because he was a BETTER symbol of opposition to domination than El.

## Evidence

- The name "Israel" (yiśrā'ēl) = "El rules" or "may God show himself as ruler" — formed with the divine name El, not Yahweh
- Gen 33:20: Jacob names an altar "El, the god of Israel" ('ēl 'ĕlōhê yiśrā'ēl) — 'ēl is clearly a divine name, not a generic term
- The Merenptah stele (1219 BCE) attests "Israel" as a people in Canaan before the Exodus group's likely arrival
- The Rachel tribes (Manasseh, Ephraim, Benjamin) — those associated with the Exodus group — represent a later layer of the alliance compared to the Leah tribes
- Yahweh was "almost predestined" to become the alliance's god: as a solitary southern god not integrated into the Canaanite pantheon, who proved his divinity in liberation from state oppression, "bound unilaterally and exclusively to a group from the lower classes"
- "Nowhere in the Old Testament is there any polemic against the god El" — the fusion was so smooth it left no trace of conflict

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** The name "Israel" and Gen 33:20 are intriguing but not definitive. The Merenptah stele's "Israel" could theoretically be a different group. The argument from the absence of anti-El polemic is an argument from silence.

## Stakes

This is a major claim: Israel's GOD was originally El, not Yahweh. Yahweh was a latecomer adopted for political-ideological reasons. If true, this is a more radical version of the "Yahweh was not originally Israel's god" thesis than even Smith or Römer present — they tend to see Yahweh present from early on, just as one god among many. Albertz has El as the original deity of the entity called "Israel."

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue the name "Israel" need not contain the divine name El specifically (it could be generic "god"). Those who see Yahweh present in the earliest Israel. Mark Smith argues Yahweh absorbed El's characteristics but doesn't claim El was worshipped by "Israel" before Yahweh. Frank Moore Cross argued Yahweh was originally an epithet of El, meaning they were never separate deities.

**Alternative reading:** The El-worshipping stage could predate the formation of "Israel" as a distinct entity — "Israel" may have formed around Yahweh from the beginning, with the name being older than the entity. Or El and Yahweh may always have been identified in early Israel (as Cross argued: Yahweh was originally an epithet of El).

## Assessment

This is Albertz's most original and most debatable claim. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. The Merenptah stele dating is a real problem for a purely Exodus-based origin of Israel. And the absence of anti-El polemic IS striking — contrast the vehement anti-Baal polemic throughout the OT. But the counter-argument (Cross's position that Yahweh was an El-epithet from the start) explains the same data without requiring a separate El-worshipping phase. I'd rate this as plausible but underdetermined by the evidence.
