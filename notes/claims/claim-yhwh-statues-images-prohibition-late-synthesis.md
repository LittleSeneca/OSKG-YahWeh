# romer-inv-8-9.1: Yhwh was represented by statues and images; the prohibition was late

**Source:** [[Romer — Chapters 8-9 — Statue and Asherah]] — Thomas Römer, *The Invention of God* (2015), Chapters 8-9 synthesis

## The Claim

Contrary to the traditional view that Israelite religion was aniconic from the beginning (no images of God), Yhwh WAS represented by statues in pre-exilic Judah. Standing stones (*maṣṣēbôt*) functioned as representations of Yhwh, seals depict an enthroned god who is probably Yhwh, and a Persian-era coin shows Yhwh as a sky god on a winged wheel. The prohibition on images was a LATE innovation — probably 6th century — and was then projected backward as if it had always been the rule. The invisible, transcendent God of Jewish and Christian theology is a late theological construction.

## Evidence

**Standing stones as Yhwh representations:**
- *Maṣṣēbôt* are frequently mentioned in the Bible and found archaeologically — at Mari (2nd millennium), at Arad, and at other Israelite sites
- Four functions: funerary markers, commemorative steles, contract witnesses, and **divine representations**
- Genesis 28: Jacob anoints a *masseba* at Bethel and calls it "a house of God" (*bêt 'ĕlōhîm*). "The masseba was a way of representing the god Yhwh."
- The Arad temple had two steles — "representing Yhwh and another deity"
- "The presence of standing stones is no evidence at all in favor of assuming that there was an aniconic cult of Yhwh."
- Deuteronomy 16:22 and Leviticus 26:1 prohibit BOTH statues AND standing stones — proving they were considered equivalent
- At Mari, betyles and statues COEXIST. "It would be a mistake to oppose aniconism and iconism sharply."

**Seals and coins depicting Yhwh:**
- 7th-century Hebrew seal: a god seated on a throne flanked by two trees of life
- Two 7th-century seals with lunar attributes may represent Yhwh — the Yahwistic names of their owners support this
- Persian-era coin (c. 380 BCE) from Judah: a deity seated on a winged wheel — almost certainly Yhwh as sky god, drawing on Mesopotamian models (Assur)
- "Yhwh was represented by images."

**The prohibition on images was LATE:**
- The Decalogue's second commandment dates to the 6th century at the earliest
- Older texts show no concern about divine images (ark, cherubim throne, temple)
- The prohibition was formulated during the Exile, when contact with Babylonian idolatry made image-worship seem like "paganism"
- It was PROJECTED BACKWARD — the golden calf story (Exodus 32) was written to make image-worship seem like primordial sin

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** HIGH that steles represented Yhwh (Arad evidence, prohibition texts). MEDIUM-HIGH that seals depict Yhwh (plausible but unproven without inscriptions). HIGH that image prohibition was late (scholarly consensus).

## Stakes

The second commandment — "you shall not make for yourself a graven image" — was not given to Moses on Sinai. It was invented by 6th-century priests who wanted to distinguish Yahwism from Babylonian religion. Yhwh was originally a god you could SEE — on seals, on coins, as a standing stone, as a bull. The invisible, transcendent God of Jewish and Christian theology is a LATE theological construction.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Conservatives who date the Decalogue to Moses. Scholars who argue Israelite aniconism was genuinely ancient (Mettinger). Those who say the seals depict other deities, not Yhwh.

**Alternative reading:** The aniconic tradition may be older than Römer allows — the ark and cherubim throne represent "empty space" where the deity dwells, which IS a form of aniconism. Standing stones could be memorials, not divine images.

## Assessment

Römer is persuasive on the standing stones (the prohibition texts lumping *massebot* with idols is the strongest evidence). The seals and coins are more debatable. But the overall trajectory — from represented to unrepresentable God — fits the larger pattern of convergence and differentiation. The invisible God is the product of making God different from other gods.
