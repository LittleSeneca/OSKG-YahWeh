# dever-dghw-intro.5: Dever directly criticizes Mark Smith for ignoring archaeological evidence

**Source:** [[Dever — Introduction]] — William Dever, *Did God Have a Wife? Archaeology and Folk Religion in Ancient Israel* (2005), Introduction

## The Claim

Smith's work is "not informed by any archaeological data that would change his almost exclusively text-based approach in any essential way." This is Smith's blind spot — and it leads him to wrong conclusions, particularly on the Asherah question. Despite the new archaeological evidence since 1990, Smith is still "not inclined to accept the likelihood that it names a goddess." Dever attributes Smith's resistance to psychology — Smith "rejects the observation of some critics that his interpretation is 'psychologically unprepared to deal with the opposite outlook.'"

## Evidence

**Dever's critique:**
"Despite his critique of the inadequacies of many other scholars and his acknowledgment that 'synthetic archaeological research has reached a new level of sophistication'... I regret to say that I find Smith's work is not informed by any archaeological data that would change his almost exclusively text-based approach in any essential way. Thus he says of the crucial question of understanding the term 'A/asherah' that despite the new evidence since 1990 he is still not inclined to accept the likelihood that it names a goddess."

**Dever on Smith's resistance:** He attributes Smith's position to psychology — Smith "rejects the observation of some critics that his interpretation is 'psychologically unprepared to deal with the opposite outlook,' countering tentatively with the claim that the 'Zeitgeist (world view) of our age psychologically preconditions advocates to desire a goddess in ancient Israel.'"

## Confidence

**Rating:** debatable

**Rationale:** HIGH that Dever genuinely disagrees with Smith on this. MEDIUM on whether Smith's position is psychologically motivated (Dever's claim) vs. methodologically motivated (Smith's claim).

## Stakes

This is the central scholarly disagreement in the field. Smith says the evidence is insufficient to prove Asherah was a goddess consort of Yahweh. Dever says the evidence is overwhelming and Smith is ignoring it. The reader has to decide who's right based on the evidence Dever presents in the rest of the book.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Smith himself (and scholars who follow his text-first methodology) would argue that Dever overstates what archaeology can prove — artifacts require interpretation, and the jump from "female figurines exist" to "Yahweh had a consort goddess" is not straightforward.

**Alternative reading:** Smith's caution may be methodologically sound — he's refusing to make claims the evidence doesn't support — rather than psychologically motivated. Dever's eagerness to find a goddess may itself be motivated by his desire to dethrone patriarchal monotheism.

## Assessment

Dever is a polemicist. When he says "the evidence is overwhelming," you need to ask: "Is it, or is he just saying it is?" The Asherah question cannot be settled by archaeology alone — the Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions are grammatically ambiguous, and the iconographic evidence can be read multiple ways. Dever's critique of Smith is valid insofar as Smith does underweight material evidence, but Dever's confidence may outrun what the evidence can actually support.
