# stav-god-5-8.2: God's masculinity was performed through phallic imagery — his 'right hand' and 'holy arm' are phallic proxies, his 'mighty deeds' are displays of masculine potency, and his 'glory' (kavod) connotes both luminous radiance and the bodily 'weight' of male honor.

**Source:** [[Stavrakopoulou — Part II — Genitals]] — Francesca Stavrakopoulou, *God: An Anatomy* (2021), Chapter 6

## The Claim

Stavrakopoulou argues that God's body "conforms to the gendered ideals of the ancient world: he is muscular, virile, physically powerful — and his genitals perform accordingly." God's masculinity is not metaphorical but is performed through phallic imagery embedded in biblical language.

## Evidence

- God's "right hand" and "holy arm" (Isaiah 52:10) are phallic proxies — the arm/hand as extension of male power
- God's "mighty deeds" are displays of masculine potency — strength, virility, the ability to act forcefully in the world
- God's "glory" (*kavod*) is both luminous radiance AND bodily substance — the same word used for the "weight" of male honor and physical presence
- The ANE context: male deities were depicted with exaggerated masculine features; power was gendered male

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium-high

**Rationale:** The ANE context supports reading power imagery as gendered; some specific interpretations (reading every "hand" reference as phallic) may be overread.

## Stakes

If God's power is performed through phallic imagery, then divine power IS masculine power. This means the masculinity of God is not incidental to his nature — it is constitutive of how his power operates. Feminist critiques of patriarchal religion find their foundation not in later interpretation but in the biblical texts themselves. God is not just called "he" by convention; his body performs masculinity.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Scholars who argue that ANE power imagery is not inherently phallic — the "right hand" and "arm" are general power symbols, not specifically genital. Feminist theologians who argue God's masculinity is a social construction that can be deconstructed.

**Alternative reading:** The "hand" and "arm" language is standard ANE royal and divine power imagery, not specifically phallic. Reading every power reference as genital is an over-interpretation.

## Assessment

Stavrakopoulou reads phallic symbolism where others might see general power imagery. The ANE context DOES gender power as male, but the specific claim that "right hand" is a phallic proxy rather than a general power metaphor is debatable. Her reading is provocative and not without insight, but it occasionally overreaches.
