# Tigay — Introduction

## The Problem: The Debate Has No External Controls

**The state of the question:** Two positions — both based entirely on the biblical texts.

1. **The critical consensus:** Israelites were polytheistic until shortly before or after the exile. The biblical writers admit polytheism was rampant; therefore monotheism was a late development.
2. **Kaufmann/Albright:** Monotheism was essential to Mosaic religion from the start. Polytheism was fetishistic (using figurines without genuine mythology), and the prophets exaggerated.

**The deadlock:** "Both sides agreed that literary texts such as those in the Bible cannot be used for historical research before the historian evaluates what they are reliable for, but so long as this evaluation was based on evidence from within the Bible itself, with no external controls, it could not escape its notorious subjectivity."

**Tigay's solution:** Use INSCRIPTIONS as external controls. Gather ALL personal names and religious references from pre-exilic Hebrew inscriptions and see what they indicate about actual religious practice.

**The origin story (from his Preface):** Visiting the Israel Museum in 1978, Tigay examined the catalogue of Hebrew seals. "I expected to find on the Hebrew seals numerous examples of pagan personal names which were not found in the Bible because of the kind of scribal revisions which had turned Eshbaal into Ishbosheth and Beel(=Baal)i ada into Eliada. But the index showed so few examples of such names that I was forced to wonder whether they had been as common as I had assumed."

**What previous scholarship had done:** "Only scattered details have been cited in such arguments, as their proponents found helpful. So far as I am aware, no attempt has been made to interpret either the pagan or the Yahwistic personal names in the light of the total onomastic picture from Israelite inscriptions."

**Confidence:** HIGH on the methodological observation that the debate lacked external controls. This is simply a fact about the state of the field in 1986.

**What's at stake:** If Tigay is right, the entire polytheism-to-monotheism narrative is built on literary evidence that is contradicted by the only objective evidence we have. The consensus has the Bible's editorial Tendenz backwards: the biblical writers weren't covering up polytheism but magnifying it to explain national catastrophe.

**Who disagrees:** The entire critical consensus. Smith's rebuttal of Tigay (in his Introduction to *The Early History of God*) is the most direct counter-argument. Smith says personal names are "notoriously difficult to assess" and gives counter-examples from Ugarit and Punic culture.

**My assessment:** Tigay's methodological observation — that the debate needs external evidence — is unassailable. Whether his data actually proves what he claims it proves is the question for Chapter I.
