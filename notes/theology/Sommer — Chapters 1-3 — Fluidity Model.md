# Sommer, The Bodies of God — Chapters 1-3

---

## Chapter 1: Fluidity in Mesopotamia and Canaan

### Claim: The ancient Near East had a "fluidity model" — gods had multiple bodies and overlapping selves
→ [[claim-ane-gods-fluid-selves-multiple-local-manifestations]] | **sommer-bog-1.1** | Confidence: high
  ANE gods had fluid selves with multiple local manifestations (Ishtar of Arbela/Nineveh, Baal of Saphon/Ugarit/Lebanon, Marduk's fifty names) — a single deity could exist simultaneously in several bodies with fragmented or overlapping selfhood. This model was present in Mesopotamia and Canaan but ABSENT from classical Greece.
  **See also:** [[claim-cult-statues-were-the-god-not-representations-mis-pi]] (sommer-bog-1.2) — cult statues WERE the god; [[claim-ane-fluidity-absent-greece-mono-poly-less-fundamental]] (sommer-bog-1.3) — Greece lacked fluidity entirely.

## Chapter 2: The Fluidity Model in Ancient Israel

### Claim: J and E sources preserve the fluidity model — Yhwh had multiple bodies and a fluid self
→ [[claim-je-sources-preserved-ane-fluidity-model-yhwh-multiple-manifestations]] | **sommer-bog-2.1** | Confidence: high
  J and E texts preserve the ANE fluidity model: Yhwh had multiple local manifestations ("Yhwh of Samaria," "Yhwh of Teman," "Yhwh of Hebron") and his *mal'akh* functioned as an avatar, not a separate messenger.
  **See also:** [[claim-yhwh-local-manifestations-fluid-monotheism-kuntillet-ajrud]] (sommer-bog-2.2) — geographic fragmentation; [[claim-malakh-avatar-yhwh-not-separate-angel-je-sources]] (sommer-bog-2.3) — *mal'akh* as avatar; [[claim-je-sources-monotheistic-fluid-not-polytheistic]] (sommer-bog-2.4) — J/E were monotheistic, not polytheistic.

## Chapter 3: The Rejection of the Fluidity Model

### Claim: P and D sources systematically rejected the fluidity model
→ [[claim-priestly-deuteronomistic-sources-rejected-fluidity-model]] | **sommer-bog-3.1** | Confidence: high
  P and D rejected divine fluidity. For P and D, God is transcendent and accessed only through the *kavod* (Glory) or *shem* (Name) — hypostases that allow God to act at a distance without being locally present. J/E had multiple bodies, fluid selfhood (ANE pattern) → D: God in heaven, only Name on earth → P: transcendent God, only Glory appears → later Judaism: God has NO body.
  **See also:** [[claim-deuteronomic-shem-theology-rejects-divine-fluidity]] (sommer-bog-3.2) — D's *shem* theology; [[claim-priestly-kavod-theology-rejects-divine-fluidity]] (sommer-bog-3.3) — P's *kavod* theology; [[claim-priestly-deuteronomistic-anti-fluid-not-anti-corporeal]] (sommer-bog-3.4) — P/D are anti-fluid, not anti-corporeal per se; [[claim-genesis-1-26-anti-fluid-manifesto-image-without-embodiment]] (sommer-bog-3.5) — Genesis 1:26 as P's anti-fluid manifesto.

## Chapters 1-3 Synthesis

### Synthesis Claim: Sommer reframes the polytheism→monotheism debate as fluid→anti-fluid monotheism evolution
→ [[claim-sommer-reframes-polytheism-monotheism-evolution-as-fluid-embodiment-shift]] | **sommer-bog-1-3.1** | Confidence: high
  Smith, Romer, and Dever trace a polytheism → monotheism evolution. Sommer shows that WITHIN monotheism, there was a FLUIDITY → ANTI-FLUIDITY evolution. J and E were monotheistic (one God) but fluid (multiple bodies). P and D were monotheistic and anti-fluid (transcendent God). The evolution wasn't polytheism to monotheism — it was fluid monotheism to non-fluid monotheism. This reframes the entire debate.
