# Schmid — §§2-4: The Concept of Theology from Plato to Gabler

These three short subsections trace the evolution of "theology" as a term: from Plato, where it meant myth, through the Reformation's existential reinterpretation, to Gabler's 1787 lecture that split biblical theology from dogmatics — the birth of OT theology as a discipline. Schmid's purpose is to show that the current confused state of the term is the product of contingent historical developments, not a stable definition.

---

## §2: The Premodern Conception — From Mythology to Systematic Doctrine

### Claim 1: "Theology" originates in Greek philosophy as derogatory — it meant myth, not systematic doctrine
→ [[claim-theology-originated-greek-philosophy-myth-not-systematic-doctrine]] | **schmid-ht-2-4.1** | Confidence: very-high
  Philological fact: in Plato (Republic 379a), Aristotle (Metaphysics), Josephus, and Philo, θεολογία means mythology or religion — not systematic reflection. The term's later development into systematic doctrine is a historical contingency. Supports: [[claim-normative-ot-theology-recent-suspect-development]], [[claim-gabler-biblical-theology-historical-discipline-solution-failed]].

---

### Claim 2: Scholasticism transformed theology from "doctrine of God" to "entirety of Christian doctrine" and made it an academic discipline
→ [[claim-scholasticism-transformed-theology-entirety-doctrine-academic-discipline]] | **schmid-ht-2-4.2** | Confidence: high
  The university facultas theologica (11th-12th c.) and Aristotelian reception gave theology its academic connotation: reason and systematic reflection. The "theology = systematics" equation that dominates English-speaking scholarship is a medieval development. Supports: [[claim-protestant-orthodoxy-returned-prooftext-method-dicta-probantia]].

## §3: The Reformation's Reconstrual — From Doctrine to Relationship

### Claim 3: Luther redefined theology from speculative doctrine of God to existential relationship with God — creating confusion between "theology" and "religion"
→ [[claim-luther-redefined-theology-existential-relationship-god]] | **schmid-ht-2-4.3** | Confidence: high
  Luther: "true theology is practical, therefore speculative theology belongs in hell with the devil." The theology/religion confusion that plagues modern scholarship traces back to Luther's redefinition. Supports: [[claim-schleiermacher-religion-theology-distinction-biblical-studies]], [[claim-schmid-protestant-history-of-scholarship-not-content]].

---

### Claim 4: This book is "Protestant in terms of the history of scholarship" — not in content
→ [[claim-schmid-protestant-history-of-scholarship-not-content]] | **schmid-ht-2-4.4** | Confidence: medium-high
  Schmid acknowledges Protestant scholarly lineage while maintaining his method is not confessionally determined. More honest than the "neither Christian nor Jewish" framing but still evasive. Supports: [[claim-historical-theology-descriptive-reconstructive-not-normative]]. Challenged by: [[claim-schmid-hebrew-bible-neither-christian-nor-jewish]].

---

### Claim 5: Protestant orthodoxy returned to scholastic systematizing — and the dicta probantia method treated the Bible as prooftext quarry
→ [[claim-protestant-orthodoxy-returned-prooftext-method-dicta-probantia]] | **schmid-ht-2-4.5** | Confidence: high
  After Luther's existential turn, Protestant orthodoxy (Keckermann 1602, Gerhard 1610-22, Schmidt 1671) returned to scholastic systematizing via prooftext method. The collapse of this method under historical criticism created the need for Gabler's mediating discipline. Supports: [[claim-gabler-biblical-theology-historical-discipline-solution-failed]].

## §4: From Biblical Theology to the Theology of the Old Testament

### Claim 6: Gabler's 1787 lecture created "biblical theology" as a HISTORICAL discipline mediating between Bible and systematics — but his solution failed
→ [[claim-gabler-biblical-theology-historical-discipline-solution-failed]] | **schmid-ht-2-4.6** | Confidence: high
  Gabler distinguished "true biblical theology" (historical) from "pure biblical theology" (timeless truths bridging to systematics). The latter was a Trojan horse. Schmid's project chooses the historical side while abandoning the normative bridge. Supports: [[claim-historical-theology-descriptive-reconstructive-not-normative]], [[claim-bauer-ot-nt-split-introduced-theological-plurality]].

---

### Claim 7: The split of biblical theology into separate OT and NT theologies (Bauer, 1796) introduced theological PLURALITY into the concept of "theology"
→ [[claim-bauer-ot-nt-split-introduced-theological-plurality]] | **schmid-ht-2-4.7** | Confidence: very-high
  The move from one Bible → two Testaments → two theologies → many theologies is a logical unfolding of implications present in Gabler and Bauer. The tension between singular and plural is baked into the discipline's history. Supports: [[claim-hebrew-bible-comparable-plato-diverse-coherent-thought-world]].

## Cross-Cutting Assessment

**Strongest section:** The genealogy from Plato through Gabler is clean, well-documented, and genuinely illuminating. Schmid shows that every major definition of "theology" was a response to a prior definition's failures — there is no stable meaning, only a series of reactions.

**Weakest section:** The "Protestant only in terms of scholarly history" claim is more honest than the Preface but still somewhat evasive. Schmid wants to have it both ways: Protestant enough to claim continuity with the tradition, not Protestant enough to be bound by its normative commitments.

**Overall:** These background sections are historiographical rather than argumentative, so there's less to evaluate critically. They succeed in their purpose: showing that the term "theology" has no stable meaning and that Schmid's descriptive-historical approach is as legitimate as any other in the term's long and contested history. The genealogy is efficient and well-sourced. The real test is whether Schmid's constructive proposal (Parts E-H) delivers on the promise set up here.
