# Heiser — Part 1: First Things (Chapters 1-2)

---

## Chapter 1: Reading Your Bible Again for the First Time

### Claim: Psalm 82 broke Heiser's theological filter — and should break yours too

**The watershed moment:** "My friend handed me his Hebrew Bible, open to Psalm 82. He said simply, 'Here, read that... look at it closely.' The first verse hit me like a bolt of lightning: 'God [*elohim*] stands in the divine assembly; he administers judgment in the midst of the gods [*elohim*].' I saw immediately that the second instance needed to be translated as plural. The God of the Old Testament was part of an assembly — a pantheon — of other gods."

**What he was taught vs. what the text says:**
- **Evangelical explanations:** The *elohim* are human judges, Jewish elders, or Trinity references
- **Heiser's response:** "Psalm 82 states that the gods were being condemned as corrupt in their administration of the nations of the earth. The Bible nowhere teaches that God appointed a council of Jewish elders to rule over foreign nations, and God certainly wouldn't be railing against the rest of the Trinity, Jesus and the Spirit, for being corrupt."
- **The critical scholarship:** Dozens of articles showing "parallels between the psalm and its ideas and the literature of other civilizations of the biblical world — in some cases, matching the psalm's phrases word for word"

**The decision point:** "Was my loyalty to the text or to Christian tradition? Did I really have to choose between the two? I wasn't sure, but I knew that what I was reading in Psalm 82, taken at face value, simply didn't fit the theological patterns I had always been taught."

**Confidence:** HIGH that this is Heiser's genuine experience. The theological tension between Psalm 82's plain reading and evangelical theology is real.

**What's at stake:** "I still believe in the uniqueness of the God of the Bible. I still embrace the deity of Christ." Heiser is not deconstructing — he's RECONSTRUCTING. Psalm 82 didn't destroy his faith. It forced him to build a better theology that takes the text seriously.

## Chapter 2: Rules of Engagement

### Claim: We must read the Bible through ancient eyes, not modern filters

**The filter/metaphor:** "Most of my education was conducted using filters. The content I learned was filtered through certain presumptions and traditions that ordered the material for me, that put it into a system that made sense to my modern mind. Verses that didn't quite work with my tradition were 'problem passages' that were either filtered out or consigned to the periphery."

**The mosaic/metaphor:** "The Bible is really a theological and literary mosaic. The pattern in a mosaic often isn't clear up close. It may appear to be just a random assemblage of pieces. Only when you step back can you see the wondrous whole."

**Heiser's three rules of engagement:**

1. **Let the text speak:** Don't filter passages through later theological systems. Read what's actually there.
2. **Read in context:** The ancient Near Eastern context, not modern Western assumptions, is the proper lens.
3. **Trust the biblical writers:** They weren't stupid or primitive. If they used the word *elohim* for multiple beings, they knew what they were doing.

**Confidence:** HIGH that Heiser's methodology is sincere. It's essentially the same methodology as Smith and Römer — read the texts in their ANE context — but applied to defend rather than deconstruct traditional theology.
