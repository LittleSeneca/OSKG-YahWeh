## Chapter 4: How Did Yhwh Become the God of Israel?

### Claim: Israel originally belonged to El, not Yhwh
→ [[claim-israel-name-el-originally-el-worshippers]] | **romer-inv-4.1** | Confidence: high
  The name "Israel" contains *'ēl*, not *yāhû*. The Merneptah Stele shows a group called "Israel" in Canaan by 1200 BCE. Yhwh was a later addition — a warrior god imported from the south. Individual claim extracted in Session 10. See also [[claim-biblical-texts-acknowledge-yhwh-chose-israel-later]] (romer-inv-4.3) and [[claim-merneptah-stele-first-mention-israel-ethnic-group]] (romer-inv-4.2).

## Chapter 5: The Entrance of Yhwh into Jerusalem

### Claim: Yhwh entered Jerusalem through David's political genius
→ [[claim-david-brought-yhwh-to-jerusalem-political]] | **romer-inv-5.2** | Confidence: medium
  Jerusalem was originally an El/Elyon city. David conquered this neutral, non-tribal city and brought Yhwh's ark from Shiloh, merging El's city with Yhwh's cult. Individual claim extracted in this session (Batch 9). See also [[claim-shiloh-yhwh-first-major-sanctuary]] (romer-inv-5.1), [[claim-saul-david-solomon-paradigmatic-constructs]] (romer-inv-5.3).

### Synthesis Claim: Two-stage political process
→ [[claim-yhwh-became-israel-god-two-stage-political-process]] | **romer-inv-4-5.1** | Confidence: medium
  Yhwh became Israel's god through a two-stage political process: (1) adopted by the monarchy as patron deity, (2) installed in Jerusalem (El's old city) by David. The El-Yhwh identification was accomplished politically, not theologically. This is the combined note's unique synthesis contribution bridging Chapters 4 and 5.

## Cross-Cutting: Römer's Narrative Arc Through Chapter 5

1. Yhwh originates in the southern deserts (Ch. 2)
2. Yhwh moves north through Midianite/Kenite intermediaries (Ch. 3)
3. Yhwh is adopted by the group "Israel" — originally an El-worshipping people (Ch. 4)
4. Yhwh is installed in Jerusalem by David — the political masterstroke that unified northern Yhwh worship with southern El traditions (Ch. 5)

We are now at the threshold of the monarchy. Chapters 6-7 will cover Yhwh's cult in Israel and Judah. The narrative arc is remarkably clean — cleaner than Smith's more cautious approach. Römer is telling you a story. Smith was building you a case. Both are working from the same evidence.
