# Heiser — Part 3: Divine Transgressions (Chapters 10-15)

## Genesis 6:1-4 and the Watchers

### Claim: The "sons of God" in Genesis 6 were divine beings who produced the Nephilim

**Heiser's claim:** Genesis 6:1-4 describes divine beings (*bĕnê hā'ĕlōhîm*) descending to earth and fathering children with human women. This is NOT about the line of Seth marrying the line of Cain (the standard evangelical reading). The "sons of God" are the same divine council members from Job and Psalms. Their offspring were the Nephilim — giants, demigods. This event was a cosmic transgression that corrupted humanity and prompted the Flood.

**Evidence:**
- *Bĕnê hā'ĕlōhîm* ALWAYS refers to divine beings in the Hebrew Bible (Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7)
- The Septuagint translates "sons of God" as "angels of God"
- Second Temple Jewish literature (1 Enoch, Jubilees) unanimously reads it as angels
- Jude 6-7 and 2 Peter 2:4-6 reference "angels who sinned" — connecting to Genesis 6
- The Sethite interpretation originated with Julius Africanus (3rd century CE) — it's late and apologetic

**Confidence:** HIGH on the "sons of God = divine beings" reading. This is actually the scholarly consensus, even among critical scholars. It's only conservative evangelicals who insist on the Sethite view.

**What's at stake:** This interpretation connects Israelite religion to a broader ANE pattern of divine-human hybridization myths. It also explains the persistent biblical theme of giants/Nephilim/Rephaim (Numbers 13:33, Deuteronomy 2-3, Joshua, Goliath).
