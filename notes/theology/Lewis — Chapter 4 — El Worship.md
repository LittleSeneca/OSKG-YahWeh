# Lewis — Chapter 4: El Worship

Lewis argues that El was the original god of Israel — his name appears in "Israel" itself, and the patriarchal narratives preserve genuinely archaic El traditions even though redacted through later Yahwistic hands. The chapter surveys El in the Late Bronze Age Levant (Amarna, Ugarit, Hittite Elkunirsha), argues against the "El disappears in the Iron Age" thesis, examines each biblical El deity (El Elyon, El Roi, El Olam, El Bethel, El Shadday), and sketches a composite portrait of El as a family/clan deity — father, kinsman, benevolent protector, head of the divine council — distinct from the national, combative Yahweh.

## Claim 1: El was the original god of Israel
→ [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]] | **lewis-ocg-4.1** | Confidence: very-high
  His name in "Israel" and the absence of Yahweh in early names demonstrate priority of El worship. Genesis 33:20 ("El, the god of Israel") and Exodus 6:3 (El Shadday revelation) preserve pre-Yahwistic memory.
  Depends on: [[claim-albertz-onomastics-revolution-personal-piety-not-state]]. Supports: [[claim-el-did-not-disappear-iron-age-onomastic-epigraphic]], [[claim-israelite-el-family-clan-deity-not-national]], [[claim-deut-32-8-9-el-elyon-superior-yahweh-divine-son]], [[claim-el-shadday-genuinely-archaic-not-priestly-invention]]. Contradicts: [[claim-israelite-religion-absolutely-different-paganism-from-beginning]], [[claim-kenite-midianite-hypothesis-baseless]].

## Claim 2: Amarna shows no El preeminence in Late Bronze Canaan
→ [[claim-amarna-no-el-preeminence-late-bronze-canaan]] | **lewis-ocg-4.2** | Confidence: high
  El was one deity among many in Amarna personal names — Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Hurrian, and West Semitic deities all appear. But Amarna letters are diplomatic correspondence, not religious texts.
  Supports: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]].

## Claim 3: Ugaritic ʾIlu is the fullest El portrait; NOT deposed by Baal
→ [[claim-ugaritic-ilu-fullest-el-portrait-not-deposed-baal]] | **lewis-ocg-4.3** | Confidence: very-high
  Fatherly, wise, benevolent, creative head of the pantheon. El and Baal are both kings in complementary ways — El is NOT degraded or demoted. KTU 1.23 (Dawn and Dusk) and KTU 1.114 (drinking binge) prove vitality, not impotence.
  Supports: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]], [[claim-ugaritic-ilu-iconography-template-not-continued-israel]], [[claim-israelite-el-family-clan-deity-not-national]], [[claim-divine-council-seventy-sons-of-el]]. Contradicts: the van der Toorn/Oldenburg conflict model.

## Claim 4: El did NOT disappear in the Iron Age
→ [[claim-el-did-not-disappear-iron-age-onomastic-epigraphic]] | **lewis-ocg-4.4** | Confidence: very-high
  Onomastic and inscriptional evidence (Sam'alian pantheon, Aramaic names, Ammonite onomastica) shows ongoing El worship across the Levant. "The name ʾIl is the commonest theophorous element in Aramaic personal names of the first millennium" (Lipiński).
  Depends on: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]], [[claim-epigraphy-rewritten-history-israelite-religion]]. Supports: [[claim-el-shadday-genuinely-archaic-not-priestly-invention]], [[claim-israelite-el-family-clan-deity-not-national]]. Contradicts: the Niehr/van der Toorn decline thesis.

## Claim 5: Deuteronomy 32:8-9 shows El Elyon superior to Yahweh
→ [[claim-deut-32-8-9-el-elyon-superior-yahweh-divine-son]] | **lewis-ocg-4.5** | Confidence: very-high
  4QDeutj and LXX read "sons of God," not the MT's "sons of Israel." El Elyon apportions nations to divine beings; Yahweh receives Jacob as his portion — he starts as one of the divine sons and ends preeminent. Psalm 82 tells a parallel story.
  Depends on: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]], [[claim-epigraphy-rewritten-history-israelite-religion]]. Supports: [[claim-deut-32-8-9-sons-of-el]], [[claim-divine-council-seventy-sons-of-el]], [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]]. Contradicts: [[claim-israelite-religion-absolutely-different-paganism-from-beginning]].

## Claim 6: El Shadday is genuinely archaic, not a Priestly invention
→ [[claim-el-shadday-genuinely-archaic-not-priestly-invention]] | **lewis-ocg-4.6** | Confidence: high
  Extra-biblical attestations (Ugaritic KTU 1.108, Deir ʿAlla Shaddayyin, Late Bronze Egyptian name) plus archaic biblical poetry (Gen 49, Num 24) prove antiquity. The Deir ʿAlla Shaddayyin alone are 8th C BCE and independent of the Bible.
  Depends on: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]], [[claim-el-did-not-disappear-iron-age-onomastic-epigraphic]]. Contradicts: minimalist readings of P as pure Persian/Hellenistic invention. Kaufmann agrees on P's antiquity but for different reasons ([[claim-torah-pre-prophetic-earliest-stage-israelite-religion]]).

## Claim 7: Israelite El was a family/clan deity, not a national deity
→ [[claim-israelite-el-family-clan-deity-not-national]] | **lewis-ocg-4.7** | Confidence: high
  El's profile (father, kinsman, protector, provider of children, shepherd) contrasts with Yahweh's (warrior, storm god, intolerant, centralized cult). El is NOT associated with the southern wilderness, NOT a storm god, NOT intolerant.
  Depends on: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]], [[claim-ugaritic-ilu-fullest-el-portrait-not-deposed-baal]]. Supports: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]], [[claim-masseboth-standing-stones-el-representations-arad]], [[claim-albertz-onomastics-revolution-personal-piety-not-state]].

## Claim 8: El as original Exodus god — tantalizing, not decisive
→ [[claim-el-original-exodus-god-tantalizing-not-decisive]] | **lewis-ocg-4.8** | Confidence: low-medium
  Numbers 23-24 ("El brought him out of Egypt") is suggestive, but El's warrior motifs are likely back-formations from the El-Yahweh merger. Fragmentary evidence recommends caution.
  Depends on: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]], [[claim-israelite-el-family-clan-deity-not-national]]. ## Chapter 4 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| El was original god of Israel | VERY HIGH | Near-consensus; strongest evidence is name "Israel" itself |
| Amarna shows no El preeminence | HIGH | Source limitation — diplomatic letters, not religious texts |
| Ugaritic ʾIlu is fullest El portrait; not deposed | VERY HIGH | Consensus position now |
| El persisted in Iron Age | VERY HIGH | Epigraphic/onomastic data is strong |
| Deut 32:8-9 shows El > Yahweh | VERY HIGH | Qumran evidence decisive on text; interpretation debatable |
| El Shadday is genuinely archaic | HIGH | Extra-biblical Shadday + archaic poetry |
| El was family deity, not national deity | HIGH | Contrast with Yahweh is well-documented |
| El as original Exodus god | LOW-MEDIUM | Fragmentary evidence; back-formation more likely |

**Strongest sections:** The treatment of Ugaritic ʾIlu (rich, detailed, corrects older misinterpretations) and Deuteronomy 32:8-9 (clear textual argument with Qumran support). The El Shadday discussion, while unresolved on etymology, convincingly establishes antiquity.

**Weakest section:** The El-as-Exodus-god argument. Lewis presents it because it's in the literature, but he clearly doesn't believe it. The section reads as dutiful coverage rather than committed argument.

**What this chapter establishes for the rest of the book:**
- El and Yahweh were originally distinct deities with different functional profiles
- El was the original god of Israel (name, patriarchal traditions)
- Yahweh arrived later from the south (to be explored in Ch 6)
- The merger happened through identification, not conflict — El wasn't defeated, he was absorbed
- The patriarchal narratives, while redacted, preserve genuine memory of pre-Yahwistic El worship

**Cross-references:** This chapter is in direct conversation with Cross's Part I ("The Religion of Canaan and the God of Israel"), which makes many of the same arguments. Lewis is more cautious than Cross on Proto-Sinaitic evidence (he rejects readings Cross relied on) and more detailed on Ugaritic material. The chapter should be read alongside Heiser's Part 2 ("The Households of God") for a contrasting approach that collapses El and Yahweh from the beginning. Smith's Part I of *Origins* covers similar divine council material but with more emphasis on the theogonic dimension. Dever has almost nothing on El as a distinct deity — his focus is almost entirely on Asherah and popular religion.
