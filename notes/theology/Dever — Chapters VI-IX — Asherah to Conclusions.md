## Final Assessment: Dever's Book as a Whole

| Strength | Weakness |
|----------|----------|
| **Overwhelming archaeological evidence** — the figurines, shrines, inscriptions are undeniable | **Rhetorical overreach** — Dever sometimes claims more than the evidence strictly proves |
| **Honest about personal biases** — more methodologically transparent than most scholars | **Dismissive of theology** — his "useless... barrier to understanding" verdict on biblical theology alienates readers who might otherwise be persuaded |
| **Centers women's experience** — a necessary corrective to androcentric scholarship | **Binary thinking** — Book religion vs. folk religion may be too sharp a distinction |
| **Directly engages the faith question** — knows what's at stake for believers | **The Asherah-as-goddess question** — his case is strong but not absolutely decisive on every piece of evidence |

**Dever's contribution to the three-book synthesis:** Smith provided the text-critical framework. Römer provided the historical narrative. Dever provides the material evidence — the figurines, the shrines, the standing stones. Together, they make a cumulative case that is very difficult to dismiss. The biblical God was not always alone.
