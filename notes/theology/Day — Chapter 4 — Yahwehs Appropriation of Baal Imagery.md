# Day — Chapter 4: Yahweh's Appropriation of Baal Imagery

The longest and most ambitious chapter. Day argues that Yahweh absorbed massive amounts of Baal's mythological portfolio: storm theophany, cloud-riding, thunder-voice, dragon-slaying, the sacred mountain Zaphon, and death-and-resurrection imagery. This is not peripheral borrowing — it's wholesale appropriation of Baal's defining characteristics.

---

## Claim 1: Psalm 29 is a Yahwistic adaptation of Canaanite Baal mythology, including Baal's seven thunders

→ [[claim-day-psalm-29-yahwistic-baal-hymn-seven-thunders]] | **day-ygc-4.1** | Confidence: very-high
  The sevenfold *qôl yahweh* corresponds to Baal's "seven lightnings... eight storehouses of thunder" in KTU² 1.101 — Day's own discovery. The *benê ʼēlîm* council and enthronement over the flood parallel Ugaritic Baal language. Not wholesale substitution (the wilderness of Kadesh reference proves Israelite composition), but modelled on Canaanite language. One of the most widely accepted cases of direct Canaanite borrowing in the OT.

---

## Claim 2: The OT dragon/sea conflict derives from Baal's defeat of Yam/Leviathan, NOT from Babylonian Marduk/Tiamat

→ [[claim-day-chaoskampf-canaanite-baal-yam-not-babylonian-marduk-tiamat]] | **day-ygc-4.2** | Confidence: very-high
  This is the scholarly consensus since Ugarit. OT Leviathan = Ugaritic Litan (precise name correspondence). *Tehôm* in Gen 1:2 lacks Tiamat's feminine ending — a Canaanite prototype via Ugaritic *thm* is more natural. Behemoth = Ugaritic Arsh. Three modes of OT reuse: creation, historicization (Rahab = Egypt), and eschatologization (Isa 27:1; Daniel 7). Gunkel's late 19th-century Babylonian theory is superseded.

---

## Claim 3: Mt Zaphon (Baal's mountain) was transferred to Yahweh's dwelling — both heaven and Jerusalem

→ [[claim-day-mt-zaphon-baal-transferred-yahweh-heaven-zion]] | **day-ygc-4.3** | Confidence: high
  Ps 48:3 calls Jerusalem "the heights of Zaphon" — applying Baal's sacred mountain name to Zion. Papyrus Amherst 63 replaces Zion with Zaphon in a paganized Psalm 20. Isa 14:13 and Job 26:7 use Zaphon for the cosmic mountain/heaven. The transfer to Jerusalem was likely mediated through the Jebusite cult of El-Elyon. Jerusalem's sacred geography is built on Baal's.

---

## Claim 4: Baal was genuinely a dying and rising god, and Hosea appropriated this imagery to describe Israel's exile and restoration — which eventually led (via Isaiah 26) to Daniel's literal resurrection doctrine

→ [[claim-day-baal-dying-rising-hosea-isaiah-daniel-resurrection-chain]] | **day-ygc-4.4** | Confidence: medium-high
  The chain: Baal (dying and rising god) → Hosea 5-6 and 13-14 (death/resurrection imagery for national exile/restoration — the verbs *qûm* and *ḥāyâ* imply resurrection, not healing) → Isaiah 26:19 (eight structural parallels with Hosea 13-14 in order — Day's *JTS* 1980 discovery) → Daniel 12:2 (literal individual resurrection, "remythologized"). Against Zoroastrian origin: Jewish resurrection speaks of "awakening from sleep" — alien to Zoroastrianism but attested in Canaanite tradition (Melqart's *egersis* festival). Jewish resurrection has a Canaanite genealogy.

---

## Claim 5: Yahweh-as-cloud-rider derives from Baal's epithet *rkb ʻrpt* "rider of the clouds"

→ [[claim-day-yahweh-cloud-rider-derived-baal-rkb-rpt]] | **day-ygc-4.5** | Confidence: high
  Deut 33:26; Ps 18:11; 68:34; 104:3; Isa 19:1 all associate Yahweh with cloud-riding. Ugaritic *rkb* elsewhere always means "ride, mount." KTU² 1.5.V.6b-11: Mot commands Baal to take his cloud chariot team to the underworld. However, the widely-held equation of Ps 68:5's *rōkēb bāʻărābôt* with *rkb ʻrpt* is probably wrong — *ʻărābâ* means "desert" and context supports this. Daniel 7:13's cloud-rider is the apocalyptic endpoint of this Baal-to-Yahweh tradition.

## Chapter 4 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Psalm 29 = Baal hymn adaptation (seven thunders) | VERY HIGH | Loretz/Mittmann's source-critical fragmentations |
| Dragon/sea conflict = Canaanite, not Babylonian | VERY HIGH | Mari *tâmtum* text could complicate the neat Babylonia-vs-Canaan dichotomy |
| Zaphon transferred to Yahweh (heaven + Jerusalem) | HIGH | Ps 89:13 reading debate; El-Elyon mediation is plausible but speculative |
| Baal as dying/rising god → Hosea → Isaiah → Daniel resurrection | MEDIUM-HIGH | Baal as "dying" god contested by Smith/Barstad; Zoroastrian alternative still has proponents |
| Cloud-rider imagery from Baal | HIGH | Ps 68:5 debate; Ullendorff/Brock's "gatherer" reading (minority) |

**Strongest section:** The Hosea → Isaiah → Daniel chain (Day's own research). The eight structural parallels between Hosea 13-14 and Isaiah 26-27 are a genuine scholarly discovery that has held up well.

**Weakest section:** The Baal-as-dying-and-rising-god debate. Day acknowledges the controversy and engages it, but the evidence is more contested than he suggests. Mettinger's subsequent work would help, but the "disappearing and returning" counter-position is not simply rhetorical.

**Most important methodological move:** Day demonstrates a three-stage process: (1) mythological appropriation (Baal → OT), (2) demythologization (cosmic battle → national restoration metaphor), (3) remythologization (metaphor → apocalyptic literal resurrection). This is a genuinely sophisticated literary-historical model, not a crude "borrowing" claim. The entire trajectory from Baal to Daniel 12 is one of Day's most original contributions to the field.
