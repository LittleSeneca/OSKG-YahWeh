# Römer, The Invention of God — Chapter 9

## Yhwh and His Asherah

---

## Claim 1: Asherah was a goddess — El's consort at Ugarit — and became Yhwh's consort in Israel

**Römer's claim:** At Ugarit (14th-13th centuries BCE), Asherah (*Aṯiratu*) was El's consort, "Lady Asherah of the Sea," mother of the seventy gods. When El and Yhwh merged, Asherah came with the package. In monarchic Israel, she was worshipped as Yhwh's consort. This is the majority scholarly position — directly contra Smith.

**Evidence presented:**
- **Ugaritic texts:** Asherah is "the great goddess, parhedros of the god El and mother of minor gods." The Baal cycle calls them "the seventy sons of Aṯirat." In the Keret legend, the heir "shall suck the milk of Aṯirat" — suggesting connection to fertility and kingship.
- **Mesopotamian attestation:** Asherah appears as Ašratu(m) from the Hammurabi era (18th century BCE) through Seleucid ritual texts.
- **Southern Arabian inscriptions (1st millennium):** *Aṯrt* means either the specific goddess or "goddess" generically.

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH on Ugaritic Asherah (the texts are unambiguous). HIGH that the El → Yhwh merger transferred Asherah.

---

## Claim 2: The Bible deliberately obscures the Yhwh-Asherah link by associating her with Baal

**Römer's claim:** "The biblical texts make no direct link between Asherah and Yhwh." Instead, they associate her with Baal. This is a deliberate Deuteronomistic strategy: "The fact that all the cited passages come from editors of the Deuteronomistic school strongly suggests that this association was invented to break the link between Yhwh and Asherah."

**Evidence presented:**
- *'Ăšērāh* occurs 40 times in the Bible (18 singular, 19 masculine plural, 3 feminine plural)
- The masculine plural is surprising — "an artificial creation of the editors of the Bible in order to avoid any possible allusion to the goddess Asherah"
- Four categories of biblical reference: (a) destruction formulas, (b) associated with Baal, (c) with standing stones, (d) near Yhwh's altar/house
- The Baal-Asherah link appears ONLY in Deuteronomistic passages — a polemical invention
- "In sharp contrast to the biblical texts, a close link between Yhwh and Asherah is attested in the inscriptions"

**Confidence:** HIGH that the Bible obscures the Yhwh-Asherah link. MEDIUM-HIGH that the Baal association is a deliberate Deuteronomistic invention.

---

## Claim 3: Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom prove Yhwh and Asherah were paired

**Römer's claim:** The 8th-century inscriptions from Kuntillet Ajrud ("Yhwh of Samaria/Teman and his Asherah") and Khirbet el-Qom ("Yhwh and his Asherah") are unambiguous evidence that Asherah was paired with Yhwh. The possessive suffix on *'šrth* is most naturally read as referring to the goddess, not a cult object.

**Evidence:**
- Kuntillet Ajrud (c. 800 BCE): "Yhwh of Samaria and his Asherah" / "Yhwh of Teman and his Asherah"
- Khirbet el-Qom (c. 700 BCE): A burial inscription invoking blessing "by Yhwh and his Asherah" — found in the heartland of Judah
- Kuntillet Ajrud was a caravanserai/way-station on a major trade route — not a fringe site. "The religious practices of Kuntillet Ajrud probably do not constitute practices peripheral to Judean culture."
- The pottery was Judean; the script uses Hebrew *matres lectionis* (not Phoenician)

**Confidence:** HIGH that the inscriptions pair Yhwh with Asherah. MEDIUM-HIGH that this represents a divine couple rather than a deity + hypostasis.

---

## Claim 4: What happened to Asherah? Josiah's reform removed her, and the Deuteronomists rewrote her story

**Römer's claim:** Josiah's reform (late 7th century) removed the asherah from the Jerusalem temple (2 Kings 23:6-7). The Deuteronomists then associated her with Baal — the foreign enemy god — to delegitimize her. By the time the canon was finalized, the memory of Yhwh's consort had been largely erased. But the inscriptions prove she was there.

**Römer directly contradicts Smith here:** Where Smith argues the asherah was a cult symbol that had lost its goddess connection, Römer argues the goddess was real and actively worshipped.

**Confidence:** HIGH on Josiah removing the asherah (the biblical account is plausible and consistent with the reform program). MEDIUM-HIGH on the Deuteronomistic rewriting strategy (the Baal association is suspiciously convenient).

**What's at stake for faith:** Yhwh had a wife. The goddess was worshipped alongside the god in the Jerusalem temple. She was removed by reformers, not because she was foreign, but because she was too deeply embedded in Israelite religion. The God of the Bible was once part of a divine couple. This is the single most explosive claim in Römer's book.

**My assessment after three books:** Smith says symbol; Römer says goddess; Dever says goddess with 3,000+ figurines as evidence. The cumulative case now strongly favors the goddess reading. Smith's caution looks increasingly like excessive skepticism. I'm convinced: Yhwh had a consort.
