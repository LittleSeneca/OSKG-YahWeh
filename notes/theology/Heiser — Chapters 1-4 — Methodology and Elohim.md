# Heiser — Chapters 1-4: Methodology and the Elohim

---

## Claim 1: Modern theological systems are filters that prevent us from seeing the Bible's supernatural worldview

**Heiser's claim:** "Most of my education was conducted using filters... Verses that didn't quite work with my tradition were 'problem passages' that were either filtered out or consigned to the periphery of unimportance." The Bible was written by people with a supernatural worldview. Modern readers — even evangelical ones — have a rationalistic worldview. Reading the Bible "unfiltered" means recovering the ancient authors' perspective.

**Psalm 82 as the "filter-breaker":** Heiser's conversion moment: reading Psalm 82 without English camouflage. "God stands in the divine assembly; among the *elohim* he judges." His tradition had filtered this out. The psalm doesn't describe human judges or the Trinity — it describes a divine council of supernatural beings.

**Confidence:** HIGH that Heiser's description of his experience is authentic. MEDIUM on whether his "unfiltered" reading is genuinely unfiltered (all readings involve a framework).

**What's at stake:** Heiser's approach challenges BOTH sides. Against critical scholars: you're right about what the texts SAY (divine council, multiple *elohim*), but wrong about what they MEAN (this isn't polytheism). Against conservative evangelicals: you're right about monotheism, but wrong about the texts (they DO describe other *elohim* — you've been filtering them out).

---

## Claim 2: The word *elohim* means "disembodied being from the spiritual realm" — not "being with all divine attributes"

**Heiser's claim:** "Biblical authors did not assign a specific set of attributes to the word *elohim*." The word is used for Yahweh, divine council members, foreign gods, demons (*shedim*), the deceased Samuel, and angels. Would any Israelite believe these all had the same attributes as Yahweh? No. Therefore *elohim* is not about attributes — it's about NATURE. It means a being whose proper domain is the spiritual world.

**The six categories of *elohim* in the Hebrew Bible:**

| Entity | Example | Attributes vs. Yahweh |
|--------|---------|---------------------|
| Yahweh | Thousands of times | Unique: omnipotent, creator, sovereign |
| Divine council members | Psalm 82:1, 6 | Real spiritual beings, subordinate |
| Gods of other nations | Judges 11:24; 1 Kings 11:33 | Real but inferior |
| Demons (*shedim*) | Deut 32:17 | Malevolent spiritual beings |
| Deceased Samuel | 1 Samuel 28:13 | Disembodied human |
| Angels | Genesis 35:7 | Divine messengers |

**The implication:** "There is no warrant for concluding that plural *elohim* produces a pantheon of interchangeable deities. There is no basis for concluding that the biblical writers would have viewed Yahweh as no better than another *elohim*." The presence of multiple *elohim* in the Bible is NOT evidence of polytheism — it's evidence of a supernatural worldview that INHERENTLY distinguishes between Yahweh and all other supernatural beings.

**Confidence:** HIGH that *elohim* is used for multiple types of beings (textually demonstrable). MEDIUM-HIGH on Heiser's claim that this means the word denotes "place of residence" rather than "level of power" (this is an inference from usage).

**What's at stake:** This is Heiser's most important move. He accepts the critical data (Deut 32:8-9 describes multiple divine beings; Psalm 82 is a council scene) but rejects the critical conclusion (this represents polytheism). The critical scholars (Smith, Römer) have mistaken the word *elohim* for meaning "god" in the modern sense — that's anachronistic. The biblical writers used a different taxonomy: Yahweh was unique among the *elohim*.

**Who disagrees:** Smith and Römer. They would argue that Heiser is redefining *elohim* to protect monotheism — that the biblical writers inherited a polytheistic worldview and gradually transformed it.

## Claim 3: The "denial statements" ("there is none besides me") are incomparability claims, not denials of existence

**Heiser's claim:** When the Bible says "there is no god besides me" (Deut 4:35, Isa 45:5), it does NOT mean other *elohim* don't exist. It means they don't COMPARE to Yahweh. The same language is used of Babylon: "there is none besides me" (Isa 47:8). Does this mean no other cities existed? No — it means Babylon considered itself incomparable.

**The evidence — context within the same chapters:**
- Deut 4:35 says "there is none besides him" — but Deut 4:19-20 acknowledges other gods were "allotted" to the nations
- Deut 32:17 acknowledges *elohim*/*shedim* exist — Paul (1 Cor 10:20) takes this literally
- "How is it coherent to say that verses extolling the superiority of Yahweh above all *elohim* (Psa 97:9) are really telling us Yahweh is greater than beings that don't exist?"
- "To my ear, it mocks God to say, 'You're greater than something that doesn't exist.' So is my dog."

**Confidence:** HIGH that the same denial language is used for cities (Isa 47:8, Zeph 2:15). HIGH that Deut 4 acknowledges other gods exist. MEDIUM-HIGH that the denial statements are consistently incomparability claims (some may be genuinely existential, especially in Second Isaiah).

**Alternative reading:** The critical reading distinguishes between pre-exilic texts (which acknowledge other gods exist) and exilic texts (Second Isaiah: other gods DON'T exist). Heiser collapses this development into a single consistent theology.

## Chapters 1-4 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|-----------|
| Modern filters distort biblical worldview | HIGH |
| *Elohim* = disembodied spiritual being | MEDIUM-HIGH |
| Multiple *elohim* ≠ polytheism | MEDIUM (depends on definition of polytheism) |
| Denial statements = incomparability | MEDIUM-HIGH |

**Heiser's rhetorical strategy:** He's very effective at showing that the biblical texts describe multiple *elohim* — this is the evidence critical scholars use. His counter-argument is that "multiple *elohim*" doesn't mean "multiple co-equal gods," and the ancient writers never intended it that way. Whether this succeeds depends on whether you accept his definition of *elohim* as a "place of residence" term rather than a "rank" term.

**Heiser's weakness:** He collapses the entire biblical tradition into a single consistent theology. The critical position — that the biblical texts reflect a DEVELOPMENT from acknowledging other gods (Deut 32) to denying them (Second Isaiah) — cannot be dismissed simply by redefining *elohim*. Heiser needs to account for the DEVELOPMENT as well as the word's meaning.
