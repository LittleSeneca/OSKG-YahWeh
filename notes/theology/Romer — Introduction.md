## Claim 2: Yhwh was originally a southern desert/war god who gradually became Israel's god
→ [[claim-yahweh-southern-desert-war-god-origin]] | **romer-inv-intro.2** | Confidence: medium (preview only)
  Programmatic claim for the book: desert war god → national god → the only God after 587. Evidence promised for later chapters. Supports: [[claim-yahweh-southern-storm-warrior-origin]], [[claim-yahweh-originated-south-midian-edom]].

## Claim 3: The Bible must be analyzed historically, "without preconception, just like any other document from antiquity"
→ [[claim-bible-must-be-analyzed-historically]] | **romer-inv-intro.3** | Confidence: medium
  Romer's middle position between maximalism and minimalism: texts are ideologically shaped but preserve "archaic kernels of fact" and "traces of memory." The most important and most vulnerable methodological claim in the book.

---

## Claim 4: "Invention" does not mean fabrication — it means progressive construction
→ [[claim-invention-means-progressive-construction]] | **romer-inv-intro.4** | Confidence: high
  Sedimentary metaphor: layers accumulate over centuries, disrupted by historical events. "Collective invention" by the community. Supports: [[claim-yahweh-southern-desert-war-god-origin]], [[claim-documentary-hypothesis-obsolete]], [[claim-four-stage-model-monotheism-development]].

---

## Claim 5: The Documentary Hypothesis is obsolete; Deuteronomy dates to ~620 BCE; oldest Pentateuchal traditions to ~8th century
→ [[claim-documentary-hypothesis-obsolete]] | **romer-inv-intro.5** | Confidence: medium
  Romer rejects the classic J, E, D, P model (European consensus), retaining only Deuteronomy ~620 and oldest traditions ~8th century. The scroll recopying argument: texts were continually revised. Contradicts Smith's cautious retention of the Documentary Hypothesis.

---

## Claim 6: Terminology matters — "Israel," "Judah," "Jew," and "Canaan" have specific historical meanings
→ [[claim-israel-judah-jew-terminology-historical]] | **romer-inv-intro.6** | Confidence: very-high
  "Israel" = northern kingdom until 722; "Judah" = southern kingdom until 587; "Jews/Judaism" only after Persian period (4th century BCE); "Canaan" is a vague geographic term. Prevents retrojecting later categories onto earlier periods. Supports: [[claim-israel-broadly-west-semitic-religion-not-pristine-monotheism]].

## Methodological Assessment

Römer's Introduction does three things well:

1. **States the thesis clearly:** Yhwh was not always the only God; monotheism was a historical development.
2. **Acknowledges limitations:** "The results of our investigation cannot be more than hypothetical."
3. **Defines his methodological position:** Middle ground between maximalism (the Bible is reliable history) and minimalism (the Bible tells us nothing about pre-exilic Israel).

The vulnerability is the "traces of memory" claim. Römer needs his texts to preserve genuine historical information from periods for which we have no other sources. If that claim fails, the whole book fails with it. The Introduction acknowledges but does not resolve this tension.

**Compared to Smith's Introduction:** Römer is more confident, more accessible, and more willing to tell a story. Smith is more cautious, more text-critical, and more methodologically anxious. Both are honest about uncertainty. Römer is more fun to read. Smith is harder to challenge on specifics. Reading them together is ideal — Römer gives you the narrative arc; Smith gives you the footnotes.
