# Dever, Did God Have a Wife? — Chapter V

## Archaeological Evidence for Folk Religions

---

## Overview: Dever's Eight Categories of Evidence

This is the core of Dever's book. He presents eight categories of archaeological evidence for folk religion, organized from public to private. The cumulative claim: [[claim-eight-categories-archaeology-folk-religion-asherah-cumulative]] | **dever-dghw-5.2** | Confidence: high.

---

## 1. Local Shrines and Family Religion

**Evidence:** At least a dozen household/family shrines excavated from 12th-7th century BCE:

| Site | Date | Key Finds |
|------|------|-----------|
| Tell el-Wawiyat (Galilee) | 12th c. | Column base, basalt tripod bowl, jewelry mold, female figurine |
| `Ai (Room 65) | 12th-11th c. | Benches, cult stand, animal figurines, jewelry |
| Kh. Raddana | 12th-11th c. | Stone platforms, multi-handled bowl with bullhead spouts |
| Megiddo (Shrine 2048) | 10th c. | Two standing stones, four-horned altar, offering table, grain, astragali |
| Tel Rehov | 10th c. | Stone altar, four standing stones, cult stand, chalices, figurines, bronze bull head |
| Tel `Amal | 10th c. | Votive vessels, cult stand, female figurine |
| Tell el-Farah (Tirzah) | 10th c. | Model temple (naos), female figurines |
| Lachish (Cult Room 49) | 10th c. | Benches, four-horned altars, cult stands |

**What they reveal:** "These shrines were for private worship, in which there were no regular fixed services, no priestly supervision, no prescribed theology, no need to conform... And here women played a significant role."

**Confidence:** HIGH. The pattern is consistent across sites.

## 2. Standing Stones (*Massebot*)

**Evidence:**
- Found at nearly every excavated Israelite site
- At Arad: two stones in the holy of holies — "representing Yhwh and another deity" (almost certainly Asherah)
- At Hazor: a row of standing stones at the city gate
- At Gezer: ten massive standing stones
- Functions: divine representations, memorials, covenant witnesses

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 3. Altars

**Evidence:**
- Large four-horned stone altar at Beer-Sheba — deliberately dismantled during Hezekiah's reform
- Small portable limestone incense altars found at domestic sites
- Simple earth-and-stone altars at household shrines

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 4. Figurines — The Key Evidence

**Dever's claim:** This is the strongest archaeological evidence for folk religion — and the evidence Smith ignores.

<!-- Implicit claim: [[claim-judean-pillar-figurines-asherah-goddess-worship-domestic]] | dever-dghw-5.1 | Confidence: very-high -->

**Judean Pillar Figurines (JPFs):**
- Over 3,000 found, overwhelmingly in DOMESTIC contexts (houses, not temples)
- Mass-produced from molds — cheap, accessible, everywhere
- Nude female with emphasized breasts, hands supporting or holding breasts
- "Found in almost every excavated 8th-6th century house in Judah"
- Dated to 8th-6th centuries BCE — the period of the monarchy, the prophets, the reforms
- Most scholars identify them as Asherah representations

**What they were used for:**
- "Prayers in clay" — votive offerings for fertility, conception, safe childbirth, milk production
- "They were part of the everyday religious paraphernalia of the typical Judean household"
- Used by women, for women's concerns

**Dever's argument:** You cannot explain away 3,000+ nude female figurines in domestic contexts. They are material evidence of popular goddess worship. Smith's text-based approach cannot account for them.

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH on the existence and domestic context of the figurines. HIGH that they represent Asherah. MEDIUM-HIGH on Dever's specific claims about how they were used (inferred from context and comparative ethnography).

---

## 5. Cult Stands

**Evidence:** The Taanach cult stand (10th century BCE) — four tiers showing a nude female (Asherah?), lions, cherubim, a tree of life, and a young bull. "An encyclopedia of Canaanite-Israelite religion." Similar stands found at other sites.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

---

## 6. Inscriptions

**Evidence:**
- **Kuntillet Ajrud** (c. 800 BCE): "Yhwh of Samaria/Teman and his Asherah"
- **Khirbet el-Qom** (c. 700 BCE): Burial inscription invoking "Yhwh and his Asherah"

Dever reads these as unambiguous evidence that Asherah was Yhwh's consort.

<!-- Implicit claim: [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-khirbet-el-qom-asherah-consort-not-symbol]] | dever-dghw-5.4 | Confidence: medium-high -->
Contradicts: [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-symbol-not-goddess]], [[claim-his-asherah-cultic-symbol-stylized-tree-not-goddess-consort]].

**Confidence:** HIGH on the inscriptions themselves. MEDIUM-HIGH on the goddess-vs-symbol interpretation.

---

## 7. Votive Offerings

Small objects deposited at shrines: miniature vessels, beads, amulets, jewelry — evidence of personal piety and petitionary religion.

---

## 8. Tombs and Mortuary Practices

Bench tombs with multiple burials, food and drink offerings, lamps, personal items. Evidence of feeding the dead and ancestor cults — practices condemned in the Bible but archaeologically ubiquitous.

**Dever's cumulative argument:** Taken together, these eight categories of evidence demonstrate that folk religion was widespread, persistent, and centered on a female deity (Asherah) worshipped alongside Yhwh. The biblical picture of a monotheistic, aniconic, Jerusalem-centered Yahwism describes the religion of a tiny elite — not the lived religion of most Israelites.

**The challenge to Smith:** Smith's text-based caution about Asherah ("the evidence is insufficient") looks increasingly untenable in light of the archaeological evidence Dever presents. The figurines alone — 3,000+ of them, in domestic contexts, mass-produced for popular use — are a material fact that text-based scholarship cannot explain away.
