# Albertz — §3.1–3.2: Formation of the Monarchy and the Dispute Over Its Religious Legitimation

These sections mark the pivot of Albertz's narrative: the monarchy — not Canaanite syncretism, not "settlement" — was the decisive crisis for Yahweh religion. A religion born in liberation from state oppression now had to legitimate a state. The result was a theological civil war between court theologians (who imported ANE sacral kingship) and tribal opposition groups (who wielded pre-state anti-domination traditions against the monarchy). Albertz's thesis: it took over 300 years for Yahweh religion to cope with the challenge posed by the state — and the resolution only came after the state was destroyed.

## §3.1 The Formation of a Monarchical Territorial State

### Claim 1: The monarchy, not "settlement," was the decisive challenge to Yahweh religion
→ [[claim-monarchy-not-settlement-decisive-challenge-yahweh-religion]] | **albertz-hir-3.1-3.2.1** | Confidence: medium-high
  "A religion which had arisen out of the liberation from state oppression... necessarily faced considerable problems as a result of the establishment of a centralistic state." The old nomad/sedentary model is wrong. Supports: [[claim-monarchy-social-transformations-contradicted-pre-state-yahwism]], [[claim-david-wars-theological-vacuum-ane-sacral-kingship-imported]]. Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-kingship-non-sacral-prophetic-election-not-divine-essence]].

### Claim 2: Saul = chiefdom; David/Solomon = genuine state
→ [[claim-saul-chiefdom-david-solomon-genuine-state]] | **albertz-hir-3.1-3.2.2** | Confidence: medium-high
  Saul's kingship was "still compatible with... tribal organization." David built a mercenary army, captured Jerusalem as a neutral capital, created a bureaucracy — transforming Israel into a sacral monarchy on the ANE pattern under Solomon. Supports: [[claim-monarchy-social-transformations-contradicted-pre-state-yahwism]], [[claim-david-wars-theological-vacuum-ane-sacral-kingship-imported]].

### Claim 3: Social transformations directly contradicted pre-state Yahwistic values
→ [[claim-monarchy-social-transformations-contradicted-pre-state-yahwism]] | **albertz-hir-3.1-3.2.3** | Confidence: high
  Taxation, forced labor (mas), land concentration, class stratification, reurbanization — the monarchy's social effects "can hardly be overestimated." The "ideal of the small farmer" was being destroyed. Supports: [[claim-rebel-movements-anti-domination-yahwism-against-monarchy]].

## §3.2 The Dispute Over the Religious Legitimation of Kingship

### Claim 4: David's wars created a theological vacuum — ANE sacral kingship imported
→ [[claim-david-wars-theological-vacuum-ane-sacral-kingship-imported]] | **albertz-hir-3.1-3.2.4** | Confidence: medium-high
  David's wars could no longer be seen as battles for liberation. Court theologians imported ANE kingship theology: divine sonship (Ps 2:7), eternal dynasty (2 Sam 7), king as mediator of blessing, royal cultic functions — "a closer fusion of throne and altar" than anywhere in the ANE. Supports: [[claim-jerusalem-temple-theology-alien-to-liberation-yahwism]]. Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-kingship-non-sacral-prophetic-election-not-divine-essence]].

### Claim 5: Rebel movements used pre-state anti-domination Yahwism against the monarchy
→ [[claim-rebel-movements-anti-domination-yahwism-against-monarchy]] | **albertz-hir-3.1-3.2.5** | Confidence: medium
  Absalom, Sheba, and Jeroboam wielded Yahweh's kingship AGAINST human kingship — "the establishment of a human kingship is an attack on Yahweh's kingly rule" (1 Sam 8:7). Before writing prophets, tribal resistance movements used Yahwistic anti-domination traditions. Supports: [[claim-jeroboam-revolt-shaped-exodus-narrative-propaganda]], [[claim-jeroboam-bethel-cult-conservative-archaizing-bull-pedestal]]. Contradicted by: [[claim-antimonarchic-passages-premonarchic-not-postexilic]].

### Claim 6: The Succession Narrative is a mediating position — humanizes the king
→ [[claim-succession-narrative-mediating-position-humanizing-king]] | **albertz-hir-3.1-3.2.6** | Confidence: low-medium
  The first literary works of Israel arose from the monarchy's need to justify itself. David is depicted with human strengths and weaknesses — "all young men and fathers of families can identify with" him. This was done "to bring him closer to the way of thinking among the small farmers." Alternative: propaganda, apologetic, or literary artistry.

## §3.1–3.2 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1. Monarchy (not settlement) was the decisive challenge | MEDIUM-HIGH | Self-reinforcing if you define Yahwism as anti-domination |
| 2. Saul = chiefdom; David/Solomon = state | MEDIUM-HIGH | Historicity of the United Monarchy (minimalist challenge) |
| 3. Social transformations contradicted pre-state values | HIGH | Interpretation, not fact, is at issue |
| 4. Kingship theology imported from ANE to fill theological vacuum | HIGH (theology existed); MEDIUM (vacuum framing) | Organic development vs. conscious import |
| 5. Rebel movements used anti-domination Yahwism | MEDIUM | Dating of anti-monarchical texts; possible later retrojection |
| 6. Succession Narrative = mediating position | LOW-MEDIUM | Alternative literary explanations (propaganda, apologetic) |

**Strongest section:** The description of Davidic-Solomonic social transformations (Claim 3) and the content of kingship theology (Claim 4). Both are well-supported textually and archaeologically.

**Weakest section:** The "mediating position" interpretation of the Succession Narrative (Claim 6). This feels like Albertz forcing his model onto a text that may have quite different purposes.

**Albertz's narrative arc clarified:** Yahweh religion (liberation from state oppression) → tribal alliance (anti-domination social experiment) → monarchy (state oppression returns, but now in Israel's name). The history of Israelite religion is the story of this contradiction being worked out — through prophetic protest, reform movements, and eventually the destruction of the state itself. The monarchy sections that follow (§3.3-3.9) will trace this working-out in detail.
