## Yehezkel Kaufmann — The Religion of Israel (1960)

The classic Jewish counter-position to the evolutionary consensus. Kaufmann (Hebrew University) argued Israelite monotheism was a *sui generis* REVOLUTION with Moses, not an evolution from polytheism. Israel's religion was fundamentally different in KIND from paganism from the very beginning. The prophets were not innovators — they called Israel back to a monotheism it always had. The Torah literature (including P) is pre-prophetic, not post-exilic. This is the position the entire critical consensus positions itself AGAINST — cited 51 times across our existing notes.

- **[[Kaufmann — Introduction]]** — The Wellhausen consensus persists on inertia; Torah is pre-prophetic; Israelite religion was an original creation
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch I — The Basic Problem]]** — The Bible is utterly ignorant of pagan religion's nature; this is genuine ignorance, not polemic
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch II — Pagan Religion]]** — The "fundamental idea" of paganism: the metadivine realm above the gods
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch III — Israelite Religion]]** — No theogony, no myth, no metadivine realm; aniconic from the start
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch IV — The Religion of the People]]** — Popular religion was NOT pagan; "idolatry" was vestigial fetishism, not genuine polytheism
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch V — The Sources]]** — P is earliest, not latest; reverses the Documentary Hypothesis sequence
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch VI — The Origins of Israelite Religion]]** — Moses and the monotheistic revolution; engagement with the Kenite hypothesis
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch VII — The Conquest and Settlement]]** — Herem warfare, tribal league as kingdom of God, first idolatrous period limited
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch VIII — The Monarchy]]** — Israelite kingship non-sacral; golden calves were YHWH worship, not paganism; Baal worship was foreign import
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch IX — Some Aspects of the Popular Religion]]** — Sin, evil, priesthood, cult, death, morality — popular religion was fundamentally monotheistic
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch X — The Literature and the Age]]** — Legacy of early religion; chronological limits of classical prophecy
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch XI — Amos and Hosea]]** — These prophets inherited monotheism, didn't invent it; Hosea's marriage metaphor as covenant theology
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch XII — Isaiah Micah Habakkuk]]** — Isaiah's Zion theology; Habakkuk first to declare idolatry sinful for nations
- **[[Kaufmann — Ch XIII — The Prophecy of the Fall]]** — Jeremiah and Ezekiel were traditionalists rooted in Torah, not innovators; Ezekiel's visions are fantasy, not historical sources
- **[[Kaufmann — Epilogue and Prospect]]** — Exile transformed Israel from nation to text-based community; Torah book liberated monotheism from territory

## Konrad Schmid — A Historical Theology of the Hebrew Bible (2019)

Schmid (U Zurich) argues the Pentateuch and Deuteronomistic History were composed in the Persian period (5th-4th c. BCE). If correct, biblical texts are evidence for what Persian-period scribes WANTED TO BELIEVE, not what pre-exilic Israelites actually practiced. The single biggest challenge to the consensus's use of biblical texts as pre-exilic evidence.

- **[[Schmid — Preface]]** — Project as descriptive, reconstructive "historical theology"
- **[[Schmid — §1 — Is There a Theology of the Hebrew Bible]]** — HB as elite scribal literature; Persian period more important than preexilic
- **[[Schmid — §§2-4 — Concept of Theology from Plato to Gabler]]** — Genealogy of "theology" from myth to academic discipline
- **[[Schmid — §5 — Romantic Devaluation of Theology]]** — Schleiermacher, Duhm, Gunkel: religion vs. theology
- **[[Schmid — §§6-9 — Jewish Reception to Implicit Theology]]** — Neo-orthodoxy, von Rad, Schmid's "implicit/stereometric theology"
- **[[Schmid — §11 — The Tanakh as the Hebrew Bible]]** — KEY SECTION: Torah formed in Persian period under imperial authorization
- **[[Schmid — §§10, 12, 13 — Canon Pluriformity and Relativity]]** — Multiple canons; canon boundaries as historically contingent
