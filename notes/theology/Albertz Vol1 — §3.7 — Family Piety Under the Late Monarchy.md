# Albertz — §3.7: Family Piety Under the Late Monarchy

This section documents how Assyrian occupation transformed family piety. The long period of Assyrian dominance (c. 730-630 BCE) produced an unexpected result: family religion became MORE syncretistic, not less, enthusiastically adopting Assyrian practices (roof-top cults, astral rituals, professional manticism). Meanwhile, Yahweh names rose to 80% of personal names — but this didn't indicate exclusivity; it meant Yahweh had become the default family god while other deities were worshipped alongside him. The Queen of Heaven cult and child sacrifice (Molech) were expressions of desperate family piety in crisis conditions.

## Claim 3: Child Sacrifice and Queen of Heaven Were Crisis Expressions of Family Piety
→ [[claim-child-sacrifice-molech-queen-heaven-crisis-family-piety]] | **albertz-hir-3.7.3** | Confidence: high
  Child sacrifice (mlk) at Topheth in the Hinnom Valley and the Queen of Heaven cult (female-dominated, baking cakes, pouring libations) were real practices, not polemical inventions. Carthage evidence confirms mlk-sacrifice. Both represent family piety adapting to extreme conditions under Assyrian domination.

## §3.7 Overall Assessment

**Strongest insight:** The paradox that Yahweh-name dominance and religious pluralism increased simultaneously. This is exactly what Albertz's model predicts (different strata, different logics) and what confuses models that treat "Yahwization of names" as evidence of monotheization.

**Weakest section:** The reconstruction of Assyrian religious influence on family practice is necessarily inferential from polemical sources.
