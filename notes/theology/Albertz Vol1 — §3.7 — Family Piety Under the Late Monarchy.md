# Albertz — §3.7: Family Piety Under the Late Monarchy

This section documents how Assyrian occupation transformed family piety. The long period of Assyrian dominance (c. 730-630 BCE) produced an unexpected result: family religion became MORE syncretistic, not less, enthusiastically adopting Assyrian practices (roof-top cults, astral rituals, professional manticism). Meanwhile, Yahweh names rose to 80% of personal names — but this didn't indicate exclusivity; it meant Yahweh had become the default family god while other deities were worshipped alongside him. The Queen of Heaven cult and child sacrifice (Molech) were expressions of desperate family piety in crisis conditions.

---

## Claim 1: The late monarchy saw contradictory developments in family piety — Yahweh became the predominant family god, but religious pluralism intensified, not decreased

**Albertz's claim:** "On the one hand the national god Yahweh increasingly naturally took the place of the family god" — Yahweh-name proportion rose from ~25% (early monarchy) to ~80% (late monarchy). "But how little this development in family religion can be interpreted as a sign of an increasingly exclusivist worship of Yahweh is shown... by the fact that internal religious pluralism not only continued in the seventh century but even intensified." Jeremiah (2:27, 28a) accused people of turning to "wood and stone" in private distress while appealing to Yahweh only in national catastrophes. "Internal religious pluralism had even taken on syncretistic dimensions."

**Evidence:**
- Seals and inscriptions confirm the 80% Yahweh-name pattern (aligns with Tigay's onomastic data)
- Royal family pioneered Yahweh-names from Solomon onward, but Maacah still had an Asherah image (1 Kings 15:13) as late as the 9th century
- Assyrian deportation policy in the North created a genuinely pluralistic religious environment — remaining Israelite families adopted neighbor gods into their personal piety

**Confidence:** HIGH. The onomastic data is solid. The "pluralism despite Yahwization of names" paradox is a key finding that supports Albertz's model.

---

## Claim 2: Assyrian religious practices were enthusiastically adopted at the family level — roof-top cults, astral worship, incense/libation rituals

**Albertz's claim:** "The population of Judah did not fight against the alien religious customs and ideas of its occupiers, but adopted them voluntarily and almost enthusiastically." The real syncretism was not at the official level (the Jerusalem priesthood maintained theological separation) but at the level of private piety. Assyrian practices were attractive because they were more sophisticated and professionalized.

**Evidence:**
- Roof-top cult places (Zeph 1:5; 2 Kings 23:12; Jer 19:13; 32:29): family shrines moved to flat roofs following Assyrian/Babylonian practice where prayer and invocation ceremonies took place
- Astral elements: connected with Babylonian astral religion where gods were identified with stars. Rituals took place at night or sunrise
- Typical cultic actions: incense altars (qiṭṭēr) and libations (nāsak) — standard in Babylonian invocation rituals
- Professional manticism (hepatoscopy, necromancy) from Mesopotamia was more sophisticated than local Israelite equivalents and became popular
- Zephaniah's charges of "star worship" may be exaggerated — these cults were primarily concerned with healing sick family members, not theological astral worship

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. Albertz is reading between the lines of polemical texts. The Assyrian influence on family cult is plausible but the specific mechanisms (roof-top transfer, mantic professionalization) are inferential.

---

## Claim 3: Child sacrifice (Molech) and the Queen of Heaven cult were expressions of family piety pushed to extremes by crisis

**Albertz's claim:** Child sacrifice (offering children to "Molech") was practiced in Judah in the 7th century, including by kings Ahaz and Manasseh. The Queen of Heaven cult (Jer 7:18; 44:17-19, 25) was a female-dominated family cult involving baking cakes, pouring libations, and burning incense. Both represent family piety adapting to extreme conditions.

**Evidence:**
- Child sacrifice attested at Topheth in the Hinnom Valley (2 Kings 23:10; Jer 7:31; 19:5f.; 32:35)
- Phoenician/Punic evidence (Carthage) demonstrates mlk-sacrifice was a real practice — not just polemical invention
- The Queen of Heaven cult: women took the lead; the cult promised prosperity and protection ("we had plenty of food, and prospered, and saw no evil," Jer 44:17)
- Jeremiah's polemic shows the cult was popular and persistent — the women explicitly defend it

**Confidence:** HIGH that child sacrifice and Queen of Heaven cult existed. MEDIUM on interpretation as "crisis expressions of family piety."

**What's at stake:** Albertz's model of internal religious pluralism reaches its limit here. The very family piety that was supposed to be "pre-political and pre-moral" has become deeply syncretistic. The Deuteronomic reform (§3.8) will attempt to suppress these family-level practices — marking the first systematic attempt by official religion to control family piety.

---

## §3.7 Overall Assessment

**Strongest insight:** The paradox that Yahweh-name dominance and religious pluralism increased simultaneously. This is exactly what Albertz's model predicts (different strata, different logics) and what confuses models that treat "Yahwization of names" as evidence of monotheization.

**Weakest section:** The reconstruction of Assyrian religious influence on family practice is necessarily inferential from polemical sources.
