# Römer, The Invention of God — Chapter 3

## Moses and the Midianites

---

## Claim 1: Moses is a constructed figure with an Egyptian name — no extra-biblical evidence exists for him
→ [[claim-moses-constructed-figure-egyptian-name]] | **romer-inv-3.1** | Confidence: very-high
  Moses has zero extra-biblical attestation. His Egyptian name (from *msj*, "to engender") is rendered with archaic *sin* transcription, possibly reaching back to the second millennium. Two Egyptian parallels (Beya, Ben-Ozen) are plausible but neither fully matches. Moses is a construct combining multiple memory traces.
  Key edges: Supports [[claim-kenite-midianite-hypothesis-best-explanation]] (the Kenite hypothesis must work with the constructed figure). Depends on [[claim-yahweh-original-pronunciation-yaho-yahu]] (shared philological method).

## Claim 2: The Midianites were a real historical people in northwestern Arabia who may have mediated Yhwh worship to Israel
→ [[claim-midianites-historical-people-northwest-arabia]] | **romer-inv-3.2** | Confidence: medium-high
  Archaeological evidence (distinctive pottery, trade routes, Timna mining) confirms Midianites as a real historical group in NW Arabia. The Jethro narrative suggests they already worshipped Yhwh — but the biblical text's historical value is debated. If true, Yhwh was not revealed TO Israel through Moses but brought TO Israel FROM Midian.
  Key edges: Depends on [[claim-yhwh-originated-southern-deserts-edom-seir]]. Supports [[claim-four-biblical-poems-preserve-southern-origin-tradition]] and [[claim-kenite-midianite-hypothesis-best-explanation]].

## Claim 3: The Kenite/Midianite hypothesis is the best available explanation, but it remains unproven
→ [[claim-kenite-midianite-hypothesis-best-explanation]] | **romer-inv-3.3** | Confidence: low-medium
  The hypothesis connects Soleb evidence (Yhwh in the south) with the biblical narrative (Moses marries into Midianite priesthood) in a coherent chain. Elegant, plausible, underdetermined. Depends on treating literary narrative as historical memory — a tension Romer acknowledges.
  Key edges: Depends on [[claim-yhwh-originated-southern-deserts-edom-seir]], [[claim-four-biblical-poems-preserve-southern-origin-tradition]], [[claim-midianites-historical-people-northwest-arabia]]. Supports [[claim-bible-acknowledges-yhwh-foreign-origin]].

## Chapter 3 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Moses has no extra-biblical attestation | VERY HIGH | None — this is simply a fact |
| Moses' name is Egyptian, possibly archaic | HIGH | Name could be a literary choice, not a historical trace |
| The Midianites were a real historical group | MEDIUM-HIGH | Archaeological evidence is solid |
| The Kenite/Midianite hypothesis | LOW-MEDIUM | Depends on treating literary narrative as historical memory |
| Beya/Ben-Ozen as models for Moses | LOW | Parallels are interesting but impossible to verify |

**Strongest section:** The archaeological-geographic description of Midian — grounded in material evidence.

**Weakest section:** The Kenite hypothesis — elegant, unprovable, and in tension with Römer's own skepticism about the Moses narrative.

**The fundamental tension in Römer's project:** He wants the biblical texts to preserve genuine historical memories (so he can reconstruct pre-exilic history), but he also acknowledges they are literary artifice shaped by ideology. He can't have it both ways, and Chapter 3 is where this tension is most visible.
