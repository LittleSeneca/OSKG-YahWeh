# Cross — Preface + Part I: The Religion of Canaan and the God of Israel

---

## The Preface: Cross's Methodology

### Claim 1: Cross writes against BOTH Wellhausen's evolutionary idealism AND Kaufmann's radical uniqueness

**Cross's position:** He's methodologically between two extremes. Against Wellhausen: the older synthesis based on Hegelian idealism is "obstinately surviving" and must be cleared away. Against Kaufmann: "Kaufmann's insistence that Israelite religion 'was absolutely different from anything the pagan world ever knew' violates fundamental postulates of scientific historical method."

**Cross's alternative:** Israelite religion emerged from Canaanite culture in an "orderly set of relationships which follow the usual typological sequences of historical change." Novelty is real — but it must be described as transformation of existing materials, not creation ex nihilo. "Characteristic of the religion of Israel is a perennial and unrelaxed tension between the mythic and the historical."

**The "epic" as key genre:** "Perhaps the term 'epic' best designates the constitutive genre of Israel's religious expression. Epic in interpreting historical events combines mythic and historical features in various ways and proportions." This is Cross's signature concept: Israel took Canaanite MYTH and transformed it into historical EPIC. The mythic raw material (divine warrior battles chaos, divine council, sacred mountain) was historicized into Israel's story.

**Confidence:** HIGH that this is Cross's methodology and that it shaped the entire field.

**What's at stake:** Cross is the bridge between Albright (who argued for early Mosaic monotheism) and Smith (who argues for late evolutionary monotheism). Cross emphasizes CONTINUITY with Canaanite culture while preserving Israelite NOVELTY. His students (Smith, Friedman, Miller) took the continuity emphasis further than he did.

### The God of the Fathers (Chapter 1)

**Cross's claim:** The patriarchs worshipped a deity distinct from both Canaanite El and later Yahwistic religion. He called this deity "the God of the Fathers" — a personal clan god identified by the patriarch's name ("God of Abraham," "Fear of Isaac," "Mighty One of Jacob"). This was NOT the high god El of the Canaanite pantheon, but a distinct type of deity: a personal patron who protected a specific kinship group.

**The key evidence:** The patriarchal narratives consistently use epithet constructions: "God of Abraham" (*'ĕlōhê 'abrāhām*), "Fear of Isaac" (*paḥad yiṣḥāq*), "Mighty One of Jacob" (*'ăbîr ya'ăqōb*). These follow a Northwest Semitic pattern of naming a deity by the patriarch he protects.

**Why this matters:** Cross argues that the God of the Fathers was ORIGINALLY distinct from El — they were merged later when Israel identified the patriarchal God with the Canaanite high god. This merger was the first step in the process that eventually absorbed El entirely into Yahweh.

### 'El in the Ugaritic Pantheon (Chapter 2)

**Cross's claim:** Understanding Ugaritic El is essential for understanding Israelite El. Cross provides an exhaustive catalogue of El's characteristics, epithets, and roles.

**Key attributes of Ugaritic El:**
- **Aged and wise:** "Father of Years" (*'ab šnm*), grey-bearded patriarch
- **Creator:** "Creator of Creatures" (*bny bnwt*)
- **Compassionate:** "the Compassionate One" (*lṭpn 'il dpid*)
- **Royal:** King of the gods, enthroned at the source of the cosmic rivers
- **Distant:** Lives at the edge of the world, not directly involved in the mythic action
- **Fertile:** Still sexually active despite age

**The El-Ba'l dynamic:** El is the passive, distant high god. Ba'l is the active, young warrior. This pattern — old high god + young warrior god — is crucial for understanding Israel: Yahweh inherits El's kingship AND Ba'l's warrior attributes.

### Yahweh and 'El (Chapter 3)

**Cross's central claim:** Yahweh was originally NOT identified with El. The identification happened historically — it's visible in the biblical texts. "We can trace the process by which Yahweh came to be identified with El, absorbing El's characteristics and epithets."

**The evidence — El epithets applied to Yahweh:**

| El's Ugaritic Title | Hebrew Equivalent | Applied to Yahweh in |
|---------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| "Father of Years" | "Ancient of Days" | Daniel 7 |
| "Creator of Creatures" | "Creator of Heaven and Earth" | Genesis 14:19 |
| "The Compassionate One" | "Compassionate and Gracious" | Exodus 34:6 |
| El's divine council | Yahweh's divine council | Psalm 82, 1 Kings 22 |
| El's mountain abode | Yahweh on Sinai/Zion | Exodus 19, Psalm 48 |
| El's epithet *šadday* | *'Ēl Šadday* | Patriarchal narratives |

**The key text — Exodus 6:2-3:**
> "I am Yahweh. I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as El Shadday, but by my name Yahweh I did not make myself known to them."

**Cross's reading:** This text explicitly acknowledges that the patriarchs worshipped El, not Yahweh. The Priestly writer preserves the memory of a time when Yahweh was NOT known — and then identifies Yahweh with the patriarchal god. "The formula is a theological statement. It identifies Yahweh with El Shadday, the patriarchal god, but it distinguishes the old cultus from the new."

**The Name Yahweh (the etymology problem):**

Cross reviews the evidence:
- Yahweh is the primitive form, confirmed by 9th-century Mesha Stele and 8th-century seals
- The earliest attestation is Egyptian *yhw3* in 14th-13th century Shasu lists
- The name is a verbal form, probably causative: "He causes to be" / "He creates"
- Cross favors the reading: *yahweh ṣĕbā'ôt* = "He creates the (heavenly) armies"
- This makes Yahweh originally an epithet of El — El who creates the heavenly hosts
- "Yahweh is a liturgical title of El, specifically El as creator of the divine council"

**The profound implication:** Yahweh was not an independent god who later merged with El. Yahweh began as an EPITHET of El — one of El's titles. Over time, the epithet became a proper name, and the proper name displaced the original deity. What we call the "merger of Yahweh and El" was actually the elevation of one of El's titles to independent deity status. This is a radical claim even within the evolutionary consensus.

**Confidence:** HIGH on the El epithets being applied to Yahweh (textually demonstrable). MEDIUM on Cross's claim that Yahweh was originally an epithet of El (linguistically plausible but not provable). MEDIUM that the God of the Fathers was distinct from both El and Yahweh (Cross's typology is elegant but the evidence for three-way distinction is thin).

**What's at stake:** If Cross is right, "Yahweh" is not a foreign southern god's name. It's a TITLE of the Canaanite high god El that eventually became a proper name. The God of Israel is not a newcomer from the desert — he's an aspect of the old Canaanite creator god that broke free and became independent.

**Who disagrees:** Römer (Yahweh was a southern storm god, not originally an El epithet). Smith (Yahweh merged with El but originated independently). Tigay (the names show YHWH-dominance; El's name was already fading).

**My assessment:** Cross's philological argument is elegant but the evidence for "Yahweh as El-epithet" depends heavily on the etymology of the name — and as Römer showed (see our Römer Ch. 1 note), ALL etymologies of Yahweh are speculative. The stronger part of Cross's argument is the El epithets applied to Yahweh, which is textually verifiable regardless of the etymology.

## Part I Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|-----------|
| Israelite religion emerged from Canaanite culture | HIGH — Cross makes this case definitively |
| The God of the Fathers was a distinct deity type | MEDIUM — typologically elegant but thinly evidenced |
| El's epithets were absorbed by Yahweh | HIGH — textually demonstrable |
| Yahweh originated as an epithet of El | MEDIUM — depends on unprovable etymology |
| Exodus 6:2-3 preserves historical memory | MEDIUM-HIGH — the text is explicit |

**Cross's contribution:** He established the methodological framework that Smith, Römer, and Dever all work within: Israelite religion must be understood as growing organically from Canaanite culture. Continuity is the baseline; novelty must be demonstrated, not assumed. This is the intellectual foundation of the entire evolutionary consensus.
