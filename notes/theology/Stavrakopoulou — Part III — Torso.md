# Stavrakopoulou — Part III: Torso (Chapters 9-11)

---

## Chapter 9: Back and Beyond

### Claim: God had a back — Moses saw it — and it was made of divine radiance

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** Exodus 33:23 — "You will see my back, but my face shall not be seen" — is literal. God has a back. Moses glimpsed it. The story is not a metaphor for partial revelation. It describes a physical encounter with a deity whose body was dangerously luminous.

**Evidence:** The Exodus 33 theophany is the most detailed description of God's body in the Bible. God's "glory" (*kavod*) is his bodily radiance — so bright it must be screened by cloud and rock. His hand covers Moses during the passing. His back becomes visible as he departs. "The passage anatomizes the divine body in precise, sequential terms."

**Confidence:** HIGH. The text is unambiguously describing a body.

## Chapter 10: Inside Out

### Claim: God had internal organs — heart, lungs, belly — described in anatomical detail

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** "The biblical God is not only armoured in an outer layer of skin and glory, but constructed of inner organs too. His anatomy is interior as well as exterior."

**Evidence — God's internal organs catalogued:**
- **Heart** (*lēb*): God's heart grieves (Genesis 6:6), is moved to compassion (Hosea 11:8), plans and intends (Jeremiah 23:20)
- **Breath/Wind** (*rûaḥ*): God breathes — into Adam (Genesis 2:7), into the dry bones (Ezekiel 37), in storm theophany
- **Bowels/Womb** (*raḥămîm*): God's "compassion" derives from the word for womb/innards — his mercy is visceral
- **Belly** (*me'eh*): The seat of deep emotion — God's innards "roar" and "yearn"

**Confidence:** HIGH on the vocabulary (the Hebrew terms are anatomically specific). MEDIUM-HIGH on Stavrakopoulou's claim that these are literal rather than metaphorical (the terms CAN be used metaphorically, but the cumulative evidence supports embodiment).

## Chapter 11: From Belly to Bowel

### Claim: God's digestive and reproductive systems were part of his embodied reality

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** The biblical God eats, digests, and perhaps even defecates. He is a consuming fire — literally, a digestive fire. The sacrificial system is "the care and feeding of the gods" (Dever's phrase), with God consuming the offerings through fire.

**Evidence:** The sacrificial system as divine feeding. "A pleasing odor to Yahweh" (Leviticus 1:9, etc.) — God SMELLS the sacrifices. The "bread of the Presence" in the temple — literal food set before the deity. Ezekiel's vision of the temple includes kitchens for preparing God's meals.

**Confidence:** HIGH on the sacrificial system as divine feeding (standard scholarly reading). MEDIUM on the more provocative claims about defecation (Stavrakopoulou pushes further than the evidence clearly supports).
