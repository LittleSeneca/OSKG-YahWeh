# Stavrakopoulou — Part III: Torso (Chapters 9-11)

---

## Chapter 9: Back and Beyond

### Claim: God had a back — Moses saw it — and it was made of divine radiance
→ [[claim-god-back-moses-saw-divine-radiance-exodus-33]] | **stav-god-9-11.1** | Confidence: high
  Exodus 33 describes a physical encounter where Moses glimpsed God's back after his hand covered Moses. The passage anatomizes the divine body in precise, sequential terms — hand, face, back. God's *kavod* (glory) is his bodily radiance, so bright it must be screened.
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]]

## Chapter 10: Inside Out

### Claim: God had internal organs — heart, lungs, belly — described in anatomical detail
→ [[claim-god-internal-organs-heart-bowels-anatomical]] | **stav-god-9-11.2** | Confidence: high
  God's interior anatomy is catalogued: heart (*lēb*) grieves and plans, breath (*rûaḥ*) breathes into Adam, bowels/womb (*raḥămîm*) are the seat of compassion, belly (*me'eh*) roars with emotion. The Hebrew terms are anatomically specific. "His anatomy is interior as well as exterior."
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]]

## Chapter 11: From Belly to Bowel

### Claim: God's digestive and reproductive systems were part of his embodied reality
→ [[claim-god-ate-sacrifices-divine-digestion-temple-feeding]] | **stav-god-9-11.3** | Confidence: high
  The sacrificial system was "the care and feeding of the gods" (Dever). "A pleasing odor to Yahweh" describes God smelling sacrifices. The "bread of the Presence" was literal food set before the deity. Ezekiel's temple vision includes kitchens for preparing God's meals. God consumed offerings through fire — literally, digestive fire.
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]]
