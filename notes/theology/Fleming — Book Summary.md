# Fleming — Yahweh Before Israel: Book Summary

## The Argument in One Paragraph

Yahweh was not "taken from outsiders" (the Midianite/Kenite Hypothesis, dominant since 1872). The divine name is evidence for the diverse background of Israel itself. In the oldest evidence (Egyptian lists from c. 1400 BCE), Yhwʒ is a Shasu people-name, not a divine name. The Bible preserves traces of this pre-Israelite identity in its "people of Yahweh" ('am Yhwh, Judges 5:13). Yahweh was a god of people without kings, defined by identification with a specific community — like the South Arabian political gods Almaqah and 'Amm — not a storm god or a patriarch. Only later did he acquire the trappings of Baal, merge with El, and become the sole God of Israel.

## The Three Pillars Fleming Destroys

**Pillar 1 — Egyptian geography (Ch 2):** Seir was probably NOT in the original Amenhotep III Soleb list. Column N4 has room for only 9 names (4+5), not 10 (6+4). Without Seir, the sole geographical anchor for Yhwʒ disappears. The Shasu-land names are kinship-based people-groups (mhwt = "families"), not towns or sanctuaries. Yhwʒ is a people, not a place and not a god.

**Pillar 2 — Biblical prose (Ch 3):** Exodus 3 and 6 do not preserve historical memory of Yahweh being unknown before Moses. They are schematic texts bridging Genesis and Exodus — theological bridge-work, not ancient tradition. Exodus 18 does not show Jethro as priest of Yahweh — he responds to demonstrated power, he doesn't introduce the god. Tiele (1872), not Ghillany, created the hypothesis, and his original was Kenite/Rechabite, not Moses-Midian.

**Pillar 3 — Old poetry (Ch 4):** Yahweh's march from Seir/Edom/Paran/Teman is theophanic movement from a distant divine home, not evidence of historical origin. The Ugaritic Baal myth provides the analogy: gods live on distant mountains and travel from them. Yahweh is no more "from" Seir than Baal is "from" Sapan. The shared core of Judges 5 and Psalm 68 lacks Seir/Edom — they are secondary elaborations.

## The Positive Case

1. **Yhwʒ as people-name (Ch 2, 5):** The verbal form y-h-wʒ fits the pattern of Amorite shortened personal names. Peoples named after persons are common (Israel = yiśrā'ēl, Yamutbal, etc.). The name is a people-name, not a divine name.

2. **'Am Yhwh as pre-Israelite identity (Ch 6):** The Song of Deborah's battle account knows an alliance called "the people of Yahweh" WITHOUT reference to Israel. "Israel" is imposed only by the later introductory hymn. Exodus 15:16 independently attests the same pattern: "your people, Yahweh."

3. **South Arabian analogies (Ch 7):** The "Descendants of 'Amm" / "Descendants of Almaqah" in South Arabia show how gods could bind communities through kinship, with the king latching onto pre-existing conceptions. The "people of Kemosh" in Moab (Num 21:29) shows the same pattern closer to Israel.

## What Survives from the Midianite Hypothesis

Fleming does not reject everything. He affirms:
- The Shasu connection is real and central
- Israel had genuine kinship with inland/southern pastoralist peoples
- Yahweh was "before Israel" chronologically and socially
- The southern wilderness is theologically significant (divine dwelling)
- Yahweh was not originally Canaanite/urban

## Key Scholarly Positions Engaged

|---------|----------|-------------------|
| Römer (2015) | Most sophisticated MH defense | Dismantled on prose, poetry, Egyptian evidence |

| Smith (2001a, 2014) | Complex MH, old poetry as memory | Shared redaction-critical insights; disagrees on 'am Yhwh |

## Assessment

**Strongest contribution:** The Soleb column measurement argument (Seir probably absent) is genuinely original and potentially field-changing if verified by Egyptologists.

**Weakest link:** The 'am Yhwh argument depends on a specific reading of Judges 5 that Mark Smith — Fleming's most important dialogue partner — disputes.

**Most underappreciated:** The South Arabian analogies (Ch 7) open up genuinely new comparative possibilities neglected in Yahweh-origins scholarship.

**Overall:** The most important contribution to the Yahweh-origins debate since Römer (2015) and arguably the most original since Cross (1973). Even where Fleming fails to persuade, he has permanently changed the terms of the debate by demonstrating that the Midianite Hypothesis rests on foundations far weaker than its longevity suggests.
