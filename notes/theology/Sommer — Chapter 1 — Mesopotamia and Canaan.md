# Sommer — Chapter 1

## Fluidity of Divine Embodiment and Selfhood: Mesopotamia and Canaan

---

## Claim 1: ANE gods had "fluid selves" — multiple local manifestations with partial independence
→ [[claim-ane-gods-fluid-selves-multiple-local-manifestations]] | **sommer-bog-1.1** | Confidence: high
  Ishtar of Arbela, Ishtar of Nineveh, and Venus-Ishtar were grammatically distinct goddesses yet functionally "one and the same" — demonstrating that ANE deities could be simultaneously distinct and identical.
  Supports: [[claim-je-sources-preserved-ane-fluidity-model-yhwh-multiple-manifestations]], [[claim-divine-fluidity-cross-cuts-monotheism-polytheism-distinction]]

---

## Claim 2: ANE gods had MULTIPLE BODIES — cult statues were the god, not representations of the god
→ [[claim-cult-statues-were-the-god-not-representations-mis-pi]] | **sommer-bog-1.2** | Confidence: very-high
  After the mīs pî and pīt pî rituals, the ṣalmu WAS the god — not a symbol or dwelling place. A deity could have multiple simultaneous embodiments: cultic (statue), cosmic (astral body), and mythic-narrative body.
  Supports: [[claim-je-sources-preserved-ane-fluidity-model-yhwh-multiple-manifestations]], [[claim-priestly-deuteronomistic-sources-rejected-fluidity-model]]

---

## Claim 3: ANE fluidity was ABSENT from ancient Greece — the key religious divide isn't mono/polytheism
→ [[claim-ane-fluidity-absent-greece-mono-poly-less-fundamental]] | **sommer-bog-1.3** | Confidence: medium-high
  Greek gods had discrete bodies and discrete selves LIKE HUMANS; ANE gods were fundamentally UNLIKE humans in their fluidity. The mono/poly divide is less fundamental than the fluid/non-fluid divide.
  Supports: [[claim-divine-fluidity-cross-cuts-monotheism-polytheism-distinction]]
