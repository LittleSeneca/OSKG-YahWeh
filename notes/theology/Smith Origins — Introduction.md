# Smith — Origins of Biblical Monotheism: Introduction

---

## Claim 1: The Bible's own narrative — that monotheism was revealed at Sinai — is contradicted by evidence within the Bible itself

**Smith's claim:** "According to the story of the Bible, monotheism was not the original condition of the world. Instead, it stepped onto the world stage with the appearance of Israel... the revelation of the one God known by the one name of the Tetragrammaton." But "various passages in the Bible suggest a more complicated picture lying behind the more dominant narrative of Israelite monotheism. Signs of polytheism are apparent."

**Evidence:** Exodus 15:11: "Who is like You among the gods, O Yahweh?" — the question "rather neutrally implies the existence of other deities." Other biblical passages "contain vestiges of Israelite polytheism."

**Smith's project:** This book is about the CULTURAL AND INTELLECTUAL PROCESSES by which monotheism emerged FROM polytheism. It's not about whether monotheism is true — it's about HOW it came to be.

**Confidence:** HIGH that the Bible contains polytheistic vestiges (this is the starting point of the entire critical enterprise).

**What's at stake:** Smith frames his project as the sequel to *The Early History of God*. That book covered convergence and differentiation (HOW Yahweh absorbed other gods). This book covers monotheism's emergence (WHY Israel moved from many gods to one).

---

## Claim 2: "Canaanite" is a problematic term — but Ugaritic texts remain essential

**Smith's claim:** "The term 'Canaanite' is a modern scholarly convenience that flattens significant diversity." But the Ugaritic texts from the 14th-13th centuries BCE remain the single most important comparative corpus for understanding Israelite religion. They provide the "polytheistic background" of the subtitle.

**The Ugaritic advantage:** Unlike later Phoenician or Punic texts, Ugaritic literature preserves full mythological narratives — the Baal Cycle, the Kirta Epic, the Aqhat Legend — that show how a West Semitic pantheon actually functioned. These are the texts that illuminate the divine council, the divine family, and the conceptual structures Israel inherited.

**Confidence:** HIGH on the importance of Ugaritic texts (universally recognized). HIGH on the problems with "Canaanite" as a category.

---

## Claim 3: Monotheism must be defined carefully — it's not just "belief in one God"

**Smith's claim:** The terms "monotheism," "polytheism," "monolatry," and "henotheism" carry modern baggage. Smith uses "monotheism" to mean the explicit denial of other gods' existence — the position of Second Isaiah (Isa 43:10: "Before me no god was formed, nor shall there be any after me"). Earlier Israelite religion was polytheistic (multiple gods exist) or monolatrous (only Yahweh should be WORSHIPPED), but not monotheistic (only Yahweh EXISTS).

**The key distinction:** "Polytheism" = many gods. "Monolatry" = many gods exist, but only one is worshipped. "Monotheism" = only one god EXISTS. The Bible moves through all three stages.

**Confidence:** HIGH that these definitions are useful. MEDIUM that they precisely map onto the historical development (the boundaries between stages are blurry).

**Who disagrees:** Heiser would say the Bible was always monotheistic but acknowledged OTHER *elohim* (created divine beings, not rival gods). Tigay would say the data suggest widespread monolatry, not polytheism.
