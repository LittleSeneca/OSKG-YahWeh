# Heiser — Part 5: Conquest and Failure (Chapters 21-25)

---

## Chapter 21: God's Law, God's Council

### Claim: The Law was given in the presence of the divine council — they were witnesses to the covenant

**Heiser's claim:** "The link between the law and the heavenly council is noted several times in the New Testament, which uses for the divine council the umbrella term 'angels.'" Acts 7:53 says the law was "delivered by angels." Hebrews 2:2 says it was "declared by angels." This is not a New Testament innovation — it comes from the Old Testament, specifically the Septuagint version of Deuteronomy 33.

**Evidence — the Septuagint's reading of Deut 33:1-4:**
The Masoretic Text has Yahweh coming from Sinai alone. The Septuagint has "angels" (divine beings) accompanying him. The LXX was the Bible of the early church — Acts and Hebrews draw on it. "There are passages that describe angels at Sinai, but none of them specifically reference the law" — unless you're reading the Septuagint.

**Psalm 68:15-18 as additional evidence:**
> "The chariots of God are twice ten thousand, with thousands doubled. The Lord is among them at Sinai, distinctive in victory."

Tens of thousands of divine chariots. Yahweh among them. At Sinai. This is the setting of the law-giving — a cosmic assembly, not a private mountain meeting.

**The theological implication:** "The link between the law and the heavenly council becomes a key part of Paul's theology of the law in Galatians 3:19 ('ordained through angels')." The law was mediated by divine beings. It was never meant to be the final word — it was a temporary arrangement administered by the council, later superseded by Christ.

**Confidence:** HIGH that the LXX reads divine beings at Deut 33. HIGH that Acts and Hebrews draw on this tradition. MEDIUM on Heiser's specific claim that this was "a key part of Paul's theology" (Paul mentions it once in Galatians).

**What's at stake:** The Law — the Torah, the foundation of Judaism — was not delivered directly by God in private to Moses. It was delivered in a cosmic assembly, witnessed by divine beings, and administered by them. This demystifies the Law while simultaneously embedding it in a richer supernatural framework.

## Chapter 22: Realm Distinction

### Claim: Holiness is about SEPARATION between realms, not primarily morality

**Heiser's claim:** "The Hebrew vocabulary for holiness means to be set apart or to be distinct. While the idea has a moral dimension related to conduct, it is not intrinsically about morality. It is about distinction." Yahweh is not of earth — a place of death, disease, and imperfection. His realm is supernatural. To enter his space requires purification from everything associated with mortality.

**Evidence — the logic of the purity laws:**
- Sexual fluids, uterine discharges, menstruation: "the body had lost the fluids that contain, create, and sustain life. That which is not whole and is associated with loss of life cannot enter Yahweh's presence"
- Physical handicaps, disease, corpse contact: same logic — death and defect cannot be in the presence of life and perfection
- "Sexual intercourse... was not considered unclean out of prudishness"

**The tabernacle as microcosm:** "As the divine abode, the tabernacle was also analogous to Eden... the place where heaven and earth met, a veritable microcosm of the Edenic creation where God first dwelt on earth." The tabernacle's graded holiness zones (court → holy place → holy of holies) mapped the cosmic distance between divine and human realms. The high priest entering the holy of holies was ritually enacting a journey from earth to heaven.

**Confidence:** HIGH that the Hebrew root *qdš* means "set apart/distinct." MEDIUM-HIGH that the purity laws are fundamentally about realm distinction rather than morality (this is a scholarly consensus reading but not the only one).

**What's at stake:** The Torah's "arbitrary" laws (don't mix fabrics, don't eat shellfish, wash after sex) are not arbitrary — they're a system of boundary markers between realms. This makes sense of laws modern readers find incomprehensible. It also means the moral content of the Law is secondary to the spatial/cosmic content.

## Chapter 23: Giant Problems

### Claim: The conquest was about eliminating Nephilim bloodlines — not ethnic cleansing

**Heiser's claim:** "The conquest of Canaan was not about invasion of land belonging to other people. It was about taking back land Yahweh had claimed for his own from the descendants of the Nephilim, the giant clan bloodlines that had survived the flood." The *ḥērem* (total destruction) commands target specific bloodlines — the Anakim, Rephaim, Zamzummim — not generic Canaanite populations.

**Evidence — the bloodlines the Bible specifically targets:**
- Numbers 13:28-33: "We saw the Nephilim there (the descendants of Anak came from the Nephilim)"
- Deuteronomy 2:10-11: "The Emim... were also regarded as Rephaim, as the Anakim"
- Deuteronomy 2:20-21: "It is also regarded as a land of Rephaim; Rephaim formerly lived there"
- Joshua 11:21-22: "Joshua came at that time and cut off the Anakim from the hill country... No Anakim were left in the land of the people of Israel; only in Gaza, in Gath, and in Ashdod did some remain"

**The Goliath connection:** "Goliath was from Gath (1 Sam 17:4). He was a descendant of the Anakim" — survivors of Joshua's campaign. The Philistine champion wasn't just a tall human. He was a remnant of the divine-human hybrid bloodlines.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. The biblical texts DO name specific giant clans. But many *ḥērem* commands target generic "Canaanites." Heiser's reading explains the targeted passages but doesn't fully account for the broader conquest narrative.

**What's at stake:** Heiser is trying to solve the moral problem of the conquest — God commanding genocide. His solution: it wasn't genocide of humans; it was elimination of non-fully-human hybrid bloodlines corrupted by divine transgression. Whether this actually resolves the moral problem is debatable. Whether it's true to the texts is also debatable — the Bible says "men, women, and children" were killed, not just giants.

**Who disagrees:** Virtually every critic of the conquest narrative would say Heiser is creating a supernatural framework to justify morally indefensible texts.

## Chapter 24: The Place of the Serpent

### Claim: The serpent in Eden was a divine being (*nāḥāš*), not a talking snake

**Heiser's claim:** The Hebrew word *nāḥāš* used in Genesis 3 is not the normal word for "snake." It's related to words meaning "diviner" and "shining one." The serpent was a divine being — specifically, one of the *elohim* who rebelled in the garden — not a literal snake that God cursed to crawl on its belly.

**Evidence:**
- The same root (*nḥš*) appears in contexts of divination and sorcery (Numbers 23:23; 24:1)
- Isaiah 27:1: "Leviathan the fleeing serpent (*nāḥāš bāriaḥ*), Leviathan the twisting serpent" — a cosmic dragon, not a garden snake
- Revelation 12:9: "the great dragon... that ancient serpent (*ophis*), who is called the devil and Satan" — the serpent is identified as Satan across the canon
- The curse "on your belly you will go" describes a cosmic being humiliated, not a biological change to reptiles

**The *nāḥāš* as throne guardian:** In ANE iconography, serpentine beings (seraphim, cherubim) guarded divine thrones. The *nāḥāš* in Eden was likely a throne guardian who rebelled — an insider, not an intruder. "The enemy in the garden was a supernatural being, not a member of the animal kingdom."

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH on *nāḥāš* as a divine being rather than a literal snake (most critical scholars agree the serpent is supernatural). MEDIUM on Heiser's specific "throne guardian" reading.

## Chapter 25: Holy War

### Claim: *Ḥērem* was cosmic warfare — reclaiming territory from corrupt divine beings

**Heiser's claim:** The conquest was Yahweh reclaiming the nations from the corrupt *sons of God* to whom they'd been assigned at Babel. The *ḥērem* (devotion to destruction) was a ritual act of cosmic warfare, not ethnic cleansing. The destruction was directed at the spiritual powers behind the nations, not the people per se.

**Evidence:** The language of "driving out" in Deuteronomy and Joshua uses cosmic-warfare terminology. The "hornet" Yahweh sends ahead of Israel (Exodus 23:28; Deuteronomy 7:20; Joshua 24:12) is a metaphor for supernatural intervention. The ban on plunder (*ḥērem*) marks the conquered goods as belonging to Yahweh alone — like firstfruits, but total.

**The ethical problem Heiser is trying to solve:** "The conquest of Canaan is one of the most troubling portions of the Old Testament. In the effort to protect God's goodness, many Christians have sought to deny that God ordered the killing of noncombatants or to suggest that the conquest never really happened. These approaches either dismiss the biblical text or take the edge off its straightforward meaning. In my judgment, we need not fear the text."

**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM. Heiser's framework is coherent within his larger system, but it requires reading the conquest narratives through the lens of the Deut 32 worldview. The texts themselves don't say "we're killing giants, not humans." They say "devote to destruction men and women, young and old" (Joshua 6:21). Heiser tries to restrict this to Nephilim bloodlines, but the text doesn't cooperate.

**What's at stake:** This is where Heiser's framework faces its hardest test. If his reading of the conquest fails, it doesn't necessarily fail his divine council thesis — but it shows the limits of using the Deut 32 worldview as a universal hermeneutical key.

**My assessment:** Heiser's reading of specific giant passages is plausible. His attempt to extend this to a general theory of the conquest is strained. The ethical problem of *ḥērem* remains, regardless of the cosmic framework.
