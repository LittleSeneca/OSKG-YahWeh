# Smith, Early History of God — Chapter 1

## Deities in Israel in the Period of the Judges

---

## Claim 1: El was the original god of Israel; Yahweh was imported later
→ [[claim-el-original-god-israel-yahweh-imported-later]] | **smith-ehg-1.1** | Confidence: medium-high
  The cumulative case: the name "Israel" contains El, Genesis 49 separates El epithets from Yahweh, Deut 32:8-9 shows Yahweh as one of El's sons, El was never polemicized against, and El's traits were systematically transferred to Yahweh.
  Supports: smith-obm-7.1-7.7, day-ygc-1.1-1.6, smith-ehg-3.1. Depends on: smith-ehg-intro.1.

## Claim 2: Baal and Yahweh coexisted peacefully in the Judges period
→ [[claim-baal-yahweh-coexisted-judges-period-no-conflict]] | **smith-ehg-1.2** | Confidence: medium
  The Deuteronomistic anti-Baal polemic was retrojected from the ninth century. Saul's family had both Yahwistic and Baal names (later censored: Eshbaal → Ishboshet). The conflict was real but LATER than the Bible depicts.
  Related to: day-ygc-3.4 (boshet substitution), day-ygc-3.1 (one cosmic Baal). Supports: smith-ehg-2.1.

## Claim 3: Early poetry shows convergence of multiple deities' imagery into Yahweh
→ [[claim-early-poetry-shows-convergence-multiple-deities-to-yahweh]] | **smith-ehg-1.3** | Confidence: high
  Deut 33 mixes Baal and El language in adjacent verses. Ps 18 conflates Baal's thundering with El's title and cosmic waters. Gen 49:25 preserves goddess epithets. The convergence was creative synthesis, not polemic.
  Supports: smith-ehg-2.2, smith-ehg-3.1, smith-ehg-4.1, smith-ehg-4.2, smith-ehg-2.3, smith-ehg-3.4.

## Claim 4: Asherah is poorly attested as a goddess in the Judges period
→ [[claim-asherah-poorly-attested-judges-yahwistic-symbol]] | **smith-ehg-1.4** | Confidence: low
  The asherah was a Yahwistic cult symbol, not a goddess's representation. Judges 6 mentions only the pole, not the goddess. Taanach cult stand's goddess is probably Astarte. Smith is probably wrong; majority of scholars disagree.
  Contradicts: romer-inv-9.3, day-ygc-2.1, day-ygc-2.3, romer-inv-9.1. Supports: smith-ehg-3.1, smith-ehg-3.2, smith-ehg-foreword.2.

## Claim 5: Israel's pantheon was typical for the first-millennium Levant
→ [[claim-israel-pantheon-typical-for-region]] | **smith-ehg-1.5** | Confidence: medium
  Ammon, Moab, Edom, and Phoenician states all had ~10-deity pantheons headed by a national god. The regional trend from 200+ gods (Ugarit, 2nd millennium) to ~10 gods (1st millennium) was universal. Israel took it to its extreme: one god.
  Contextualizes all polytheism claims. Comparative data is sparse but useful for countering both "always monotheistic" and "uniquely depraved" narratives.

## Cross-Cutting Assessment: Chapter 1's Overall Strength

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| El was original god; Yahweh imported | MEDIUM-HIGH | Schmid's late dating (if texts are Persian, this reconstruction collapses) |
| Baal coexisted peacefully in Judges period | MEDIUM | Alternative reading of Baal-names as genuine syncretism |
| Early poetry shows convergence | HIGH | Dating of the poems (if late, convergence is late too) |
| Asherah was symbol, not goddess | LOW | Majority scholarly disagreement |
| Pantheon typical for region | MEDIUM | Sparse comparative data |

**The single biggest threat to this entire chapter:** Konrad Schmid's argument that the Pentateuch and early poetry are Persian-period compositions. If Smith is wrong about the dating of his sources, the entire reconstruction of "early Israelite religion" is built on texts that don't describe early Israelite religion. Smith is aware of this challenge (he mentions it in the preface) but doesn't resolve it.
