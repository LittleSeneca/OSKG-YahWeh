# Stavrakopoulou — Part II: Genitals (Chapters 5-8)

---

## Chapter 5: Cover Up

### Claim: God's genitals were deliberately obscured by Bible translators — but the texts preserve them

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** Biblical translators have systematically covered up references to God's genitals. Words that clearly refer to male genitalia are translated with euphemisms. "This is not prudishness for its own sake; it is a theological reflex. To grant that God had genitals is to grant that God had a sexed body — a male body — and all the problematic implications that follow."

**Evidence — the offending words and their translations:**
- Hebrew *yārēk* consistently means "thigh, loins, genitals" (used of Abraham's servant placing his hand "under Abraham's thigh" = oath on the genitals in Genesis 24:2). When applied to God (Exodus 33:21-23), translations render it "rock" or "cleft" rather than "loins"
- Ezekiel's vision of God: "a form that had the appearance of a man. Downward from what appeared to be his loins" (*motnayim* + *yārēk* — waist-to-genital region). Translations obscure the anatomical precision.

**Confidence:** HIGH that the Hebrew terms refer to the genital region. HIGH that translations obscure this. Stavrakopoulou's philological case is strong.

**What's at stake:** If God has genitals, God has a SEX. The God of the Bible is male — not metaphorically, but anatomically. This has profound implications for gender, sexuality, and the construction of religious authority.

## Chapter 6: Phallic Masculinities

### Claim: God's masculinity was performed through phallic imagery — size, power, virility

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** "God's body conforms to the gendered ideals of the ancient world: he is muscular, virile, physically powerful — and his genitals perform accordingly."

**Evidence:** God's "right hand" and "holy arm" (Isaiah 52:10) are phallic proxies — the arm/hand as extension of male power. God's "mighty deeds" are displays of masculine potency. His "glory" (*kavod*) is both luminous radiance AND bodily substance — the same word used for the "weight" of male honor and physical presence.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH on the phallic imagery (the ANE context supports this reading). MEDIUM on some specific interpretations (Stavrakopoulou reads phallic symbolism where others might see general power imagery).

## Chapter 7: Perfecting the Penis

### Claim: Circumcision marked Israelite males as belonging to a god with a penis

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** Circumcision was a bodily mark that identified Israelite men with their male deity. "By cutting the foreskin, Israelite males were made in the image of their circumcised god." This is why circumcision was THE covenant sign — it physically marked males as belonging to the male deity's people.

**Evidence:**
- Genesis 17: Circumcision is the sign of the covenant — permanent, bodily, on the male organ
- Exodus 4:24-26: The bizarre story of Zipporah circumcising her son and touching Moses' "feet" (= genitals) with the foreskin — "a bridegroom of blood" — "the episode makes sense only if circumcision is understood as marking the male body as belonging to the male deity"
- The covenant sign is on the penis because the deity HAS a penis

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH on circumcision as identification with a male deity. MEDIUM on the specific claim that God was understood to be circumcised.

**What's at stake:** The Abrahamic covenant — the foundation of Jewish identity — is literally inscribed on the male penis. It's a bodily mark of belonging to a MALE god. The exclusion of women from the covenant sign (no female circumcision requirement) reflects the gendered nature of the deity. God's masculinity structures the very terms of belonging.

## Chapter 8: Divine Sex

### Claim: God had sex — with his consort Asherah, and metaphorically with Israel

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** God was not celibate. He had a wife (Asherah) and the relationship was understood in sexual terms. The prophetic metaphor of Israel as God's unfaithful wife (Hosea, Ezekiel) draws on the cultural reality of a married deity.

**Evidence:**
- Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom: "Yahweh and his Asherah" — paired as divine couple
- Hosea 1-3: Yahweh as betrayed husband, Israel/Gomer as unfaithful wife — the metaphor makes sense because the deity IS understood as a sexual being
- Ezekiel 16 and 23: Explicitly sexualized allegories of God's relationship with Jerusalem — "your breasts were formed, your hair had grown... I spread my cloak over you and covered your nakedness; I pledged myself to you and entered into a covenant with you, declares the Lord Yahweh, and you became mine"

**Confidence:** HIGH on the Asherah pairing (epigraphic evidence). HIGH on the sexualized prophetic imagery (textually explicit). MEDIUM on whether God was understood to have literal sex (the imagery is clearly sexual; whether it was understood literally or metaphorically is debated).
