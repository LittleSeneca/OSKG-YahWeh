# Stavrakopoulou — Part II: Genitals (Chapters 5-8)

---

## Chapter 5: Cover Up

### Claim: God's genitals were deliberately obscured by Bible translators — but the texts preserve them
→ [[claim-god-genitals-deliberately-obscured-bible-translators]] | **stav-god-5-8.1** | Confidence: high
  Hebrew *yārēk* ("thigh, loins, genitals") and *motnayim* (waist-to-genital region) are translated with euphemisms when applied to God. "This is not prudishness for its own sake; it is a theological reflex."
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]]

## Chapter 6: Phallic Masculinities

### Claim: God's masculinity was performed through phallic imagery — size, power, virility
→ [[claim-god-masculinity-phallic-imagery-divine-virility]] | **stav-god-5-8.2** | Confidence: medium-high
  God's "right hand" and "holy arm" are phallic proxies; his "mighty deeds" are displays of masculine potency; his "glory" (*kavod*) connotes both radiance and the bodily "weight" of male honor.

## Chapter 7: Perfecting the Penis

### Claim: Circumcision marked Israelite males as belonging to a god with a penis
→ [[claim-circumcision-covenant-mark-male-deity-identification]] | **stav-god-5-8.3** | Confidence: medium-high
  "By cutting the foreskin, Israelite males were made in the image of their circumcised god." The covenant sign is on the penis because the deity HAS a penis. Genesis 17 and Exodus 4:24-26 ("bridegroom of blood").

## Chapter 8: Divine Sex

### Claim: God had sex — with his consort Asherah, and metaphorically with Israel
→ [[claim-god-had-sex-consort-asherah-prophetic-marriage-metaphor]] | **stav-god-5-8.4** | Confidence: medium-high
  Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom: "Yahweh and his Asherah" as divine couple. Hosea 1-3 and Ezekiel 16, 23: sexualized prophetic metaphor. The metaphor works because the deity IS understood as a sexual being.

## Part II Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|-----------|
| God's genitals obscured by translators | HIGH |
| God's body is male — not metaphorically | HIGH |
| Circumcision = identification with male deity | MEDIUM-HIGH |
| God had sex (consort and/or metaphorically) | MEDIUM-HIGH |

**Part II is the most provocative section of the book.** Stavrakopoulou deliberately pushes the reader to confront the full implications of an embodied God. If God has a body, that body has genitals. If God has genitals, God has a sex. If God has a sex, the entire architecture of gendered religious authority (male priesthood, male language for God, male covenant signs) is built on a literal anatomical fact, not a metaphor.
