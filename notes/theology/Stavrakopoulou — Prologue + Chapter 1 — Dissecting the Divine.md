## Chapter 1: Dissecting the Divine

### Claim 1: The biblical God had a literal, physical body — and this is the CONSENSUS reading, not a fringe position
→ [[claim-biblical-god-literal-physical-body-consensus]] | **stav-god-pro-1.2** | Confidence: very-high
  Comprehensive catalogue of God's body parts drawn from the Bible itself: head, hair, face, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, arms, hands, legs, feet, chest, back, heart, tongue, teeth, genitals. God walked, ate, fought, and had a supersized humanoid body.
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]], [[claim-genesis-1-26-anti-fluid-manifesto-image-without-embodiment]]

### Claim 2: The incorporeal God is a Greek philosophical invention — not a biblical idea
→ [[claim-incorporeal-god-greek-philosophical-invention-not-biblical]] | **stav-god-pro-1.3** | Confidence: high
  Plato, Aristotle, and Xenophanes introduced the idea of an incorporeal, abstract deity that gradually shaped Jewish and Christian thought toward the close of the first millennium BCE. The God of Western theism is a Greek philosophical construct, not the embodied deity of the Bible.
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]], [[claim-genesis-1-26-anti-fluid-manifesto-image-without-embodiment]]

### Claim 3: The Genesis creation account reflects a polytheistic divine council — God addresses other deities
→ [[claim-genesis-1-26-divine-council-consensus-reading]] | **stav-god-pro-1.4** | Confidence: high
  Genesis 1:26 ("Let US make humankind in OUR image") depicts a polytheistic divine council. This is the scholarly consensus, not a fringe position. God is one deity among others, the chief of a pantheon.
  Supports: [[claim-divine-council-pervasive-biblical-reality-not-pagan-import]], [[claim-deut-32-8-9-el-elyon-superior-yahweh-divine-son]]
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]]

**Stavrakopoulou's rhetorical strategy:** She positions herself as the honest reader cutting through centuries of theological censorship. The facts are on her side — the texts DO describe an embodied God. But her tone (scandalous, provocative) is designed to unsettle, not just inform. She's writing for a popular audience that will be shocked by what she reports, not for scholars who already know it.

**Relationship to Sommer:** Same data, different framework. Sommer: fluid embodiment — God has MULTIPLE bodies, overlapping selves. Stavrakopoulou: one literal body — God is a supersized humanoid. The difference matters: Sommer's reading allows for monotheism with complexity; Stavrakopoulou's reading pushes toward a more radically physical God that is harder to reconcile with later theology.
