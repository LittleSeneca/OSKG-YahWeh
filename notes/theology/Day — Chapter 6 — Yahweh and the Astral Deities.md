# Day — Chapter 6: Yahweh and the Astral Deities (Sun, Moon, and Lucifer)

Day examines the astral cult in Israel. His key arguments: the sun and moon worship in Judah was native Canaanite, not Assyrian import; Yahweh was NOT equated with the sun (contra a growing scholarly trend); Samson is a solar hero behind the legend; Sheger is a demythologized Canaanite moon god; and the famous "Lucifer" passage (Isa 14:12-15) reflects a Canaanite myth about Athtar/Venus attempting to usurp Baal's throne on Mt Zaphon, applied polemically to Nebuchadnezzar's destruction of Jerusalem.

---

## Claim 1: The sun cult in Judah was native Canaanite, not Assyrian import

**Author's claim:** "Various pieces of evidence suggest that the sun cult was Canaanite rather than Assyrian" (p. 152). Against the view that Manasseh's astral cults and Josiah's reform targeted Assyrian-influenced worship.

**Evidence presented:**
1. **All other cults Josiah removed were Canaanite:** Baal, Asherah, Molech (2 Kgs 23). "Consequently, unless there is strong evidence to the contrary, we should expect the sun and astral cults to be Canaanite as well" (p. 153)
2. **Place names:** Beth-Shemesh "house of the sun" (5 sites across Palestine), En-Shemesh "spring of the sun," Timnath-heres "portion of the sun" (later distorted to Timnath-serah to avoid polytheistic allusion). "A higher number of theophoric place names than those of any deity apart from El and Baal" (p. 153)
3. **Thutmose III's Karnak list** (15th c. BCE): Shamash-Edom — showing sun cult in Palestine predates Assyrian influence by centuries
4. **Taanach cult stand** (10th c. BCE): Horse and sun disc on top tier — well before Assyrian period
5. **Hazor horse's head** (c. 925-905 BCE): Disc symbol on forehead — "the association of the horse with the sun had clearly already been a feature of Syro-Palestinian religion for several centuries" (p. 154). 2 Kgs 23:11 mentions "horses and chariots of the sun" removed from temple
6. **Josh 10:12-13 is NOT evidence of sun/moon cult:** The sun "standing still" at Gibeon reflects a thunderstorm blotting out sun and moon (cf. Hab 3:10-11), not a cult. Day provides an extensive footnote surveying alternatives (p. 155-157)

**Confidence:** HIGH. The pre-Assyrian archaeological evidence (Taanach, Hazor) and place names make the Canaanite origin clear. The argument from Josiah's reform context (all other removed cults were Canaanite) is methodologically sound.

**What's at stake:** If the sun cult was native Canaanite, Israel's solar worship wasn't late foreign contamination but early indigenous religion. The Deuteronomistic opposition to sun worship wasn't anti-Assyrian polemic — it was anti-Canaanite.

**Alternative reading:** Spieckermann, Oestreicher, Gressmann — Assyrian influence on Manasseh's astral cults. Day counters effectively with contextual evidence.

**My assessment:** The Taanach and Hazor evidence is decisive for the pre-Assyrian date. Day's point that "Shamash" would have been used if Mesopotamian, but the OT always uses *šemeš* — the Canaanite term — supports the argument by implication.

## Claim 2: Yahweh was NOT equated with the sun (contra Taylor, Stähli, Niehr, Keel/Uehlinger)

**Author's claim:** Despite a growing body of scholarship arguing for solar Yahwism, "it is clear that Yahweh is not here being seen as the sun, but rather as one greater than, and transcending, the sun" (p. 161). The sun was part of Yahweh's entourage (the host of heaven = sons of God), not Yahweh himself.

**Evidence presented (refuting solar equation arguments):**
1. **East-west temple orientation:** Non-solar ANE temples also had this. The Solomonic temple was "completely covered over, which is not suggestive of a solar temple" (p. 158). Van Dyke Parunak's astronomical calculations: the sun did NOT penetrate the Holy of Holies at equinoxes
2. **1 Kgs 8:12 (LXX):** "The Lord has set the sun in the heavens, but he has said that he would dwell in thick darkness." Yahweh is CONTRASTED with the sun, not identified with it. "Thick darkness" (*ʻărāpel*) is storm-god imagery (clouds), not solar (p. 158-159)
3. **Sun cult in temple (2 Kgs 23:5, 11; Ezek 8:16):** The sun was worshipped alongside moon and host of heaven. "Hardly anyone seriously believes that Yahweh was equated with them." The host of heaven = Yahweh's heavenly court (sons of God). The sun "most naturally belonged in the same category" — subordinate, part of the entourage (p. 159)
4. **Ps 84:12 (ET 11):** "The Lord is a sun (*šemeš*) and a shield." But *šemeš* can also mean "rampart" (attested in Isa 54:12). The parallelism with "shield" (*māgēn*) supports "rampart," not "sun." Elsewhere "shield" parallels "fortress" (*meṣûdâ*) and "stronghold" (*miśgāb*) — never "sun" (p. 159-160)
5. **Theophany language (*zrḥ* and *ypʻ*):** Used of Yahweh at Sinai — but Sinai theophany is lightning/thunder, not solar (Exod 19:16). Isa 60:1-2 uses *zrḥ* of Yahweh but vv. 19-20 explicitly say the sun will no longer be needed — Yahweh transcends the sun (p. 160-161)
6. **No Yahweh-Šemeš theophoric names:** "If Yahweh had been equated with the sun, it is surprising that no Hebrew theophoric names combining Yahweh and Šemeš are attested" — unlike Yahweh-Baal (Bealiah, Yehobaal) and Yahweh-El (Elijah, Joel) compounds (p. 161)
7. **J.G. Taylor's book-length argument:** Day dismisses most of Taylor's arguments as "very weak." Examples: (a) Taylor claims Josh 10:12 implies sun-Yahweh equation because the Deuteronomistic framework says Joshua "spoke to the Lord" — but Joshua addressed the moon too; (b) Hezekiah's sun miracle implies solar equation — unlikely given Deuteronomistic esteem for Hezekiah; (c) "Lord of hosts" implies equation with chief member of host (sun) — "by no means obvious" (p. 161-162)
8. **Taanach cult stand:** Taylor's identification of the third tier (cherubim) as Yahweh's throne is "not certain" — cherubim are elsewhere guardians, not necessarily a throne (p. 162)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Day makes a strong negative case (Yahweh was NOT the sun), but the positive case for sun AS entourage (subordinate, not equated) relies on the "host of heaven = sons of God" framework which he established in Chapter 1.

**What's at stake:** If Yahweh WAS identified with the sun, then solar monotheism (like Akhenaten's Aten) rather than Canaanite polytheism would be the relevant comparative framework. Day's position preserves the Canaanite paradigm while acknowledging genuine solar influence.

**Who disagrees:** Morton Smith, H.-P. Stähli, H. Niehr, O. Keel & C. Uehlinger, E. Lipinski, and especially J.G. Taylor (*Yahweh and the Sun*, 1993). Mark Smith argues for solar INFLUENCE without full equation.

**My assessment:** Day's strongest point is the absence of Yahweh-Šemeš names. If people equated Yahweh with the sun, why did no one name their child "Yahweh-is-sun" or "Sun-of-Yahweh"? The presence of Yahweh-Baal names proves the principle. The "rampart" reading of Ps 84:12 is philologically defensible but not certain. The overall case is solid: the sun was worshipped IN Yahweh's temple as part of his court, not AS Yahweh.

## Claim 3: Samson is a solar hero behind the Israelite legend

**Author's claim:** "Although certainty is not possible, it seems likely that behind the figure of the judge Samson there lies ultimately a solar hero" (p. 162). "Samson is more than a solar hero and this latter aspect has been overlaid with Israelite features" (p. 163).

**Evidence presented:**
1. **Name:** *Šimšôn* = "sunny" (from *šemeš*). The only Hebrew name of this type in the OT
2. **Geography:** Samson's birthplace (Zorah) is 2.5 miles from Beth-Shemesh ("house of the sun"). All locations in the Samson cycle (Eshtaol, Timnah, valley of Sorek) are within 6 miles of Beth-Shemesh (p. 163)
3. **Ps 19:5-6:** The sun "comes forth like a bridegroom... like a strong man (*gibbôr*) runs its course with joy." Samson IS the frolicking strong man (*gibbôr*) of the OT — "a motif which pervades the stories about him" (p. 163)
4. **Seven locks of hair = sun's rays:** Delilah (*delîlâ*) sounds like *laylâ* "night." Helios is depicted with seven rays. The sun's rays are often depicted as hair (p. 163)
5. **Masculine sun deity:** At Ugarit the sun deity Shapash is feminine, but Samson presupposes a masculine sun — consistent with Hebrew *šemeš* being more frequently masculine (p. 164)

**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM. Day appropriately hedges: "a reasonable case for this can be made" but it's not certain. The name + geography + strong man motif are suggestive but not probative.

**What's at stake:** If Samson was originally a solar hero, the biblical narrative has demythologized a Canaanite sun myth into an Israelite judge story — the same pattern Day traces for Baal → Hosea → Daniel resurrection.

**Who disagrees:** Those who reject mythological readings of the Judges narratives generally. The solar-hero theory was more popular in older scholarship but Day notes it's still found in Martin, Soggin, and Lindars.

**My assessment:** This is the weakest claim in the chapter, and Day knows it. The evidence is suggestive but thin. The name *Šimšôn* is genuinely striking, and the Beth-Shemesh proximity is notable, but the seven-locks/Delilah wordplay argument is the kind of cleverness that can create patterns where none exist. Day's caution is appropriate.

## Claim 4: Sheger is a demythologized Canaanite moon god (parallel to Astarte in "Astartes of your sheep")

**Author's claim:** "Just as in the Deuteronomistic references *ʻaštārôt*... is a demythologization of the goddess Astarte, so it is clear that *šeger*... is the demythologization of a god Sheger" (p. 166). New evidence proves Sheger was a moon god.

**Evidence presented:**
1. The phrase *šegar ʼălāpêkā wĕʻaštĕrōt ṣōʼnekā* (Deut 7:13; 28:4, 18, 51) = "the increase of your cattle and the young of your sheep" — both terms are demythologized deities
2. Punic personal name *ʻbdšgr* "servant of Sheger" — long suspected
3. Ugaritic attestation (KTU² 1.148.31): *šgr* mentioned alongside *ʻṯtrt* as receiving offerings
4. Deir ʻAlla (Balaam text): *šgr* and *ʻštr* paired — exactly matching the OT collocation
5. **NEW: Sheger identified as moon god** — hieroglyphic Hittite correspondences (d30 = *ša-ga-ra/i*), and at Emar the 15th day of the month (full moon) was sacred to Shaggar (p. 166)
6. **Crescent = bull's horn:** "It is probable that the connection between Sheger, the moon god, and the Old Testament's use of the name in connection with the herd, lies in the crescent moon's looking like a bull's horn" (p. 166-167)

**Confidence:** HIGH. The Emar and Hittite evidence for Sheger as moon god is recent and strong.

**What's at stake:** This is a parallel case to Astarte/Astartes-of-sheep — the OT preserves the names of Canaanite deities fossilized in agricultural vocabulary, long after the deities themselves were forgotten. It's evidence of how thoroughly Canaanite religion permeated Israelite daily life and language.

**Alternative reading:** None. The evidence is straightforward.

**My assessment:** Clean and convincing. Day's parallel with Astarte makes the case intuitive — if "Astartes of your sheep" is demythologized, "Sheger of your cattle" must be too. The Emar evidence clinches the moon-god identification.

## Claim 5: Isa 14:12-15 ("Lucifer") reflects a Canaanite myth of Athtar/Venus attempting to usurp Baal's throne on Mt Zaphon — applied to Nebuchadnezzar's destruction of Jerusalem in 586 BCE

**Author's claim:**
- The "Shining One, son of the dawn" (*hêlēl ben šāḥar*) = the morning star Venus = the Canaanite god Athtar (p. 168-172)
- The myth: Venus/Athtar attempts to ascend to Baal's throne on Mt Zaphon but is cast down. This is a VARIANT (not identical to) the Ugaritic Athtar myth (KTU² 1.6.I.43-67) where Athtar is APPOINTED to Baal's vacant throne but proves too small (p. 172-175)
- **Historical referent:** "The attempted usurpation of Zaphon is not simply a symbol of the king's hubris in general... but specifically reflects Nebuchadrezzar's capture of Jerusalem and destruction of the temple in 586 BCE" (p. 184) — Day's own original proposal
- The myth was appropriated through Jebusite Jerusalem: Elyon (Jebusite god), Zaphon applied to Zion (Ps 48:3), Shalem (god of dusk) = Jerusalem's name, and Shahar (dawn, Shalem's brother at Ugarit) all pointing to Jebusite mediation (p. 180-181)

**Evidence presented (Venus = Athtar):**
1. LXX, Vulgate, Targum all understand the figure as the morning star (p. 168-169)
2. Classical parallel: Eos (Dawn) brought forth Eosphoros (Morning Star) — Hesiod, *Theogony* 381 (p. 169)
3. Against alternatives: Jupiter-Marduk and Enlil — both already supreme, no need to usurp. Moon — no known moon usurpation myth. Sun — doesn't match astral rivalry pattern. Halley's Comet — wrong date (before 550 BCE required) (p. 169-171)
4. South Arabian Athtar = Venus; Mesopotamian Ishtar = Venus; Astarte (Athtart) = Aphrodite (= Venus). Athtar and Astarte likely represent Venus as morning and evening star respectively (p. 172-173)

**Evidence presented (Canaanite background):**
- Zaphon (v. 13) = Baal's mountain
- "Above the clouds" (v. 14a) = Baal as *rkb ʻrpt*
- "Mount of Assembly" (v. 13) = Ugaritic *phr mʻd*
- Elyon (v. 14) = Canaanite god attested at Ugarit, Philo, Sefire, and as Jebusite god of Jerusalem

**Evidence presented (NOT the same as Ugaritic Athtar myth):**
- Differences too great: (a) Shining One acts from pride; Athtar is appointed by El and Athirat; (b) Shining One is cast down to Sheol; Athtar meekly descends to rule earth; (c) Shining One is deprived of power; Athtar "ruled on earth, god of it all" (p. 174-175)
- But both provide "variations on the theme of Athtar's inability to ascend the divine throne on Mt Zaphon, and so there is probably some ultimate connection" (p. 175)

**Evidence presented (NOT the same as Ezek 28 or Psalm 82):**
- Ezek 28: figure is HUMAN (first man in Eden), not a star; figure is initially PERFECT; God PLACED him on the mountain. "Stones of fire" = lightning, not stars (p. 177-179)
- Psalm 82: all seventy sons of Elyon die collectively — not one figure's hubris
- Gen 6:1-4: sons of God descend to earth for sex — opposite movement

**Evidence presented (Nebuchadnezzar and 586 BCE):**
1. "Zaphon was the name of both Yahweh's heavenly dwelling place (Isa 14:13; Job 26:7) and his earthly dwelling place on Mt Zion (Ps 48:3). Any attempted usurpation of the earthly throne naturally had repercussions in the heavenly sphere" (p. 184)
2. The most flagrant act of hubris by Nebuchadnezzar was destroying the temple — Isa 14:12-15 "is a heavenly reflection of this"
3. Daniel 8:9-14 (little horn rising above stars to "Prince of the host") also reflects temple violation (Antiochus IV) — same pattern
4. Jeremiah 50-51 (dependent on Isa 13-14) has the recurring theme that Babylon's downfall = revenge for violating Zion's temple (Jer 50:28-29; 51:11, 24, 34-37, 49-51). Jer 51:53 alludes to Isa 14:12-15

**Confidence:** HIGH for Venus identification; MEDIUM-HIGH for Athtar connection; MEDIUM for the specific Nebuchadnezzar/586 BCE proposal (Day's own original suggestion, well-argued but novel).

**What's at stake:** The "Lucifer" passage that became foundational for Christian demonology (Satan as fallen angel) was originally a Canaanite astral myth about the morning star, applied by Isaiah to a historical Babylonian king. The entire Lucifer/Satan tradition is a remythologization of a polemical reuse of a Canaanite myth.

**Who disagrees:** Those identifying Helel with Jupiter-Marduk (Langdon, G.R. Driver), Enlil (Gallagher), the moon (Winckler, Snaith), the sun (May, Spronk), Halley's Comet (Etz). Those equating the myth with Ezek 28 (Clements, Barth, Page). Those seeing Greek Phaethon influence (Gruppe, Duhm, Grelot).

**My assessment:** This is Day at his best — philological precision, mythological comparison, historical contextualization, and an original thesis all in one section. The Venus/Athtar identification is the scholarly consensus and well-supported. The distinction from Ezek 28 is important and underappreciated. The Nebuchadnezzar/586 BCE proposal is elegant — it explains why a myth about Zaphon would be applied to a Babylonian king (because Zaphon = Zion, and Babylon destroyed Zion). The Jeremiah 50-51 parallel strengthens it considerably.

## Chapter 6 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Sun cult = Canaanite, not Assyrian | HIGH | Spieckermann's Assyrian thesis (weakened by pre-Assyrian archaeology) |
| Yahweh NOT equated with sun | MEDIUM-HIGH | Taylor's book-length counter-argument (Day engages but dismisses quickly) |
| Samson = solar hero | LOW-MEDIUM | Thin evidence; clever wordplay, not primary data |
| Sheger = demythologized moon god | HIGH | Emar/Hittite evidence is recent and strong |
| Isa 14:12-15 = Venus/Athtar myth → Nebuchadnezzar/586 BCE | HIGH (Venus); MEDIUM-HIGH (Athtar); MEDIUM (586 BCE specific) | Novel proposal; needs more testing against alternative historical identifications |

**Strongest section:** The Isa 14:12-15 analysis. The multi-stage argument (Venus identification → Athtar myth → distinction from Ezek 28/Ps 82 → Jebusite mediation → Nebuchadnezzar application) is Day's most original contribution in the chapter.

**Weakest section:** The Samson-as-solar-hero theory. Day appropriately hedges, but some of the evidence is the kind of punning and pattern-seeking that can generate false positives.

**Most important implicit contribution:** Day demonstrates that the "host of heaven" framework (sun, moon, stars as Yahweh's subordinate entourage = sons of God from Chapter 1) provides a coherent model for how astral worship could coexist with Yahwism without equating Yahweh with the astral bodies. This is the Catholic "veneration not worship" analogy he explicitly makes (p. 159).
