# Heiser — Parts 5-6: Conquest, Failure, and the Prophets (Chapters 21-30)

## Part 5: Conquest and Failure

### Claim: The conquest of Canaan was Yahweh reclaiming territory from corrupt *elohim*

**Heiser's framework:** After Babel, nations were assigned to "sons of God" who became corrupt (Psalm 82). The conquest was NOT ethnic cleansing — it was cosmic warfare. Yahweh was displacing the corrupt divine beings who had claimed the land and its peoples. The *ḥērem* (total destruction) commands reflect the severity of this cosmic battle.

**Evidence:** The language of "driving out" in Deuteronomy and Joshua uses terminology associated with divine beings, not just human populations. The giants (Anakim, Rephaim) are remnants of the Nephilim — they must be eliminated because they're not fully human.

**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM. This is Heiser's most speculative argument. The conquest narratives can be read without the cosmic warfare framework. The ethical problems remain regardless.

## Part 6: Thus Says the Lord

### Claim: The prophets operated within the Deuteronomy 32 worldview

**Heiser's framework:** The prophets' oracles against the nations aren't just political commentary — they're declarations that Yahweh is coming to judge the corrupt *elohim* behind those nations. The "Day of the Lord" is the day Yahweh reclaims the nations from their rebellious administrators.

**Evidence:** 
- Isaiah 24:21-22: "On that day Yahweh will punish the host of heaven in heaven, and the kings of the earth on earth" — cosmic AND earthly judgment
- Daniel 10: "the prince of Persia" resists the angel for 21 days — territorial divine being
- The "little gods" language in the prophets fits the council framework

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. The prophetic texts certainly use cosmic language. Whether they mean literal territorial divine beings or whether this is metaphorical is debatable.
