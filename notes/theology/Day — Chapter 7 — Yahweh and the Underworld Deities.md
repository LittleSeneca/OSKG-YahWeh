# Day — Chapter 7: Yahweh and the Underworld Deities (Mot, Resheph, Molech, and the Rephaim)

Day covers four categories of netherworld beings in Israelite and Canaanite religion. Mot provides language for Sheol. Resheph is a plague-demon archer. Molech is a genuine Canaanite underworld god who received child sacrifice and was NOT identified with Yahweh. The Rephaim are deified royal dead at Ugarit whose name was transferred to both underworld shades and legendary giant inhabitants of Canaan.

## Claim 1: Sheol language in the OT derives from Canaanite Mot imagery, but Sheol is not a fully personified deity
→ [[claim-sheol-language-canaanite-mot-imagery-demythologized]] | **day-ygc-7.1** | Confidence: high
  Mot's swallowing jaws (KTU² 1.5.II.1-6) become Sheol's enlarged throat (Isa 5:14). Mot's insatiable appetite matches Sheol's (Hab 2:5; Prov 30:15b-16). "Death shall be their shepherd" (Ps 49:15) recalls Mot making Baal "as lamb in my mouth." Isa 25:8: "He will swallow up death forever" — ironic reversal of the swallower being swallowed (Day reads this as exile metaphor, not literal abolition). Azmaweth = "Mot is strong" preserves mythological awareness. Day corrects Cassuto: Jer 9:20's "Death at the window" is Yam, not Mot.
  Depends on: [[claim-day-chaoskampf-canaanite-baal-yam-not-babylonian-marduk-tiamat]] (Baal-Mot cycle), [[claim-resheph-canaanite-plague-demon-archer-preserved-in-ot]] (Cant 8:6 pairs Mot + Resheph)

## Claim 2: Resheph is a Canaanite underworld plague god whose arrows bring pestilence, and the OT preserves him as a demonic personification
→ [[claim-resheph-canaanite-plague-demon-archer-preserved-in-ot]] | **day-ygc-7.2** | Confidence: high
  Resheph = Nergal (underworld/plague god equation at Ugarit). Hab 3:5: *deber* and *rešep* as Yahweh's attendants mirrors Ugaritic KTU² 1.82 where "the archer Resheph" shoots. Job 5:7: "sons of Resheph fly upwards" — mythological reading over "sparks" (KJV/RSV). Deut 32:24: verb *lḥm* "fight" with *rešep* implies personal archer-god, not impersonal plague. Cant 8:6: love's arrows derive from Resheph's pestilential arrows. The clearest case of a Canaanite demon surviving with identity intact in biblical literature.
  Depends on: [[claim-sheol-language-canaanite-mot-imagery-demythologized]] (Cant 8:6 pairs Mot + Resheph; both are underworld deities)

## Claim 3: Molech was a genuine Canaanite underworld deity (not a type of sacrifice), received child sacrifice, and was NOT equated with Yahweh
→ [[claim-molech-genuine-canaanite-god-child-sacrifice-not-yahweh-equated]] | **day-ygc-7.3** | Confidence: very-high
  Eissfeldt's *mlk*-sacrifice theory refuted on two grammatical points: (1) OT speaks of "playing the harlot after pagan deities" but never "after a sacrifice"; (2) verbs used with Molech are attested with *lĕ* + divine name, never *lĕ* + sacrificial term alone. Ugaritic *mlk* attested as a god (KTU² 1.100.41). Molech = Nergal (underworld god equation in two god-lists). RS 1986.2235 pairs Mlk + Resheph. Gehenna etymology: valley of Hinnom where Molech's fiery rites occurred — NOT the medieval "burning rubbish dump" theory. NOT equated with Yahweh: children to Molech were boys AND girls, while children to Yahweh were only firstborn males.
  Depends on: [[claim-resheph-canaanite-plague-demon-archer-preserved-in-ot]] (RS 1986.2235 pairs Mlk + Resheph), [[claim-ethnic-rephaim-derive-from-underworld-rephaim-ugaritic]] (shared Ashtaroth location + adjacent Jerusalem valleys)

## Claim 4: The OT Rephaim (underworld shades) derive from Ugaritic *rpʼum* (deified royal dead), and the ethnic Rephaim (giants) developed from the underworld concept
→ [[claim-ethnic-rephaim-derive-from-underworld-rephaim-ugaritic]] | **day-ygc-7.4** | Confidence: high
  The smoking gun: KTU² 1.108.2-3 says Rpʼu "dwells in Ashtaroth, judges at Edrei" — and Josh 12:4 says Og, king of Bashan, Rephaim remnant "dwelt at Ashtaroth and at Edrei" — virtually identical language. Molech also dwelt at Ashtaroth (KTU² 1.100.41), and in Jerusalem the valley of Hinnom (Molech's site) joins the valley of Rephaim (Josh 18:16). Ugaritic *rpʼum* are clearly dead kings invoked in ritual (KTU² 1.161). OT preserves terminology of divinized dead: Samuel's shade called "a god" (1 Sam 28:13). Pattern: divine beings → legendary human figures (Nephilim from sons of God, Nimrod from Ninurta).
  Depends on: [[claim-molech-genuine-canaanite-god-child-sacrifice-not-yahweh-equated]] (shared Ashtaroth location + adjacent Jerusalem valleys), [[claim-sheol-language-canaanite-mot-imagery-demythologized]] (Rephaim as shades inhabit Sheol)

## Chapter 7 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Sheol language from Mot imagery (demythologized) | HIGH | Personification degree subjective — some passages may be more personified than Day allows |
| Resheph = Canaanite plague demon preserved in OT | HIGH | "Sparks" reading in Job 5:7 (RSV/NRSV) still has defenders |
| Molech = god (not sacrifice term) with real child sacrifice | VERY HIGH (god); HIGH (child sacrifice) | Weinfeld's polemic-as-fiction position (weak) |
| Molech NOT equated with Yahweh | MEDIUM-HIGH | Levenson's counter-argument from Philo of Byblos |
| Ethnic Rephaim derive from underworld Rephaim | HIGH | Ashtaroth/Edrei parallel is the clincher |

**Strongest section:** The Molech analysis. Day's 1989 monograph expertise shows — the refutation of Eissfeldt is methodical and devastating, and the underworld identification via Nergal equation and RS 1986.2235 (Mlk + Resheph pairing) is original and convincing.

**Weakest section:** The discussion of whether Anat's treatment of Mot lies behind the golden calf destruction (Exod 32:20). Day says no, but this is a minor point and doesn't affect the chapter's main arguments.

**Most important cross-reference:** The Rephaim section ties the entire chapter together — Mot, Resheph, and Molech are all underworld deities, and the Rephaim (shades) provide the conceptual framework for understanding Israel's netherworld. The Ashtaroth location connects Rephaim + Molech + the ethnic giants. Day's ability to trace a single location through Ugaritic, Phoenician, and biblical texts is exemplary comparative method.
