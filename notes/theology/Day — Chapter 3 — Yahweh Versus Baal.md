# Day — Chapter 3: Yahweh Versus Baal

Day covers Baal's manifestations in Israel, the identity of Jezebel's Baal, Hebrew scribal distortions of Baal's name (Baal-zebub, bosheth, abomination of desolation), and the nature of the god Dagon. The chapter is more philological and text-critical than theological — it's about how the OT polemically handles the name of its greatest rival deity.

---

## Claim 1: "The Baals" are local manifestations of ONE cosmic deity (Hadad/Baal), not distinct gods
→ [[claim-baals-local-manifestations-one-deity]] | **day-ygc-3.1** | Confidence: high
  Ugaritic pantheon list (KTU² 1.47) shows Baal named seven times. Local Baals = local manifestations, analogous to Catholic Virgin Mary manifestations. Now standard in the field.
  Key edges: Supports [[claim-baal-accepted-israelite-god-persistent]] (one coherent cult explains persistence) and [[claim-jezebel-baal-was-baal-shamem]] (Baal-Shamem as candidate follows naturally).

---

## Claim 2: Jezebel's Baal was Baal-Shamem (the universal storm god), NOT Melqart (city god of Tyre)
→ [[claim-jezebel-baal-was-baal-shamem]] | **day-ygc-3.2** | Confidence: medium-high
  Esar-haddon treaty (7th c.) distinguishes Melqart from storm Baal deities. Carmel = Zeus = Baal-Shamem. Majority view favors Melqart; Day is in the minority with Eissfeldt, Mulder, and Smith.
  Key edges: Depends on [[claim-baals-local-manifestations-one-deity]] (one cosmic Baal framework). Supports [[claim-yahweh-absorbed-baal-storm-warrior-imagery]] and [[claim-baal-accepted-israelite-god-persistent]].

---

## Claim 3: Baal-zebub is a deliberate polemical distortion of Baal-zebul ("Prince Baal")
→ [[claim-baal-zebub-distortion-of-baal-zebul]] | **day-ygc-3.3** | Confidence: high
  Ugaritic *zbl bʻl* + Symmachus + NT Beelzebul converge. "Lord of the Flies" is a scribal insult, not a translation error. Part of a broader pattern of theological name-deformation.
  Key edges: Supports [[claim-boshet-scribal-substitution-for-baal]] (same scribal strategy) and [[claim-abomination-desolation-baal-shamem-wordplay]] (same strategy in apocalyptic).

---

## Claim 4: *Bošet* ("shame") is a deliberate scribal substitution for Baal in personal names
→ [[claim-boshet-scribal-substitution-for-baal]] | **day-ygc-3.4** | Confidence: very-high
  Ish-bosheth = Eshbaal, Mephibosheth = Meribaal, Jerubbesheth = Jerubbaal. Hos 9:10 and Jer 11:13 use *bošet* for Baal explicitly. Tsevat/Hamilton rebutted. Settled scholarship.
  Key edges: Depends on [[claim-baal-zebub-distortion-of-baal-zebul]] (same distortion pattern). Supports [[claim-abomination-desolation-baal-shamem-wordplay]] and [[claim-baal-accepted-israelite-god-persistent]] (Baal names were common enough to require editing).

---

## Claim 5: "Abomination of desolation" in Daniel is a wordplay on Baal-Shamem
→ [[claim-abomination-desolation-baal-shamem-wordplay]] | **day-ygc-3.5** | Confidence: high
  *Šiqqûṣ* ("abomination") replaces Baal; *šōmēm* ("desolating") replaces *šāmēm* ("heavens"). Nestle's 1884 wordplay theory. Daniel continues the OT tradition of polemical name-distortion.
  Key edges: Depends on [[claim-baal-zebub-distortion-of-baal-zebul]] and [[claim-boshet-scribal-substitution-for-baal]] (same strategy across genres).

---

## Claim 6: Dagon was a storm/fertility god (not a fish god), and Baal was his literal son
→ [[claim-dagon-storm-fertility-god-baal-father]] | **day-ygc-3.6** | Confidence: medium-high
  Fish-god theory dead. Dagan = Enlil (CT 24) → storm/fertility. *bn dgn* is literal parentage. Dual paternity solution: Baal literally Dagon's son, "son of El" = pantheon membership.
  Key edges: Supports [[claim-baals-local-manifestations-one-deity]] and [[claim-yahweh-absorbed-baal-storm-warrior-imagery]] (storm profile inherited from Dagon).

## Chapter 3 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| "The Baals" = local manifestations of one god (day-ygc-3.1) | HIGH | Essentially uncontested since Ugarit |
| Jezebel's Baal = Baal-Shamem (not Melqart) (day-ygc-3.2) | MEDIUM-HIGH | Majority view still favors Melqart |
| Baal-zebub = distortion of Baal-zebul (day-ygc-3.3) | HIGH | Ugaritic + NT evidence converging |
| *Bošet* = scribal substitution for Baal (day-ygc-3.4) | VERY HIGH | Tsevat/Hamilton rebuttals fail |
| "Abomination of desolation" = wordplay on Baal-Shamem (day-ygc-3.5) | HIGH | Nestle's theory widely accepted; Keel's objections addressed |
| Dagon = storm/fertility god, literal father of Baal (day-ygc-3.6) | MEDIUM-HIGH | Etymology uncertain; dual paternity is speculative but reasonable |

**Strongest section:** The Baal-zebub/Baal-zebul argument. This is Day at his philological best — Ugaritic, Symmachus, NT manuscripts, and the broader pattern of scribal distortion all converge on the same conclusion.

**Weakest section:** The Jezebel's-Baal-as-Baal-Shamem argument. Day makes a strong case but is fighting the majority, and the Melqart identification has the advantage of explaining the specifically Tyrian character of Jezebel's cult.

**Methodological note:** This chapter reveals Day's method: he's a text scholar working through philology, not an archaeologist. When he needs to decide between competing identifications, he reaches for inscriptions and manuscripts, not material culture. This is both a strength (precision with texts) and a limitation (Dever would say he's missing the ground-level picture). The chapter also demonstrates that OT hostility to Baal operated at the level of *names* — the scribes didn't just argue against Baal, they linguistically deformed his name into insults.
