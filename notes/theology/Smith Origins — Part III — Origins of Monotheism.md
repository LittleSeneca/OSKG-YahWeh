# Smith — Part III: The Origins of Monotheism in the Bible (Chapters 7-10)

---

## Chapter 7: El, Yahweh, and the Original God of Israel and the Exodus

### Claim: El was Israel's original god — Yahweh was a later arrival from the south

**Smith's claim:** "Was El Israel's original God? The answer... is yes." The name "Israel" itself contains the theophoric element *'ēl*, not *yāhû*. The patriarchal narratives consistently use El epithets (El Shadday, El Elyon, El Olam). Exodus 6:2-3 explicitly distinguishes El Shadday (patriarchal god) from Yahweh (Mosaic god). "The original god of Israel was El."

**Evidence — El in Iron Age Israel:**
- The name "Israel" = "May El rule" (not "May Yahweh rule")
- Patriarchal narratives preserve El traditions independent of Yahweh
- The "Elohistic Psalter" (Psalms 42-83) systematically replaces Yahweh with Elohim — preserving the El name in liturgical contexts
- Hosea 12:5 preserves the memory of Jacob wrestling with El (MT obscures this)

**The Exodus question:** "Was El the original God of the Exodus?" Smith is more cautious here. The Exodus tradition may have originally been associated with El and later transferred to Yahweh. Or Yahweh may have been the Exodus god from the beginning, with El as the patriarchal god. The traditions were merged.

**Yahweh's original character:** Yahweh was likely a southern storm/war god from Edom/Midian/Seir. "The question of Yahweh's original character" remains debated — but Smith leans toward the southern origin hypothesis.

**Confidence:** HIGH on El as Israel's original god (the onomastic evidence is strong). MEDIUM on the Exodus question. HIGH on Yahweh's southern origin (consistent with Römer).

## Chapter 8: The Emergence of Monotheistic Rhetoric in Ancient Judah

### Claim: Monotheistic STATEMENTS (denying other gods' existence) are late, rare, and rhetorical — not descriptive of practice

**Smith's claim:** "The relative infrequency and rhetorical purpose of monotheistic statements" shows that monotheism was a LITERARY and RHETORICAL achievement of specific groups (priests, prophets, scribes), not the popular religion of the masses.

**The monotheistic texts — when and where:**
- **Pre-exilic:** Essentially absent. Deuteronomistic theology is monolatrous (worship YHWH only), not monotheistic (other gods don't exist). Ashima of Hamath, Nibhaz of the Avvites — these gods EXIST, they're just not Israel's.
- **Exilic (Second Isaiah, c. 540 BCE):** The first unambiguous monotheistic statements. "I am Yahweh and there is no other; besides me there is no god" (Isaiah 45:5). These are POLEMICAL — directed against Babylonian gods who appear to have defeated Yahweh. "Your god didn't lose. There ARE no other gods for him to lose to."
- **Post-exilic:** Monotheism becomes normative in literary/theological circles.

**The social and historical context:**
- "The national god and royal ideology" — the Davidic king was Yahweh's son; when the monarchy fell, the royal theology had to be reformulated
- Monotheism emerged from the CRISIS of the exile — a theological response to political catastrophe
- "The prior context of Judean discourse about divinity" — monotheism built on earlier monolatrous rhetoric

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH on the dating of monotheistic statements. Second Isaiah is the watershed.

**Who disagrees:** Tigay (the onomastic evidence suggests early monolatry). Heiser (monotheism was always present; the *elohim* are created beings).

## Chapter 9: The Formation of Monotheistic Theologies in Biblical Literature

### Claim: Three priestly-scribal groups produced three different monotheistic theologies — Creation theology, Wisdom theology, and Apocalyptic theology

**Smith's claim:** Monotheism wasn't a single development. Different scribal groups produced different VERSIONS of monotheism, each reflecting their own social location and concerns.

**1. Creation, Paradise, and the Priesthood (P source):**
- Priestly monotheism: God as transcendent creator who orders the cosmos through separation and distinction (Genesis 1)
- Holiness as the boundary between divine and human — maintained through ritual
- The tabernacle as microcosm of creation
- Social location: Jerusalem priesthood, post-exilic

**2. Wisdom and the Levites:**
- Levitical/sapiential monotheism: God as wise creator whose order can be discerned through observation and reflection (Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes)
- Universal — not tied to Israel's particular history
- "The fear of Yahweh is the beginning of wisdom"
- Social location: Levitical teachers, scribal schools

**3. Apocalyptic Literature — The Case of Daniel 7:**
- Apocalyptic monotheism: God as the Ancient of Days who will ultimately defeat the chaotic powers and establish his eternal kingdom
- The "one like a son of man" — a divine figure receiving kingship
- The four beasts = four empires — history as a sequence of monstrous powers that God will destroy
- Social location: Persecuted visionaries under Hellenistic rule (2nd century BCE)

**Confidence:** HIGH on the three theological streams. This is standard critical analysis of post-exilic literature.

**What's at stake:** Monotheism isn't ONE thing. It's THREE different theological programs, produced by different groups, serving different agendas. The "one God" of the priests (transcendent creator) is not the same "one God" of the wisdom teachers (observable order) or the apocalyptic visionaries (coming judge). They share the FORM (one God) but not the CONTENT (what kind of God).

## Chapter 10: Monotheism in Isaiah 40-55

### Claim: Second Isaiah is the BIRTHPLACE of biblical monotheism — and it was born from trauma

**Smith's claim:** "Monotheism in Isaiah 40-55 represents the most concentrated and explicit denial of other deities in the Hebrew Bible." This was not a gradual evolution. It was a POLEMICAL ERUPTION — a theological response to the crisis of exile.

**The historical situation:** Babylon has destroyed Jerusalem. The temple is gone. The Davidic king is in chains. By every ANE measure, Marduk (Babylon's god) has defeated Yahweh. The prophet's response: "Marduk didn't defeat Yahweh. Marduk DOESN'T EXIST. There IS no Marduk. There never was." This is not philosophical argument. It's theological triage.

**Key passages:**
- Isaiah 43:10: "Before me no god was formed, nor shall there be any after me"
- Isaiah 44:6: "I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god"
- Isaiah 45:5-7: "I am Yahweh and there is no other... I form light and create darkness, I make well-being and create calamity"
- Isaiah 44:9-20: The satire on idol-making — the idol is wood, half burned for warmth, half carved into a god

**Reading for monotheism:** Smith concludes with a reflection on how to read monotheistic texts. They are not philosophical propositions. They are POLEMICAL, RHETORICAL, SITUATED. They respond to a specific crisis. They make a claim that was revolutionary in its context. Reading them as timeless metaphysical truths misses their genre and their historical moment.

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH on Second Isaiah as the first unambiguous monotheistic text. HIGH on the polemical/rhetorical reading.

**What's at stake:** "I am Yahweh and there is no other" — the most famous monotheistic declaration in the Bible — was written in Babylon by a displaced priest trying to prevent his community from concluding that their God had lost. It was NOT a timeless philosophical truth discovered by reflection. It was a desperate theological assertion made in response to catastrophe. Monotheism was born from trauma.

## Smith: Book-Level Assessment

**Smith's argument across both books:**
1. *The Early History of God* (1990/2002): HOW Yahweh absorbed other gods — convergence and differentiation
2. *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism* (2001): WHY monotheism emerged — the structures, the crisis, the rhetorical response

**The complete picture:** Israel inherited a polytheistic Canaanite religion. Yahweh, a southern storm god, was introduced to a people who worshipped El. Over centuries, Yahweh absorbed El, Baal, Asherah, and the sun. The exile created a crisis that demanded a theological response: the denial that other gods exist. Monotheism was the solution to the problem of divine defeat.

**Smith's honesty:** He is a Roman Catholic who accepts the full critical reconstruction. His faith survives his scholarship. He notes that "believers perhaps have a critical contribution to make in the study of ancient texts" — they are "more prepared to accept ancient religious experience... as valid." But he never lets his faith determine his conclusions. He follows the evidence where it leads.

**The challenge he poses:** Can you accept Smith's historical reconstruction AND maintain religious faith? Smith does. But he never tells you how. That's your work.
