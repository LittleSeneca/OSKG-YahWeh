# Stavrakopoulou — Part I: Feet and Legs (Chapters 2-4)

---

## Chapter 2: Grounded

### Claim: God's feet marked his physical presence on earth — temples were his footprints

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** "The force of his feet splits mountains. They shake the earth as he strides out from the desert. They crush the bodies of his enemies. They transform dust and dirt into holy ground." God's feet were not metaphorical — they were the mechanism by which he rendered himself present in specific places. Temples were the "place of my feet," the "place for the soles of my feet."

**Evidence — the 'Ain Dara temple:**
- 40 miles NW of Aleppo, Syria. "The closest we can come to the temple that stood in Jerusalem"
- Two giant footprints, about a metre in length, carved into the threshold, pointing INTO the temple
- A left footprint on the vestibule entrance, a right footprint inside the holy of holies
- "There were no exiting footprints; no indication that the deity had left the temple. Rather, the footprints signalled the permanent presence of the god within"
- "I stepped into these enormous, yet delicate feet. They dwarfed my own. I was standing in the bare footsteps of a god"

**Biblical evidence — God's feet on earth:**
- Genesis 3:8: Adam and Eve hear Yahweh's FOOTSTEPS approaching in the garden
- Genesis 18: Abraham sees Yahweh STANDING with two divine beings
- Exodus 24:9-10: "They saw the God of Israel. And under his feet there was something like a brick pavement of lapis lazuli, like the very heavens for clarity" — a close-up of the floor beneath God's feet
- Isaiah 60:13: Temple garden trees "glorify where my feet rest"
- Ezekiel 43:7: "This is the place for the soles of my feet, where I will reside among the people of Israel forever"

**The theological mechanism:** A footprint "is a material memory of its owner treading upon the surface on which the print appears, conjuring the presence of the whole body — the whole person." God's feet render him LOCALLY present. He is HERE, not everywhere.

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH on the biblical texts describing God's feet. HIGH on the 'Ain Dara temple as a parallel to Jerusalem's temple.

## Chapter 3: Footloose

### Claim: God walked — his locomotion was real, not metaphorical, and marked sacred space

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** "When the biblical story moves to Jerusalem, God's feet are there, too." God walks, strides, treads, and stamps. His movement through the landscape transforms it. Where his feet touch, the ground becomes holy.

**Evidence — God walking:**
- Genesis 3:8: God "walking in the garden at the breezy time of the day"
- Genesis 17:1, 22: God "appeared" to Abraham — the Hebrew verb suggests visible, bodily manifestation, often including locomotion
- Genesis 18: Abraham walks alongside God toward Sodom
- Genesis 28: Jacob's dream at Bethel — "Yahweh is in this standing-place!" — God was literally standing there

**The massebah connection:** Standing stones (*massebot*) marked the places where God had stood. Genesis 28: Jacob's stone pillow becomes a *massebah*. "I am the god of Bethel, whom you anointed there in the massebah," God later tells Jacob (Genesis 31:13). The stone IS the standing place — God identifies with it.

**The maqom wordplay:** "A sacred location at which these stones were placed is a *maqom*, a term commonly translated 'place,' but which more precisely means 'a standing place.'" The very word for sacred space derives from standing.

**Confidence:** HIGH on the biblical evidence. Stavrakopoulou is at her strongest when she catalogs textual data.

## Chapter 4: Sensational Feet

### Claim: God used his feet as weapons — crushing enemies, stamping territory, marking ownership

**Stavrakopoulou's claim:** "Whether divine or human, feet communicated ownership and identity throughout ancient south-west Asia. And the God of the Bible used his to stamp his authoritative presence onto the world of his worshippers."

**Evidence — feet as weapons:**
- Isaiah 63:1-6: God returns from Edom with garments stained red — "I trampled them in my anger, I trod them down in my wrath"
- Psalm 110:1: "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet" — the ultimate image of domination
- Habakkuk 3:12-13: "In fury you strode the earth, in anger you trampled nations"

**Evidence — feet as property markers:**
- Mesopotamian legal rituals: "tracts of land were transferred in a series of legal rituals which included references to the seller raising a foot from the land" — lifting the foot = transferring ownership
- Deuteronomy 11:24: "Every place on which the sole of your foot treads shall be yours"
- Joshua 1:3: God to Joshua — "Every place that the sole of your foot will tread upon I have given to you"
- God's feet on temple ground = his ownership of the site

**Confidence:** HIGH on the textual evidence for feet as weapons. HIGH on feet as property markers (the ANE legal parallels are well-documented).

**Stavrakopoulou's strength in Part I:** Her ability to connect material culture (the 'Ain Dara footprints) to biblical texts (God's feet on the lapis lazuli pavement) to ANE social practice (feet as property markers). This is interdisciplinary scholarship at its best. She's not just reading texts — she's reconstructing an embodied world.

**Comparison to Sommer:** Where Sommer provides the philosophical framework (fluidity vs. non-fluidity), Stavrakopoulou provides the sensory, material, bodily experience. Sommer tells you God had bodies. Stavrakopoulou tells you what those bodies FELT LIKE — how the feet pressed into stone, how the ground became holy under God's tread, how the enemies were crushed underfoot.
