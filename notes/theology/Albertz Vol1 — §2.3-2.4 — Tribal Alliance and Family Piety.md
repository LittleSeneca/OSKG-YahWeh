# Albertz — §2.3–2.4: The Pre-State Tribal Alliance and Family Piety

These sections complete Part 2 (Before the State). §2.3 describes the social and religious organization of the Israelite tribal alliance — Albertz's answer to how a decentralized, anti-monarchical society could function. His key claim: Israel was originally El-worshipping; Yahweh was adopted secondarily by the alliance when the Exodus group arrived, and Yahweh's alien/outsider status made him a BETTER symbol of opposition to domination than El. §2.4 documents how family piety continued largely untouched by the rise of official Yahweh religion — demonstrating "internal religious pluralism" at work.

## §2.3–2.4 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1. Segmented, acephalous, anti-monarchical tribal society | MEDIUM-HIGH | Idealization of pre-state period; African analogies may not fit |
| 2. Original El-worship; Yahweh adopted secondarily | MEDIUM | Name "Israel" may not prove El-worship; Cross's El-Yahweh identity theory |
| 3. Yahweh as both solidarity principle AND anti-domination guarantee | MEDIUM | Gideon story may be late retrojection |
| 4. Family piety uninfluenced by Yahweh religion (internal pluralism) | HIGH | Personal names may reflect convention, not theological gap |

**Strongest section:** Claim 4 — the evidence from personal names and individual laments for the gap between family piety and official religion. This is empirically grounded and not dependent on Albertz's theoretical framework to be significant.

**Weakest section:** Claim 2 — the "original El-worshipping Israel" thesis. The evidence is limited (one name, one altar inscription, and the Merenptah stele dating) and can be explained by alternative models (Cross's El-Yahweh identity, or El as simply the generic word for "god" in the name). Albertz needs this claim for his narrative (Yahweh as superior liberation symbol replacing El) but the evidence doesn't fully carry it.

**Cross-reference:** Cross's *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* argues Yahweh was originally an epithet OF El ("El who creates the hosts"), which would mean El and Yahweh were never separate deities to be fused. Albertz's claim of a two-stage religious history (El → Yahweh) directly contradicts Cross on this point. The absence of anti-El polemic in the OT supports BOTH positions but for different reasons: Cross says it's because El and Yahweh were always the same god; Albertz says it's because the fusion was peaceful and complete. Which is right? The Merenptah stele is the key evidence Albertz adds — if "Israel" existed as an El-worshipping entity before the Exodus group arrived, Cross's model has a chronological problem.

**Connection to Tigay:** Albertz's onomastic observations in §2.4 (personal names don't reference Exodus/Sinai) complement Tigay's broader finding that 94%+ of Hebrew inscriptions contain YHWH theophoric names. The combined picture: names are overwhelmingly Yahwistic (Tigay) but their content reflects family piety concerns, not official salvation-history (Albertz). Names prove popular monolatry but not popular engagement with official theology.

**What to watch:** The "internal religious pluralism" model has now been demonstrated for the pre-state period. The key test in Chapter 3 (the monarchy) will be whether this gap persists, narrows, or becomes contentious — and whether the monarchy represents integration of the two strata or exacerbation of their tension.
