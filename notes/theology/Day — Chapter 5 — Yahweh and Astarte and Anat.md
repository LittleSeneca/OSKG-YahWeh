# Day — Chapter 5: Yahweh and the Goddesses Astarte and Anat (and the Queen of Heaven)

Day covers two major Canaanite goddesses and the Queen of Heaven debate. His key arguments: Astarte was Baal's consort (likely the "Queen of Heaven"), Anat was a warrior goddess who functioned as Yahweh's consort at Elephantine, and both goddesses were sufficiently associated with Yahweh (through the Yahweh-Baal equation) that they could serve as divine consorts in syncretistic Israelite religion.

---

## Claim 1: The vocalization "Ashtoreth" is a deliberate scribal distortion (vowels of *bošet* "shame")
→ [[claim-ashtoreth-vocalization-boshet-deliberate-scribal-distortion]] | **day-ygc-5.1** | Confidence: very-high
  The OT vocalization "Ashtoreth" is a theological insult — scribes deformed the goddess's name using the same *bošet* strategy applied to Baal names. Greek transcriptions preserve *Astartē* with no trace of the o-vocalization, decisively refuting Cooper's natural-vowel-shift theory.
  Supports: [[claim-boshet-scribal-substitution-for-baal]] (same strategy), [[claim-queen-of-heaven-jeremiah-astarte-ishtar-syncretism]] (explains why Astarte needed polemical treatment)

---

## Claim 2: Shamgar ben Anat = "son of Anat" as an honorific military title, not a patronymic
→ [[claim-shamgar-ben-anat-honorific-military-title]] | **day-ygc-5.2** | Confidence: medium-high
  Two inscribed arrowheads owned by warriors bear *bn ʻnt* — warrior weapons inscribed with "son of [the warrior goddess]" make the military-title reading natural. Counter to the older Beth-Anat geographical theory (Albright, Alt, Noth, Mazar).
  Supports: [[claim-anat-yahu-elephantine-yahweh-consort]] (if Anat was known as military title in early Israel, Elephantine syncretism has indigenous prehistory)

---

## Claim 3: Anat-Yahu at Elephantine confirms Anat functioned as Yahweh's consort
→ [[claim-anat-yahu-elephantine-yahweh-consort]] | **day-ygc-5.3** | Confidence: medium-high
  Anat-Yahu is a deity, not a "providence" abstraction — the Esar-haddon treaty (c. 675 BCE) attests Anat-Bethel as a deity, disproving Albright's alternative reading. The Baal-Yahweh equation from Chapter 3 provides the bridge: Anat was Baal's consort, and if Yahweh = Baal, Anat could serve as Yahweh's consort. Pre-exilic Northern Kingdom origin is plausible but unproven.
  Depends on: [[claim-day-chaoskampf-canaanite-baal-yam-not-babylonian-marduk-tiamat]] (Baal-Yahweh equation)
  Contradicted by: Smith, van der Toorn — Anat worship unknown in pre-exilic Israel; Elephantine is late diaspora development

---

## Claim 4: The "Queen of Heaven" in Jeremiah is most likely Astarte (possibly in syncretism with Ishtar)
→ [[claim-queen-of-heaven-jeremiah-astarte-ishtar-syncretism]] | **day-ygc-5.4** | Confidence: medium-high
  Day systematically evaluates five candidates (Shapash, Asherah, Anat, Ishtar, Astarte) and concludes Astarte is best-supported: she's the only candidate the Deuteronomist polemicizes against elsewhere, and the Aphrodite Ourania/Astarte identification is well-attested. The Akkadian cake-offering vocabulary (*kawwānîm*) suggests Ishtar syncretism (via Ackerman).
  Depends on: [[claim-ashtoreth-vocalization-boshet-deliberate-scribal-distortion]] (Astarte's distorted name confirms she was a live threat), [[claim-boshet-scribal-substitution-for-baal]] (same Deuteronomistic scribal strategy)

## Chapter 5 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Ashtoreth = deliberate *bošet* distortion | VERY HIGH | Cooper's vowel-shift theory (refuted) |
| Shamgar ben Anat = honorific military title | MEDIUM-HIGH | Could still be geographical or literal patronymic |
| Anat-Yahu = Anat as Yahweh's consort at Elephantine | HIGH (for Elephantine); MEDIUM (for pre-exilic Israel) | Van der Toorn/Smith: late accretion, not pre-exilic |
| Queen of Heaven = Astarte (+ Ishtar syncretism) | MEDIUM-HIGH | Ishtar identification has strongest philological evidence; Astarte has strongest cultural fit |

**Strongest section:** The Queen of Heaven identification. Day systematically evaluates all five candidates, gives each its due, and reaches a differentiated conclusion. This is Day at his best — methodical, fair to alternatives, appropriately cautious.

**Weakest section:** The Anat-Yahu → pre-exilic Northern Kingdom chain. Day's preference is plausible but the evidence from Elephantine can't tell us WHEN the syncretism developed. The Hosea 14 wordplay is too weak to carry this weight alone.

**Most important implicit argument:** The cumulative picture across Chapters 2, 3, and 5 shows that Yahweh had THREE possible consorts in syncretistic Israelite religion — Asherah (through El equation), Anat (through Baal equation), and Astarte (through Baal equation + Queen of Heaven cult). This isn't a single goddess tradition but MULTIPLE competing consort traditions, which helps explain why the Deuteronomistic polemic needed to be so comprehensive.
