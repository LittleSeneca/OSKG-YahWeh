# Albertz — Chapter 6: A Prospect on the History of Religion in the Hellenistic Period

Albertz treats the Hellenistic period (332-64 BCE) as a transitional era — continuing the Persian-period dialectic of splintering and integration, while introducing new developments: the formation of a true hierocracy (high priest as political leader), the completion of the canonization process (Prophets, writings), the rise of Torah piety as a distinctive Jewish personal theology, and the emergence of apocalyptic as a theology of resistance from marginalized groups. He explicitly limits himself to developments visible within the Old Testament writings, referring readers to histories of Judaism for fuller treatment.

## Chapter 6 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1. Hellenistic hierocracy and aristocratic power struggles | MEDIUM-HIGH | Dating of high priest's political powers |
| 2. Tax-leasing, cultural split, and Maccabean preconditions | MEDIUM | Josephus's Tobiad romance reliability |
| 3. Chronicles as anti-Samaritan re-legitimation | MEDIUM | Samaritan connection inferential |
| 4. Torah piety as distinctive Jewish personal theology | MEDIUM-HIGH | Dating of Torah psalms |
| 5. Apocalyptic as theology of resistance from marginalized groups | LOW-MEDIUM | Sociological locations speculative |

**Strongest section:** 6.3 (Torah piety). Albertz's analysis of how Torah piety transformed personal religion is the best thing in the chapter — specific, textually grounded, and theologically significant.

**Weakest section:** 6.4.3 (Daniel). It's treated very briefly in the prospect, and the sociological identification of the Hasidim draws heavily on Kampen's disputed thesis.

**Overall assessment of the chapter:** This is explicitly a "prospect" not a full treatment, and it shows. The chapter is thinner than Chapters 4 and 5, with broader brushstrokes and less detailed claims. This is by design — Albertz is sketching trajectories that future scholarship would need to fill in. The most durable contributions are the Torah piety analysis and the institutional analysis of the emerging hierocracy.
