# Cross — Part II + III: Divine Warrior, League and Kingdom

---

## Part II: The Cultus of the Israelite League

### The Divine Warrior (Chapter 5)

**Cross's claim:** The central religious concept of early Israel was Yahweh as DIVINE WARRIOR — a concept borrowed directly from Canaanite myth, specifically the Ba'l cycle. Israel took the mythic pattern of the god who defeats chaos (Sea/Leviathan/Death) and historicized it: Yahweh the Divine Warrior leads Israel's armies in historical battles. This is the key mechanism by which myth became epic.

**Canaanite pattern → Israelite transformation:**

| Canaanite Mythic Element | Israelite Historicized Form |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Ba'l defeats Sea (*Yamm*) | Yahweh defeats Egypt at the Red Sea |
| Ba'l defeats Death (*Mot*) | Yahweh defeats Pharaoh's army |
| Ba'l builds temple on holy mountain | Yahweh dwells on Sinai, then Zion |
| Ba'l enthroned as king of gods | Yahweh enthroned as king of Israel |
| Divine council acclaims Ba'l | Divine council acclaims Yahweh |

**Psalm 24 as classic Divine Warrior text:**
> "Lift up your heads, O gates! Be lifted up, O ancient doors, that the King of Glory may come in! Who is this King of Glory? Yahweh, strong and mighty, Yahweh, mighty in battle!"

**Cross's reading:** This is a liturgy for Yahweh's entrance into his temple as conquering Divine Warrior — almost certainly used in the Jerusalem cult. The form is borrowed from Canaanite liturgy. The content has been Yahwized.

**The "Ritual Conquest" theory:**
Cross argues that the Israelite league (the pre-monarchic tribal confederation) reenacted the Divine Warrior's victory in ritual form — possibly at Gilgal or Shiloh. The battles of Judges reflect both historical skirmishes AND ritual patterns. The "conquest" narrative in Joshua blends both.

**Confidence:** HIGH on the Canaanite parallels (the Ba'l cycle texts are unambiguous). HIGH on the Divine Warrior imagery in early poetry (Exodus 15, Judges 5, Psalm 68). MEDIUM on the "Ritual Conquest" theory (creative but speculative).

### The Song of the Sea and Canaanite Myth (Chapter 6)

**Cross's claim:** Exodus 15 (the Song of the Sea) is one of the oldest texts in the Bible — and it's constructed almost entirely from Canaanite mythic motifs.

**The parallels:**

| Exodus 15                                            | Ba'l Cycle (KTU 1.1-1.6)                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| "Yahweh is a man of war"                             | Ba'l the warrior                           |
| "Your right hand, Yahweh, shatters the enemy"        | Ba'l's club drives back Sea                |
| "At the blast of your nostrils the waters piled up"  | Ba'l's meteorological weapons              |
| "You led the people you redeemed to your holy abode" | Ba'l's temple on Saphon after victory      |
| "Yahweh will reign forever and ever"                 | Ba'l enthroned as king after defeating Mot |

**Cross's conclusion:** "The Song of the Sea is a historicized version of the mythic conflict between the Divine Warrior and the Sea. The historical event (the escape from Egypt) has been shaped and interpreted by mythic patterns. This is not to say the event didn't happen. It is to say that the event was experienced and narrated through inherited mythic categories."

**The dating question:** Cross dates the Song of the Sea to the 12th-11th centuries based on archaic linguistic features. If correct, this is VERY early evidence for Yahweh's absorption of Ba'l's warrior mythology.

**Confidence:** HIGH on the mythic parallels. MEDIUM on the dating (archaic language can be consciously archaized).

## Part III: League and Kingdom

### Yahweh and Ba'l (Chapter 7)

**Cross's claim:** The conflict between Yahweh and Ba'l in the Bible reflects a real historical struggle — not between Israelites and Canaanites, but WITHIN Israelite society. Two models of divine kingship competed: the old Divine Warrior model (Yahweh as conquering king) and the Canaanite royal model (Ba'l as fertility-bestowing king associated with the monarchy).

**The political-theological dynamic:**
- The Israelite league (pre-monarchic): Yahweh as Divine Warrior who leads the tribal militia
- The monarchy under Solomon: Ba'l-ized Yahwism — Yahweh takes on Ba'l's attributes as the king's patron deity
- The northern kingdom (Ahab/Jezebel): Phoenician Ba'l competes directly with Yahweh
- The prophetic response (Elijah/Elisha): Reassertion of old Divine Warrior theology against Ba'l-ized monarchy

**The Elijah cycle (1 Kings 17-19):**
Cross reads it as a polemic against Ba'l-ized Yahwism. Elijah's contest on Carmel is not pure Yahwism vs. pure Ba'l worship — it's old Yahwistic Divine Warrior theology vs. the syncretistic royal cult that had absorbed too much Ba'l.

**The irony:** The prophets WON the battle against Ba'l's name, but LOST the battle against Ba'l's attributes. Yahweh became the storm god, the fertility god, the rider of clouds — everything Ba'l had been. The name was purged; the mythology was absorbed.

**Confidence:** HIGH on Ba'l attributes absorbed by Yahweh. MEDIUM on Cross's specific political reading of the Elijah cycle.

### Cross's Relationship to the Field

Cross stands between Albright (his teacher) and Smith (his student). Compared to both:
- Cross is MORE willing than Albright to acknowledge Canaanite continuity
- Cross is LESS willing than Smith to see late evolutionary development — he finds early monotheistic impulses
- Cross provides the philological and mythological foundation that Smith built on

**The key tension in Cross:** He simultaneously argues that Israelite religion grew organically from Canaanite culture AND that Israelite religion was distinctive from the beginning. The Divine Warrior theology was BOTH borrowed from Ba'l AND original to Israel's experience. This tension is productive — it's what makes his work generative rather than reductive. It's also what allowed his students to move in different directions: some toward more continuity (Smith), others toward more distinctiveness (Miller).

### What to Read Him For

Cross is not where you go for the most current conclusions — many of his specific claims have been superseded. He's where you go for the INTELLECTUAL FOUNDATION. When Smith argues convergence/differentiation, when Römer traces Yahweh's southern origin, when Sommer discusses divine fluidity — they're all working within a framework Cross established. Reading Cross tells you where the questions came from, even if the answers have moved on.
