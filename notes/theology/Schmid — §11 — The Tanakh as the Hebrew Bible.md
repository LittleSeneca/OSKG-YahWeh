# Schmid — §11: The Tanakh as the Hebrew Bible

This is the section where Schmid stakes his controversial claim: the Torah formed in the PERSIAN PERIOD (5th-4th centuries BCE). The canon's three-part structure (Torah, Nevi'im, Ketuvim) was a slow development, not an original design. The Torah is the oldest and most important part — and its formation was likely driven by Persian imperial authorization of local laws. This dating has massive implications for the Truth project: if Schmid is right, the Pentateuch's narratives about Israel's origins are Persian-period theological constructs.

## Claim 1: The Hebrew Bible as a fixed canon is a post-biblical phenomenon — the texts are "pre-biblical" and become biblical only through canonization
→ [[claim-hb-fixed-canon-post-biblical-phenomenon]] | **schmid-ht-11.1** | Confidence: high
  Standard critical scholarship. The canon's authority is a function of reception history, not an inherent property of the texts. Supports: [[claim-canon-boundaries-not-absolute-center-periphery-model]]

## Claim 2: The Synod of Jamnia is an academic fallacy — there was no council that decided the canon
→ [[claim-jamnia-synod-academic-myth-no-canon-council]] | **schmid-ht-11.2** | Confidence: very-high
  Now scholarly consensus (Stemberger). Only Qoheleth and Song of Songs were debated at Jamnia — not the canon as a whole. Supports: [[claim-hb-fixed-canon-post-biblical-phenomenon]]

## Claim 3: The canon was originally TWO parts ("Law and Prophets"), not three — the tripartite structure developed later
→ [[claim-canon-originally-two-parts-law-prophets-not-three]] | **schmid-ht-11.3** | Confidence: high
  Convergent witnesses: Ben Sira (pre-Maccabean), Qumran (Hasmonean), NT (1st c.) all attest to a two-part canon. Ketuvim crystallized after Nevi'im's closure.

## Claim 4: THE TORAH FORMED IN THE PERSIAN PERIOD — driven by Persian imperial authorization of local laws
→ [[claim-torah-formed-persian-period-imperial-authorization]] | **schmid-ht-11.4** | Confidence: medium
  Schmid's most important claim for the Truth Project. The conflicting D and P material represents a Persian-period COMPROMISE document. If correct, Genesis-Deuteronomy are NOT sources for pre-exilic religion. Contradicts: [[claim-four-stage-model-monotheism-development]], [[claim-invention-means-progressive-construction]]

## Claim 5: The Nevi'im was closed pre-Maccabean (~200 BCE) — Daniel missed the cut
→ [[claim-neviim-closed-pre-maccabean-daniel-missed-cut]] | **schmid-ht-11.5** | Confidence: medium-high
  Daniel (167-164 BCE) was too late for the already-closed prophets. Joshua 1 and Malachi 3 form a literary inclusio around the Nevi'im.

## Claim 6: The Ketuvim served as a "melting pot" — and its theological contribution is the "mundane everyday application" of Torah and Prophets
→ [[claim-ketuvim-melting-pot-mundane-everyday-application]] | **schmid-ht-11.6** | Confidence: medium
  Underdeveloped theological rationale. At least 70 different book orders across manuscripts show its fluidity.

## Chapter 11 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Fixed canon is post-biblical | HIGH | Qumran evidence is standard |
| Jamnia synod is myth | VERY HIGH | Now scholarly consensus |
| Original two-part canon | HIGH | Convergent witnesses across sources |
| Torah formed in Persian period | MEDIUM | Circumstantial evidence; no direct proof of imperial authorization |
| Nevi'im closed ~200 BCE | MEDIUM-HIGH | Daniel's exclusion is strong evidence |
| Ketuvim as melting pot | MEDIUM | Underdeveloped theological rationale |

**Strongest section:** The evidence for an original two-part canon (Ben Sira, Qumran, NT). Multiple independent witnesses converge.

**Weakest section:** The Persian imperial authorization theory. It's plausible but the evidence chain is: Persian policy of local laws + heterogeneous Torah = compromise = external pressure. Each link is debatable.

**Overall significance for the Truth project:** This is where Schmid delivers his most direct challenge to the American consensus. If the Torah formed in the Persian period (not earlier), then Genesis through Deuteronomy are NOT windows into pre-exilic Israelite polytheism. They are Persian-period theological constructions. The conflicting D and P material — which traditional source criticism interpreted as evidence of earlier, independent sources — Schmid interprets as evidence of a Persian-period COMPROMISE document. This is a radically different reading of the same textual evidence. The dating question is the single most important unresolved issue for the entire Truth project, and Schmid's position represents the maximal skeptical end of the spectrum.
