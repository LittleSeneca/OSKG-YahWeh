34|# Keel & Uehlinger — Part II: Egyptian Colonialism and the Prevalence of Political and Warrior Deities — Late Bronze Age
35|
36|This Part covers §§30–60 (plus an Excursus) — the core of the Late Bronze Age (~1550–1150 BCE) iconographic evidence from four key Palestinian cities (Hazor, Megiddo, Lachish, Beth-Shean), each representing a different regional subsystem. The central thesis: Egyptian colonialism reshaped Canaanite religion by suppressing the public, monumental representation of goddesses in favor of male warrior and political deities (Baal, Reshef, Seth-Baal, Amun, the Pharaoh), while goddess worship survived through cheap terra-cotta figurines in domestic settings — a split between "official" and "private" religion that anticipates Dever's book-religion vs. folk-religion distinction.

40|## Claim 1: MB→LB transition was gradual and regionally variable — no single "Canaanite religion"
→ [[claim-mb-lb-transition-gradual-regional-variability-religious-continuity]] | **keel-ggi-p2.1** | Confidence: high
  Transitional period of 100-150 years (LB I, ca. 1550-1450). Four cities (Hazor, Megiddo, Lachish, Beth-Shean) tell different stories. Hazor's Temple H shows "gentle and actually unbroken transition." The hill country where Israel emerged was likely its own regional subsystem, not simply modified coastal Canaanite religion.
  Contradicts: Albright's sharp-transition conquest model

## Claim 2: Hazor = northern Syrian traditions — male heavenly deities eclipse goddess
→ [[claim-hazor-northern-syrian-traditions-male-heavenly-deities-eclipse-goddess]] | **keel-ggi-p2.2** | Confidence: high
  Syrian-style longroom temple. Weather god on bull with sun/moon disk. Moon god stele (crescent with disk above outstretched arms). No anthropomorphic goddesses in later Temple H phases. Mitannian seals avoid anthropomorphic deities. "First evidence of piety oriented toward heavenly beings" in Palestine.
  Contradicts: Kempinski's triad (El, Baal, fertility goddess) at Megiddo

## Claim 3: Megiddo LB shift — vegetation goddess out, warrior deities in
→ [[claim-megiddo-lb-shift-vegetation-goddess-to-warrior-deities]] | **keel-ggi-p2.3** | Confidence: high
  Metal figurines: warrior gods (Baal, Reshef) replace naked goddesses. Goddess survives in cheap clay. Military themes replace erotic scenes. Ivories: women as servants or absent. Egyptian chariot motif introduced. Warrior Yahweh stepped into a role Baal and Reshef were already playing.
  Contradicts: Kempinski's triad interpretation; elite-product bias alternative

## Claim 4: Lachish = intense Egyptian-Canaanite syncretism — qudshu, Baal-Seth, Hathor
→ [[claim-lachish-egyptian-canaanite-syncretism-qudshu-baal-seth]] | **keel-ggi-p2.4** | Confidence: high
  Qudshu goddess: Canaanite nakedness + Egyptian Hathor locks and lotus stems; 70+ examples. Inscribed pitcher from Fosse Temple: "gift for my lady Elat" — strongest LB material link to Asherah. Baal-Seth as serpent-conquering "savior" prefigures Yahweh's Chaoskampf. Revadim/Aphek nursing goddess with Q-symbol pendant.
  Contradicts: Cross/Maier/Hadley identify qudshu with Atirat/Asherah — Keel & Uehlinger more cautious

## Claim 5: Beth-Shean = extreme Egyptian state religion — Amun, pharaoh, goddess exclusion
→ [[claim-beth-shean-egyptian-state-religion-amun-goddess-exclusion]] | **keel-ggi-p2.5** | Confidence: high
  Monumental art purely Egyptian: Amun (58% of double-sided plaques), Re-harakhty, Ptah, divine pharaoh. No goddess on royal monuments. Terra-cotta goddesses survive in indigenous form. Anat appears on stelae as warrior goddess. Male monotheism didn't invent goddess suppression — it continued a LB trajectory.
  Contradicts: Helck disputes Anat identification on stele

## Claim 6 (Excursus): Astarte Plaques = goddesses in domestic piety, continuous from MB IIB through Iron Age
→ [[claim-lb-astarte-plaques-goddess-domestic-piety-survival]] | **keel-ggi-p2.6** | Confidence: high
  The synthetic treatment of the Excursus within Part II. Rebuts Tadmor's "concubine" thesis on three fronts. Tell Qasile shrine plaque as decisive iconographic evidence. Goddess tradition runs unbroken: MB IIB metal → LB terra-cotta plaques → Iron II Judean Pillar Figurines. Asherah worship was the indigenous default, not a late pagan corruption.
  Contradicts: Tadmor (1982), H. Weippert (1988), Hadley (1989)

## Claim 7: Radical LB split — official (male, warrior, costly, Egyptian) vs. private (goddess, cheap, domestic, indigenous)
→ [[claim-lb-official-vs-private-religion-split-warrior-male-vs-goddess-terracotta]] | **keel-ggi-p2.7** | Confidence: high
  Costly/monumental: male warrior/political deities, Egyptian style, temple/state context. Cheap/domestic: naked goddess terra-cottas, indigenous Canaanite style, home context. Egyptian colonialism created "official religion" as distinct category. The Deuteronomic reformers attacked a goddess tradition already pushed underground for centuries before Israel existed.
  Contradicts: Text-based scholars who treat LB Canaanite religion as unified pantheon (Smith, de Moor)

235|### Key takeaways for the Truth Project:
236|
237|1. **Yahweh emerged in a landscape already dominated by male warrior gods.** Baal, Reshef, Seth-Baal, Amun, and the divine pharaoh all competed for supremacy in LB Palestine. Yahweh's character as a divine warrior (Exodus 15, Deuteronomy 33) was not an innovation — it was Yahweh's entry into a role already occupied by multiple competitors.
238|
239|2. **The goddess was already marginalized in official/public religion before Israel existed.** This challenges any narrative that presents pre-Yahwistic Canaan as a goddess-centered paradise. The material evidence shows that Egyptian colonialism had already driven the nurturing goddess into domestic, cheap, "private" forms by the 13th century.
240|
241|3. **Continuity, not rupture, characterizes the MB→LB→Iron Age transition in goddess worship.** The same naked-goddess-with-breasts type appears in MB metal, LB terra-cotta, and Iron II Judean Pillar Figurines. Dever's JPFs are the latest phase of a 700+ year tradition. This makes it harder to argue that Asherah worship was a late "pagan corruption" of Yahwism — it was the indigenous default.
242|
243|4. **Regional diversity is essential.** Hazor (northern Syrian), Megiddo (mixed), Lachish (Egyptian-Canaanite fusion), and Beth-Shean (Egyptian state cult) each represent different religious subsystems. The hill country where Israel emerged was likely its own subsystem, not a direct continuation of any one of these.
244|
245|5. **The name of the goddess matters less than her function.** Keel & Uehlinger's caution about naming the "Astarte plaque" goddess (she could be Asherah, Anat, or Astarte — or all three) is methodologically sound. The iconographic evidence reveals what the goddess *did* (fertility, protection, childbirth, nursing, erotic blessing) more reliably than it reveals what she was *called* in any particular place and time.
246|
247|6. **The methodological integration of iconography and text is still incomplete.** Keel & Uehlinger provide the comprehensive iconographic evidence that Smith, de Moor, and other text-based scholars lack. But they also acknowledge that iconography and texts "belong to completely different functional spheres of religion" (§58 n.49). The Truth Project needs both — iconography for what people actually did, texts for what the literate élite thought should be done.
