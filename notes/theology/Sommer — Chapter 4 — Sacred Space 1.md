# Sommer — Chapter 4

## God's Bodies and Sacred Space (1): Tent, Ark, and Temple

---

## Claim 1: The fluidity model explains the paradox of sacred space
→ [[claim-fluidity-model-explains-sacred-space-paradox-multiple-bodies]] | **sommer-bog-4.1** | Confidence: high
  How can God be simultaneously "in heaven" AND "in the Temple"? The traditional answer ("God transcends space") is philosophical, not biblical. The Bible's answer: the ANE fluidity model. God has multiple bodies — one in heaven, one in the Temple. No paradox, just multiple simultaneous embodiments.
  Supports: [[claim-ane-gods-fluid-selves-multiple-local-manifestations]], [[claim-fluidity-model-survived-kabbalah-christianity]]

## Claim 2: The Tent of Meeting (Exodus 33) and the Ark reflect fluid theology
→ [[claim-tent-of-meeting-ark-fluid-theology-god-travels-with-israel]] | **sommer-bog-4.2** | Confidence: high
  Moses speaks with God "face to face" at a tent outside the camp — God has a body (face!) that Moses sees. The Ark is a portable throne for one of Yhwh's bodies (1 Sam 4:4: "enthroned on the cherubim"). Both are mobile because God has multiple bodies. Physically impossible with ONE body; perfectly natural with MULTIPLE.
  Supports: [[claim-je-sources-preserved-ane-fluidity-model-yhwh-multiple-manifestations]]

## Claim 3: 1 Kings 8 (Solomon's Temple Dedication) represents D's anti-fluid correction
→ [[claim-1-kings-8-anti-fluid-d-theology-name-dwells-not-god]] | **sommer-bog-4.3** | Confidence: high
  "The heavens and the highest heaven cannot contain You; how much less this house!" (1 Kings 8:27). This is D theology — a deliberate correction of older fluid theology. The Temple contains only God's Name, not God himself. Solomon is arguing AGAINST what people used to believe.
  Supports: [[claim-deuteronomic-shem-theology-rejects-divine-fluidity]], [[claim-priestly-deuteronomistic-sources-rejected-fluidity-model]]
