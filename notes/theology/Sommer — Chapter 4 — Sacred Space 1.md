# Sommer — Chapter 4

## God's Bodies and Sacred Space (1): Tent, Ark, and Temple

---

## Claim 1: The fluidity model explains the paradox of sacred space

**The problem:** How can God be simultaneously "in heaven" AND "in the Temple"? This paradox has troubled Jewish and Christian theology for millennia. The traditional solution — "God is everywhere," "God transcends space" — is a philosophical answer, not a biblical one.

**Sommer's solution:** The Bible's own answer is simpler. The ANE fluidity model: God has multiple bodies. One in heaven, one in the Temple. No paradox — just multiple simultaneous embodiments. The text means what it says.

---

## Evidence — The Tent of Meeting (Exodus 33)

**The older tradition:** Moses sets up a tent "outside the camp." "Yhwh would come down to the tent surrounded by (or in the form of?) a pillar of cloud, and Moses would speak with God face to face, as a man speaks with his friend" (Exodus 33:9, 11).

- This is J/E material — fluid theology
- God has a body (face!); Moses sees it
- The tent contains one of God's bodies
- Simultaneously, God also dwells in heaven
- No philosophical explanation needed — the fluidity model makes sense of it

---

## Evidence — The Ark

**The Ark as divine throne:**
- The Ark was a portable throne/footstool for one of Yhwh's bodies
- 1 Samuel 4:4: "Yhwh of hosts who sits enthroned on the cherubim" — the cherubim are the Ark's lid
- The Ark moves; Yhwh's embodied presence moves with it
- This is physically impossible if Yhwh has only ONE body — perfectly natural if he has MULTIPLE

---

## Evidence — 1 Kings 8 (Solomon's Temple Dedication)

**The anti-fluid reaction:**
> "The heavens and the highest heaven cannot contain You; how much less this house which I have built!" (1 Kings 8:27)

**Sommer's reading:** This is D theology. Solomon's prayer represents the anti-fluid revolution. The Temple does NOT contain God — it only contains God's Name. God is too transcendent to dwell in a building. This is a CORRECTION of the older fluid theology.
