# Day — Chapter 1: Yahweh and El

Day's foundational chapter. He argues that Yahweh and El were originally separate deities who became equated, that Yahweh originated in the south (Midian/Edom), and that Israel appropriated massive amounts of El language and imagery — aged god, wisdom, creator, divine council, paradise — while eventually rejecting only two El elements: the bull cult and El's consort Asherah.

---

## Claim 1: Yahweh and El were originally DISTINCT deities, not the same god
→ [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]] | **day-ygc-1.1** | Confidence: high
  "El and Yahweh were originally distinct deities that became amalgamated" (p. 15). Cross's formula (*ˀil ḏū yahwī ṣabaˀôt*) is unattested anywhere, and his grammatical objection fails — Kuntillet Ajrud shows proper names CAN appear in construct. E and P sources signal the discontinuity: the patriarchs didn't know the name Yahweh.
  Supports: [[claim-asherah-el-consort-transferred-to-yahweh]] (the transfer requires distinct deities that merged), [[claim-asherah-goddess-in-ot-passages]] (Ch1 lays foundation for Ch2 consort argument). Contradicts: Cross's El-Yahweh identity thesis.

---

## Claim 2: Yahweh originated OUTSIDE Canaan, in the south (Midian/Edom)
→ [[claim-yahweh-originated-south-midian-edom]] | **day-ygc-1.2** | Confidence: high
  The Egyptian Soleb inscription (14th c. BCE) references "Shasu of Yhw" alongside "Shasu of Seir." Biblical poetry (Judg 5, Deut 33, Hab 3) has Yahweh marching from Edom/Seir/Teman. Kuntillet Ajrud: "Yahweh of Teman." Even Lemche finds this convincing. Fleming (2021) and Frevel offer the most recent challenges.
  Supports: [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]] (southern origin makes Yahweh foreign to Canaan).

---

## Claim 3: The OT's divine council derives from the seventy sons of El
→ [[claim-divine-council-seventy-sons-of-el]] | **day-ygc-1.3** | Confidence: very-high
  At Ugarit: "the seventy sons of Asherah" (KTU² 1.4.VI.46). Deut 32:8 originally read "sons of God" (4QDeut, LXX); MT's "sons of Israel" is deliberate censorship. Genesis 10 lists precisely seventy nations. The OT NEVER calls them "sons of Yahweh" — always "sons of God" with *ʼl* — revealing their origin in the Canaanite god El.
  Supports: [[claim-ot-asherah-equals-ugaritic-athirat]] (the seventy sons of El AND Asherah connect the divine couple), [[claim-asherah-el-consort-transferred-to-yahweh]] (the council comes with the consort).

---

## Claim 4: The Garden of Eden story derives from El's dwelling place
→ [[claim-eden-derives-from-el-dwelling-place]] | **day-ygc-1.4** | Confidence: medium-high
  Gen 2 places Eden at the source of the Tigris and Euphrates — Armenia, not the Persian Gulf. The Hittite-Canaanite Elkunirsa myth places El at the Euphrates source. Ezek 28 places Eden on a mountain (El characteristically dwells on a mountain). Eden's wisdom emphasis connects to El, who was particularly noted for wisdom.
  Supports: [[claim-el-shaddai-means-el-mountain-one]] (El's mountain dwelling reinforces the etymology), [[claim-divine-council-seventy-sons-of-el]] (divine council meets on El's mountain).

---

## Claim 5: El-Shaddai means "El, the Mountain One," not "God Almighty"
→ [[claim-el-shaddai-means-el-mountain-one]] | **day-ygc-1.5** | Confidence: medium
  Akkadian *šadû* = "mountain." The Deir 'Alla inscription calls divine beings "Shaddayyin" in parallel with "gods" — the divine assembly on a mountain. Shaddai appears parallel with El "a remarkable number of times, especially in Job." Day hedges: "though we cannot be certain, a plausible case can be made" (p. 35).
  Depends on: [[claim-eden-derives-from-el-dwelling-place]] (El's mountain dwelling is the conceptual foundation).

---

## Claim 6: Jeroboam's golden calves were Yahwistic images (not pedestals), derived from El's bull symbolism
→ [[claim-jeroboam-golden-calves-yahwistic-el-bull]] | **day-ygc-1.6** | Confidence: high
  Jehu's revolution wiped out Baal but left the calves (2 Kgs 10:28-29) — they weren't Baal symbols. Elijah never condemned them. Samaria ostracon 41: *ʻglyw* = "calf of Yahweh." One site was Bethel ("house of El") where the god was called El-Bethel. Bull symbolism at Ugarit is supremely associated with El ("Bull El," *tr ʼil*), not Baal.
  Supports: [[claim-divine-image-prohibition-late-exilic]] (mainstream bull images for two centuries), [[claim-yhwh-represented-by-standing-stones-massebot]] (another pre-exilic non-aniconic practice).

## Chapter 1 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Yahweh and El originally distinct | HIGH | Cross/de Moor's counter-arguments about shared identity |
| Yahweh originated in the south (Midian) | HIGH | Fleming (2021) and Frevel's complications |
| Divine council = seventy sons of El | VERY HIGH | Heiser's ontological reinterpretation |
| Eden = El's dwelling in Armenia | MEDIUM-HIGH | Multiple competing sacred mountains; monocausal explanation |
| El-Shaddai = "El, the mountain one" | MEDIUM | Etymological uncertainty; competing "field" derivation |
| Golden calves = Yahwistic images from El | HIGH | Pedestal theory from Syrian iconography |

**Strongest section:** The divine council / seventy sons argument. The convergence of Ugaritic numbers, Deut 32:8 textual criticism, MT alteration, and later Jewish tradition makes this nearly unassailable.

**Weakest section:** The "character" argument for El/Yahweh distinction. "Wholly benevolent" is a caricature of Ugaritic El, and the ferocity/kindness contrast collapses when you consider the full biblical portrait of Yahweh.

**Most important insight:** Day's observation that the OT "has no qualms in equating Yahweh with El" — in marked contrast to its "vehement opposition to Baal." This selective appropriation (El = good, Baal = bad, Asherah = rejected, bull = eventually rejected) reveals a deliberate theological filtering process, not passive cultural absorption.

**Cross-reference notes:** Smith (*Early History*, Ch 1) agrees on El/Yahweh separation and convergence but emphasizes sociological mechanisms (convergence and differentiation) more than Day's comparative mythology. Dever barely engages Day's textual methodology but reaches similar conclusions archaeologically. Cross (*Canaanite Myth*) is the primary dissenting voice Day targets throughout.
