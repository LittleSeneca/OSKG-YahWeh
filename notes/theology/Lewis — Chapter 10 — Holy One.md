# Lewis — Chapter 10: Yahweh as the Holy One

Lewis examines holiness as the "deepest and innermost nature" of the Hebrew Bible's God (quoting Sellin). The chapter traces holiness from its earliest non-cultic meanings (awesome power, military might) through the Ark narrative's lethal holiness to the cultic management of holiness in P, H, and Ezekiel — where holiness becomes an ideological tool for constructing priestly hierarchy and marking sacred space/personnel/objects. It concludes with the Holiness Code's radical expansion of holiness to the entire community.

## Chapter 10 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |

| Early holiness = power, not purity | VERY HIGH | Some early texts may also imply separation |
| P's holiness = ideological hierarchy tool | HIGH | Moses omission could be accidental |
| H democratizes holiness to entire community | VERY HIGH | Practical vs. rhetorical democratization debated |
| No cultic prostitution in ancient Israel | VERY HIGH | Scholarly consensus now; older translations persist |
| Yahweh manifests holiness through military power | HIGH | Ezekiel's usage is exceptional (power in exile context) |

**Strongest sections:** The early holiness = power argument (genuinely original synthesis). The P vs. H contrast (clear and well-argued). The cultic prostitution debunking (necessary and well-sourced).

**Weakest section:** The extensive treatment of priestly ritual details (clothing, anointing, blood manipulation) can become a catalogue. Necessary for completeness but less analytically sharp than the earlier sections.

**What this chapter establishes:**
- Holiness has a diachronic development: power → cultic danger → priestly hierarchy → democratized ethics
- P and H represent competing visions of holiness preserved together
- The Hebrew Bible's holiness traditions are remarkably diverse — and this diversity is the point
- Holiness was "the deepest and innermost nature" of Yahweh not because of abstract theology but because it structured Israelite society at every level

**Cross-references:** Sommer's Chapter 4 covers sacred space in a complementary way. Heiser's Part 5 covers holiness in the context of holy war (complementing Lewis's early holiness = power claim). The P vs. H tension is an important corrective to readings that flatten Israelite religion into a single system.
