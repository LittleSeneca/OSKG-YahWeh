# Smith, Early History of God — Chapter 2

## Yahweh and Baal

---

## Claim 1: Baal was an accepted Israelite god from the Judges period through the fall of Judah
→ [[claim-baal-accepted-israelite-god-persistent]] | **smith-ehg-2.1** | Confidence: high
  Baal worship persisted for centuries as an accepted feature of Israelite religion — not a brief apostasy under Ahab and Jezebel. The cumulative evidence from Judges through Jeremiah (Samaria ostraca, Kuntillet Ajrud, Hosea, the Hezekiah-Manasseh-Josiah reform cycle) proves popular demand, not just royal sponsorship.
  Contradicted by: traditional reading of Ahab as apostate. Supports: [[claim-asherah-was-stylized-tree]], [[claim-josiah-reform-removed-asherah]] (same persistent popular religion model).

---

## Claim 2: Yahweh absorbed Baal's storm-warrior imagery through the political machinery of the monarchy
→ [[claim-yahweh-absorbed-baal-storm-warrior-imagery]] | **smith-ehg-2.2** | Confidence: very-high
  The Davidic monarchy deliberately adopted Baal's language — storm theophany, cloud-rider, Sea/Leviathan combat, mountain Saphon/Zion, sevenfold thunder — to describe Yahweh as the national god. The motif catalogue is overwhelming. Psalm 29 was almost certainly written for Baal. Same convergence model Smith applies to Asherah in Ch3.
  Supports: [[claim-asherah-was-yahwistic-symbol]] (same model), [[claim-female-imagery-absorbed-into-yahweh]] (same pattern), [[claim-anat-martial-imagery-absorbed-into-yahweh]] (secondary case).

---

## Claim 3: Anat's martial imagery was absorbed into Yahweh even though her cult never existed in Israel
→ [[claim-anat-martial-imagery-absorbed-into-yahweh]] | **smith-ehg-2.3** | Confidence: medium
  The Canaanite warrior goddess Anat's distinctive gory, cannibalistic, ecstatic battle imagery appears in biblical descriptions of Yahweh, even though Anat was never worshipped in Israel. The monarchy probably mediated this transfer via Egyptian royal ideology. Some passages COMBINE Anat's gore with Baal's storm (Deut 32:42-43, Ps 68:24).
  Supports: [[claim-female-imagery-absorbed-into-yahweh]] (same convergence pattern — female divine imagery absorbed into male Yahweh).

---

## Cross-Cutting Assessment: Chapter 2's Overall Strength

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Baal worship persisted throughout monarchy | HIGH | None — the cumulative evidence is overwhelming |
| Yahweh absorbed Baal's storm-warrior imagery | VERY HIGH | "Common idiom" argument (but parallels too specific) |
| Monarchy drove the absorption politically | MEDIUM-HIGH | Insufficient pre-monarchic data for comparison |
| Anat's imagery transferred to Yahweh | MEDIUM | Direct dependence unprovable; could be general ANE conventions |

**Strongest section:** §2 (Imagery of Baal and Yahweh). The motif catalogue is the most empirically grounded part of the book so far. This is where Smith is hardest to challenge.

**Weakest section:** §4 (Anat excursus). Interesting but speculative. Smith acknowledges the limitations honestly.

**Most theologically disruptive:** The Psalms-as-repurposed-Baal-hymns argument. Psalm 29 in particular. If you've ever been moved by the "voice of the Lord" breaking cedars and shaking the wilderness — that was originally about Baal.
