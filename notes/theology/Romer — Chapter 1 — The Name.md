# Römer, The Invention of God — Chapter 1

## The God of Israel and His Name

---

## Claim 1: The original pronunciation of Yhwh was "Yahô" or "Yahû," not "Yahweh"
→ [[claim-yahweh-original-pronunciation-yaho-yahu]] | **romer-inv-1.1** | Confidence: medium-high
  The tetragrammaton was originally a trigrammaton (Yhw = Yahô/Yahû); the pronunciation "Yahweh" is a later theological development from Exodus 3's wordplay. Key evidence: Elephantine papyri, Babylonian transliterations, and theophoric names.
  Supports: [[claim-yhwh-name-means-he-blows-storm-god]], [[claim-yhwh-originated-southern-deserts-edom-seir]], [[claim-yahweh-southern-desert-war-god-origin]]
  Challenged by: Church Father evidence (Iaoue, Iabe) suggests "Yahweh" was known earlier than Romer allows.

---

## Claim 2: The prohibition on pronouncing the divine name was a monotheistic development
→ [[claim-divine-name-prohibition-monotheistic-consequence]] | **romer-inv-1.2** | Confidence: high
  "Within a monotheistic view it is inappropriate for the one god to have a proper name" — a named god is a god among gods. The prohibition (Septuagint → Mishnah → Masoretes → "Jehovah") was a consequence of monotheism, not its cause.
  Depends on: [[claim-bible-preserves-polytheistic-traces]], [[claim-invention-means-progressive-construction]]
  Supports: [[claim-yahweh-original-pronunciation-yaho-yahu]], [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]]

---

## Claim 3: The name Yhwh probably means "he blows" — a storm god, not a creator god
→ [[claim-yhwh-name-means-he-blows-storm-god]] | **romer-inv-1.3** | Confidence: low
  Romer evaluates five etymologies and prefers the southern Semitic root *h-w-y* ("to blow") — consistent with a desert storm/war god. All etymologies are speculative; five exist, none commands consensus.
  Depends on: [[claim-yahweh-original-pronunciation-yaho-yahu]]
  Supports: [[claim-yahweh-southern-desert-war-god-origin]], [[claim-yhwh-originated-southern-deserts-edom-seir]], [[claim-yahweh-southern-storm-warrior-origin]]
  Note: Romer himself cautions that etymology may not matter much for understanding a deity's function.

---

## Claim 4: The biblical narrative itself acknowledges that Yhwh was not originally Israel's god
→ [[claim-bible-acknowledges-yhwh-foreign-origin]] | **romer-inv-1.4** | Confidence: high
  Exodus 6:2-3 explicitly states the patriarchs knew God as El Shadday, not Yhwh. The Bible preserves its own memory of Yhwh's foreign origin. "Scholar consensus: Yhwh was not at the beginning the god of Israel."
  Depends on: [[claim-bible-preserves-polytheistic-traces]]
  Supports: [[claim-el-original-god-israel-yahweh-imported-later]], [[claim-el-original-god-exodus]], [[claim-yahweh-southern-desert-war-god-origin]], [[claim-yhwh-originated-southern-deserts-edom-seir]], [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]]

## Chapter 1 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Original pronunciation was Yahô/Yahû | MEDIUM-HIGH | Church Father evidence suggests "Yahweh" was also ancient |
| Prohibition on pronunciation was monotheistic | HIGH (fact), MEDIUM (motive) | Magical concerns were also a factor |
| Name means "he blows" (storm god) | LOW | All etymologies are speculative |
| Bible acknowledges Yhwh's foreign origin | HIGH (Exodus 6:2-3 is explicit) | Could be theological retrojection, not memory |

**Strongest section:** The explanation of the Masoretic vocalization and the origin of "Jehovah" — clear, accessible, and well-evidenced.

**Weakest section:** The etymology of Yhwh — multiple competing theories, none provable. Römer's preferred reading ("he blows") is consistent with his larger thesis but underdetermined by the evidence.

**Römer's honesty:** He explicitly warns that etymology may not matter much for understanding a deity's function. This is a rare moment of a scholar admitting his own investigation may not be that important. Worth noting.
