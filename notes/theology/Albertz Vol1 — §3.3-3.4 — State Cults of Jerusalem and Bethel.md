# Albertz — §3.3–3.4: The Main State Cults of South and North

These sections describe how the monarchy created a new type of cult — the royal state sanctuary — fundamentally different from the decentralized local shrines of the pre-state period. Albertz's key insight: Jerusalem and Bethel represent two different responses to the same problem of reconciling monarchy with pre-state Yahwism. Jerusalem (South) was syncretistic, imperial, and priest-controlled — incorporating Jebusite/Canaanite traditions. Bethel (North) was conservative, populist, and Exodus-oriented — self-consciously archaizing against Jerusalem's innovations. The tragedy: Jerusalem's tightly controlled syncretism survived; Bethel's populist El-Yahwism slid into Baalization and was destroyed.

## §3.3 The Main State Cult in the South (Jerusalem)

### Claim 1: The monarchy created the royal state sanctuary — fusion of throne and altar
→ [[claim-monarchy-royal-state-sanctuary-fused-throne-altar]] | **albertz-hir-3.3-3.4.1** | Confidence: high
  Royal sanctuaries were "the property and the private chapel of the king" while claiming to be the public sanctuary. This "set in motion a centralization of the main Israelite cult which later... was to issue in the Deuteronomic requirement of the abolition of all local cults." Supports: [[claim-deuteronomy-written-josiah-reform]], [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]]. Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-kingship-non-sacral-prophetic-election-not-divine-essence]].

### Claim 2: David's religious policy was deliberate syncretism; Solomon tipped to Jebusite/Canaanite side
→ [[claim-david-syncretism-israelite-jebusite-solomon-tipped-canaanite]] | **albertz-hir-3.3-3.4.2** | Confidence: medium-high
  David appointed TWO priests: Abiathar (Shiloh/Israelite) and Zadok (Jebusite). Solomon exiled Abiathar, gave Zadokites monopoly. The Solomonic temple was likely a Jebusite renovation — the tradition obscures its pre-Israelite origin. Supports: [[claim-el-original-god-israel-yahweh-imported-later]], [[claim-jerusalem-temple-theology-alien-to-liberation-yahwism]].

### Claim 3: Jerusalem temple theology was fundamentally alien to pre-state liberation Yahwism
→ [[claim-jerusalem-temple-theology-alien-to-liberation-yahwism]] | **albertz-hir-3.3-3.4.3** | Confidence: medium
  Jerusalem combined El's static kingship with Baal's dynamic kingship, transposed into political history — Yahweh changed "from being liberator of the oppressed to being promoter of his own policy of expansion and oppression." Zion was identified with Zaphon, "not with Sinai in the south!" Contradicted by: [[claim-god-absolute-supremacy-no-metadivine-realm]], [[claim-israelite-kingship-non-sacral-prophetic-election-not-divine-essence]].

## §3.4 The Main State Cult in the North (Bethel)

### Claim 4: Jeroboam's revolt was fought under the Exodus banner — the Exodus narrative may have been shaped by it
→ [[claim-jeroboam-revolt-shaped-exodus-narrative-propaganda]] | **albertz-hir-3.3-3.4.4** | Confidence: low-medium
  Striking parallels between Moses (Ex 2:11-15; 5*) and Jeroboam's revolt (1 Kings 11-12): both flee after striking an overseer, return after the king's death, negotiate with a harsh successor, achieve liberation by departure. Ex 5:16 polemically identifies Solomon/Rehoboam with Pharaoh. In tension with: [[claim-exodus-group-small-declassed-mixed-forced-laborers]] (Albertz's own earlier claim).

### Claim 5: Jeroboam's Bethel cult was conservative, archaizing, Exodus-oriented — bull = pedestal, not idol
→ [[claim-jeroboam-bethel-cult-conservative-archaizing-bull-pedestal]] | **albertz-hir-3.3-3.4.5** | Confidence: medium-high
  The bull was a pedestal for invisible Yahweh (parallel to Jerusalem's cherubim), not a divine image. The cult cry was in the singular: "This is your god... who brought you out of Egypt." Deuteronomistic description (plural "calves," "heifer") is systematically polemical. Supports: [[claim-jeroboam-bulls-bethel-dan-el-images-out-archaize]], [[claim-jeroboam-golden-calves-yahwistic-el-bull]]. Contradicted by: [[claim-golden-calves-yhwh-worship-not-pagan-idolatry]].

### Claim 6: Jerusalem syncretism succeeded (state-controlled); Bethel's populist syncretism slid into Baalization
→ [[claim-jerusalem-syncretism-succeeded-bethel-populism-baalization-failed]] | **albertz-hir-3.3-3.4.6** | Confidence: medium
  "Whereas the El syncretism of Jerusalem, more strictly controlled by the state, succeeded, the El syncretism of Bethel, which was close to the people, ended in complete disaster." The bull's fertility associations overwhelmed its Exodus associations. Hosea saw a "Baalized" Yahweh cult. Contradicted by: [[claim-golden-calves-yhwh-worship-not-pagan-idolatry]], [[claim-no-genuine-syncretism-yhwh-baal]].

## §3.3–3.4 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1. Royal sanctuaries = new type of cult; fusion of throne and altar | HIGH | — |
| 2. David's syncretistic balancing act; Solomon tipped to Jebusite side | MEDIUM-HIGH | The "Solomonic temple = Jebusite renovation" is speculative |
| 3. Jerusalem theology = betrayal of liberation Yahwism | HIGH (theological elements); MEDIUM (normative judgment) | Whether this is betrayal or legitimate development |
| 4. Jeroboam's revolt shaped the Exodus narrative | LOW-MEDIUM | Literary parallels may be conventional, not causal |
| 5. Bethel cult was conservative, archaizing; bull = pedestal, not idol | MEDIUM-HIGH | Standard critical reading; depends on reading against Deuteronomistic polemic |
| 6. Jerusalem syncretism succeeded; Bethel's populism led to Baalization | MEDIUM | May be artifact of Jerusalem's editorial victory |

**Strongest section:** The interpretation of the Bethel bull as pedestal and the cult as Exodus-oriented conservatism (Claim 5). Albertz reads against the Deuteronomistic polemic convincingly and recovers a coherent Northern theology.

**Weakest section:** The claim that Jeroboam's revolt shaped the Exodus narrative (Claim 4). The parallels are interesting but the causal direction could run either way (Exodus narrative shaped Jeroboam's self-presentation; or later redactors shaped Jeroboam's story to match the Exodus pattern).

**Cross-reference:** Romer's *Invention of God* Ch 6-7 covers the cults of Israel and Judah with similar conclusions. Smith's *Early History of God* Ch 2 covers Yahweh and Baal, relevant to the Bethel bull's ambiguity. This section is where Albertz most closely parallels the standard critical account of the two kingdoms' divergent religious trajectories.
