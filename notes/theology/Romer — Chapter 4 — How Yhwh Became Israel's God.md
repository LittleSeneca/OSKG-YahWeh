# Römer, The Invention of God — Chapter 4

## How Did Yhwh Become the God of Israel?

---

## Claim 1: The name "Israel" is an El name, not a Yhwh name — the group originally venerated El

**Römer's claim:** "Israel" contains the theophoric element *'ēl* — the proper name of the Canaanite god El or a generic term for "god." Whatever the original etymology, it is an EL name, not a Yhwh name. "If Yhwh had been the original god, the group would be named something like *Yiśrâ-yāhû*" — but it isn't. Israel originally belonged to El. Yhwh was adopted later.

**Evidence presented — three proposed etymologies:**

| Etymology | Meaning | Evidence | Römer's Assessment |
|-----------|---------|----------|-------------------|
| From *ś-r-h* ("combat") | "May El combat" | The biblical folk etymology (Gen 32:29, Hos 12:4). Jacob "struggled with God." | **Unlikely.** This root is attested in Hebrew only in these two texts, and poorly in other Semitic languages. |
| From *y-š-r* ("to be just") | "El is just" | Poetic name Yeshurun for Israel (Deut 32:15; 33:5, 26). A Ugaritic name *y-š-r-'i-l* on a military charioteer list from the late 13th century — contemporary with Merneptah. | **Possible but problematic.** "A name on a list in Ugarit need not necessarily be in any way connected with the biblical Israel." The Hebrew vocalization favors a preformative conjugation, not this form. |
| From *ś-r-r* ("rule, govern, reign") | **"May El reign" / "May El impose himself as master"** | **Römer's preferred reading.** Supported by a Masoretic revision at Hos 12:5 that he argues conceals the original meaning. Names like *Śĕrāyāhû* ("Yhwh reigns") use the same root with Yhwh. | **Most plausible.** The root fits El's role as chief god and king of the pantheon. The folk etymology ("combat") supplanted this older meaning when Yhwh the warrior god replaced El. |

**The Masoretic revision at Hosea 12:5:**
- The MT reads: "He struggled with an angel and he had the upper hand" (*wayyāśar 'el-mal'āk wayyūkāl*)
- Römer argues this is "the result of a dogmatic revision." The Masoretes "wished to avoid too great a closeness between Jacob and God, and so they inserted the word *mal'āk* ('angel')"
- The original text: "El imposed himself and gained the upper hand" (*wayyāśar 'ēl wayyūkāl*)
- The consonants *'l* originally meant the god El (*'ēl*); the Masoretes changed the vocalization to make it the preposition *'el* ("toward")

**The further development:** "The folk etymology using the root *ś-r-h* ('combat')... was able to supplant the original etymology at the moment when Yhwh, a warrior god, became the tutelary god of the group 'Israel.'" The name was re-etymologized to fit the new god's warrior profile.

**Confidence:** HIGH that the name "Israel" is an El name (the theophoric element is unambiguous). MEDIUM on Römer's preferred etymology ("May El reign"). MEDIUM on the Masoretic revision hypothesis (clever but unprovable).

**What's at stake for faith:** "Israel" means "God strives" or "He struggles with God" in the biblical folk etymology — a name of intimate relationship with the divine. But historically, it probably meant "May El reign" — a declaration of allegiance to the Canaanite high god. The people who became "Israel" originally belonged to El, not Yhwh. Yhwh was a later acquisition.

## Claim 2: The Merneptah Stele (c. 1210-1205 BCE) is the first extra-biblical mention of Israel — as an ethnic group, not a kingdom

**Römer's claim:** The Merneptah Stele is the oldest clearly attested reference to "Israel" outside the Bible. Israel is described as an ethnic group (not a city or territory), located in the mountains of Ephraim. The stele "in no way presupposes an 'exodus' or any kind of emigration of this group from the land of Egypt."

**Evidence presented — the stele in detail:**

- **Date:** 1210-1205 BCE, reign of Pharaoh Merneptah
- **Physical description:** Granite stele, 3.18m high, 1.6m wide, 31cm thick — a monumental victory inscription
- **The key line:** "Israel is destroyed, its seed is no longer" — preceded by Ashkelon, Gezer, and Yenoam, all of which are city-states. But Israel is written differently.
- **The determinative:** Israel is given the determinative for an ETHNIC GROUP (a man and a woman, plus three strokes for plural). The other names (Ashkelon, Gezer, Yenoam) are given determinatives for cities/territories. "This does not mean that it is a group of nomads, but rather that it is the name of a definite group rather than of a region or locality."
- **The wordplay on "seed":** The Egyptian term *pr.t* can mean both "seed/semen" and "wheat." "The Egyptians had the habit... of destroying the wheat fields in conquered territories." It may also refer to "cutting off the penises of the dead bodies of its enemies." The ambiguity is deliberate — "the scribe could have clarified what was meant by adding the sign consisting of three kernels of grain if 'wheat' was intended, or a phallus if what was meant was 'sperm.'"
- **Geographic location:** Israel is listed between Ashkelon, Gezer, and Yenoam — placing it in the mountains of Ephraim, "the region where Saul will found his 'kingdom.'"
- **A possible earlier mention?** A Berlin pedestal statue (possibly Ramses II era) has been claimed to contain the name Israel as *Ia-cha-ri* or *a-chi-ru*. Römer is skeptical: "At best... we might have a quasi-contemporary variant; at the worst it is a completely different toponym."

**What the stele tells us:**
- By 1200 BCE, a group called "Israel" existed in the central highlands of Canaan
- They were significant enough for Pharaoh to boast about destroying them
- They were an ethnic/tribal group, not a city-state or kingdom
- There is NO indication they worshipped Yhwh at this point

**Confidence:** VERY HIGH on the stele as the first extra-biblical mention of Israel (universally accepted). HIGH that the determinative indicates an ethnic group (the hieroglyphic writing is unambiguous). HIGH that it's located in Ephraim (consistent with the geographic sequence). MEDIUM that the "seed" wordplay is deliberate (plausible but speculative).

**What's at stake for faith:** The earliest historical mention of Israel is not a story of liberation from Egypt. It's a story of destruction by Egypt. "Israel is destroyed, its seed is no longer." Pharaoh thought he had annihilated them. He was wrong — they survived and eventually produced the Bible — but the first historical mention of God's chosen people is a boast about wiping them out.

## Claim 3: The biblical texts themselves acknowledge that Yhwh and Israel were not originally connected

**Römer's claim:** "Although these texts do not agree on the place where this link was first formed, they do agree that Yhwh chose Israel at a particular point in history and that this people had not been his people from all time."

**Evidence presented:**
- Hosea 9:10: "I found Israel like grapes in the desert" — Yhwh "found" Israel; the relationship began at a specific moment
- Ezekiel 20: Yhwh "chose" Israel in Egypt — "I raised up my hand to swear an oath to the descendants of the house of Jacob and I made myself known to them in Egypt"
- Exodus 6:2-3: Yhwh was NOT known by that name to the patriarchs

**Confidence:** HIGH that these texts describe a beginning to the Yhwh-Israel relationship (the language is explicit). MEDIUM that this reflects historical memory rather than theological construction.

**What's at stake for faith:** The Bible's own narrative admits the relationship between God and Israel was not eternal. It began. God "found" Israel, "chose" Israel, "made himself known" to Israel. The covenant was formed in history, not in eternity. This is consistent with Römer's larger thesis: Yhwh was adopted.

**Strongest section:** The Merneptah Stele analysis — detailed, precise, and grounded in universally accepted evidence.

**Weakest section:** The Masoretic revision hypothesis at Hosea 12:5 — ingenious but speculative. Could be right, could be wrong; there's no way to verify.

**The narrative arc so far:** Chapter 2 established Yhwh's southern origin. Chapter 3 proposed Midianite mediation. Chapter 4 now shows that "Israel" was originally an El-worshipping group, not a Yhwh-worshipping one. Yhwh was a latecomer to a people who already had a god.
