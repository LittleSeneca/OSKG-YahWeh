# Smith — Ch7: El, Yahweh, and the Original God of Israel

This is THE pivotal chapter of the book — the hinge between Part II (Characteristics of Deities) and Part III (Origins of Monotheism). Smith makes the case that El, not Yahweh, was the original god of Israel, that Yahweh was a southern (Edomite/Midianite) warrior-storm god who was secondarily assimilated into El's identity, and that this "convergence" paradigm replaced the older Near Eastern "succession" paradigm (son replaces father). The chapter also raises — and largely endorses — the explosive possibility that El, not Yahweh, was the original god of the Exodus. Seven sections: El in the Bronze Age, Iron Age Phoenicia, Iron Age Aram/Transjordan, Iron Age Israel, whether El was Israel's original god, Yahweh's original character, and El as god of the Exodus.

## Claim 1: El's cult persisted into the Iron Age across the Levant — van der Toorn's "deus otiosus" thesis is overstated
→ [[claim-el-cult-persisted-iron-age]] | **smith-obm-7.1** | Confidence: medium-high
  Evidence from Phoenicia, Aram, Transjordan, Edom, and Ammon shows El was more than a dead god. Papyrus Amherst 63 places El in a cultic context near or within Israel. Sparse but cumulatively significant.
  Key edges: Supports [[claim-el-israelite-sanctuaries]] (El cult outside Israel → El cult inside Israel more plausible) and [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]] (two living cults merged, not a living cult absorbing a dead one).

---

## Claim 2: El was the original deity at major Israelite sanctuaries — Shiloh, Shechem, and Jerusalem — later absorbed into Yahwistic cult
→ [[claim-el-israelite-sanctuaries]] | **smith-obm-7.2** | Confidence: high
  Shiloh: tent tradition, incubation-dream, Elqanah's name. Shechem: 'ēl bĕrît = Ugaritic 'ilbrt (LB). Jerusalem: El Elyon in Gen 14. Yahweh moved into El's existing sanctuaries.
  Key edges: Depends on [[claim-el-cult-persisted-iron-age]]. Supports [[claim-name-israel-el-original]], [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]], and [[claim-three-stage-convergence-el-yahweh]] (sanctuary takeover is the material mechanism of convergence).

## Claim 3: The name "Israel" (yiśrā-'ēl) proves El was the original god — if Yahweh had been original, we'd expect *yiśrâ-yahweh or *yiśrâ-yāh
→ [[claim-name-israel-el-original]] | **smith-obm-7.3** | Confidence: high
  The simplest, most elegant evidence. The divine element is *'ēl — unambiguous. Not a silver bullet alone, but combined with sanctuary data and Deut 32:8-9, forms a triangulation.
  Key edges: Supports [[claim-el-israelite-sanctuaries]], [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]], and [[claim-deut-32-8-9-sons-of-el]] (triangulation: name + sanctuary + text-critical).

## Claim 4: Deuteronomy 32:8-9 (LXX/4QDeutj) preserves an older polytheistic theology — Yahweh was one of the "sons of El" who received Israel as his allotment
→ [[claim-deut-32-8-9-sons-of-el]] | **smith-obm-7.4** | Confidence: very-high
  MT: "sons of Israel"; LXX: "angels of God"; 4QDeutj: "sons of El." MT makes no contextual sense. The LXX/4Q reading aligns with ANE high-god-allots-nations concept. As close to a smoking gun as we get.
  Key edges: Supports [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]] (strongest textual snapshot), [[claim-divine-council-seventy-sons-of-el]], [[claim-name-israel-el-original]] (triangulation), and [[claim-three-stage-convergence-el-yahweh]] (Stage 2 anchor).

## Claim 5: A three-stage convergence model accounts for the El-Yahweh relationship — convergence replaced succession
→ [[claim-three-stage-convergence-el-yahweh]] | **smith-obm-7.5** | Confidence: medium-high
  Stage 1: El as original god. Stage 2: El as pantheon head, Yahweh as warrior-god member. Stage 3: El and Yahweh identified. "The son replaced and became the father." Yahweh did what Baal never did: absorbed the father's identity. The erasure of the father was a "requisite condition for monotheistic identity."
  Key edges: Depends on [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]], [[claim-el-israelite-sanctuaries]], [[claim-deut-32-8-9-sons-of-el]], [[claim-yahweh-southern-storm-warrior-origin]]. Supports [[claim-yahweh-absorbed-baal-storm-warrior-imagery]] (El convergence explains identity; Baal convergence explains character/function) and [[claim-yahweh-solar-language-royal-development]] (same convergence logic across deity profiles).

## Claim 6: Yahweh originated as a southern storm/warrior god (Edom/Midian/Teman/Seir/Sinai) — NOT as a title or manifestation of El
→ [[claim-yahweh-southern-storm-warrior-origin]] | **smith-obm-7.6** | Confidence: high
  Deut 33:2, Judg 5:4-5, Hab 3:3, Kuntillet Ajrud ("Yahweh of Teman") form a consistent geographical litany. Trade/kinship networks brought the southern deity north. Original profile may approximate Athtar (desert warrior/precipitation god). "Part of the original profile of Yahweh may be permanently lost."
  Key edges: Supports [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]] (geographical separation explains distinction), [[claim-yahweh-originated-south-midian-edom]], and [[claim-three-stage-convergence-el-yahweh]] (how a non-Canaanite god entered El's pantheon). Contra Cross/Albright/de Moor who see Yahweh as originally a title of El.

## Claim 7: El, not Yahweh, was likely the original god of the Exodus — Numbers 23-24 preserves this older tradition
→ [[claim-el-original-god-exodus]] | **smith-obm-7.7** | Confidence: medium
  Balaam oracles: "El who freed them from Egypt has horns like a wild ox" (Bull El). El appears ~3× as often as Yahweh in Num 23-24. Exodus 6:2-3: patriarchs knew El Shadday, not Yahweh. Egyptian names in Shilohite priesthood. The Exodus as synthesis of El-led family migration + Yahweh warrior theophany.
  Key edges: Depends on [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]], [[claim-el-israelite-sanctuaries]], and [[claim-three-stage-convergence-el-yahweh]]. Most speculative claim in the chapter; Smith hedges appropriately.

## Ch7 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1: El's cult persisted into Iron Age (smith-obm-7.1) | MEDIUM-HIGH | Sparse evidence; name survival ≠ cult survival |
| 2: El at Israelite sanctuaries (Shiloh, Shechem, Jerusalem) (smith-obm-7.2) | HIGH | Shared West Semitic typology vs. specific El cult |
| 3: Name "Israel" = El as original god (smith-obm-7.3) | HIGH | Name coinage after El=Yahweh identification |
| 4: Deut 32:8-9 (LXX/4Q) = Yahweh as son of Elyon (smith-obm-7.4) | VERY HIGH | Poetic parallelism (Elyon = Yahweh) reading |
| 5: Three-stage convergence model (smith-obm-7.5) | MEDIUM-HIGH | Regional variation vs. temporal stages; neatness |
| 6: Yahweh's southern origins (Edom/Midian) (smith-obm-7.6) | HIGH | Literary construct vs. historical memory; Athtar comparison is speculative |
| 7: El as original god of Exodus (smith-obm-7.7) | MEDIUM | Poetic archaism vs. older tradition; thin evidence |

**Strongest sections:** Section 4 (El in Iron Age Israel — the sanctuary evidence), Section 5 (the convergence model and the "son becomes father" paradigm shift), and the treatment of Deuteronomy 32:8-9. The convergence paradigm, especially the comparison with Baal (who overshadowed El but was never identified with him), is Smith's most original and generative contribution.

**Weakest section:** Section 7 (El as god of the Exodus). The evidence is thinner, the argument depends on inferences from the Balaam oracles whose dating and interpretation are contested, and the Egyptian-names argument, while clever, is indirect. Smith acknowledges this with more hedging language here than anywhere else in the chapter.

**Tension note:** Smith's argument in Section 6 that Yahweh's original profile may be "permanently lost" sits in tension with his reconstruction of that profile (Athtar-like warrior/precipitation god). If the earliest sources are secondary developments, how can we reconstruct the original? Smith's honesty about the limits of knowledge is admirable but creates an internal tension: he's reconstructing a profile he simultaneously says may be unrecoverable.

**Cross-reference to Ch6:** The convergence model here (Yahweh absorbs El) parallels the argument in Ch6 about Baal's relationship to El at Ugarit. In both cases, a younger warrior-god overshadows the patriarchal high god. But the crucial difference — Yahweh's identification WITH El — is what makes Israelite religion unique. This is the chapter that explains why Israel got monotheism and Ugarit didn't.

**Cross-reference to Römer:** Römer's "The Invention of God" also argues for Yahweh's southern origins and the secondary identification with El. Smith provides the detailed West Semitic/Ugaritic background that Römer assumes. The two books complement each other: Smith gives the conceptual prehistory, Römer gives the historical narrative of how it played out.

**Cross-reference to Cross:** Cross argued that Yahweh was originally a title of El ("Yahweh" as a cultic name of El). Smith's chapter is effectively a sustained counter-argument. The southern origin data, Deut 32:8-9, and the three-stage convergence model all point toward two originally distinct deities. Smith honors Cross's contribution while fundamentally revising his central thesis.
