# Stavrakopoulou — Part V: Head + Epilogue (Chapters 16-21)

---

## Chapter 16: Face to Face

### Claim: God's face could be seen — sometimes fatally, sometimes safely
→ [[claim-god-face-visible-dangerous-fatal-theophany]] | **stav-god-16-21.1** | Confidence: very-high
  "God's face is the most sought-after and the most dangerous part of his body." Moses sees God "face to face" (Exodus 33:11). Jacob: "I have seen God face to face, and yet my life is preserved" (Genesis 32:30). Isaiah sees and expects to die (Isaiah 6). The Aaronic blessing: "Yahweh make his face shine upon you." The prohibition to not see the face CONFIRMS its existence.
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]]

## Chapters 17-19: Headstrong Beauty, Profile, Sense and Sensitivity

### Claim: God's head had hair, a beard, eyes, ears, nose, and mouth — all functioning sense organs
→ [[claim-god-sense-organs-eyes-ears-nose-mouth-head]] | **stav-god-16-21.2** | Confidence: very-high
  Comprehensive catalogue: hair white as wool (Daniel 7:9), eyes that roam the earth (2 Chronicles 16:9), ears that hear prayer ("Incline your ear, Yahweh"), nostrils that flare in anger (the word for anger *'ap* literally means "nose"), and a mouth that speaks. God perceives the world through sense organs — his senses are enhanced, not categorically different from human senses.
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]]

## Chapter 20: Gasp and Gulp

### Claim: God breathed, ate, drank, and spoke — his mouth was fully functional
→ [[claim-god-breathed-ate-drank-spoke-functional-mouth]] | **stav-god-16-21.3** | Confidence: high
  God's mouth was a functioning biological organ: He breathed into Adam (Genesis 2:7). He ate meals with Abraham (Genesis 18:8). He drank wine (implied by sacrificial libations). Psalm 50:12-13: "Do I eat the flesh of bulls?" — the rhetorical question implies other deities DO eat; Yahweh's transcendence of this is a LATER development.
  Contradicted by: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]]

## Epilogue: Chapter 21 — An Autopsy

### Claim: God's body was killed — by theology
→ [[claim-god-body-killed-by-theology-philosophical-abstraction]] | **stav-god-16-21.4** | Confidence: high (historical), medium (value judgment)
  Stavrakopoulou's closing argument: over centuries, Jewish and Christian intellectuals shaped by Greek philosophy replaced the embodied God with an immaterial abstraction. The autopsy metaphor: the book dissected God's body part by part; the epilogue declares cause of death = philosophical abstraction. "Worshippers have been praying to a ghost."

## Stavrakopoulou: Book-Level Assessment

| Strength | Weakness |
|----------|----------|
| **Overwhelming textual evidence** — the body-part catalogue is irrefutable | **Rhetorical overreach** — occasionally claims more than the evidence supports (God defecating) |
| **Material culture integration** — 'Ain Dara, standing stones, seals | **Tone** — deliberately provocative; may alienate readers who would otherwise be persuaded |
| **Comparative ANE grounding** — situates Israelite embodiment in wider context | **Single-body model** — doesn't engage Sommer's more nuanced fluidity model |
| **Readable, engaging, accessible** — written for a general audience | **Atheist polemic** — her conclusion that God was "killed" is more editorial than analytical |

**Stavrakopoulou's contribution to the project:** She provides the most visceral, sensory, material account of divine embodiment. Where Sommer provides the philosophical framework (fluidity vs. non-fluidity), Stavrakopoulou provides the body. She doesn't argue about whether God had multiple bodies or one. She argues that God HAD a body — and that recovering that body requires reading the Bible without the filters of later theology. Her book is the strongest possible case for divine corporeality. Whether you accept her conclusion that the embodied God was later "killed" is a separate question from whether she's right about the texts. On the texts, she's overwhelmingly right.
