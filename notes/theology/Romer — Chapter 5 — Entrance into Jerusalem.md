# Römer, The Invention of God — Chapter 5

## The Entrance of Yhwh into Jerusalem

---

## Claim 1: Shiloh was Yhwh's first major sanctuary in Israel
→ [[claim-shiloh-yhwh-first-major-sanctuary]] | **romer-inv-5.1** | Confidence: high
  Shiloh in Ephraim was Yhwh's primary sanctuary before Jerusalem — archaeology confirms a major cult site destroyed c. 1050 BCE. Samuel (an El name) was the transitional bridge figure between the pre-monarchic El-worshipping "Israel" and the royal Yhwh cult.
  Supports: [[claim-david-brought-yhwh-to-jerusalem-political]], [[claim-israel-name-el-originally-el-worshippers]]

---

## Claim 2: David brought Yhwh to Jerusalem — a political masterstroke
→ [[claim-david-brought-yhwh-to-jerusalem-political]] | **romer-inv-5.2** | Confidence: medium
  Jerusalem was originally a Canaanite city whose deity was El Elyon. David conquered this neutral, non-tribal city and brought Yhwh's ark from the northern Shiloh tradition, merging El's city with Yhwh's cult. The El-Yhwh identification was accomplished politically, not theologically.
  Depends on: [[claim-shiloh-yhwh-first-major-sanctuary]], [[claim-israel-name-el-originally-el-worshippers]], [[claim-merneptah-stele-first-mention-israel-ethnic-group]]
  Supports: [[claim-yhwh-became-israel-god-two-stage-political-process]]

---

## Claim 3: Saul, David, and Solomon are paradigmatic constructs with historical foundations
→ [[claim-saul-david-solomon-paradigmatic-constructs]] | **romer-inv-5.3** | Confidence: high
  The biblical portraits of the first three kings are heavily shaped by the Deuteronomistic editors' theological program, but "several traits in the narrative cannot be pure invention." David was originally a Philistine vassal; Samuel (an El name) was the transitional figure anointing Saul. The Tel Dan stele confirms a Davidic dynasty existed by the 8th century but tells us nothing about David himself.
  Depends on: [[claim-documentary-hypothesis-obsolete]], [[claim-bible-must-be-analyzed-historically]]
  Supports: [[claim-israel-name-el-originally-el-worshippers]]

**Strongest section:** The analysis of Jerusalem's political geography — David's choice of a neutral, non-tribal capital makes excellent political sense.

**Weakest section:** The claim that David deliberately engineered the El-Yhwh merger — plausible but we have no direct evidence of David's religious policies.

**The narrative arc through Chapter 5:** Southern desert god → Midianite mediation → adopted by El's people → installed in El's city by a political warlord. Yhwh now sits where El sat. The identification of the two gods is accomplished politically, not theologically.
