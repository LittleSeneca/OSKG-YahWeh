# Römer, The Invention of God — Chapter 7

## The Cult of Yhwh in Judah

---

## Claim 1: Jerusalem was not the only Yahwistic sanctuary — Judah had multiple temples

**Römer's claim:** The Deuteronomistic ideal of one temple in Jerusalem was not the historical reality. Multiple Yahwistic sanctuaries existed in Judah at Arad, Lachish, Beer-Sheba, and elsewhere. "These high places were probably typically 'Israelite' or 'Judean,' but they had finally to cede their places to the Temple of Jerusalem."

**Evidence presented:**
- **Arad:** A Yahwistic temple with one or two standing stones (*maṣṣēbôt*) in the holy of holies — possibly representing Yhwh and Asherah. The stones were deliberately hidden (buried) to protect them from destruction — possibly during Hezekiah's reform or the Assyrian invasion.
- **Lachish:** An Assyrian relief from Sennacherib's palace depicts soldiers carrying an extremely large incense burner from Lachish — too large for private use, almost certainly from a Yahwistic temple.
- **Beer-Sheba:** Remains of a large four-horned altar — a Yahwistic sanctuary.
- **High places (*bāmôt*):** "These high places were in most cases Yahwistic sanctuaries." The editors of Kings confirm this while complaining that "the high places did not disappear; the people still offered sacrifices and perfumes there" (2 Kings 12:14). Only two kings (Hezekiah and Josiah) attempted to eliminate them — proving they were the NORM.

**Confidence:** HIGH on multiple sanctuaries (archaeological evidence is unambiguous at Arad, Beer-Sheba). HIGH that the high places were Yahwistic (the biblical texts themselves say so).

---

## Claim 2: Yhwh was not Jerusalem's original god — he rose to supremacy gradually, competing with a solar deity

**Römer's claim:** "Even in Jerusalem Yhwh was not the only god worshipped. He at first occupied the temple together with a solar god, to whom he was perhaps even subordinated." Yhwh rose to supremacy through a gradual process paralleled in other Levantine kingdoms (Moab, Ammon), where national gods absorbed older pantheons.

**Evidence presented:**
- **The solar competition:** Joshua 10:12-13 ("Sun, stand still over Gibeon") may preserve a memory of Yhwh's competition with Jerusalem's older solar deity. Either the text "insists on the superiority of Yhwh over solar and lunar gods" (7th century) or "shows traces of the competition between Yhwh, god of the army of Israel, and the tutelary deities of Jerusalem" (older).
- **The El-Yhwh identification at Jerusalem:** Genesis 14 (Melchizedek, "priest of El Elyon" at Salem = Jerusalem) preserves a tradition of Jerusalem as an El city. The Masoretic text identifies El Elyon with Yhwh — the identification was accomplished textually.
- **Regional parallel:** "In comparably sized kingdoms to the east of the Jordan, such as Moab and possibly also Ammon... the members of the Canaanite pantheon began to some extent to fade away behind the dynastic god who took up more and more space." Yhwh's rise fits a regional pattern.

**Confidence:** HIGH that Yhwh was not Jerusalem's original god (the El Elyon tradition is strong). MEDIUM on the solar competition (Joshua 10 is a difficult text to interpret historically).

---

## Claim 3: Centralization of worship in Jerusalem was a late political project

**Römer's claim:** The requirement that all worship be centralized at Jerusalem (Deuteronomy 12) was a 7th-century innovation, not the original norm. The kings who enforced it (Hezekiah, Josiah) were pursuing political consolidation as much as religious reform. "The irony of history: these high places were probably typically 'Israelite' or 'Judean,' but they had finally to cede their places to the Temple of Jerusalem."

**Evidence:**
- Before the Temple was built, high places were legitimate — even Solomon worshipped at Gibeon (1 Kings 3:3-15)
- The Deuteronomists rationalized this with a footnote: "the people were sacrificing at the high places, however, because no house had yet been built for the name of the Lord" (1 Kings 3:2)
- This footnote reveals their awareness that centralization was an INNOVATION that needed explaining

**Confidence:** HIGH. The biblical texts themselves acknowledge the shift. The historical development is clear.

## Chapter 7 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|-----------|
| Multiple Yahwistic temples in Judah | HIGH |
| Yhwh not Jerusalem's original god | HIGH |
| Solar competition in Jerusalem | MEDIUM |
| Centralization as late political project | HIGH |

**Key takeaway:** The Jerusalem-centered, exclusive Yahwism of the Bible was not the original religion of Judah. It was imposed by reforming kings — especially Josiah — who destroyed their own people's traditional sanctuaries. What the Bible presents as "restoring pure religion" was actually "suppressing traditional religion."
