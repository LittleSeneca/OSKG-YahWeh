# Smith — Chapter 2: The Divine Council

Smith maps the Ugaritic divine council as a multi-tiered structure of assemblies — not one monolithic "pantheon" but several god-specific groupings centered on El, Baal, and Ditanu. He then traces how Israel inherited this four-tier structure, but collapsed it through convergence: El (tier 1) merged with Yahweh (originally tier 2), and the middle tiers telescoped, leaving only a single ruler with subservient powers. The chapter's most explosive claim: Psalm 82 and Deuteronomy 32:8-9 preserve the older theology where Yahweh was NOT the head god, but one of El's sons.

  Supports: [[claim-israel-inherited-four-tier-pantheon-collapsed]], [[claim-four-tiers-map-divine-household-levels]].

## Claim 3: Israel inherited the four-tier pantheon — it collapsed through convergence
→ [[claim-israel-inherited-four-tier-pantheon-collapsed]] | **smith-obm-2.3** | Confidence: high
  Structural mechanism of monotheism's emergence: convergence collapsed the tiers, leaving rubble with occasional intact fragments (Psalm 82, Deut 32:8-9).
  Depends on: [[claim-four-tier-pantheon-structure]], [[claim-convergence-differentiation-framework-israelite-religion]], [[claim-yahweh-originally-tier-two-el-presided]].

## Claim 4: Psalm 82 and Deuteronomy 32:8-9 preserve older theology — Yahweh was tier 2, one of El's sons
→ [[claim-yahweh-originally-tier-two-el-presided]] | **smith-obm-2.4** | Confidence: high
  The MT alteration of Deut 32:8 is the smoking gun — scribes changed "divine sons" to "sons of Israel" because the original implied polytheism.
  Supports: [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]], [[claim-deut-32-8-9-sons-of-el]], [[claim-three-stage-convergence-el-yahweh]]. Contradicts: Heiser's Yahweh-supreme-from-beginning model.

## Claim 5: The neo-Assyrian empire's disruption of the "each nation has its god" world order drove theological change
→ [[claim-neo-assyrian-crisis-drove-theological-shift]] | **smith-obm-2.5** | Confidence: medium-high
  Monotheism as contingent historical response, not evolutionary inevitability — theological shift correlates with imperial crisis. Causation speculative; needs Ch 8-10 to bear weight.
  Depends on: [[claim-yahweh-originally-tier-two-el-presided]]. Supports: [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]].

## Claim 6: Monotheism doesn't eliminate divine council imagery — it only reduces the status of council members
→ [[claim-council-imagery-persists-monotheism-status-changes]] | **smith-obm-2.6** | Confidence: high
  Structural continuity (ruler + court) persists; ontological status of members changes from "deities" to "subservient powers." Monotheism as revaluation within inherited structure.
  Supports: [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]].

## Claim 7: The family/patrimonial household model fits Ugaritic pantheon better than bureaucratic model
→ [[claim-family-model-better-than-bureaucratic-pantheon]] | **smith-obm-2.7** | Confidence: medium-high
  No divine scribe or bureaucratic figures in Ugaritic texts; familial terminology dominates. Sets up Ch3's argument that family provided polytheism's conceptual unity.
  Supports: [[claim-four-tiers-map-divine-household-levels]], [[claim-family-model-polytheism-conceptual-unity]].

## Chapter 2 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1: Multiple distinct assemblies, not one pantheon | HIGH | Functional subsets vs. structural divisions |
| 2: Four-tier pantheon structure | MEDIUM-HIGH | Heuristic convenience vs. emic reality |
| 3: Israel inherited four-tier pantheon; it collapsed | HIGH for convergence; MEDIUM for exact structure | Vestigiality vs. original thinness of Israelite pantheon |
| 4: Yahweh was originally tier 2; El presided | HIGH | Later readers reinterpreting El as Yahweh from the start |
| 5: Neo-Assyrian crisis drove theological shift | MEDIUM-HIGH | Correlation vs. causation; other drivers |
| 6: Council imagery persists in monotheism | HIGH | Ontological shift understated |
| 7: Family model > bureaucratic model | MEDIUM-HIGH | Argument from silence; models not mutually exclusive |

**Strongest section:** Section 4 (Israel and the Tiers of the Pantheon). The reading of Psalm 82 and Deuteronomy 32:8-9 is masterful. The MT alteration of Deut 32:8 is a genuinely decisive piece of evidence that Smith deploys to maximum effect.

**Weakest section:** Section 3 (The Four Tiers). The tier structure is useful but feels taxonomic rather than analytical. Tier 3 (Kothar alone) is particularly thin.

**Most original contribution:** The argument that El's assembly (dr 'il, CAT 1.65) is NOT the same as the general pantheon. This distinction has been missed by earlier scholarship and has real implications for how we read biblical divine council texts.

**Cross-reference:** Claim 4 (Yahweh as original tier-2 god) is the key that unlocks Smith's Ch 7 argument about El as Israel's original god. Claim 5 (neo-Assyrian trigger) will need Ch 8-10 to flesh out. The family model preview (Claim 7) leads directly into Ch 3. Cross's *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* is the major dialogue partner throughout — Smith accepts Cross's basic framework but refines it at crucial points (the bn 'il vs. bn 'ilm distinction; the non-identity of El's household assembly with the general pantheon). Heiser's model keeps Yahweh supreme from the beginning — Smith's Psalm 82 reading directly challenges this.
