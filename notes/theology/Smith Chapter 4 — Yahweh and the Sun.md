# Smith, Early History of God — Chapter 4

## Yahweh and the Sun

---

## Claim 1: Yahweh was described in solar language, and this developed under royal influence

**Smith's claim:** Solar imagery for Yahweh was limited but real. It developed in Judah (not Israel) under the monarchy's influence, following the same pattern as Baal's storm imagery: the monarchy adopted established divine symbols to exalt the national god. This was not a separate "sun cult" — it was solarized Yahwism.

**Evidence presented:**

- **Psalm 84:12:** "For a sun and a shield is Yahweh" (*kî šemeš ûmāgēn yhwh*). McCarter revocalizes *māgēn* to *māgān* ("sovereign"), making it "a sun and a sovereign is Yahweh" — both royal titles.
- **Solar verbs for Yahweh:** *zrḥ* ("rise") applied to Yahweh in Deut 33:2, Isa 60:1, Hos 6:3, and the Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions.
- **Personal names:** Yahwistic names with *šḥr* ("dawn"), *zrḥ* ("rise"), *n(w)r* ("light") — e.g., *nryhw* ("Yahweh is my light"), the name of a king's son.
- **Temple orientation:** The Jerusalem temple faced east. Ezekiel 8:16 condemns priests worshipping the sun toward the east WITHIN the temple — suggesting this was an indigenous practice, not foreign import.
- **Chariots of the sun:** 2 Kings 23:11 mentions "the chariots of the sun" (*markĕbôt haššemeš*) purged by Josiah. Horse figurines with sun disks above their heads found at Lachish, Hazor, and Jerusalem.
- **Winged sun disk:** Appears on royal *lmlk* stamp seals from Judah. The winged sun disk also appears with El at Ugarit, with Baal Hammon on the Kilamuwa orthostat, and with Assur in Assyria — it was a standard symbol for chief deities.
- **Royal solar ideology:** 2 Samuel 23:3-4 compares the king to the dawning sun and life-giving rain. Psalm 72:5-6 uses the same pairing. The king was the earthly counterpart of the solarized deity.

**ANE parallels:** Assur (Assyria) absorbed the solar disk from Shamash. Marduk (Babylon) was called "the sun-god of the gods" (*Enuma Elish* 1:102). In all these cases, the national god absorbed solar imagery as the state grew in power.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH. The biblical and archaeological evidence for solar imagery is solid. The royal connection is plausible but circumstantial — we lack direct texts saying "the king instituted solar Yahwism."

**What's at stake for faith:** Moderate. The sun was a deity throughout the ANE. If Yahweh absorbed solar imagery, he absorbed characteristics of yet another god. The pattern (El, Baal, Asherah, now the sun) is cumulative — Yahweh becomes a composite of multiple deities. But the solar evidence is thinner than the Baal/El evidence and the theological implications are less direct (solar language is more clearly metaphorical than, say, "Yahweh defeated Leviathan").

**Who disagrees:** Conservatives would argue solar language is purely metaphorical (Psalm 84:12 is simile, not identification). The temple practices Ezekiel condemns were foreign imports, not indigenous Yahwism. The reforms show these were aberrations, not legitimate developments.

**Alternative reading:** The solar language is genuine but represents natural metaphor (God is like the sun — life-giving, illuminating), not theological absorption of a sun god. Every culture uses solar metaphors without implying sun worship.

**My assessment:** The cumulative pattern across chapters 2-4 is what makes this persuasive. Storm imagery (Baal), maternal imagery (Asherah), solar imagery — each individually could be "just metaphor." Together, they form a pattern of absorption that's harder to dismiss as coincidence. The strongest evidence is Ezekiel 8:16 — priests worshipping the sun WITHIN Yahweh's temple. That's not metaphor; that's practice.

## Claim 2: Solar and storm imagery were combined to express Yahweh's transcendence over nature

**Smith's claim:** By combining storm and solar imagery for Yahweh, biblical texts expressed that Yahweh was not identified with any single natural phenomenon. He had power over AND transcended nature. This was a theological innovation, not just borrowing.

**Evidence presented:**

- **Psalm 50:1-3** combines storm theophany with solar radiance
- **Ezekiel 43:1-5** describes Yahweh's return to the temple with both storm and solar elements
- **Hosea 6:3:** "His going forth is sure as the dawn; he will come to us as the showers, as the spring rains that water the earth" — combines solar (*šaḥar*, dawn) with rain imagery in a single description of Yahweh
- **ANE parallels:** The Assyrian god Ningirsu's return to his temple is rendered in both storm and solar terms. An enameled tile of Tukulti-Ninurta II depicts Assur riding the winged sun disk with rain clouds on either side. *Enuma Elish* applies both solar and storm language to Marduk.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. The combination of imagery is real. Whether it was a deliberate theological strategy (as Smith suggests) or just accumulated tradition is harder to determine.

**What's at stake for faith:** This is actually a positive theological argument within Smith's critical framework. The combination of different nature imageries wasn't just syncretism — it was a way of saying "God is bigger than any one metaphor." This anticipates later theological moves toward divine transcendence.

**My assessment:** This is Smith at his most sympathetic to the biblical theologians. He's not just cataloguing borrowings — he's recognizing genuine theological creativity. The biblical writers didn't just copy Baal; they combined Baal's storm with the sun god's radiance in ways that transcended both. This is worth noting because it complicates the simple "Israel just borrowed everything" narrative.

## Cross-Cutting Assessment: Chapter 4

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Solar language for Yahweh was real and royally-driven | MEDIUM-HIGH | Solar language could be purely metaphorical |
| Combination of storm+solar = theological transcendence | MEDIUM | Hard to prove deliberate strategy vs. accumulated tradition |

**Strongest evidence:** Ezekiel 8:16 (priests worshipping sun in temple) and the horse figurines with sun disks (archaeological confirmation of solar practice).

**Weakest:** The claim that solar language was specifically a Judean royal development. The evidence is circumstantial.

**Relationship to larger argument:** Chapter 4 extends the convergence pattern to a fourth category (after El, Baal, Asherah). The cumulative weight matters more than any single chapter.
