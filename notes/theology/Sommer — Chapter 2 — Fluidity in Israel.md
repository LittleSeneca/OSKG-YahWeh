# Sommer — Chapter 2

## The Fluidity Model in Ancient Israel

---

## Claim 1: Yhwh had local manifestations — "Yhwh of Samaria," "Yhwh of Teman," "Yhwh of Hebron"
→ [[claim-yhwh-local-manifestations-fluid-monotheism-kuntillet-ajrud]] | **sommer-bog-2.2** | Confidence: medium-high
  Yhwh had distinct local manifestations paralleling the multiple Ishtars of Assyrian treaties — fluid monotheism, not polytheism. The Kuntillet Ajrud evidence ("Yhwh of Samaria," "Yhwh of Teman") and 2 Samuel 15:7 ("Yhwh of Hebron") provide inscriptional and biblical evidence.
  Supports: [[claim-ane-gods-fluid-selves-multiple-local-manifestations]], [[claim-je-sources-preserved-ane-fluidity-model-yhwh-multiple-manifestations]] | Contradicts: [[claim-yhwh-worshipped-diverse-local-manifestations]]

## Claim 2: The *mal'akh* (angel/messenger) in J and E is an avatar of Yhwh, not a separate being
→ [[claim-malakh-avatar-yhwh-not-separate-angel-je-sources]] | **sommer-bog-2.3** | Confidence: medium-high
  Genesis 18 (three men = Yhwh), Genesis 21 (mal'akh speaks as "I"), Exodus 3 (no transition between mal'akh and Yhwh), Judges 6 (alternating identity mid-conversation). The mal'akh is a small-scale manifestation, not a separate being.
  Supports: [[claim-je-sources-preserved-ane-fluidity-model-yhwh-multiple-manifestations]], [[claim-biblical-god-has-body-standard-notion]]

## Claim 3: J and E are monotheistic — not polytheistic — but their monotheism is fluid
→ [[claim-je-sources-monotheistic-fluid-not-polytheistic]] | **sommer-bog-2.4** | Confidence: medium
  If you read Genesis 18 as polytheistic (Yhwh + subordinate gods), you get Smith/Römer/Dever: polytheism → monotheism. If you read it as Sommer does (one Yhwh with three bodies), you get fluid monotheism → anti-fluid monotheism. Same text, opposite framework.
  Supports: [[claim-divine-fluidity-cross-cuts-monotheism-polytheism-distinction]], [[claim-monotheism-well-established-before-exile-biblical-authors]] | Contradicts: [[claim-dever-monotheism-late-elite-book-religion-never-displaced-folk]], [[claim-true-monotheism-emerged-babylonian-exile]], [[claim-monotheism-was-rhetoric-not-religious-stage]]

**The most important implication:** If Sommer is right, the entire "Israelite religion evolved from polytheism to monotheism" narrative needs to be reframed. Israel was monotheistic from early on — but its monotheism included divine fluidity. What evolved was not the NUMBER of gods but the NATURE of the one God's embodiment.
