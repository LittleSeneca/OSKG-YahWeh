# Dever, Did God Have a Wife? — Chapter III

## Sources and Methods

---

## Claim 1: Archaeology is a primary source — equal or superior to texts for folk religion

**Dever's claim:** "I will consider the archaeological data as a 'primary' source. Textual scholars... will no doubt protest that I have downgraded the Hebrew Bible to a 'secondary' source. But as I shall show, it is — if one chooses to focus as I do on folk religion."

**Six arguments for archaeology's primacy:**

1. **Quantity:** "Archaeological data are already more extensive than all the biblical texts put together, and they will be much more so in future. The canonical biblical text is static, a 'closed book'... But archaeology is dynamic, making revolutionary new discoveries daily."

2. **Variety:** "Artifacts come not only from the sacred ideas and institutions that the Hebrew Bible focuses on... but from the whole range of 'secular' activities of ordinary people in every conceivable everyday-life situation."

3. **Objectivity:** "What we call 'the archaeological record' has not been edited by anyone. The artifacts are, therefore, more 'objective' than texts, at least until the modern interpretive process begins."

4. **Chronology:** "Archaeological artifacts, most short-lived, are by nature contemporary with all the periods that we are seeking to reconstruct." Carbon 14 dating delivers ±50 years; "biblical scholars disagree over the dating of the major biblical texts by centuries."

5. **Representation:** Biblical texts are "elitist. Yet the archaeological data... are 'populist' by nature. They are very broadly representative of every class of society, every profession, and in particular women."

6. **Secular history:** The Bible produces "theocratic history." Archaeology produces "a secular history... often more realistic, more comprehensive, better balanced, and ultimately more satisfying."

**Confidence:** HIGH that archaeology provides data texts can't. MEDIUM that it's "superior" — depends on the question.

## Claim 2: The biblical texts are late, elitist, and theologically biased

**Dever's claim:** The textual sources for Israelite religion are all late (8th century at earliest for composition; heavily edited through the post-exilic period), produced by a tiny literate elite (less than 1% of the population), and shaped by theological agendas that make them unreliable as direct historical sources.

**Evidence — the sources:**
- **J** (Yahwist): Originally dated to 10th century, now 9th-8th. "If the date of J were indeed post-exilic... this source would tell us nothing whatsoever about supposed 'ancestral religion.'"
- **E** (Elohist): 8th century, northern. Prophetic interests.
- **P** (Priestly): Late monarchy or post-exilic. "Irrelevant for folk religion during the monarchy."
- **Dtr** (Deuteronomistic History): Most important source. 7th-century core, exile-era editing. "A call to repentance... a demand for the wayward people of Israel to restore the pure, monotheistic ideals."

**On the prophetic books:** "It would not be misleading to say that the real religions of ancient Israel consisted precisely of all the things that the prophets condemned, all of them incidentally illustrated by archaeological discoveries."

**On the Bible's historical reliability:** "The Hebrew Bible is not history in the modern sense... I shall often regard the Hebrew Bible as 'historicized fiction' — stories that are based on some genuine historical events, but always told in such a way as to advance the ideological agenda of the writers."

**The writers:** "They were mostly elites, literati attached to court and priestly circles in Jerusalem. Taken together, they can hardly have constituted more than a fraction of one percent of the population."

**Confidence:** HIGH on the late dating (critical consensus). HIGH on the elitist character of the texts. MEDIUM on "historicized fiction" as the right characterization (some texts contain more reliable historical memory than this phrase suggests).

## Chapter III Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|-----------|
| Archaeology as primary source for folk religion | MEDIUM-HIGH |
| Biblical texts are late and elitist | HIGH |
| Texts are "historicized fiction" | MEDIUM |

**Key contribution:** Dever's reversal of the normal hierarchy of evidence. For most biblical scholars, the Bible is primary and archaeology is illustrative. For Dever, archaeology is primary and the Bible is supplementary — useful but unreliable, and always needing archaeological corroboration.
