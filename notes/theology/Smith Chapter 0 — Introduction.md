## Methodological Presuppositions (Smith's Own List)

Smith explicitly states 6 assumptions. Here's what matters:

1. **"The Bible is not a modern history book"** — fair, but Smith then uses it AS a historical source. The tension between these moves is unresolved.

2. **"The evidence is radically incomplete — a puzzle missing most pieces"** — genuinely humble, but doesn't stop him from drawing strong conclusions.

3. **"Ancient Israelite religion was communal and national, not private"** — important correction to modern assumptions. Most of the evidence we have is about state cult, not household practice.

4. **"Practice over belief"** — Smith argues the monotheism/monolatry distinction (belief vs. practice) is problematic because ancient religion was about practice. He retains the distinction anyway. This is intellectually honest but creates an unresolved tension.

5. **"No 'essence' of Israelite religion apart from the data"** — Smith rejects the idea that there's a "true" Israelite religion vs. "syncretistic corruption." All practice is legitimate historical data. This is a methodological commitment, not a neutral observation — it rules out the conservative position by definition.

6. **"The picture is partial and subjective"** — genuine acknowledgment of limits.

**The unspoken assumption:** Smith's framework assumes the biblical narrative's picture of Israel's religious history is systematically misleading. The Bible says Israel was always Yahwistic and other gods were foreign. Smith says the opposite. This isn't a conclusion he reaches after examining evidence — it's the lens through which he reads the evidence. Worth tracking: does he ever find evidence that contradicts his framework, or does all evidence get interpreted to fit?

## Overall Assessment of the Introduction

**Strongest claim:** Israelite material culture was Canaanite (HIGH confidence, archaeological consensus).

**Weakest claim:** Tigay's onomastics are irrelevant to the polytheism question (Smith's counter is defensive, not decisive).

**Most theologically explosive:** Baal/Asherah worship was indigenous, not foreign — the Bible's narrative is exactly backward.

**Most methodologically important:** Smith's stated assumptions. Track whether he follows them consistently or violates them when convenient.
