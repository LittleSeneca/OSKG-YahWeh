# Day — Chapter 2: Yahweh and Asherah

Day argues that Asherah was genuinely worshipped as a goddess in ancient Israel — not just as a cult object — and that she functioned as Yahweh's consort in syncretistic circles, an appropriation from El's consort Athirat that the Deuteronomists eventually rejected and the Chronicler had forgotten entirely.

---

## Claim 1: Asherah appears as a GODDESS (not just a cult object) in several OT passages
→ [[claim-asherah-goddess-in-ot-passages]] | **day-ygc-2.1** | Confidence: high
  Five OT passages (Judg 3:7; 1 Kgs 15:13; 18:19; 2 Kgs 21:7; 23:4) unambiguously refer to Asherah as a goddess. Day's redundancy argument ("image of a symbol") in 2 Kgs 21:7 is particularly strong.
  Contradicts: [[claim-biblical-evidence-insufficient-for-goddess]] (Smith — same passages, opposite conclusion)
  Supports: [[claim-bible-deliberately-obscures-yhwh-asherah-link]] (Römer — goddess breaks through despite Dtr editing)
  Supported by: [[claim-ot-asherah-equals-ugaritic-athirat]] (the equation provides ANE context for the goddess identification)


## Claim 2: OT Asherah = Ugaritic Athirat (El's consort) — the equation is secure
→ [[claim-ot-asherah-equals-ugaritic-athirat]] | **day-ygc-2.2** | Confidence: high
  One of the most secure equations in the field. Phonetic chain: Ugaritic *ʼaṯrt* → Ekron *ʼšrt* → Hebrew *ʼăšērâ*. The host-of-heaven connection is the clincher.
  Supports: [[claim-asherah-goddess-in-ot-passages]], [[claim-asherah-el-consort-transferred-to-yahweh]] (Römer), [[claim-asherah-was-stylized-tree]]
  Challenged by: minority positions (Caquot, Binger, Bernhardt) — all weak


## Claim 3: "Yahweh and his Asherah" — cult object that symbolized the goddess
→ [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-cult-object-symbolized-goddess]] | **day-ygc-2.3** | Confidence: medium-high
  HYBRID position. Grammar: it's the cult object (agreeing with Smith). Conclusion: the object symbolized the goddess and implies consort (agreeing with Römer). The pronominal suffix can't attach to personal names in Hebrew.
  Partially supports: [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-symbol-not-goddess]] (Smith — agrees on grammar)
  Partially supports: [[claim-kuntillet-ajrud-proves-consort]] (Römer — agrees on consort conclusion)
  Supported by: [[claim-ot-asherah-equals-ugaritic-athirat]] (the El-consort background makes the consort reading natural)
  Most nuanced position of the three — splits the difference between Smith and Römer on evidentiary grounds.


## Claim 4: The Asherah was a stylized tree, not a living tree, grove, shrine, or simple image
→ [[claim-asherah-was-stylized-tree]] | **day-ygc-2.4** | Confidence: high
  The asherah was a recognizable religious symbol with consistent iconography across centuries. Lachish ewer is closest thing to a labeled depiction ("my Lady Elat" above tree). The verb usage (ʻāśâ, bānâ, nāṣab) is decisive against living-tree theories.
  Depends on: [[claim-ot-asherah-equals-ugaritic-athirat]] (shared tree symbolism)
  Supports: [[claim-asherah-goddess-in-ot-passages]], [[claim-asherah-was-yahwistic-symbol]] (Smith — describes physical form of the symbol)
  Supports: [[claim-female-imagery-absorbed-into-yahweh]] (Smith — tree-of-life imagery in Proverbs maps onto the stylized tree)
  Weakest link: Hosea 14 wordplay (double wordplay on Anat AND Asherah in one verb is a lot)


## Chapter 2 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Asherah as goddess in OT (not just cult object) | HIGH | Smith/Hadley reading all references as cult object |
| OT Asherah = Ugaritic Athirat | HIGH | Bernhardt's objections (individually weak, collectively insufficient) |
| "His Asherah" = cult object at Kuntillet Ajrud | HIGH | Other Semitic languages allow pronominal suffix on personal names |
| Consort relationship with Yahweh | MEDIUM-HIGH | Alternative: cult object as Yahwistic symbol without divine marriage |
| Asherah was a stylized tree | HIGH | Lemaire's living tree theory (verb usage decisive against it) |

**Strongest section:** The rebuttal of "living tree" and "grove/shrine" theories. Day systematically eliminates wrong answers before presenting his positive case. The verb usage point (ʻāśâ, bānâ, nāṣab) is simple and devastating.

**Weakest section:** The Hosea 14 wordplay. It's creative and consistent with Hosea's methods, but a double wordplay (Anat + Asherah from a single verb *ʼăšûrennû*) is a lot to ask of a consonantal text with no vowels. The Isaiah apocalypse dependence is a better argument than the wordplay itself.

**Most important methodological move:** Day's treatment of the Baal-Asherah pairing as Deuteronomistic polemic (following Olyan). This is crucial — if the Baal-Asherah pairing is authentic tradition rather than polemic, the El-Asherah → Yahweh-Asherah chain weakens. Day's argument that no extra-biblical text pairs Baal with Asherah is a strong point.

**Cross-reference notes:** Smith (*Early History*, Ch 3) agrees on many details but disagrees on the goddess question and is far more cautious about the consort relationship. Dever (Ch V-VI) provides archaeological evidence Day barely touches — 3000+ pillar figurines, multiple shrines. Römer (Ch 9) largely agrees with Day. The Kuntillet Ajrud debate has evolved since Day wrote: Smoak and Schniedewind (2019) now argue the Bes identification definitively rules out the drawings as Yahweh/Asherah, confirming Day's already-stated position.
