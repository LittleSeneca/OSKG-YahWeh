# Albertz — §2.1: Religious Elements of Early Small Family Groups ('Patriarchal Religion')

This is the first substantive historical section. Albertz argues that what Genesis depicts as "patriarchal religion" is not a chronological predecessor to Yahweh worship but a *sociological substratum* — family-level personal piety that existed alongside, beneath, and before official Yahweh religion. His key move: the patriarchal narratives in Gen 12-50 are tradents retrojecting what they knew of family piety onto their ancestors.

---

## Claim 1: "Patriarchal religion" is not a pre-Yahwistic stage but a substratum of family piety
→ [[claim-patriarchal-religion-family-piety-substratum-not-pre-yahwistic]] | **albertz-hir-2.1.1** | Confidence: medium-high
  Albertz's most creative move: patriarchal narratives preserve authentic family-level religious practice contemporaneous with (and older than) Yahweh religion, retrojected onto the ancestors. Alt's "God of the Fathers" hypothesis has collapsed; the difference is between social levels (family vs. people), not periods. Supports: [[claim-yahweh-el-originally-distinct-deities]], [[claim-el-original-god-israel-yahweh-imported-later]]. Contradicts: [[claim-moses-creative-genius-monotheistic-revolution]]. Depends on: [[claim-internal-religious-pluralism-official-vs-personal-piety]].

## Claim 2: Early Israelite families worshipped regional forms of El in practical monolatry without exclusiveness
→ [[claim-early-families-worshipped-el-deities-practical-monolatry]] | **albertz-hir-2.1.2** | Confidence: medium
  Before Yahweh at the tribal level, families worshipped regional El manifestations (El-Shaddai, El-'Olam, El-Bethel) with one god per family but no intolerance toward other families' gods. No Yahweh-names appear in patriarchal narratives. Strongest evidence for pre-Yahwistic El worship. Supports: [[claim-el-was-original-god-israel-name-and-absence-yahweh]], [[claim-el-original-god-israel-yahweh-imported-later]], [[claim-el-shadday-genuinely-archaic-not-priestly-invention]], [[claim-israelite-el-family-clan-deity-not-national]].

## Claim 3: Family piety was pre-cultic, pre-political, pre-moral — unconditional care, female religious experience
→ [[claim-family-piety-pre-cultic-pre-political-pre-moral-unconditional]] | **albertz-hir-2.1.3** | Confidence: medium
  Family piety was characterized by unconditional divine care, short time-horizons (promise of a son, one pregnancy cycle), female-centered religious experience, unwarlike divine action, and absence of specialized cult personnel. Sets up the structural contrast with Yahweh religion. The "unconditional vs. conditional" dichotomy may reflect Lutheran categories. Supports: [[claim-yahweh-liberation-origin-structural-contrast-family-piety]]. Contradicts: [[claim-convergence-differentiation-framework-israelite-religion]] (smooth convergence understates structural tension). Challenged by: [[claim-yahweh-family-god-father-mother-el-benevolence-integration]] (Lewis suggests more continuity).

## Claim 4: No nomadic/sedentary opposition; Passover originated as transhumance ritual later historicized
→ [[claim-no-nomadic-sedentary-opposition-passover-transhumance-origin]] | **albertz-hir-2.1.4** | Confidence: medium-high (symbiosis) / low-medium (Passover)
  Herders and farmers lived in symbiosis; the real opposition was hill-country vs. city-states. Passover probably began as a family-level nomadic transhumance ritual (spring, haste, blood rite) later historicized into the Exodus narrative — a case study in family piety reinterpreted by official religion. Supports: [[claim-yahweh-liberation-origin-structural-contrast-family-piety]].

## Claim 5: Ancestor cult (teraphim, necromancy) was present in family religion but suppressed by official Yahweh religion
→ [[claim-ancestor-cult-teraphim-suppressed-official-yahweh-religion]] | **albertz-hir-2.1.5** | Confidence: low-medium
  Teraphim were likely deified-ancestor figurines in household cults; necromancy was widespread. Yahweh religion actively suppressed these family-piety elements. The negative biblical evidence is partly "dogmatic correction." Albertz hedges — wants to acknowledge the evidence but minimize its scope. Contradicts: [[claim-israelite-religion-non-mythological-fundamentally-unique]] (Kaufmann's denial of ancestor cult), [[claim-no-ancestor-cult-death-de-religionized-israel]].

## §2.1 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1. Patriarchal religion = family piety substratum, not pre-Yahwistic stage | MEDIUM-HIGH | If the family/official distinction is artificial or if patriarchal narratives are pure literary invention |
| 2. Early families worshipped El deities; practical monolatry without exclusiveness | MEDIUM | If the El-names are later aetiological constructions by sanctuary priesthoods |
| 3. Family piety was pre-cultic, pre-political, pre-moral | MEDIUM | If Albertz has suppressed moral/ethical dimensions of family religion; if the "contrast" is a Lutheran projection |
| 4. No nomad/sedentary opposition; Passover from transhumance ritual | MEDIUM-HIGH on symbiosis; LOW-MEDIUM on Passover | The Exodus-Passover connection is ancient and deeply embedded |
| 5. Ancestor cult present but already suppressed | LOW-MEDIUM | Bloch-Smith's burial evidence suggests more robust ongoing cult of the dead |

**Strongest section:** The rejection of Alt's "God of the Fathers" and the sociological reinterpretation of patriarchal religion as family piety (Claim 1). This is Albertz at his best — using social theory to resolve a genuine puzzle in the evidence.

**Weakest section:** The ancestor cult discussion (Claim 5). Albertz seems to want it both ways — acknowledging the evidence for ancestor veneration while downplaying its scope to preserve his picture of family piety as primarily oriented toward the personal father-god.

**Key cross-reference:** Dever's *Did God Have a Wife?* documents extensive archaeological evidence for domestic cult (female figurines, household shrines) that complement Albertz's textual reconstruction of family piety. Dever would agree that family religion was a distinct stratum, but would emphasize the goddess dimension more than Albertz does here (Albertz barely mentions goddesses in this section).

**What to watch:** As Albertz moves to §2.2 (the Exodus group), the contrast between family piety (unwarlike, unconditional, pre-moral) and Yahweh religion (warlike, conditional, ethical) will become the engine of his narrative. If that contrast is overblown, the narrative loses its dramatic tension.
