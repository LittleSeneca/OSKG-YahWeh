33|# Keel & Uehlinger — Part I: Equality of the Sexes — Middle Bronze Age IIB (1750–1550 BCE)
34|
35|Part I covers the first flourishing period of Canaanite urban culture with a distinct iconographic tradition, establishing the baseline against which all later Iron Age developments must be measured. The title — "Equality of the Sexes" — signals the core finding: male and female deities, symbols, and worshipers appear in roughly balanced proportions. The goddess is not marginal, not a consort-afterthought, but a fully independent power represented more frequently in metal figurines than any male deity. This is the religious world into which — centuries later — Yahweh would enter.
36|
37|The chapter covers nine topics: caprids and lions (§10–12), the Hippopotamus Goddess and Q-symbol (§13–14), the Naked Goddess, goddess heads, and trees (§15–17), cult installations at Nahariyah and Gezer (§18–20), the temples of Tel Kitan and Megiddo (§21–22), the weather god and his consort (§23–24), the falcon-headed figure (§25), the ruler with worshipers (§26–27), and couples (§28).

41|## Claim 1: Caprids and lions are gender-ambiguous symbols revealing gender fluidity
→ [[claim-caprids-lions-gender-ambiguous-egyptian-near-eastern-context]] | **keel-ggi-p1.1** | Confidence: high
  Caprids (virility, goddess's sphere) and lions (Egyptian = pharaoh, Near Eastern = Mistress of the Animals) cannot be assigned to a single gender sphere without full iconographic context. Reveals a pre-monotheistic imagination without rigid gender segregation.
  Contradicts: Hornung and Staehelin (1976 — all as generic "wild beasts")

## Claim 2: Naked Goddess transformed into Canaanite "Branch Goddess" on mass-market scarabs
→ [[claim-naked-goddess-transformed-branch-goddess-mass-market-scarabs]] | **keel-ggi-p1.2** | Confidence: high
  82% branch association on 44 scarabs; two-thirds frontal view. Cylinder seal goddess (elite, erotic, consort-focused) → scarab goddess (popular, vegetation-focused, worshipped directly). Democratization of goddess religion a thousand years before Dever's "folk religion."
  Contradicts: "Hathor fetish" interpretation; generic "fertility amulet" readings

## Claim 3: Q-symbol and Hippopotamus Goddess = female-centered apotropaic religion
→ [[claim-q-symbol-hippopotamus-goddess-female-apotropaic-religion]] | **keel-ggi-p1.3** | Confidence: medium-high
  Hippo Goddess (Thoeris) protects mothers and children. Q-symbol = mother's womb, persists ~1200 years from MB IIB through Iron Age IIC. Female-centered religion addressing pregnancy, childbirth, infant mortality — invisible in texts, revealed by iconography.
  Contradicts: Q-symbol as potter's mark or abstract decoration

## Claim 4: Nahariyah/Gezer goddess cults — massebot can represent female deities
→ [[claim-nahariyah-gezer-goddess-cult-massebah-can-be-female]] | **keel-ggi-p1.4** | Confidence: high
  Nahariyah: steatite mold for mass-producing goddess figurines, 19 figurines in hoard, dove figures. Gezer: bronze goddess figurine from cache. Tel Kitan: stele shaped as naked woman with sculpted genital lips — unambiguous female massebah. Reframes Iron Age massebot interpretation.
  Contradicts: Scholars maintaining massebot are exclusively male/phallic; Kempinski's earlier dating of Gezer

## Claim 5: MB IIB weather god = branch-holding vegetation deity, not warrior-smiter
→ [[claim-mb-iib-weather-god-vegetation-branch-not-warrior]] | **keel-ggi-p1.5** | Confidence: medium-high
  Scarabs show weather god without weapons — raised hand = victory gesture, other hand holds branch/blossom. Armed type exists (cylinder seals, figurines) but branch-holding type dominates. Projects LB warrior image backward is anachronistic. Yahweh's warrior aspect may have developed later than standard models assume.
  Contradicts: Smith (2001), Day (2000), Cross (1973) — all project Ugaritic warrior Baal backward onto MB IIB

## Claim 6: Falcon-headed figure = deliberate Horus-Baal syncretism for Hyksos legitimation
→ [[claim-falcon-headed-figure-horus-baal-canaanite-egyptian-syncretism]] | **keel-ggi-p1.6** | Confidence: medium-high
  Jasper group cylinder seals (1720-1650 BCE): falcon-headed figure with Egyptian double crown = Horus. Scarabs: falcon head preserved, double crown lost, branch/blossom added from Canaanite weather god. Merging of royal gods for political-ideological purposes. Foreshadows later Yahweh solarization.
  Contradicts: Purely Egyptian (Re) or purely Canaanite (Horon) identification; Egyptian reaction was hostile

## Claim 7: Fringed-garment ruler = deified Canaanite kingship mediating divine-human boundary
→ [[claim-fringed-garment-ruler-deified-canaanite-kingship-mediator]] | **keel-ggi-p1.7** | Confidence: medium-high
  Dominant human figure in MB IIB glyptic. Appears as both supplicant and object of veneration on cylinder seals. Enemy prostrate beneath throne. Stelae from Tell Beit Mirsim and Shechem. Emerges ~1700-1650 when Canaanite city-state kings asserted independence. Israelite anti-monarchic polemic reacts against this model.
  Contradicts: Scholars reading figure as pious ruler without divine claims

## Claim 8: Erotic couples mirror divine intimacy — sexuality integrated into MB IIB religion
→ [[claim-erotic-couples-mirror-divine-sexuality-mb-iib-religion]] | **keel-ggi-p1.8** | Confidence: medium-high
  Divine couple (weather god + disrobing goddess) on cylinder seals. Human couples on scarabs drinking together with Ishtar/Astarte symbols (star, dove). "Such representations are common for the Middle Bronze Age but disappear in the later periods." The Hebrew Bible's suppression of sexuality from the sacred sphere reacted against this integration.
  Contradicts: "Sacred marriage" as literary motif only; scenes = court entertainment rather than religious

307|**Key methodological contribution:** The chapter demonstrates Keel & Uehlinger's methodology in action: treating iconography as a system where meaning is determined by combinations (branch + goddess = fertility, uraeus + lion = protection), context (Egyptian vs. Near Eastern tradition history), and medium (cylinder seal vs. scarab = different audiences and functions). This is the approach that will make the Iron Age chapters so powerful — by the time they reach Iron Age IIB–C, they've established how to read combinations, contexts, and media shifts.

309|**Cross-reference connections:**
310|- **Dever (Chapter V):** The JPF evidence from Iron Age Judah is the clear Iron Age descendant of the MB IIB goddess figurine tradition documented at Nahariyah and Gezer. Dever's "folk religion" has a 1,000-year prehistory that Keel & Uehlinger are documenting here. The transformation from MB IIB Branch Goddess (genitalia emphasis, arms at sides, branch flanking) to Iron Age JPFs (breast emphasis, hands supporting breasts) is a story the later chapters will need to tell.
311|- **Smith (Origins):** Smith's West Semitic conceptual structures (divine family, divine council) are reconstructed from Ugaritic texts (14th–13th c. BCE). Keel & Uehlinger's MB IIB evidence shows a divine pair (weather god + goddess) that precedes the Ugaritic texts by centuries. This suggests Smith's conceptual structures have deeper roots than Ugarit, which supports his argument for broad West Semitic patterns — but the MB IIB evidence is geographically closer to Israel than Ugarit, which supports Keel & Uehlinger's argument that you don't need Ugarit to understand Canaanite religion.
312|- **Römer (Introduction):** Römer argues Yahweh was not always Israel's god and that the Bible preserves polytheistic traces. Keel & Uehlinger's MB IIB evidence shows the polytheistic baseline — this is what religion looked like in Canaan before Yahweh arrived. The weather god + goddess pair, the female-centered fertility cult, the divine kingship — all of these are the religious structures that Israelite religion either inherited, adapted, or rejected. The "equality of the sexes" in the chapter title is significant: monotheism around a male deity would represent a radical break from this balanced system.
313|
314|**For the Truth Project:** Part I establishes that the pre-Yahwistic religious baseline in Canaan was characterized by (1) roughly balanced male and female divine representation, (2) a goddess cult with substantial institutional infrastructure (molds, workshops, permanent installations), (3) a weather god who was primarily a fertility/vegetation consort, not a warrior, (4) permeable boundaries between divine and human (king as mediator, human couples mirroring divine couples), and (5) a religious imagination comfortable with combining Egyptian and Near Eastern elements. Every one of these features will be transformed, suppressed, or polemicized against as Yahwism develops. The later chapters will show how — and how incompletely.
315|
316|**Summary note (pp. 47–48):** The authors' own summary emphasizes the creative merging of Egyptian and Near Eastern traditions, the goddess's dominance over the male deity in representational frequency, and the crucial observation that "the religious system of signs that used massebahs, sacred trees, deities and rituals that had a patently sexual character, regarded by many as typical of 'the' Canaanite religion for the entire period from 1500 to 500, may in reality have played a decisive role only during Middle Bronze Age IIB and might have merely undergone radical modifications during the following periods, surviving only in a marginal way as the religion of the pagani, the villagers" (p. 48). This is a warning against flattening "Canaanite religion" into a timeless monolithic category — what biblical texts condemn as "Canaanite" may actually describe religious practices that were already marginal or transformed by the time the texts were written. The biblical polemic may be against a memory, not a living reality.
