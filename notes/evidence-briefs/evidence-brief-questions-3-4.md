## QUESTION 3: DID YAHWEH HAVE A WIFE? (Kuntillet Ajrud, Khirbet el-Qom, Asherah Debate)

### (a) SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS — KEY EVIDENCE

**The Majority Position: Yes, Asherah was a goddess and Yahweh's consort** (Dever, Römer, Day, Olyan, Zevit, Ackerman, Keel/Uehlinger on the cult symbol as mediating divine presence)

#### 1. The Kuntillet Ajrud Inscriptions (c. 800 BCE)

Two pithos inscriptions from a Judean caravanserai in northeast Sinai:

- **Pithos A:** "I bless you all by Yahweh of Samaria and by his Asherah" (*lyhwh šmrn wl'šrth*)
- **Pithos B:** "I bless you to Yahweh of Teman and to his Asherah" (*lyhwh tmn wl'šrth*)

The formula pairs Yahweh with Asherah in a blessing context. The possessive suffix *-h* on *'šrth* is the crux of the debate — can a personal name take a possessive suffix in Hebrew? The majority say yes in this case, reading it as "his Asherah" (goddess). Smith and Keel/Uehlinger say no — Hebrew grammar forbids it — and read it as "his asherah" (cult object).

These were royal Judean inscriptions, not marginal folk doodles. The wall plaster fragments also mention Yahweh and El.

#### 2. The Khirbet el-Qom Inscription (c. 700 BCE)

A burial inscription excavated by Dever in 1967 (Tomb 2, Judaean territory):

> "Blessed is Uriyahu by Yahweh, and from his oppressors by his Asherah he has saved him. By Oniyahu."

Same pattern: Yahweh paired with "his Asherah" in a blessing/salvation formula. Unlike Kuntillet Ajrud, no geographic epithet for Yahweh (post-fall-of-Samaria Judah). Associated with a "magic hand" apotropaic symbol.

#### 3. The Ugaritic Background — Asherah as El's Consort

- At Ugarit (14th-13th c. BCE), *Aṯiratu* (Asherah) is El's consort, "Lady Asherah of the Sea," "Creatress/Mother of the Gods," mother of the seventy divine sons
- Mesopotamian attestation as *Ašratu(m)* from Hammurabi era through Seleucid period
- Southern Arabian inscriptions: *Aṯrt* — the goddess
- Römer's argument: "When El and Yhwh merged, Asherah came with the package." The simplest explanation for why Yahweh is paired with Asherah in 8th-century Israel is that the Israelite merger of El and Yahweh brought El's consort along.

#### 4. The Biblical Evidence — Five Passages Where "Asherah" Must Mean a Goddess (Day)

Day identifies five OT passages where *'asherah* unambiguously refers to the goddess, not a cult object:

| Passage | Argument |
|---------|----------|
| **2 Kgs 23:4** | Asherah listed between "the Baal" and "host of heaven" — both deities. "Extremely forced" to read as cult object |
| **2 Kgs 21:7** | Manasseh's *pesel ha'asherah* — "image of the goddess Asherah." An "image of a symbol" would be redundant |
| **1 Kgs 15:13** | Asa removes Maacah's "horrid thing made for Asherah." Making an idolatrous object FOR an idolatrous object "not very plausible" |
| **1 Kgs 18:19** | "Prophets of Baal and prophets of Asherah" — parallelism demands a divine name. (Post-exilic addition, but proves late awareness of Asherah as goddess) |
| **Judg 3:7** | "Serving the Baals and the Asheroth" — plural parallelism makes divine reference "undeniable" |

#### 5. The Figurine Evidence (Dever)

- **3,000+ Judean Pillar Figurines (JPFs)** : nude female, emphasized breasts, hands supporting/holding breasts
- Found in domestic contexts — "almost every excavated 8th-6th century house in Judah"
- Mass-produced from molds — cheap, accessible, ubiquitous
- Most scholars identify them as Asherah representations
- Dever: "prayers in clay" for fertility, conception, safe childbirth
- **Critical refinement (Keel & Uehlinger):** JPFs are Iron Age IIC (late 8th-7th c.), NOT Iron IIB. They represent a REVIVAL of goddess imagery after a hiatus, not continuous worship from the Bronze Age. This is a crucial chronological correction, but it doesn't change the fact of goddess worship — it changes when and how.

#### 6. The Bible's Deliberate Obfuscation (Römer)

- *'Asherah* occurs 40 times in the Bible; the masculine plural form *'asherim* is "an artificial creation to avoid allusion to the goddess"
- The Baal-Asherah link appears ONLY in Deuteronomistic passages — a polemical invention. Extra-biblical texts never pair Baal with Asherah
- "In sharp contrast to the biblical texts, a close link between Yhwh and Asherah is attested in the inscriptions"
- The Deuteronomists rewrote Asherah's story: they associated her with Baal to break the Yhwh link, then destroyed her cult objects under Josiah

#### 7. Additional Archaeological Evidence

- **Taanach cult stand** (10th c.): Four tiers showing nude female, lions, cherubim, tree of life, young bull — "an encyclopedia of Canaanite-Israelite religion"
- **Arad temple:** Two *massebot* (standing stones) in holy of holies — probably Yahweh and Asherah as divine couple
- **Lachish ewer** (13th c.): Tree flanked by ibexes, inscribed "Mattan. An offering to my Lady Elat" — Elat (= Asherah) directly above the tree drawing

#### 8. Elephantine — Post-Exilic Consort Continuation (Day)

At the 5th-century Jewish garrison at Elephantine, Egypt: Anat-Yahu ("Anat of Yahweh") is attested as a deity with a treasury in Yahweh's temple. Day: "It seems indubitable that the goddess Anat, in the form of Anat-Yahu, did function as Yahweh's wife amongst the Jews at Elephantine." Whether this reflects pre-exilic Israelite religion or diaspora development is debated, but it shows consort theology persisted among some Jews centuries after monotheism supposedly emerged.

### (b) COUNTER-POSITIONS

#### Position 1: The Asherah Was a Cult Symbol, Not a Goddess (Smith, minority)

Smith's argument, from his *Early History of God* (1990, rev. 2002):

1. **Grammatical argument:** Hebrew never uses a possessive suffix with a proper name. You can't say "his Mary." The *-h* on *'šrth* forces it to be a common noun ("asherah" = cult object), not a personal name ("Asherah" = goddess).

2. **Biblical passages ambiguous:** Each "goddess" passage can be read as referring to the cult object. 2 Kgs 23:4's asherah IS burned in verse 6 — you can't burn a goddess, you burn a pole. 1 Kgs 18:19's "prophets of Asherah" may be a scribal confusion with Astarte (who is not attested in Phoenician texts as "Asherah").

3. **No onomastic evidence:** No Israelite personal names contain Asherah as a theophoric element (contrast El, Baal, Yahweh, even Anat).

4. **The prophets never polemicize against the goddess** — only against the symbol/pole. Hosea repeatedly names Baal but never once names Asherah.

5. **The asherah was a Yahwistic symbol:** Associated with Yahweh, not Baal. Present in both northern and southern royal cults. No prophetic opposition before the 8th century. "Olyan has argued that the asherah was associated historically with Yahweh and not with Baal."

#### Position 2: Keel & Uehlinger's Refined Symbol Position (iconographic evidence)

This is the most systematic counter-position, based on the largest catalogue of Iron Age seals ever assembled (8,500+):

1. **No goddess in Iron IIB glyptic:** "Not a single seal" from Israel/Judah glyptic art (9th-8th c.) depicts a goddess anthropomorphically. Hebrew name seals show zero goddesses. The absence is statistically significant — these were personal items chosen to represent religious identity.

2. **Kuntillet Ajrud pithoi are paratactic:** The paintings are by different artists at different times — not a unified mythological scene. The "Yahweh and his Asherah" inscription runs across Bes figures (Egyptian apotropaic daemons), not Yahweh and a goddess. Beck's art-historical analysis disproves the "depiction of the divine couple" theory.

3. **Chronological correction on figurines:** Dever's 3,000+ JPFs are Iron IIC (7th century), NOT the period of the Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions (8th century). Dever conflates periods. During the very time the "Yahweh and his Asherah" inscriptions were written, goddess imagery was MINIMAL.

4. **"His asherah" = cultic symbol (stylized tree):** The grammatical argument (no possessive suffix on proper names), the singular-verb blessing formulas (Yahweh alone blesses), and the iconographic evidence (asherah consistently depicted as stylized tree) converge on the symbol reading.

5. **No divine couple imagery:** "There are no pictures of a pair of deities in Judah." Even during the JPF revival in IIC, the pillar figurines are never grouped with male figurines as couples.

**Keel & Uehlinger's nuanced conclusion:** The asherah was a cultic symbol that *mediated* Yahweh's blessing — a stylized tree that functioned as a substitute for the goddess. "Yahweh's asherah does not have equal rank with Yahweh but is rather a mediating entity." The experience of ordinary Israelites may have been more goddess-like than the official theology, but the iconographic record shows the asherah as subordinate mediating entity, not consort.

#### Position 3: The Kaufmann Position — No Genuine Polytheism at All

Kaufmann argues Israelite "idolatry" was vestigial fetishism — using figurines and charms without genuine mythological belief. The asherah was a fetish, not a goddess. His argument rests on:

- The biblical polemic is against cultic PRACTICE ("wood and stone," "handiwork of men"), never against mythological THEOLOGY
- No native Israelite theogonies, divine biographies, or mythological epics exist
- Archaeological evidence (Kuntillet Ajrud, which he never addresses directly) would be classified as "vestigial fetishism"

This is the firmest counter-position but widely rejected today because it defines "genuine polytheism" too narrowly and ignores the evidence of blessing formulas that pair Yahweh with Asherah.

### (c) POINTS OF AGREEMENT

Across all positions, scholars agree on:

1. **The asherah was a widespread, persistent element of Israelite religion** throughout the monarchy — in both north and south, in both royal and popular contexts
2. **The asherah was associated with Yahweh, not Baal** — Olyan's argument has been broadly accepted
3. **The Deuteronomistic reformers violently opposed the asherah** and Josiah removed it from the Jerusalem Temple (2 Kgs 23:6-7)
4. **The biblical text deliberately obscures the Yahweh-Asherah relationship** — whether to hide a goddess (Römer/Day) or because they didn't know what it was (Dever)
5. **The Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom inscriptions are authentic and pair Yahweh with "his asherah"** — the dispute is over what *'šrth* means, not whether the inscriptions exist
6. **The Ugaritic Asherah (Athirat) was El's consort** — this background is uncontested
7. **The JPFs represent some form of female divine presence** in Judahite domestic religion — the dispute is about chronology (IIB vs. IIC) and whether they represent a goddess per se

### (d) DISPUTES

| Issue | Majority (Dever/Römer/Day) | Smith | Keel & Uehlinger |
|-------|---------------------------|-------|------------------|
| *'šrth* = goddess or symbol? | Goddess | Symbol | Symbol (mediating entity) |

### (e) DIRECT QUOTES

**Römer:** "In sharp contrast to the biblical texts, a close link between Yhwh and Asherah is attested in the inscriptions."

**Day:** "There can therefore be no doubt that there are several places in the Old Testament where the word 'Asherah' denotes the goddess herself, and not simply the cult symbol of Asherah."

**Dever:** "It is precisely my intent here to demonstrate that a cult of Asherah did flourish in ancient Israel... The biblical writers' embarrassment about a 'Hebrew Goddess' led them to systematically obscure her, but they could not erase her entirely."

**Smith (2001, shifting from earlier skepticism):** "Currently... most scholars believe that Asherah was a goddess in ancient Israel, possibly even Yahweh's consort. In short, the jury seems still to be out on the issues, which are undeniably complex."

**Keel & Uehlinger:** "Was the female form of Asherah in seventh-century Jerusalem the partner/consort of Yahweh? As attractive as such a conclusion might be, it is difficult to support. Even now, we have no clear evidence for a strict partner/consort relationship between Yahweh and Asherah."

**Keel & Uehlinger (on the grammar):** "The controversial reference to *'šrth* in the inscription from Kuntillet Ajrud thus most likely refers to such a cultic object... 'Yahweh's asherah' does not have equal rank with Yahweh but is rather a mediating entity that brings his blessing."

**Smith (on his own position):** "What would it have meant to say that the goddess belonged to Yahweh?" (citing Zevit's question)

**Lewis (on Keel & Uehlinger's Beth Shean seal):** "The difficulties in interpreting the evidence from this cylinder seal provide enough reasonable doubt that such a certain verdict cannot be rendered."

**Overall assessment for Q3:** The weight of cumulative evidence — Ugaritic background, two separate inscriptions pairing Yahweh with "his asherah" in blessing formulas, five OT passages where the term must refer to a goddess, the 3,000+ figurines, and the Deuteronomistic effort to suppress and rewrite — strongly favors Asherah as a goddess who was Yahweh's consort in popular/monarchic Israelite religion. The counter-arguments from Smith and Keel & Uehlinger raise genuine methodological concerns (grammar, iconographic absence in IIB, lack of divine couple imagery) but their alternative — a mediating cult symbol that somehow attracted the same fierce polemic as rival deities — requires more explanation than the simpler consort model. The truth likely lies in Keel & Uehlinger's nuanced insight: the asherah was a cultic symbol that *functioned* as a goddess for many worshipers, even if elite/official theology framed it as subordinate to Yahweh.

## QUESTION 4: WHAT WAS ISRAELITE RELIGION ACTUALLY LIKE?

### (a) BOOK RELIGION VS. FOLK RELIGION — THE TWO-RELIGIONS THESIS

Dever's framework, now broadly accepted:

**Book Religion** (the Bible's prescribed norm):
- Monotheistic — Yahweh alone
- Aniconic — no images
- Centralized — Jerusalem Temple only
- Male, priestly, literate
- The religion of MAYBE 5% of Israelites
- A "minority report" that won by writing the history

**Folk Religion** (what archaeology reveals):
- Polytheistic in practice — multiple deities, divine couples
- Image-rich — figurines, standing stones, cult stands
- Local and domestic — household shrines, high places
- Women-led — domestic religion was women's domain
- The religion of 95% of Israelites

**Key evidence for the gap between ideal and reality:**

- **High places (*bāmôt*) were originally legitimate Yahwistic sites.** Saul, Samuel, Solomon all worshipped at them without censure. The Deuteronomists' footnote: "The people were sacrificing at the high places, however, because no house had yet been built for the name of the Lord" (1 Kgs 3:2).
- **The Jerusalem Temple was a royal chapel**, not a national center. "Many people in ancient Israel had probably never been to Jerusalem in their whole lives" (Dever). The requirement of three annual pilgrimages was "the ideal, not the reality."
- **Literacy was below 5%.** "Ordinary people could not have read the Bible if they had had it" (Dever).
- **The population was tiny:** Israel the size of New Jersey; Jerusalem max 5,000 people; total population never exceeding 150,000; life expectancy ~30 years; 80-90% in hamlets of a few hundred.

### (b) ONOMASTIC EVIDENCE FROM TIGAY — THE COUNTER-WEIGHT

Tigay's *You Shall Have No Other Gods* (1986) provides the strongest empirical challenge to the "rampant polytheism" narrative:

**Biblical names:**
- 466 individuals with theophoric names: 413 (89%) Yahwistic, 53 (11%) plausibly pagan
- Pagan names cluster early (Judges/United Monarchy: 14%); drop to 3% by Divided Monarchy
- The 8 *ba'al* names underwent deliberate scribal revision (Eshbaal → Ishbosheth, etc.)

**Epigraphic names (seals, ostraca, bullae, letters) — even MORE Yahwistic:**
- The inscriptions preserve fewer pagan names because there was no editorial process to filter
- Tigay's Appendices A-E list them exhaustively — the Yahwistic dominance is overwhelming

**Letters and blessing formulas:**
- ALL Israelite letters invoke YHWH alone — contrast Phoenician (multiple deities) and Assyrian (Ashur and others)
- The standard formula: "I bless you by YHWH" (*brktk lyhwh*)
- Oaths, graffiti, temple vessel inscriptions, amulets — all YHWH-only

**Tigay's seven conclusions:**

1. **Polytheism was limited, not rampant:** "After the United Monarchy, and perhaps even earlier, the evidence currently available makes it very difficult to suppose that many Israelites worshipped gods other than YHWH."
2. **The biblical writers magnified polytheism, not minimized it:** The prophetic indictments "are based more on theological axioms than historical data" — the exile demanded an explanation; polytheism provided it.
3. **Even court-connected circles were not polytheistic:** The inscriptions from Manasseh's time don't show the polytheism the Bible attributes to his reign.
4. **What polytheism existed was mostly fetishistic** (following Kaufmann): using figurines and charms without genuine mythological theology.
5. **Indifference to nature gods implies implicit monotheism:** Israelites ignored sun, rain, and fertility gods — deities whose spheres weren't limited to specific nations.
6. **Population estimates:** Even 5-10% polytheists in Jerusalem (500-2,400 people) would be visible enough to alarm prophets without being the majority.
7. **The biblical onomasticon was not systematically censored:** The *bosheth* revisions are exceptions that prove the rule.

### (c) FIGURINE EVIDENCE FROM DEVER — THE OTHER SIDE OF THE COIN

Dever's archaeological data is the direct counter-weight to Tigay:

**Eight categories of evidence for folk polytheistic religion:**

1. **Local shrines** — At least a dozen excavated from 12th-7th c.: Tell el-Wawiyat, 'Ai, Kh. Raddana, Megiddo Shrine 2048, Tel Rehov, Tel 'Amal, Tell el-Farah, Lachish Cult Room 49. Domestic, unsupervised, woman-centered.

2. **Standing stones (*massebot*)** — Found at nearly every excavated Israelite site. At Arad: two stones in holy of holies ("representing Yhwh and another deity").

3. **Altars** — Large four-horned stone altar at Beer-Sheba dismantled during Hezekiah's reform; small portable incense altars at domestic sites.

4. **Figurines** — 3,000+ Judean Pillar Figurines, found in domestic contexts, mass-produced, overwhelmingly 8th-6th c. "Found in almost every excavated 8th-6th century house in Judah."

5. **Cult stands** — Taanach stand: nude female, lions, cherubim, tree of life, young bull.

6. **Inscriptions** — Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom pairing Yahweh with "his asherah."

7. **Votive offerings** — Miniature vessels, beads, amulets, jewelry at shrines.

8. **Tombs and mortuary practices** — Bench tombs, food and drink offerings, ancestor cult — practices condemned in the Bible but archaeologically ubiquitous.

### (d) INSCRIPTIONS SHOWING POLYTHEISTIC TRACES

**Kuntillet Ajrud:** "Yahweh of Samaria/Teman and his Asherah" — blessing paired with a divine entity alongside Yahweh.

**Khirbet el-Qom:** Same pairing in a burial blessing. "Magic hand" apotropaic symbol.

**Deuteronomy 32:8-9 + 4QDeut-j (Qumran):**
- MT (theologically corrected): "according to the number of the sons of Israel"
- 4QDeut-j (earliest recoverable): "according to the number of the sons of God" (*bene 'el*)
- The passage distinguishes Elyon (Most High) who distributes nations from YHWH who receives Israel — two deities in view, reflecting older polytheistic cosmology

**Deut 32:43 (4QDeut-q + LXX):** "Bow down to him all you gods" — calling on gods to worship YHWH, preserving a more polytheistic original.

**1 Kings 18:19:** "Prophets of Baal and prophets of Asherah" — even if a post-exilic addition, proves continued awareness of Asherah as goddess.

**Manasseh's reign (2 Kgs 21:7):** Installation of *pesel ha'asherah* (image of the asherah) in the Jerusalem Temple — irrefutable evidence that a goddess image stood in Yahweh's own house during the 7th century.

**Elephantine papyri (5th c. BCE):** Anat-Yahu ("Anat of Yahweh") worshipped with a treasury in Yahweh's temple by diaspora Jews.

### (e) HOW POLYTHEISTIC PRACTICE AND YAHWISTIC NAMES CAN BE TRUE SIMULTANEOUSLY — THE SYNTHESIS

The apparent contradiction — Tigay's data showing overwhelmingly Yahwistic personal names vs. Dever's data showing polytheistic practice — is resolvable when understood as different dimensions of the same religious system:

**1. Public face vs. private practice:**

Tigay's evidence (names, letters, seals, oaths) captures the PUBLIC/VERBAL dimension of religion — how people identified themselves, wrote letters, sealed documents. Dever's evidence (figurines, shrines, cult stands) captures the PRIVATE/MATERIAL dimension — what people did at home for fertility, healing, and protection. These are not contradictory. A Judean official could seal documents with his Yahwistic name AND have an Asherah figurine in his house. Yahweh was how you identified publicly; Asherah was who you turned to for conception and safe childbirth.

**2. Hierarchical polytheism, not egalitarian polytheism:**

Keel & Uehlinger's evidence suggests Israelite religion was hierarchical: Yahweh at the top, with subordinated mediating entities (asherah, cherubs, Bes figures, solar powers) below him. This IS polytheism — just not egalitarian polytheism with a divine couple on equal footing. The asherah was subordinate to Yahweh, mediating his blessing. This fits both the inscriptions ("may HE bless you" — singular verb, Yahweh is the active agent; "his asherah" is the medium) and the figurines (she's important enough to have images but never depicted as Yahweh's equal).

**3. Functional polytheism without mythological elaboration:**

Kaufmann was partially right: Israelite polytheism lacked the elaborate mythology (theogonies, divine biographies, epic conflicts) that characterized Mesopotamian and Ugaritic religion. It was a PRACTICE-based polytheism — you used figurines, visited shrines, made offerings — without a developed theology of multiple gods. Most ancient polytheism was like this. The "Queen of Heaven" worshippers in Jeremiah 44 baked cakes and poured libations; they didn't recite theogonies. The distinction between "genuine polytheism" (with mythology) and "vestigial fetishism" (without) is a modern scholarly imposition.

**4. Tigay's data is skewed toward elite/literate contexts:**

Inscriptions come disproportionately from administrative and military contexts — seals, ostraca from forts, letters — the very circles most likely to be Yahwistic. Women (the primary users of pillar figurines) and rural peasants are underrepresented in the epigraphic record. The figurines are a DIFFERENT dataset from the names — and both are valid.

**5. The centrality/periphery model:**

Yahweh was the CENTRAL deity — the one you named children after, the one in the official cult, the one in letters and oaths. Other divine beings (Asherah, Baal, the Queen of Heaven, astral powers, the dead) occupied the PERIPHERY — important for specific domains (fertility, weather, protection, ancestors) but not replacing Yahweh's centrality. This is how polytheism typically works: one high god at the center, many specialized divine beings at the periphery. Israel was unusual not in having Yahweh at the center but in eventually DENYING the periphery's existence.

**6. The convergence/differentiation framework (Smith):**

- **Convergence (Judges through early monarchy):** Yahweh ABSORBED the characteristics of other deities — El's wisdom and fatherhood, Baal's storm theophany, Asherah's nurturing, the sun's radiance. This made Yahweh increasingly comprehensive — the one god who did what many gods used to do.
- **Differentiation (late monarchy through exile):** The same characteristics that Yahweh had absorbed were CONDEMNED when practiced as independent cults. You can't worship Baal for storms because Yahweh IS the storm-god now. You can't use Asherah figurines for fertility because Yahweh IS the one who opens the womb. The reform wasn't a return to original purity — it was an INNOVATION of exclusivity.

This framework explains the paradox: Israelites used Yahwistic names because Yahweh had absorbed all divine functions into himself, making him the comprehensive deity. Yet they also used figurines and practiced domestic cults because old religious habits die hard, and because different life-domains (fertility, childbearing, ancestor care) had always been the province of other divine beings.

**7. The Keel & Uehlinger synthesis — three simultaneous currents in Iron IIC:**

The 7th century shows the full complexity:
- **Astralization** under Assyrian influence (Moon God of Haran identified with El/Yahweh, astral symbol system)
- **Goddess revival** in domestic piety (3,000+ pillar figurines, Queen of Heaven cult)
- **Aniconic orthodoxy** among Jerusalem elite (70% of Hebrew name seals are image-free by late 7th c.; Ketef Hinnom silver amulets use TEXT instead of images for divine protection)

All three coexisted simultaneously. The "monotheistic reform" narrative is not wrong — it's just that it describes ONE of three currents, and the one that was initially the weakest. It won because its adherents survived the exile and wrote the history.

### (f) THE CUMULATIVE PORTRAIT OF ISRAELITE RELIGION

**What Israelite religion was NOT:**
- NOT the "pure Mosaic monotheism" of traditional theology
- NOT "rampant Canaanite polytheism" either — Yahweh was always central
- NOT a single, unified system — it was diverse, contested, and changing

**What Israelite religion WAS:**
- A hierarchical polytheism with Yahweh as the supreme national god
- A religion where public/official practice (names, letters, Temple cult) centered on Yahweh
- A religion where private/domestic practice included goddess figurines, ancestor veneration, local shrines, and multiple mediating divine entities
- A religion that ABSORBED traits from El, Baal, Asherah, and solar/astral deities into Yahweh's profile
- A religion that gradually DIFFERENTIATED from its Canaanite heritage, especially after the 8th century
- A religion where women's domestic practice was systematically suppressed by male priestly reformers
- A religion where monotheism emerged as a late, elite, text-based innovation — not an original revelation

**The three-book synthesis (Smith + Römer + Dever):**

| Dimension | Smith | Römer | Dever |
|-----------|-------|-------|-------|
| Primary evidence | Ugaritic texts, biblical texts | Biblical texts, inscriptions, history | Archaeological artifacts |
| Method | Text-critical, comparative ANE | Historical reconstruction | Material culture, ethnography |
| Asherah | Symbol (minority), later agnostic | Goddess, Yahweh's consort | Goddess, Yahweh's consort |
| Monotheism's emergence | Exile; convergence/differentiation | Exile; political crisis | Exile; elite "Book religion" |
| Key contribution | Framework for understanding development | Narrative of how God was "invented" | Proof that folk religion was polytheistic |

**Where all three converge:**
- Israelite religion was polytheistic in practice until the late monarchy
- Monotheism emerged in the exile
- The biblical narrative of pure origins → corruption → reform is exactly backward
- Yahweh absorbed characteristics of multiple earlier deities
- The Bible is a "minority report" (Dever) — the polemic of the winners

**Where Tigay provides a necessary corrective:**
- The onomastic evidence shows Yahweh was OVERWHELMINGLY dominant in how Israelites named themselves and wrote letters
- "Rampant polytheism" overstates the case — polytheism was real but limited
- The truth is between "rampant polytheism" and "pure monotheism": a society where Yahweh was central in public identity but domestic practice included other divine beings

**Kaufmann's partial insight:**
- Israelite polytheism DID differ from Mesopotamian/Canaanite polytheism — no native theogonies, no divine biographies, no mythological epics
- The biblical polemic IS against "wood and stone" rather than against rival theologies
- The disappearance of Israelite idolatry after the exile IS historically anomalous
- But Kaufmann's conclusion (original monotheism) is wrong — the evidence shows gradual convergence toward monotheism, not original monotheism requiring later cultic enforcement

**The people's religion in one sentence:** Most Israelites worshipped Yahweh as their supreme national god, used figurines and local shrines for fertility and domestic concerns, named their children after Yahweh, wrote letters invoking Yahweh alone, and would have been shocked to learn that later theology would declare their household piety "idolatry." The religion of the Bible was the religion of the minority who won — not the religion of the majority who lived and died in the villages of Israel and Judah.

### (g) BIBLIOGRAPHY OF SOURCES
